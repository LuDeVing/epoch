## 1. What the loop does today (read from the code)

- `deepen.premortem_prompt` runs once per interrogated root idea with one to three packs (`facts`, `script-only`, `evidence`). The default `--premortems 1` runs only `facts`. It returns objections tagged high/medium/low plus one VERDICT sentence. The prompt asks for "the cheapest check that would show it", but `parse_premortem` keeps 300 characters of text, and the interrogation's ASSUMES list is never shown to the critic. The `evidence` pack is cut at 30 lines, so diagnostics drop out when the brief is long.
- Objections reach the builder as "WATCH FOR ... address each" (`loop.knowledge_for`). They do not touch selection and are never scored.
- `predict.py` parses three claim types (parameters, step time, loss gap), checks them against fixed tolerances at the ladder, and feeds the result into `history_text`. No probabilities, no intervals, no record per card.
- `controls.py` audits the ladder (placebo, winners, a loser), not the critic. `calibrate.py` joins cluster features to ladder and overnight outcomes, not objections.
- Data: about 8 builds per run (6 then 2) against a few dozen pre-mortems (12 ideas interrogated per generation in round 1, 6 later). The two real runs predate the pre-mortem stage; mock runs repeat the same two objections for every idea. Critic quality is unmeasured today.

## 2. Evidence

**Pre-mortem and prospective hindsight: thin, and about confidence. No effect size on decision or project outcomes was found.**
- The "+30% more correct reasons" figure travels via Klein; the abstract mirror of the 1989 paper says temporal perspective "had little influence" and outcome certainty drove the differences, so 30% is unverified here [1][2] (D).
- Veinott et al. 2010 (178 students, hypothetical H1N1 plan): the pre-mortem cut overconfidence more than critique or pros-and-cons. It is the only study Klein cites there, and it measures confidence, not outcomes [1] (C). A thesis with 52 cadets found fewer fouls and less fixation than standard plan review, but against a worst-case method reasons were equal in one experiment and higher in another [3] (C).
- No controlled test of an LLM pre-mortem prompt was found. Nearest: a pre-registered study of 23,256 LLM revision plans (SESOI d=0.15). Telling the model to "anticipate" added nothing beyond real prior reviews; the reviews raised coverage of later concerns; multi-agent debate degraded results [4] (B, other domain, abstract only).
- Related: teaching people to build test cases from the opposite of each hypothesis property lifted rule discovery from 37% to 78% (N=180) [5] (B); falsification instructions changed what 293 police officers proposed next but not their verdicts [6] (B); verbalising counter-evidence moved confidence d≈0.25 to 0.29, no control arm [7] (C). The outside view (base rates from comparable past cases) is the standard counter to inside-view optimism; the evidence cited is one worked case [43] (D).

**Red teams and devil's advocates: no outcome evidence.** Red-team and ACH literatures give anecdote and intent, no controlled gain [8][9] (D). Assigned devil's advocacy is reported weaker than authentic dissent and can harden views [10] (nf, C). Debate lifts weak judges (76% vs 48% for models, 88% vs 60% for humans) [11] (B), mainly under information asymmetry and mixed elsewhere [12] (B), and often fails to beat chain-of-thought or self-consistency (GSM8K, 9 answers: 83.0 vs 88.2) [13][14] (B). Dissent helps when the critic knows something the builder does not.

**LLM critics: what makes them useful.**
- Trained and scored on planted flaws: OpenAI's critics learned from contractor-inserted bugs and were preferred to human critiques 62.8% of the time on natural bugs; human+critic teams hallucinate less than the critic alone; longer critiques catch more and nitpick more; no calibration analysis; no significant help on competition code [15] (B).
- Weak without a signal: recall at most 21.1% and precision at most 6.1% on 91 real paper errors [16] (B); off-the-shelf frontier models at chance on which of two AI-research ideas wins, 77% when fine-tuned with retrieval [17] (B); concerns are characterised well (0.754) but "was it addressed, with evidence?" only 0.501 [18] (C); self-preference is not reduced by capability across 20 models [19] (C); style bias 0.10 to 0.76 against position at most 0.04 [20] (B).
- Calibration: verbalised confidence roughly halves ECE against token probabilities [21] yet failure-prediction AUROC stays near 0.52 to 0.61 [22] (B); a judge's selective accuracy fell 82.8% to 56.9% as wins accumulated and recovered to 83.5% with focused memory of similar past outcomes [23] (C); monitors scored AUROC 0.527 cold, 0.885 recalibrated [24] (C).
- Tests beat opinions: pilot experiments lifted a research preference model 0.684 to 0.711 to 0.729 [25] (C); a trained test generator beat other LLM test generators by 3.2 to 12.4 points pass@1 in debugging, with a trade-off between revealing inputs and correct expected outputs [26] (B); execution plus execution-free verifiers reach 51% against 42 to 43% alone [27] (B); mutation-guided tests at Meta: 571 tests, 73% accepted, 36% relevant to the target concern [28] (B); structured pre-action conditions raised SWE-bench Verified Pass@1 by 4.8 to 11.8 points and beat an equivalent prose summary [29] (C); recomputing from tool outputs caught 60% of failures (96% with a coverage check) with no false positives, a learned monitor 54% at 17% false positives [24] (C); process supervision beats outcome supervision [30] (B).

**Self-correction needs an external signal, and the gap is shrinking.** Intrinsic self-correction lowered accuracy (GPT-4 GSM8K 95.5 to 89.0; GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA 75.8 to 41.8); earlier gains used oracle labels to stop [13] (A). A survey finds no prompted success outside exceptionally suited tasks, and success with reliable external feedback or fine-tuning [31] (A). Models fail to find reasoning errors but fix them given the location [32]; self-repair is bottlenecked by feedback quality [33] (B). Newer: a 64.5% blind spot in 14 non-reasoning models, small or negative in reasoning models, and an appended "Wait" removes 89.3% of it [34] (B); RL training gives +15.6% on MATH [35] (B). For a strong reasoning builder the 2023 penalty is smaller, but only a measurement adjudicates.

**Falsification and pre-registration.** A six-field proposal contract with a stated falsifying outcome ranked four frontier models by test quality, not fluency [36] (C). POPPER designs falsification experiments, pools them with sequential e-value testing for type-I control, and needs a strong LLM (Haiku 3.5 failed the control) [37] (B). Large NHLBI trials: 17 of 30 positive before preregistration, 2 of 25 after, observational [38] (B). Registered reports: about 44% positive against 96% in the standard literature [39] (B, figures secondary); roughly three in four preregistered studies deviate from their plan [40] (C). Freeze the criterion, then audit deviations.

**Adversarial collaboration.** Cogitate pre-registered divergent predictions for two theories (256 participants, three modalities) and challenged both [41] (B); it is "most feasible when least needed" [42] (D).

## 3. Improvement ideas for this part

Rules from the evidence: end doubt in a measurement; give the critic evidence the builder lacks; score the critic on planted flaws and outcomes before it influences anything; freeze the kill criterion before the build; doubt the wins as well as the ideas. Already have: three-pack pre-mortem, severity tags, forwarding, point-claim prediction check, placebo/positive/loser controls, fidelity probe, `--no-premortem`.

**C1. Objection ledger: each objection becomes a typed, testable claim (new).** Mechanism: extend `PREMORTEM_FORMAT` with class (breaks_constraint, hidden_cost, interaction_cancels, early_only, below_noise, impl_slip, metric_blind, scale_only), the ASSUMES or PREDICTION line attacked, and `if_true` = metric, direction, threshold, chosen from instruments we own (params ratio, step_ratio, VRAM, learn_z at 45 steps, long_gap/half_gap/trend at 240, fidelity cell) plus two cheap additions, loss at init and overfit-one-batch [47]. Tell the critic which rungs answer anyway, so it spends effort on what only overnight, scale or metric-blind checks reveal. A resolver in `verify`/`long_runs` marks each objection confirmed, refuted, pending or untestable; the builder sees measured resolutions and only the open items. Effect: forwarding becomes testing, and objections a one-minute rung can refute stop costing builder attention (analogs [26][29][18]). Cost: about a day, no extra LLM call. Measure: share machine-resolvable (target 60%), confirm rate by class, builder pass rate with resolutions versus forwarded text.

**C2. Frozen kill criterion with a deviation audit (partial: PREDICTION/TEST are free text).** Mechanism: a `KILL:` field (metric, threshold, rung) parsed to a predicate, hashed with the sketch before the build, evaluated by the ladder; the builder's diff against the TARGET cell, via the fidelity probe, flags deviations. Effect: no post-hoc reading of results, and clean numerators for C5 (analogs [36][38][40]). Cost: half a day. Measure: parsable share, predicate-versus-ladder agreement, deviation rate.

**C3. Evidence-first packs, ASSUMES-targeted, with a genericness filter (partial).** Mechanism: default to the existing `evidence` pack (research brief with ledger verdicts, plus diagnostics), add the nearest past attempts by mechanism-text similarity with their measured deltas instead of the part's whole block (the outside view [43]), and keep `script-only` as the information-poor control. Every objection must cite one ASSUMES or PREDICTION line; objections recurring in over 40% of ideas collapse into a standing checklist that controls handle once. Effect: higher hit rate per objection, fewer nitpicks (analogs [4][15][16][23]). Cost: one extra call per interrogated idea. Measure: per-pack hit rate (confirmed/total), cross-idea similarity, and pre-mortem on versus `--no-premortem` at matched tokens, since nothing shows yet that forwarded objections help the builder.

**C4. Calibrate the critic with seeded flaws now (new; extends `controls.py`).** Mechanism: add five labelled mutants: +30% step time via a redundant matmul (the ladder tolerates 2x, so only the critic or the prediction check can flag it), an early-only schedule trick, a silent no-op, a noise-sized init change, a wrong residual scale. Run the pre-mortem blind on them, the winners and the placebo, once per baseline version and critic model; score class recall on mutants, severity gap against winners, false alarms on the placebo. A critic with no gap is demoted to annotation. Effect: an answer this week to "does the critic tell losers from winners", plus a mutation test of each rung (analogs [15][28][24]). Cost: about 10 critic calls and 6 short ladder runs per version. Measure: severity AUC (n≈10, a sanity gate, not a calibration) and the rung-by-mutant kill matrix.

**C5. Scorecard on outcomes with probabilities and shadow builds (partial: `calibrate.py`, `predict.py`).** Mechanism: the critic emits P(fail rung 3), P(fail overnight) and the class; sketchers give 80% intervals for params delta, step ratio, 45-step and 240-step gaps. Join `deepen.json` in `calibrate.rows_for`; report Brier skill, interval coverage and AUC per pack and class; show each sketcher its last-10 coverage (an A/B in round 2); push confident-wrong cases into `history_text` [44]. Build a random 20% of unselected interrogated ideas to rung 3 so scoring is not restricted to the selector's favourites, and exclude implementation slips [45]. Gate on the critic only at AUC ≥ 0.6 on 200+ resolved ideas (rule from idea-generation brief 07), cutoff set for recall of resolved winners [46]. Effect: a measured answer to "does the critic predict failures"; surprise-weighting cut overconfident failures from 34.2% to 20.9% in RL training [44]. At 8 builds per run, 200 resolved ideas is about 25 runs (17 with shadow builds), hence C4 first. Cost: 1 to 2 days. Measure: reliability diagram, coverage against nominal, Brier skill.

**C6. Winner's pre-mortem chooses the discriminating control (new).** Mechanism: after a rung 3 or 4 pass, a second critic is told the candidate beat the baseline at 240 steps and then failed overnight, at 2x tokens or under matched tuning. It names three reasons, each with the cheapest discriminating run (matched LR/WD duel, parameter-matched control, longer horizon, seed replicate, scale transfer, gate read-out); the cheapest runs before an overnight slot. Effect: fewer overnight slots spent on artefacts; spurious wins are the strongest convergent failure in the field briefs (idea-generation synthesis C3), and pre-agreed discriminating tests are what adversarial collaboration contributes [41][37]. Cost: one call and one 5-minute run per passer. Measure: share exposed as spurious; overnight verification rate with versus without.

**C7. Two independent critics, different evidence and model family, no rebuttal rounds (partial: `--premortems`).** Mechanism: an `evidence` critic on the main model and a `script-only` critic on another family or size; union the objections; never a builder-critic debate. Effect: wider failure coverage if errors are not shared; rebuttal rounds are left out because they showed no gain [4][12][13][14][19]. Cost: one call per idea. Measure: share of observed failure classes anticipated by any objection, marginal yield of the second critic, error correlation.

**C8. Post-mortem labels feed the critic (new).** Mechanism: label every failed or checked candidate with the same class set from `feedback.reason` and metrics; keep a predicted-versus-observed confusion matrix; show the misses as few-shot "your critic missed this" cases in the next round's `evidence` pack. Effect: the critic improves on its own misses (analog: root-cause feedback up to +26% relative success [45]). Cost: small. Measure: top-1 class agreement, recall of observed classes.

**Avoid:** letting any LLM critic rank or gate ideas before C4 and C5 show signal; "anticipate harder" wording as the lever; builder-critic debate; reading severity labels as probabilities; counting implementation slips as idea failures.
