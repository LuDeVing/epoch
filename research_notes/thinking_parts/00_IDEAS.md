# Improving how the loop thinks: the ideas, ranked

*2026-10-14. Twelve Sonnet research briefs (01 to 12 in this folder), one per part of thinking from `00_DECOMPOSITION.md`, each checked against the code. This file merges their roughly one hundred proposals into 42 items, removes the overlaps the briefs themselves flagged, marks what the loop already has, and ranks them. Evidence: strong = replicated or a meta-analysis; medium = one controlled study or ablation; weak = single run, preprint or analogy; none = our own reasoning. Cost: small = hours, medium = a day, large = several days. When: now = build next; arm = build behind a flag and compare at matched tokens; later = needs ledger data first (the lab holds 7 real builds today, so anything learned from outcomes waits). Pick what you like; nothing here changes the exam or the verdict rules.*

## 0. Fixed today, while the briefs were being written

The briefs read the code and found defects. These are repaired and tested (175 tests green):

1. The placebo control edited the protected data loader and died at the boundary check, so it never tested the ladder. It now changes the data seed where the training shards are made, and a control that stops before the learning rung is reported as broken, not as a pass.
2. The re-plan step did nothing, silently, when the planner's answer was unusable. It now logs that, and the dry run exercises a real re-plan.
3. Build slots and interrogation picks went round the parts in map order every pass, so with fewer slots than parts the last parts (data, recipe, objective) never got a build. Each pass now starts from the parts with the fewest so far.
4. A research brief that kept receiving facts never expired, because merging rewrote the file. Briefs now age from their own research time.
5. Stackable verdicts never reached the briefs; mock runs and control candidates did. Fixed both ways.
6. Every ledger row was printed twice and placed before the literature, so the adapt card and the critic's evidence pack filled with our own rows. Rows now print once, after the literature, twelve at most, winners first.
7. A failure against an older baseline stayed invisible to ideation yet blocked its own retry in the near-duplicate gate. The gate now keeps only rows of the current baseline, plus verified and stackable ones.
8. The hash memory replayed a noisy timing or learning failure from an earlier run as truth. It now replays only a pass or a deterministic failure (boundary, build), at the same number of learning steps.
9. The exam runs through the launcher since yesterday, and the launcher wiped the weights file from the output folder before every script, so every overnight candidate and control errored. The exam now keeps the weights. Overnight controls are judged by their verdict, not by a ladder they never climbed.
10. Interrogation offshoots meant for another part were filed under the part being questioned, with the wrong boundary. Offshoots now name their part and are filed there.
11. A predicted gain smaller than the noise between seeds counted as a held prediction. It is now marked untestable at that rung.
12. The parallel program-search calls differed only by the label "Variation i". Each is now told a different thing to change.
13. A dead context-count parameter, a docstring promising a rule that was never wired, and a docstring promising a build that does not exist were removed or corrected.
14. The calibration script zeroed a hand-set prior whenever a bucket had fewer than five rows, and used a stale list of departure cards.

Documented, not fixed: on this GPU two runs of the same code differ by about 0.01 in loss within twelve steps, so the silent-no-op rule (identical curves) never fires here; a no-op passes the ladder and is caught overnight. Item 21 closes that gap another way.

Known design weaknesses still open, covered by items below: the generic card is chosen by list position and the lens by replicate number, so neither can be credited (item 1); the long-shot lane is one build per run and off in round two (item 26); the near-duplicate gate never compares the chosen ideas with each other (item 27); upward reframing proposals are logged and never used (item 39); the "recurse" operator is described but never issued (item 36).

## 1. What the twelve briefs agree on

- **Compute, do not narrate.** A model's own confidence predicts failure at about chance (AUROC 0.5 to 0.65), its stated reasons name the real cause a quarter of the time, and it ranks ideas from text at chance. Every signal the loop acts on should be computed from measurements, with the model as adviser and a script as decider.
- **Doubt must end in a measurement.** Objections, predictions and kill criteria are only worth having if the ladder resolves them. Critics are useful once scored on planted flaws and outcomes, never before.
- **Elaboration must become checkable.** A free question list produces text nothing checks. A chain of mechanism links with falsifiers, premises probed by measurement, and facts tagged by provenance turn interrogation into evidence.
- **Memory: small, itemised, outcome-anchored, merged by script.** Add-all memory lost to a small fixed one by 12 points; a wholesale rewrite collapsed a context; focused one-line outcome retrieval beat a full dump 83.5 to 70.8. Agents never rewrite lessons.
- **Credit needs provenance first.** LLM attribution of which step mattered is 14 percent accurate. Credit to cards, questions and analogues must come from logged inputs and selection probabilities, with shrinkage and an exploration floor, and it needs hundreds of builds.
- **Allocate by evidence, with floors, in shadow mode first.** Patience stop rules fire by chance half the time; expected gain against cost is the rule. Every allocation idea runs first as a log of what it would have done.
- **Representation is cheap leverage.** A skeleton beat the full file at one seventh of the cost; irrelevant context hurts (strong); diagnostics shown as contrasts beat raw tables. The problem should carry its units, its slack and a computed cost view.
- **Humans steer by typed inputs at boundaries, and read computed explanations.** Free-text notes reach no one; a few well-placed asks lifted an agent 30 to 52 percent; summaries written by a model overgeneralise five times more often.

## 3. My pick for the first batch

About three days, almost no new model calls, each item measurable in the next real run:

1. Item 1 (manifest, selection ledger, logged schedule) and item 24 (stage envelopes, control file v2): the plumbing everything else is scored through, and the budget fix the dry run asked for.
2. Items 5, 7, 8, 14 (mechanism chain, objection ledger, kill criterion, repairs with the full spec): interrogation and critique become checkable at no extra cost.
3. Item 15's cost card and typed spec, and item 16's diagnostic contrasts: computed views the agents cannot get wrong.
4. Items 26 and 27 (lane as holdout, greedy slate with cost): selection stops being biased and starts producing calibration data.
5. Item 9 (critic mutants) and item 21 (link-to-lines audit): the two cheapest ways to catch what the ladder cannot, including the no-op gap on this GPU.
6. Items 4 and 2 (newcomer brief, alerts, commitments): you read one page and trust it.

Then as arms in the first real runs: 6, 10, 11, 12, 13, 17, 20, 25, 29, 30. The rest after the ledger holds about thirty overnight pairs.

## 4. Not worth doing, on the evidence

An LLM score for plausibility, novelty or quality as a gate; builder-written test suites; test-driven or step-by-step instructions to a reasoning model; a two-model architect-and-editor chain; self-review in the generator's own context; builder-critic debate; "anticipate harder" wording; free-form "reframe this problem" prompts; decoding-level diversity tricks (unreachable through the CLI and unproven for ideas); add-all memory; whole-context rewrites by an agent; asking a model which question or analogy caused a win; model-written lessons or skills without outcome anchoring; "no new best in m builds" stop rules; secretary-problem stopping (we have recall); optimised portfolio weights at our sample sizes; letting any critic gate ideas before item 9 shows a gap.

## 5. What only runs can settle

Whether round two pays at all; whether the second interrogation generation beats building more; push versus pull memory; lessons versus focused raw outcomes; whether shown history helps or anchors; the exchange rate between tokens and GPU minutes; the real rates of build failure, prediction hits and fidelity. The lab holds seven real builds, so every effect above is a hypothesis with a measurement attached, and the first real run's job is to log the baseline rates that items 1, 24 and 38 make visible.
