# Modulators of Human Idea Generation and Practical, Evidence-Based Levers (emphasis 2023–2026)

Scope note: "Idea generation" is operationalized in nearly all of this literature as divergent thinking (DT: Alternate Uses Task fluency/originality/flexibility, Divergent Association Task, Forward Flow, story/ideation tasks) or insight/convergent problem solving (Remote Associates Test, riddles, hidden-rule tasks). Evidence tiers used below: **STRONG** = meta-analysis / preregistered or multi-sample replication; **MODERATE** = multiple independent RCTs or large-N studies; **PRELIMINARY** = single lab, small N, preprint, or conference abstract. Older foundational work is labeled [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023].

---

## Key Question 1 — Neurochemistry: dopamine, noradrenaline/LC, serotonin, acetylcholine; psychedelic trials 2023–2026

### Takeaway
The best current synthesis (Beversdorf 2025 systematic review) is that dopamine is tentatively tied to divergent performance (often inverted-U, often only in interaction with traits/stress) while noradrenaline is tied to convergent/insight performance (beta-blockade helps, stress hurts); evidence for serotonin and acetylcholine in idea generation is thin and mostly indirect. Placebo-controlled psychedelic microdosing trials (LSD 2024; psilocybin pooled 2025) are largely null on objective creativity measures, with one fragile exception (higher originality/fluency ratio for psilocybin), while acute full-dose psilocybin impairs DT during the session.

### Cited Findings

**Dopamine**
- Beversdorf (2025), *Behavioral Sciences* 15(9):1185, sole-author systematic review (PubMed through 24 March 2024; ~33 dopamine studies, 21 noradrenergic, 15 both). Of 29 dopamine studies examining divergent tasks: 6 showed better performance with higher dopamine activity, 1 worse, 1 mixed (cocaine), 3 no effect; eye-blink rate (a dopamine proxy) linked to better divergent performance in several studies, one with an inverted-U; DRD2/DRD4/COMT genetic findings were "interaction-dependent" with hard-to-interpret direction; Parkinson's results varied (one worse DT, one improved with dopaminergic therapy, others null). Author's caveat: dopamine's role in motivation/persistence could explain divergent-task effects, so specificity to creativity is unclear. Conclusion is explicitly "tentative." — [Beversdorf 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12466433/)
- Liu, Zhuang, Zeitlen, Chen, Wang, Feng, Beaty & Qiu (2024), *Communications Biology*: task-fMRI MVPA in two independent samples identified a neural pattern predicting DT (positive weights in default-mode and frontoparietal control networks, negative in visual network), which correlated with the principal connectivity gradient (sensory → abstract), and was "positively correlated with dopamine-related neurotransmitters and with genes that influence neurotransmitter release." Correlational, not causal. — [Liu et al. 2024, Comms Bio](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07007-6)
- 2025 scoping review of dopaminergic treatment and creative expression in Parkinson's disease (7 studies): some reported more artistic expression/DT on dopamine agonists, others no change; increased creativity "appeared more common in individuals with pre-existing artistic tendencies than as a new ability caused by the medication." — [PMC12193881](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12193881/)
- [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023] Chermahini & Hommel (2010), *Cognition*, N=117: spontaneous eye-blink rate predicted DT flexibility in an inverted-U (moderate dopamine best), independent of intelligence; higher blink rate impaired convergent thinking. — [Chermahini & Hommel 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010027710000715)

**Noradrenaline / locus coeruleus**
- Beversdorf (2025): of 21 noradrenergic studies, 17 with clear directionality showed better *convergent* performance with noradrenergic blockade (mostly propranolol), 3 no effect; only 2 examined divergent tasks. Stress often impaired convergent performance and many noradrenergic effects appeared only when stress/arousal was present; propranolol reversed stress-induced impairment in one study. Tyrosine (DA/NA precursor) improved convergent performance in healthy adults. — [Beversdorf 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12466433/)
- [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023] Pupillometry study (*Creativity Research Journal*, 2018, Tilburg): tonic pupil dilation (proxy for tonic LC-NA) predicted original ideas on both an AUT-type and a real-world creativity task; phasic dilation predicted effective ideas only on the real-world task; no pupil–performance relation for convergent thinking. Proposed mechanism: tonic LC-NA increases attentional flexibility and combination of remote concepts. Small study; pupil is an indirect LC proxy. — [CRJ 2018 pupillometry](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2018.1530533)
- A 2025 meta-analysis of experimentally induced stress (*Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*) found an overall negative effect of stress on creativity, d = −0.20. (Full moderator table not accessible; number reported in search abstract.) — [NBR 2025 stress meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763425001137)
- I found no 2024–2026 primary study directly linking LC/pupil measures to divergent thinking; the 2024–2026 LC literature surfaced was about implicit learning, aging, and visual-search exploration, not ideation.

**Serotonin / acetylcholine**
- *Scientific Reports* (2025): serotonergic multilocus genetic variants × parent–child relationship on adolescent creativity; the introduction cites mouse work showing prefrontal 5-HT release enhances cognitive flexibility. Results not accessible from snippet. — [Sci Rep 2025 serotonergic variants](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-03038-z)
- "Serotonin as a Creativity Pump" (arXiv, June 2024) is a speculative, non-peer-reviewed hypothesis (pollen → histamine → serotonin → creativity); treat with considerable caution. — [arXiv 2406.13563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13563)
- Direct evidence on acetylcholine and ideation is sparse. The only 2024–2025 ACh item surfaced is a nonhuman-primate study showing ACh moderately increased during cognitive task engagement in fronto-striatal circuits (not a creativity task). — [PMC13228364](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13228364/)

**Psychedelics 2023–2026**
- Prochazkova, Marschall, van Elk, Rifkin, Schon, Fiacchino, Fejer, Kuchar & Hommel, *Neuropharmacology* (online late 2025; Leiden): three double-blind placebo-controlled psilocybin-truffle microdosing trials pooled (reported N=171; Exp 1 n=59 ~0.65 g; Exp 2 n=61 ~1 g; Exp 3 n=27 crossover ~1.5 g). Pooled: active microdosing increased the originality/fluency ratio (quality of DT); no effect on fluency, other DT scores, or convergent thinking. Effect survived controlling for dose guesses and demographics; Experiment 2 alone did not replicate Experiment 1's originality result; blinding was imperfect at the highest dose; placebo effects were substantial, especially among prior psychedelic users. — [Prochazkova et al., Neuropharmacology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002839082500440X); details via [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/major-new-study-finds-psilocybin-microdoses-improve-the-quality-of-creative-ideas-but-not-the-quantity/)
- A separate 2025 *Neuropharmacology* report from two double-blind placebo-controlled psilocybin microdosing trials: microdosing "did not significantly affect behavioral or subjective measures compared to placebo." — [Cognitive and subjective effects of psilocybin microdosing, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028390825004307)
- Murphy, Sumner, Godfrey, Mabidikama, Roberts, Sundram & Muthukumaraswamy (2024), *Psychopharmacology*: RCT, 80 healthy adult males, 10 µg LSD vs placebo every third day for 6 weeks (14 doses). Creativity measured at baseline, acute first dose, and drug-free final session with AUT, RAT, Consensual Assessment Technique, and Everyday Problem-Solving Questionnaire. "No effect of drug by time was found on the AUT, RAT, CAT, or EPSQ." Participants felt more creative on dose days but objective tests showed no acute or lasting effect. — [Murphy et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-024-06680-z); [PMC11775047](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11775047/)
- 2024 review of controlled microdosing studies: creative performance "was not altered in either of the instances where it was measured"; low-dose LSD "has little effect on cognitive performance or creativity on the measures used thus far." — [Microdosing Psychedelics: Current Evidence From Controlled Studies, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451902224000156)
- Meshkat et al. (2024), *Psychiatry and Clinical Neurosciences*, systematic review of psilocybin and cognition: cognitive flexibility and creative cognition "initially decline but could potentially improve over time"; Cavanna et al. microdosing (0.5 g dried mushrooms) found no significant effects on cognitive flexibility or creative cognition. — [Meshkat et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/pcn.13741); [Cavanna et al. 2022, Transl Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-022-02039-0)
- [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023] Mason et al. (2021), *Translational Psychiatry*: 0.17 mg/kg psilocybin, double-blind placebo-controlled; DT impaired during the acute state; one DT aspect increased 7 days later vs placebo. — [Mason et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-021-01335-5)
- arXiv preprint (Jan 2026) "Past Psychedelic Use Predicts Divergent Thinking" acknowledges that "limited available evidence on psychedelic-related changes in creativity has been inconsistent." Observational; not peer reviewed. — [arXiv 2601.04380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04380)

**Emerging circuit-level mechanism (PRELIMINARY)**
- bioRxiv preprint (July 2025) "Human hippocampal ripples loosen medial prefrontal constraints to foster creative thinking": frames strong hippocampus–mPFC coupling as supporting schema-based reasoning but entrenching rigid responses; proposes ripples transiently loosen mPFC constraints. Only introduction accessible; not peer reviewed. Related: *Nature Communications* (Dec 2025) "Hippocampal-prefrontal orchestration supports higher-order learning for creative ideation" (metadata only). — [bioRxiv 2025.07.22.666046](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.22.666046.full.pdf); [Nat Commun 2025](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41467-025-67157-x)

### Inferences
- The one replicable pharmacological pattern is noradrenergic: lowering NA/arousal (propranolol) or removing stress helps *insight/convergent* solving; this is consistent with the stress meta-analysis (d = −0.20) and with Beversdorf's note that NA effects surface mainly under stress. For *divergent* ideation, the NA story is almost untested (2 studies).
- Dopamine's link to DT is probably real but small, inverted-U, and confounded with motivation; it should be treated as MODERATE-to-PRELIMINARY, not established.
- Microdosing's subjective "feels more creative" effect with null objective change across LSD and most psilocybin outcomes is the clearest demonstration in this literature of expectancy effects; the single positive psilocybin signal (originality ratio) failed within-program replication and should be labeled PRELIMINARY.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study directly tested LC-NA/pupil dynamics against divergent thinking; the LC–exploration link to ideation rests on one 2018 study plus theory.
- No human pharmacological study of acetylcholine (e.g., nicotine, cholinesterase inhibitors) on DT was found.
- Full-dose psilocybin/LSD creativity trials from 2023–2026 (as opposed to microdosing) were not found; depression trials measure "psychological flexibility," not ideation.
- Exact moderator results of the 2025 stress meta-analysis (acute vs chronic, DT vs CT) were behind a paywall.

## Key Question 2 — States: N1 hypnagogia, dream incubation, REM, boredom, mood, walking/exercise, nature, time of day/chronotype

### Takeaway
Walking is now the best-supported acute state lever for divergent thinking (2025 meta-analysis, d ≈ 0.9, moderate certainty, indoors = outdoors). Sleep-onset (N1) effects on insight (Lacaux 2021) and on DT via targeted dream incubation (Horowitz 2023) are striking but remain two-lab findings without independent replication; REM-cueing work (2026) is promising but subgroup-dependent. Positive mood has a small reliable effect (r ≈ 0.15); boredom does not reliably boost creativity and often hurts it; nature exposure and chronotype effects are inconsistent and under-powered.

### Cited Findings

**Sleep onset / N1 (hypnagogia)**
- [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023] Lacaux, Izabelle, Santantonio, De Villèle, Frain, Lubart, Pico, Arnulf & Oudiette (2021), *Science Advances*, N=103: spending ≥15 s in N1 during a rest period tripled the chance of discovering a hidden mathematical rule (83% vs 30% awake); effect vanished if participants reached N2; neither general nor task-related hypnagogic imagery predicted insight; 16 participants who solved before the break were excluded; insight came on average 94 trials after the nap. — [Lacaux et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abj5866); [Science news summary](https://www.science.org/content/article/edison-was-right-waking-right-after-drifting-sleep-can-boost-creativity)
- Lacaux, Strauss, Bekinschtein & Oudiette (2024), *Trends in Neurosciences* 47(4):273–288, "Embracing sleep-onset complexity": review arguing the sleep-onset period serves memory and creativity functions despite attentional costs. Review, not new data. — [Lacaux et al. 2024 TINS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166223624000183)
- Horowitz, Esfahany, Vega Gálvez, Maes & Stickgold (2023), *Scientific Reports* 13:7319, N=49 in four groups (sleep vs wake × incubation vs no incubation), theme "tree," Dormio glove delivering auditory cues at sleep onset: creative performance and semantic distance on three theme-related tasks were greater after N1 sleep than wake; the Sleep+Incubation group outperformed sleep-only and wake groups and incorporated dream content into responses. Single theme, single lab, small N. — [Horowitz et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-31361-w); [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2023/sleep-sweet-spot-dreams-creativity-0515)
- A 2024 *Frontiers in Sleep* paper describes a tool for cuing and capturing thought content during hypnagogic states, and notes practical limits: Dormio gloves are custom-made, few exist, break easily, and at-home studies suffer delays. — [Frontiers in Sleep 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2024.1258345/full)
- No independent 2024–2026 replication of either the Lacaux N1-insight effect or the Horowitz TDI effect was found; 2025 coverage is commentary (e.g., Psychology Today, May 2025). — [Psychology Today 2025](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/creative-insights/202505/spark-your-creativity-with-a-nap)

**REM sleep and problem solving**
- Konkoly et al. (Paller lab), *Neuroscience of Consciousness* (2026; received Sept 2025): frequent lucid dreamers failed puzzles with unique soundtracks, then slept; playing a puzzle's sound during REM reliably increased dreaming about it; post-hoc, in 12 of 20 targeted dreamers whose cue-related dreaming increased, cueing boosted later solving; solving rates were *lower* after incorporation into lucid vs nonlucid dreams; authors state they cannot say REM dreaming is superior to other states. PRELIMINARY. — [Konkoly et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2026/1/niaf067/8456489)
- Elliott, Metha, Hoyer & Jacobson (2025), *Journal of Psychopharmacology* review: REM-creativity evidence "largely used methodology which deprived subjects of REM sleep, rather than enhanced it," so benefits of REM enhancement are "largely theoretical." — [Elliott et al. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02698811251365179)
- [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023] Cai et al. (2009), *PNAS*: REM (vs quiet rest or NREM) enhanced associative integration on primed RAT items, but neither NREM nor REM improved general creative problem solving without prior exposure. — [Cai et al. 2009](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0900271106)
- Bieth (Paris Brain Institute; supervised by Volle & Oudiette, with Kenett) has a presentation "Restructuring problem-related semantic associations promotes solving success" and a 2024 *Communications Psychology* paper on semantic-memory restructuring in waking problem solving; a 2025 REM-incubation preprint was referenced in search snippets but its results could not be confirmed. — [Bieth speaker page](https://underline.io/speakers/111087-theophile-bieth)

**Sleep loss**
- *Creativity Research Journal* (2024) systematic review of experimental sleep-deprivation studies (search 2021, updated 2024): provisional evidence that sleep deprivation impairs divergent and associative thinking, verbal DT more than figural; some studies found enhancement; study quality rated fair to poor, power low. — [CRJ 2024 sleep deprivation review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2024.2343508)
- 2026 Bayesian study (*J Physiol Anthropol*): partial sleep deprivation (2-h time in bed) in young males increased sleepiness and reduced sustained attention but produced no differences on the DAT or tangram insight tasks. — [PMC13366970](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13366970/)

**Walking and exercise**
- *PLOS ONE* (2025) systematic review and meta-analysis of walking and creative thinking: 23 studies (12 randomized, 9 non-randomized, 2 observational) from 16 articles, N=1,036 (mostly students). Divergent thinking: d = 0.93 [95% CI 0.44, 1.42], moderate-certainty; randomized-only d = 0.82 [0.35, 1.28]; convergent thinking d = 0.16 [−0.31, 0.63] (null, very uncertain); indoor vs outdoor no difference (p = .676); AUT vs other DT measures no difference (p = .857); no publication bias on Egger's test (p = .586). — [PLOS ONE 2025 walking meta-analysis](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0347878)
- [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023] Oppezzo & Schwartz (2014), *JEP:LMC*: treadmill walking raised DT (Guilford Alternate Uses) for 81% of participants but convergent (Compound Remote Associates) for only 23%. — [Oppezzo & Schwartz 2014](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xlm-a0036577.pdf)
- Acute aerobic exercise more broadly: a 2021 systematic review found "weak to modest support" for acute moderate-intensity exercise benefiting creativity, with 77% of studies at moderate–high risk of bias; Aga et al. (2021, N=40) found 15 min aerobic exercise raised DT fluency and flexibility, not mediated by mood; a recent "Creativity in Motion" study found a single 20-min moderate-to-vigorous session did not improve post-exercise DT; a 2024 narrative review concludes natural-pace walking has the clearest support. Rominger et al. (2022) multilevel meta-analysis reports both acute and chronic physical activity increase creative ideation (pooled effect sizes not accessible). — [Systematic review 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33680164/); [Aga et al. 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/11/5/546); [Creativity in Motion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392737960_Creativity_in_Motion_How_a_Single_Aerobic_Exercise_Session_Affects_Divergent_Creative_Thinking); [2024 narrative review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377353787_Exploring_the_impact_of_acute_physical_activity_on_creative_thinking_a_comprehensive_narrative_review_with_a_focus_on_activity_type_and_intensity)

**Nature exposure**
- A 2025 meta-analysis of nature exposure (80 studies, 273 outcomes) could not produce a reliable estimate for creativity: funnel asymmetry and model non-convergence after outlier removal. — [Nature exposure & attention restoration meta-analysis 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S027249442500115X)
- 2025 study (N=64 students): physical nature increased creativity over time; virtual nature increased self-perceived creativity more. — [PMC12832259](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832259/)
- Three-month field experiment with factory employees (2026, *J Environ Psychol*): nature exposure had no unique effect on creativity vs non-natural stimuli. — [Nature vs non-natural environments 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494426000307)
- 2025 study: originality gains after nature videos only for participants high in fascination/inspiration. — [Fascination moderates nature video effects 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494425001823)
- 2022 child study: digital nature scenes did not enhance verbal creativity. — [Ibrahim et al. 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/eco.2021.0037)

**Boredom**
- Zeißig et al. (2024), *Review of Education* scoping review (19 publications, 27 studies): experimental/quasi-experimental results mixed (5 positive, 4 negative, 2 contradictory, 3 null); correlational studies mostly null or negative (3 negative, 1 contradictory, 7 null); conclusion that "boredom leads to increased creativity is not sufficiently supported." — [Zeißig et al. 2024](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rev3.3470)
- Nettinga, Gutglick & Danckert (2023), *Journal of Boredom Studies* (Waterloo), two experiments: induced state boredom (phone-book copying; laundry video) was associated with *poorer* DT on a creative-uses task; trait boredom proneness linked to lower creative self-belief and fewer everyday creative pursuits; Experiment 2 (creative foraging task) found no relation. — [Nettinga et al. 2023](https://www.boredomsociety.com/jbs/index.php/journal/article/view/18)
- Krannich et al. (2025), *Education Sciences*: in school math, boredom × underchallenge predicted higher creativity, boredom × overchallenge lower, on the first task only. — [Krannich et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci15030330)

**Mood**
- [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2023] Baas, De Dreu & Nijstad (2008), *Psychological Bulletin*, 25-year meta-analysis: positive vs neutral mood r = 0.15; negative vs neutral r = 0.03; positive vs negative r = 0.04; activating approach-oriented moods (happiness) helped; deactivating moods (relaxation) did not. — [Baas et al. 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23421968_A_Meta-Analysis_of_25_Years_of_Mood-Creativity_Research_Hedonic_Tone_Activation_or_Regulatory_Focus)
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Davis (2009) meta-analysis: effect depends on baseline mood and task type. — [Davis 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S074959780800054X)
- Yamada (2015): positive mood increased divergent but not convergent ideas (single study). — [Yamada 2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpr.12093)
- No 2023–2026 mood–creativity meta-analysis was found.

**Time of day / chronotype**
- Three-sample workplace study (~260/238/319 employees): chronotype moderates time-of-day effects; late chronotypes more creative late afternoon, early types in the morning; alignment linked to positive affect and creative self-efficacy (broad creativity ratings, not DT tests). — [There Is a Time to Be Creative](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347898708_There_is_a_Time_to_Be_Creative_The_Alignment_between_Chronotype_and_Time_of_Day)
- 2016 *Chronobiology International* (36 evening + 36 morning types): DT not predicted by chronotype, asynchrony, or interaction; late types tested at non-optimal times did *better* on convergent thinking. — [Chronobiology International 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/07420528.2016.1246454)
- Alabbasi, Acar, Runco et al. (2025), *SAGE Open*: setting, time of day, and giftedness on DT test scores; some group-DT studies report midday/afternoon peaks for fluency/originality. — [Alabbasi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440251320442)

**Mind wandering / incubation states (bridges to Q4)**
- Preregistered *Scientific Reports* (2025) writing study: more mind wandering during an incubation break predicted larger within-person gains in semantic distance, only when the same prompt was repeated. — [Sci Rep 2025 mind wandering](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-09736-y)
- 2025 study (N=85): incubation improved fluency (especially for repeated items) but mind wandering per se had no significant effect on DT. — [PMC12190454](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12190454/)
- Live jazz improvisation (2025/2026, *Consciousness & Cognition*): mind-wandering predicted higher rated creativity than focused attention; benefit strongest for less-experienced improvisers. — [Mind-wandering and improvisation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810026000565)

### Inferences
- The state levers with STRONG support: walking (large DT effect; convergent null) and positive activating mood (small). Everything else in this section is MODERATE or PRELIMINARY.
- N1/hypnagogia work is internally consistent across two labs (Paris, MIT) and mechanistically plausible (loosened control, hyperassociation), but zero independent replications in five years is a real limitation; the Lacaux task is also insight-type, not open-ended ideation.
- Boredom's popular reputation is not supported; the better-supported mechanism is that undemanding activity (walking, light tasks) rather than boredom per se enables incubation-type benefits.

### Gaps
- No independent replication of Lacaux 2021 or Horowitz 2023 found (2024–2026).
- Results of the Bieth/Oudiette/Volle REM incubation preprint could not be confirmed.
- No pooled effect sizes accessible for nature exposure or for Rominger et al.'s exercise meta-analysis.
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis on mood or chronotype and ideation.

## Key Question 3 — Individual differences: openness, intelligence, working memory, ADHD, autism, expertise, age

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses converge on modest trait predictors of DT: intelligence r ≈ .25 (up to .31–.37 with be-creative instructions/originality scoring), openness r ≈ .20, memory/WMC r ≈ .08–.19 (stronger for convergent than divergent). ADHD traits (especially subclinical) link to higher DT and creative achievement, likely via mind wandering (2025, N=750, not yet peer reviewed); autism shows lower fluency/flexibility but relatively higher originality (small, mixed). Expertise and age findings are domain- and task-dependent, with verbal DT relatively preserved into older age.

### Cited Findings

**Intelligence**
- Gerwig, Miroshnik, Forthmann, Benedek, Karwowski & Holling (2021), *Journal of Intelligence*, three-level meta-analysis: 849 correlations, 112 studies, N = 34,610; overall r = .25; up to r = .31–.37 with test-like DT assessment, "be creative" instructions, and originality scoring; g vs Gf vs Gc did not differ; average sample age positively moderated the correlation. Updates Kim (2005; r ≈ .17, no support for a threshold). — [Gerwig et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8167550/)
- Miroshnik et al. (2023), *Intelligence*: broad retrieval ability (Gr) r = .47 and processing speed (Gs) r = .31 with DT. — [Miroshnik et al. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016028962300020X)
- 2025 meta-analysis (*High Ability Studies*): gifted students show moderate-to-large DT advantage, g = .658. — [Gifted vs nongifted DT meta-analysis 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13598139.2025.2607991)
- Threshold hypothesis: a 2020 reappraisal notes Guilford's own data showed a gradual continuous relation; Harris et al. (2019) propose a *conditional* threshold where intelligence matters most for creative achievement among people high in openness. No 2023–2026 meta-analytic test found. — [Reappraisal 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7709632/); [Harris et al. 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/325919675_A_Conditional_Threshold_Hypothesis_for_Creative_Achievement_On_the_Interaction_Between_Intelligence_and_Openness)

**Openness**
- Grajzel et al. (2023), *Personality and Individual Differences*, Big Five–DT meta-analysis: openness r = .20; extraversion r = .09. — [Grajzel et al. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886923002611)
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Kaufman et al.: Openness predicts arts achievement, Intellect predicts science achievement. — [Kaufman et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/269286890_Openness_to_Experience_and_Intellect_Differentially_Predict_Creative_Achievement_in_the_Arts_and_Sciences_Openness_Intellect_and_Creativity)
- Context for what DT predicts: updated meta-analysis (*PACA*, print 2024; 766 effects, 70 studies, N=14,901): DT–creative achievement link is positive but weak, ~3% shared variance, strongest among gifted samples in performance domains. A 2026 *CRJ* second-order meta-analysis reports DT effect size .14 vs creative process .27. — [DT–CA meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361681918_Divergent_thinking_and_creative_achievement-How_strong_is_the_link_An_updated_meta-analysis); [Second-order meta-analysis 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2026.2644245)

**Working memory**
- Memory–creativity meta-analysis (2023, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*; 79 studies, N=12,846): overall r = .19; WMC r = .23 with convergent vs r = .15 with divergent thinking (interaction not significant, Q = 5.52, p = .14). — [Memory and creativity meta-analysis 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-023-02303-4)
- WMC-focused meta-analysis (2023; 28 studies, N=3,104): r = .083 [.050, .115]. — [WMC meta-analysis 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36662145/)

**ADHD**
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Hoogman et al. (2020), *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, review of 31 behavioral studies: several studies show higher DT in ADHD; advantage concentrated in elevated-trait but sub-threshold individuals; no evidence of increased convergent thinking; stimulant effects mixed. — [Hoogman et al. 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347270270_Creativity_and_ADHD_A_review_of_behavioral_studies_the_effect_of_psychostimulants_and_neural_underpinnings)
- Fang et al. (Radboud UMC), ECNP Congress Amsterdam, Oct 2025 (conference presentation, not yet peer reviewed): two independent samples, N=750 total (ECNP-curated European sample; UK sample), ADHD patients and controls. Higher ADHD symptoms → more mind wandering and higher creative achievement in both studies; deliberate mind wandering mediated the ADHD-trait → DT/creative-achievement link; in one study inattentiveness/hyperactivity showed no direct correlation with DT. No effect sizes released. — [ScienceDaily/ECNP 2025](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/10/251012054608.htm)
- Beversdorf (2025): stimulants showed some DT benefit in ADHD in a few studies, many null; "not substantial evidence that stimulants interfere with creativity." — [Beversdorf 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12466433/)
- A small 2022 meta-analysis (4 studies, N=129) found no significant ADHD–creativity difference. — [Atlantis Press 2022](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/125975563.pdf)

**Autism**
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Pennisi et al. (2020), *Cognitive Processing*, systematic review and meta-analysis: overall creative performance lower in autism, d = 0.24; fluency deficit d = 0.38 (I² = 33); flexibility deficit reported; originality and detail relatively high; better language skills → better creative performance. — [Pennisi et al. 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10339-020-00992-6)
- 2022 *Frontiers in Psychiatry* review: a separate meta-analysis found no relation between the full autism construct and creativity; imagination-domain problems related to lower DT originality; a preregistered study of 352 matched adults found no DT group differences, and higher self-reported real-world creative behavior in autistic adults disappeared after accounting for ADHD. — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.909202/full)
- *Creativity Research Journal* (2024): in children, autism-linked empathy and sensory sensitivities predicted creative activities and openness. — [CRJ 2024 autism-linked traits](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2024.2333633)

**Expertise / knowledge**
- Dumas, Forthmann & Alexander (2024), *Educational Psychologist*: model of domain learning positions subject-specific knowledge as the foundation of creative production (secondary summary; full text not accessed). — [Teacher Magazine summary of Dumas et al. 2024](https://www.teachermagazine.com/au_en/articles/nurturing-creativity-and-the-role-of-subject-specific-knowledge)
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Wiley (1998), *Memory & Cognition*, "Expertise as mental set": domain knowledge can confine search and inhibit creative problem solving on RAT-type items. — [Wiley 1998](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03211392)
- Miroshnik et al. (2023) retrieval-ability r = .47 is the strongest cognitive correlate of DT found, consistent with knowledge-retrieval accounts. — [Miroshnik et al. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016028962300020X)

**Age**
- Systematic review "The Controversial Effect of Age on Divergent Thinking Abilities": verbal DT relatively preserved in older adults despite executive decline; older adults sometimes produce more and longer responses; visual DT more affected. — [Systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342399284_The_Controversial_Effect_of_Age_on_Divergent_Thinking_Abilities_A_Systematic_Review)
- Cross-sectional data (N=159 across five bands 18–98): DT peaks in young adulthood, stable 36–55, declines from 56–74; another (N=150) shows plateau after 40, decline after 70. Cohort confounds. — [Role of Cognition in DT and aging (2023)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10605231/)
- *Healthcare* (2024, N=121 older adults, mean ~73): DT predicted cognitive reserve, mediating effects on emotional competence. — [Healthcare 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12030303)
- *CRJ* (2025): aging and question-asking/DT/problem solving across 20–40, 40–60, 60–80 groups (online, 2024). — [CRJ 2025 aging](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2025.2498916)
- Childhood meta-analysis (2025, *Cognitive Development*): DT increases linearly through childhood. — [Trajectories of creativity development in childhood](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608025000202)

### Inferences
- Trait predictors explain only a few percent of DT variance each; the practical implication is that state/context levers (walking, incubation, instructions to "be creative," constraints) are at least as large as most trait effects.
- The ADHD–creativity link is most defensible as a *subclinical trait* effect mediated by spontaneous/deliberate mind wandering; clinical ADHD with executive impairment may not show the advantage.
- Given that retrieval ability (Gr) correlates ~.47 with DT, knowledge breadth and retrieval fluency are probably more important levers than raw WMC.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis found for ADHD–DT, openness–achievement, or age–DT; the ECNP 2025 ADHD data are not yet peer reviewed.
- No empirical test of an inverted-U expertise–originality relation from 2023–2026 was found.
- Large-N (thousands) lifespan DT datasets were not found.

## Key Question 4 — Interventions and training: creativity training, brainstorming, constraints, incubation, mindfulness, tDCS/TMS

### Takeaway
Creativity training works but is smaller than advertised: the largest meta-analysis (Sio & Lortie-Forgues 2024, *Psych Bull*, 169 studies) finds 0.53 SD unadjusted shrinking to ~0.29–0.32 SD after publication-bias correction, with little methodological improvement over five decades. Incubation (d ≈ 0.39), constraints (positive on average, type-dependent), and mindfulness (positive, but stronger for convergent than divergent) have meta-analytic support; anodal tDCS improves convergent but not divergent thinking on average (left DLPFC helps, left IFG hurts). Face-to-face brainstorming remains inferior to nominal groups; electronic/structured formats close the gap at larger sizes.

### Cited Findings

**Creativity training**
- Sio & Lortie-Forgues (2024), *Psychological Bulletin*: 169 studies, 844 effect sizes, 48 unpublished; unadjusted mean 0.53 SD; "strong evidence of publication bias"; adjusted estimates ≈0.29–0.32 SD; widespread methodological weaknesses likely inflate effects; little improvement over time. — [Sio & Lortie-Forgues 2024 (summary)](https://ifp.nyu.edu/2024/meta-analyses-systematic-reviews/bul0000432/); [accepted manuscript PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/209749/1/Meta_Creativity_Training_accepted_pre_format_version.pdf)
- McKay (2024), *Creativity and Innovation Management*, organizational training: 32 studies, 40 independent effects, N=3,522; overall g = 0.68; learning outcomes g = 0.73; on-the-job behavior/transfer g = 0.34 (ns); immediate g = 0.86 vs delayed g = 0.40. — [McKay 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/caim.12605)
- Çelik & Akay (2025), *SAGE Open*: classroom brainstorming technique, 22 studies (achievement) and 8 (creative thinking); achievement ES = 0.958 [0.682, 1.234]. — [Çelik & Akay 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440251378562)

**Brainstorming: group vs individual**
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Mullen, Johnson & Salas (1991) meta-analysis: interacting brainstorming groups significantly less productive than nominal groups in quantity and quality; loss larger with larger groups, experimenter presence, and oral contributions. — [Mullen et al. 1991](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15324834basp1201_1)
- [FOUNDATIONAL] DeRosa, Smith & Hantula (2007) electronic brainstorming meta-analysis: large EBS groups outperform nominal groups; small nominal groups outperform small EBS groups; crossover around ~10 members. — [DeRosa et al. 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563205000506)
- Van Eeckhout, Michinov & Le Rudulier (2025), *Journal of Creative Behavior*: multilevel analysis shows serially structured electronic brainstorming produced greater idea *elaboration* than free brainstorming, independent of quantity/quality. Single study. — [Van Eeckhout et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jocb.70023)
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis comparing interacting vs nominal groups was found.

**Incubation**
- Liu, Li, Gong, Tu, Shi & Guan (2024), *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts*: 195 effect sizes, 52 studies; mean d = 0.393 [0.253, 0.533]; stronger benefit when initial cues were misleading (incubation releases fixation); effect robust across cognitive-load moderators. — [Liu et al. 2024](https://www.ovid.com/journals/paca/fulltext/10.1037/aca0000722~exploration-of-impact-of-incubation-on-creativity-a)
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Sio & Ormerod (2009), *Psychological Bulletin*: incubation benefits larger for creative/divergent problems; larger with an *undemanding* filler task than a demanding one or no task; longer preparation → larger effect; possible publication bias. Conflict with Liu 2024 on cognitive-load moderation noted in later work. — [Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/2009-sio.pdf)

**Constraints and time pressure**
- Damadzic, Winchester, Medeiros & Griffith (2022), *Journal of Organizational Behavior*, meta-analysis of >100 studies: significant positive overall link between constraints and creativity, depending on constraint type and timing. (Exact pooled r not accessible.) — [Damadzic et al. 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/job.2655)
- Cromwell (2024), *Organizational Psychology Review*: typology of how combinations of constraints affect creative problem solving. — [Cromwell 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20413866231202031)
- Time-pressure literature is mixed: design-ideation review finds time pressure usually increases productivity but creativity suffers from both scarcity and abundance of time; Amabile & Gryskiewicz (1989) found no effect of experienced time pressure, Andrews & Farris (1972) found positive. — [Time constraints in design idea generation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229057530_Time_constraints_in_design_idea_generation)
- Stress meta-analysis (2025): induced stress overall d = −0.20 on creativity. — [NBR 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763425001137)

**Mindfulness**
- Hughes, Ball, Richardson & Judge (2023), *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*: two meta-analyses (20 control-group studies; 17 pre–post studies); positive effect of mindfulness on creativity in both; convergent d = 0.80 vs divergent d = 0.52 (difference not significant); moderated by intervention length, task type, and control type; the focused-attention → convergent / open-monitoring → divergent dissociation (Colzato et al. 2012) is described as "speculative until supported with additional evidence." — [Hughes et al. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-023-02327-w)
- [FOUNDATIONAL] Multilevel meta-analysis of 89 correlations (Lebuda, Zabelina & Karwowski 2016): overall mindfulness–creativity link equivocal; weaker for attention/awareness components; authors suggest open-monitoring interventions may be more useful. — [Mind Full of Ideas](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282354120_Mind_Full_of_Ideas_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Mindfulness-Creativity_Link)
- Mindfulness induction and cognition meta-analysis: small positive effect on cognition; focused-attention induction showed no effect; too few open-monitoring studies to analyze. — [Mindfulness induction meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810019304283)

**Brain stimulation**
- Chen et al. (2024), *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts*: 32 studies, 125 effect sizes, N=1,538. Anodal tDCS significantly improved convergent thinking but had no significant effect on divergent thinking (fluency, originality, flexibility); anodal left DLPFC improved both DT and CT; anodal left IFG was associated with *decreased* creative performance. — [Chen et al. 2024](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383874996_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Effects_of_Transcranial_Direct_Current_Stimulation_on_Creative_Thinking)
- Cross-domain systematic review and meta-analysis of tDCS creativity studies (*Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, 2025) builds on Chen et al.; used an uncorrected p = .05 threshold because its effect-size calculation was conservative. — [NBR 2025 tDCS review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763425002374)
- *npj Science of Learning* (2024): bilateral IFG tDCS alters cognitive processes during creative ideation; summarizes prior findings that anodal left IFG can hinder and right IFG can enhance creative performance. Single study. — [npj Sci Learn 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-024-00285-z)
- No 2023–2026 TMS–creativity meta-analysis was found.

### Inferences
- Rank-ordering interventions by meta-analytic DT evidence: constraints and incubation (moderate, replicated), training (~0.3 SD after bias correction), mindfulness (positive but convergent-leaning), tDCS (null for DT on average; site-specific).
- The consistent "convergent > divergent" asymmetry for mindfulness and tDCS suggests that interventions that increase top-down control help selection/insight more than generation; generation benefits more from control-loosening states (walking, incubation, N1).
- For groups, the actionable finding is unchanged since 1991: generate individually or electronically/structured, then converge together.

### Gaps
- Exact pooled effect sizes for Damadzic 2022 and Chen 2024 (Hedges g for DT vs CT) were behind paywalls.
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis on nominal vs interacting brainstorming, nor on constraint-based ideation prompts specifically for DT tasks.
- No 2023–2026 RCT directly comparing focused-attention vs open-monitoring meditation on DT was found.

## Key Question 5 — Technology: AI/LLMs as idea partners, homogenization, and what it reveals about human ideation

### Takeaway
A consistent 2024–2026 pattern across experiments, a 19-study meta-analysis, and real-world quasi-experiments: generative AI raises the average quality/novelty of an individual's output (largest gains for less creative people) while compressing the collective diversity of ideas; a 2026 *PNAS Nexus* study of 22 LLMs shows the models themselves are internally homogeneous. Exposure to LLM ideas can also reduce subsequent solo originality (CHI 2025), suggesting fixation/anchoring, with diverse AI personas as a partial mitigation.

### Cited Findings
- Doshi & Hauser (2024), *Science Advances* 10:eadn5290: ~300 non-professional writers randomized to no AI, one AI story idea, or up to five AI ideas (GPT-4); 600 evaluators. Stories with access to five AI ideas rated ~8% more novel and ~9% more useful than human-only; less creative writers improved novelty by 10.7% and usefulness by 11.5%; embedding-based similarity among stories increased by 10.7% in the one-idea condition vs no-AI; authors frame a social dilemma (individual gain, collective loss of diversity). — [Doshi & Hauser 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290); [UCL press release](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2024/jul/ai-boosts-individual-creativity-expense-less-varied-content); [Earth.com summary with percentages](https://earth.com/news/artificial-intelligence-boosts-creativity-but-reduces-variety)
- Lee & Chung (2024), *Nature Human Behaviour*: randomized creative challenges (gift ideas, toy design, repurposing household items) with vs without ChatGPT; ChatGPT improved individual idea quality. Meincke, Nave & Terwiesch (2025), *NHB* Matters Arising, reanalysis: across five experiments ChatGPT-assisted sessions produced narrower idea sets; in the toy task 94% of ChatGPT-group ideas shared overlapping concepts and nine participants independently named their toy "Build-a-Breeze Castle." Lee & Chung's reply: individual-level and group-level effects are different questions. — [Meincke et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02173-x); [Lee & Chung reply](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02195-5); [HR Dive summary](https://www.hrdive.com/news/ChatGPT-dulls-creativity-diversity-thought-brainstorm-wharton-school/751569/)
- Anderson, Shah & Kreminski (2024), ACM Creativity & Cognition, N=36: ChatGPT users' ideas were more alike *across* people than Oblique Strategies users', while within-person diversity was similar (group-level homogenization). — [Anderson et al. 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3635636.3656204)
- Kumar et al. (2025), CHI, randomized experiments: LLM help during a task did not improve later solo originality or fluency and in some cases decreased originality and diversity. — [Kumar et al. 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3714198)
- de Rooij & Biskjaer (2026), *Behaviour & Information Technology* (also OSF preprint), systematic review and meta-analysis: 18 articles, 19 experiments, 61 effect sizes (2022–early 2026); significant homogenization effect; strongest in constrained idea-generation tasks; real-world before/after quasi-experiments show a small but significant diversity reduction; preliminary evidence that homogenization persists into subsequent tasks; authors stress this does not mean AI reduces individual creativity. — [de Rooij & Biskjaer 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2026.2726451)
- Wenger & Kenett (2026), *PNAS Nexus* 5:pgag042: 22 LLMs vs >100 humans on AUT, DAT, and Forward Flow; LLM responses far more similar to one another than human responses; creativity-encouraging prompts only "slightly" increased variability. — [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1123936)
- Diverse AI personas mitigate the homogenization effect in human–AI collaborative ideation (2026, *Computers in Human Behavior: AI*; arXiv 2025). — [Diverse AI personas](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S294988212600040X)
- "Inspiration booster or creative fixation?" (2025, *Humanities & Social Sciences Communications*): LLM effects depend on task complexity; reports homogenization at the individual level (fixation). — [HSSC 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05867-9)
- Benchmarks: Arora et al. (2025, *Sci Rep*) and Hubert, Awa & Zabelina (2024, *Sci Rep*) report GenAI models outperform students/humans on DT and CT tests on average. — [Arora et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-21398-4)

### Inferences
- What this reveals about human ideation: (a) idea generation is strongly anchored by the first examples seen (classic fixation/conformity effects scale up when everyone sees the same model's output); (b) individual "quality" and population "diversity" are separable quantities, and most creativity metrics reward the former; (c) less-skilled ideators benefit most from external idea supply, consistent with retrieval ability (Gr) being a bottleneck.
- Practical implication: use LLMs after independent generation (as in nominal-group brainstorming), or with deliberately diverse personas/prompts, to avoid pre-emptive convergence.

### Gaps
- The "Nature Human Behaviour creativity-AI studies" appear to be the Lee & Chung (2024) / Meincke et al. (2025) exchange; no other NHB DT experiment was identified.
- Exact statistics (effect sizes) for Lee & Chung 2024 and Kumar et al. 2025 were not accessible.
- Real-world (petition platform, essays) quasi-experimental magnitudes were only available via secondary summaries.

## Key Question 6 — Social and environmental: group creativity, diversity, remote vs in-person, constraints and deadlines

### Takeaway
Videoconferencing reduces the number of creative ideas pairs generate but not their ability to select good ideas (Brucks & Levav 2022, lab + 5-country field; no replication found); bibliometric evidence (Lin, Frey & Wu 2023, 20M papers/4M patents) shows remote teams produce fewer breakthrough ideas and contribute less to conceptual (idea-conceiving) tasks. Team diversity has small, context-dependent effects on creative performance (registered-report meta-analysis 2024), with demographic diversity essentially unrelated to team creativity in field studies.

### Cited Findings
- Brucks & Levav (2022), *Nature* 605:108–112: laboratory study plus field experiment across five countries (Europe, Middle East, South Asia); videoconferencing "inhibits the production of creative ideas" but there is "no evidence" that virtual pairs are less effective at selecting which idea to pursue, with "preliminary evidence that they may be more effective"; mechanism: screen focus narrows visual and cognitive scope. A correction notice calls for future replication across in-person, electronic, and videoconference formats; no published replication found. — [Brucks & Levav 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04643-y); [ResearchBox data](https://researchbox.org/282); [abstract summary](https://werd.io/virtual-communication-curbs-creative-idea-generation/)
- Lin, Frey & Wu (2023), *Nature* 623:987–991: 20 million articles and 4 million patents over ~50 years; remote teams "consistently less likely to make breakthrough discoveries" across fields, periods, team sizes; remote members concentrate on late-stage codified technical tasks and are less likely to join conceptual tasks such as conceiving ideas. Observational. — [Lin, Frey & Wu 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v623y2023i7989d10.1038_s41586-023-06767-1.html); [Oxford Martin summary](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/publications/remote-collaboration-fuses-fewer-breakthrough-ideas)
- Wallrich et al. (2024), *Journal of Business and Psychology* 39:1303–1354, registered-report meta-analysis: demographic, job-related, and cognitive diversity all show significant, positive, but "insubstantial" average links to team performance; stronger when tasks are complex or require creativity/innovation and in low-collectivism, low-power-distance contexts. — [Wallrich et al. 2024](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/2024-wallrich.pdf)
- Traylor et al. (2024), *Organizational Psychology Review*: demographic diversity → information elaboration not significant in teams with or without creative demands. — [Traylor et al. 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20413866241245312)
- Byron et al. (2023), *Personnel Psychology*, 134 field studies: demographic diversity and team size unrelated to team creativity/innovation. — [Byron et al. 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/peps.12501)
- Wang et al. (2019) meta-analysis: deep-level (cognitive/experiential) diversity positively related to team creativity. — [Wang et al. 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331401613_Team_creativityinnovation_in_culturally_diverse_teams_A_meta-analysis)
- Constraints: Damadzic et al. (2022) positive overall; Theel & Sydow (2024), *Journal of Management Inquiry*, qualitative study of songwriting teams organizing creativity with constraints. — [Damadzic et al. 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/job.2655); [Theel & Sydow 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10564926231191087)
- Group brainstorming productivity loss and EBS crossover (see Q4): [Mullen et al. 1991](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15324834basp1201_1); [DeRosa et al. 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563205000506)

### Inferences
- Across Brucks & Levav, Lin et al., and the brainstorming literature, in-person interaction appears to matter specifically for *generation/conception* (tacit, visually/spatially broad), not for *selection* (codified, evaluative). A practical design is: generate in person or individually, select/elaborate remotely or electronically.
- Diversity effects on idea generation are small on average and depend on task creativity demands and psychological safety; deep-level diversity is the more promising lever than demographic composition.

### Gaps
- Exact lab/field sample sizes and effect sizes for Brucks & Levav were behind the Nature paywall; no independent replication (2023–2026) was found.
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis specifically on deadlines/time pressure and idea generation; evidence remains mixed and mostly older.
