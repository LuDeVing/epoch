# Memory, Semantic Networks, and Conceptual Combination: How New Ideas Are Built from Existing Knowledge (2023–2026 emphasis)

Scope note: These notes cover the cognitive/computational mechanisms by which knowledge structure plus search processes over it produce novelty. Older foundational work is included only for context and is labeled "[foundational]". Where a 2023–2026 source could not be located or retrieved, this is recorded in the Gaps subsection rather than filled in from memory.

## Key Question 1: Network science of semantic memory — "flexible" vs "rigid" networks and creativity (Kenett, Beaty, Benedek, Hills), 2023+

### Takeaway
Since 2023 the network-science program has (a) replicated the "flexible network" signature of creative individuals in new populations (high-vocabulary adults, older adults, design experts/novices), (b) begun to explain *why* flexible structure matters via process models (entropy modulation; clustering/switching foraging), and (c) acknowledged psychometric limits: global network metrics (clustering coefficient, path length) are less reliable as individual-difference measures than local ones, and a prominent 2023 commentary argued network structure may be downstream of other causes.

### Cited Findings
- Flexible structure defined: in Kenett-lab work, higher-creative individuals' semantic networks are "less structured (lower modularity), more clustered, and more interconnected (shorter path lengths)"; this was the pattern reported for high-metaphor producers (networks "more flexible, clustered, and less rigid" than low producers) — [Li, Kenett, Hu & Beaty 2021, Creativity Research Journal (via ECR Hub)](https://ecrhub.org/publications/flexible-semantic-network-structure-supports-production-creative-metaphor) [foundational, 2021].
- Li, Beaty, Luchini, Hu & Kenett (2024, *Learning and Individual Differences*, vol. 111): two studies (N = 506; N = 161). Participants split by vocabulary (crystallized intelligence, Gc) into low/medium/high; semantic networks estimated from semantic fluency; creativity via two Alternate Uses tasks. High-Gc group's networks were "less tightly organized, more clustered, and more interconnected" than the low-Gc group's, replicated across both studies, and the high-Gc group scored higher on creativity. Authors conclude flexible access to semantic memory supports both verbal intelligence and creativity — [Technion CRIS record](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-semantic-memory-networks-in-crystallized-intelligence/); [BIU record](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-semantic-memory-networks-in-crystallized-intelligence/).
- Campidelli, Domanti, Fusi, Kenett & Agnoli (2025, *Cognition* 266:106318, online 11 Sept 2025): 77 older adults (mean age ~77.8) and 81 younger adults (mean ~20.3); networks estimated from two verbal fluency + two free association tasks; creativity by two AUTs. Typical aging was associated with more rigid semantic networks and lower creative performance; but older adults with higher creativity "kept their overall semantic memory flexibility, resembling lower-creativity younger adults," whereas low-creativity older adults did not. Authors frame creativity as a possible protective factor; design is cross-sectional (association only) — [BIU record](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/creativity-the-fountain-of-youth-association-between-creativity-a/); [UniBG repository](https://aisberg.unibg.it/handle/10446/310925); [UniBZ record](https://bia.unibz.it/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Creativity-the-fountain-of-youth-Association/991007283355601241). A 2024 conference abstract of the same project reported that preliminary analyses in the young sample "replicated the typical network structures characterizing high and low creative individuals" — [SISSA abstract, Sept 2024](https://indico.sissa.it/event/149/contributions/2985/).
- Teng et al. (2025, Elsevier): expert–novice design (design students vs non-design); semantic networks from a semantic distance judgment task and an association chain task; "semantic network structure strongly predicted both divergent thinking and visual artistic creativity, with the non-design group showing particularly pronounced effects" — [search summary pointing to ECNU record](https://pure.ecnu.edu.cn/en/publications/semantic-memory-and-associative-ability-as-predictors-of-divergen/) (full statistics not retrieved).
- Ovando-Tellez et al. (2022, *Science Advances*) [foundational, 2022]: brain functional connectivity predicts real-life creativity and this link is *mediated by semantic memory network structure* — [per Technion/BIU search summary](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/creativity-the-fountain-of-youth-association-between-creativity-a/).
- Theoretical synthesis: Beaty & Kenett (2023, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 27(7):671–683), "Associative thinking at the core of creativity," argues that newer computational models of semantic memory "let researchers track how people move through a space of concepts when forming associations, which reveals key search strategies associated with creativity"; distinguishes free vs goal-directed association and links associative thinking to semantic and episodic memory brain systems — [Technion CRIS](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/associative-thinking-at-the-core-of-creativity/); [PSU Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/associative-thinking-at-the-core-of-creativity/).
- Debate: Kounios & Oh (2023, *TiCS* commentary, "Creativity and semantic memory: The answers are upstream") critiqued network-based representations of semantic memory as not causally fundamental; Kenett & Beaty replied in *TiCS* 27(11):979–980 (Nov 2023) — [NSF PAR copy of reply](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10608593).
- Kenett (2024, *Creativity Research Journal*, "The Role of Knowledge in Creative Thinking," DOI 10.1080/10400419.2024.2322858) is an 8-page position paper on how knowledge structure and search relate to creativity — [Technion lab PDF](https://cognitive-complexity.net.technion.ac.il/files/2024/03/The-Role-of-Knowledge-in-Creative-Thinking.pdf) (full text could not be parsed in this session; only bibliographic metadata confirmed).
- Psychometric caveat: a 2023 *Behavior Research Methods* study found that both weighted and unweighted graph representations "reliably capture individual differences in local measures of semantic networks, but that global metrics such as the average clustering coefficient and shortest path length are less robust across tasks and may not provide reliable individual difference measures" — [Springer, s13428-023-02271-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-023-02271-6).
- [foundational] Benedek et al. (2017) showed the associative-structure account was supported only when networks were filtered by a relatedness threshold, not by fixed edge number or weighted analysis; He et al. (2020) found verbal (not figural) creativity related to higher connectivity, shorter distances and lower modularity; Rastelli et al. (2020) found the same in children — [search summary; He et al. via jintelligence PDF](https://iris.unitn.it/bitstream/11572/287626/2/jintelligence-08-00043%20%284%29.pdf).
- Process-level explanation of structure: Hills & Kenett, "An entropy modulation theory of creative exploration," *Psychological Review* 132(1):239–251 (2025; DOI 10.1037/rev0000511; first listed 2024). Starting from the "faster-and-further" phenomenon (higher-creative people produce ideas more quickly and more novel), the theory proposes that the key difference between lower and higher creators lies in how executive processes regulate *entropy* during memory search: retrieval candidates compete to reach an activation threshold; higher activation and greater variance in activation raise retrieval entropy and speed retrieval, reproducing faster-and-further. Critically, "apparent differences in semantic structure may partly arise from this entropy-tuning retrieval process" — [Warwick WRAP](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/187197); [BIU CRIS](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/an-entropy-modulation-theory-of-creative-exploration/).

### Inferences
- The field is converging on a structure×process view: flexible network topology is a reliable correlate of creativity, but 2024–2025 theory (entropy modulation; clustering/switching) treats observed "flexibility" as partly an *outcome* of how control processes tune retrieval, not solely a fixed representational trait.
- The lifespan and Gc studies suggest flexibility is not just innate: larger knowledge bases (vocabulary) and sustained creative activity co-occur with more flexible access, consistent with Kenett's "knowledge enables creativity" framing.
- Because global metrics are psychometrically weaker, the strongest claims should rest on local/relative measures or on group-level network comparisons (which is how most of these studies are designed).

### Gaps
- Full-text effect sizes for Campidelli et al. 2025 and Teng et al. 2025 were not retrievable (abstracts/repository summaries only).
- No 2023–2026 study was found that *experimentally* changes network structure and measures downstream creativity (all evidence is correlational/cross-sectional).
- Kenett 2024 CRJ full text could not be parsed; its specific claims are not reported here.

## Key Question 2: Associative theories of creativity — Mednick revisited with embeddings/LLM measures; DAT; forward flow

### Takeaway
Modern embedding-based measures (DAT, forward flow, SemDis) have made Mednick's remote-association idea testable at scale; 2024–2025 work refines it in two important ways: (1) semantic distance predicts creativity *non-linearly* with a "sweet spot" (~0.9–1.0 cosine distance), beyond which more distance does not raise rated creativity, and (2) fine-tuned LLM raters outperform raw semantic distance at predicting human creativity ratings, implying that "remoteness" is necessary but not sufficient.

### Cited Findings
- [foundational] Mednick's theory: creative people have "flat" (broad) rather than "steep" (few, common) associative hierarchies; Benedek & Neubauer (2013) found hierarchies did *not* differ between low and high creatives, but creatives showed higher associative fluency and more uncommon responses, suggesting "a more effective way of accessing" memory rather than different organization — [PMC3924568](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3924568/); Kenett, Anaki & Faust (2014) built networks from free-association overlap and found flatter structure in creatives — [PMC4051268](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4051268/).
- Divergent Association Task (DAT): participants give 10 nouns as different as possible; score = mean pairwise cosine distance (GloVe) among first 7 valid words × 100; in the 2021 PNAS validation (8,914 participants) DAT showed "moderate to strong relationships with established creativity measures" (AUT, Bridge-the-Associative-Gap) at least as strong as those measures' inter-correlations — [McGill summary](https://healthenews.mcgill.ca/measuring-creativity-one-word-at-a-time/); [siliconcanals description of scoring](https://siliconcanals.com/t-human-ai-creativity-100000-gpt4-average-top-half/) [foundational, 2021].
- DAT extensions 2024–2025: a Japanese version (DAT-J) was developed and published in *Cognitive Studies* (Japanese Cognitive Science Society, 2024/2025) — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcss/32/1/32_2024.038/_article/-char/en); a multilingual "S-DAT" using LLM/multilingual embeddings was proposed (Haase, Hanel & Pokutta, 2025 arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed), noting the original DAT relies on English-only static embeddings — [arXiv 2505.09068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09068v1.pdf).
- Forward flow (FF): a chained free-association measure using LSA/embedding distance; in Beaty, Zeitlen, Baker & Kenett (2021, *Thinking Skills and Creativity*) FF (β = .48) and intelligence (β = .36) independently predicted divergent thinking, replicated in Study 2 — [PSU Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/forward-flow-and-creative-thought-assessing-associative-cognition/) [foundational, 2021].
- Baror, Aminoff & Kenett (2024 online; *Memory & Cognition* 53(5), 2025; DOI 10.3758/s13421-024-01672-y): Exp 1 (N = 75) and Exp 2 (N = 74). FF correlated with AUT creativity (r = .26, p = .02 in Exp 1; r = .37, p < .001 in Exp 2). Generating chained associations after viewing scenes improved scene-gist memory (d′ 1.65 vs 0.91, d = 0.65; hit rate .86 vs .52, d = 1.4) but FF itself did not predict memory, and the memory effect disappeared when scenes were first labeled — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-024-01672-y); [PMC12307568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12307568).
- Non-linearity ("sweet spot"): Orwig, Luchini, Beaty & Schacter (2025, *Journal of Creative Behavior*; CCN 2025 abstract) used generalized additive models on 3,448 AUT responses, 2,430 question-asking responses and 4,376 metaphor responses (5-point human creativity ratings; semantic distance = inverse cosine similarity cue–response). Across all three tasks, "creativity ratings increased with semantic distance up to a threshold of 0.9–1; after this point more semantic distance didn't translate into more creative ideas." Authors: "extreme distance may hinder creativity by producing ideas that lack coherence or relevance." Data/code: osf.io/74xw3 — [CCN 2025 abstract PDF](https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Orwig_2025_A_sweet_spot_creative_ideation_Non-linear.pdf).
- LLM raters beat raw distance: DiStefano, Patterson & Beaty (2024, *Creativity Research Journal*, "Automatic scoring of metaphor creativity with large language models") collected 4,589 metaphor responses from 1,546 participants; fine-tuned RoBERTa (r = .72) and GPT-2 (r = .70) predicted held-out human creativity ratings far better than semantic distance (r = .42), and generalized to untrained prompts — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10623591) (authors identified via the Orwig et al. reference list, above).
- Alternative distance metric: "maximum associative distance" (MAD) — the distance of the single most remote response to the prompt — correlated more strongly with human creativity ratings than competing methods (dissertation-level work; authors not shown in the retrieved snippet) — [University of Malta OAR](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/63037).
- Semantic distance of word pairings also shapes creativity judgments of *text*: Winter & Strik Lievers (2023, *Metaphor and the Social World*), preregistered, found a continuous measure of sensory-modality difference predicts metaphoricity and creativity judgments — [Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/semantic-distance-predicts-metaphoricity-and-creativity-judgments/).
- Aging and association: an NSF-archived study found creative performance stayed stable in older adults while associative abilities declined with age — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10582079) (full citation not retrieved).

### Inferences
- The modern reading of Mednick is "remote *and* coherent": embedding distance captures remoteness, but ratings plateau past ~0.9–1.0, so human creativity judgments (and presumably the generative process) impose a usefulness/coherence constraint that distance alone misses — consistent with the novelty×effectiveness definition and with the MemiC framework's separate evaluation stages (Q6).
- The FF–creativity correlations (r ≈ .26–.37) in 2024 data are smaller than the 2021 β = .48, suggesting FF is a modest-to-moderate individual-difference predictor whose magnitude depends on sample and task.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study was found that directly re-tests flat-vs-steep hierarchies using embeddings on continuous free-association data; the closest are the sweet-spot and MAD papers.
- DAT-J validation statistics and DAT test–retest reliability coefficients were not retrieved.

## Key Question 3: Conceptual combination / blending — cognitive and neural mechanisms (noun-noun combinations, metaphor generation)

### Takeaway
Direct 2023–2026 neuroimaging of creative noun-noun combination is sparse; the newest mechanistic evidence comes from (a) EEG dynamics during metaphor generation (early alpha synchronization then desynchronization predicts novelty), (b) behavioral evidence that a relational/analogical mindset increases metaphor production, and (c) computational work showing that creative combination benefits are non-linear in distance and that LLM-based raters capture combination quality beyond distance.

### Cited Findings
- [foundational] Neural bases of noun-noun combination: Graves et al. (2010) contrasted meaningful (LAKE HOUSE) vs reversed (HOUSE LAKE) phrases; Baron et al. (2010) showed superimposed constituent-concept activity predicts the combined-concept pattern in left anterolateral temporal lobe; the angular gyrus is argued to be critical for conceptual combination — [Graves et al. PDF](https://sc.edu/study/colleges_schools/artsandsciences/psychology/research_clinical_facilities/labs/desai_lab/desai_references/graves_et_al_2010_neural_correlates_of_implicit_and_explicit_combinatorial_semantic_processing.pdf); [Baron et al. record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/an-early-stage-of-conceptual-combination-superimposition-of-const/); [angular gyrus review, PMC4331639](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4331639).
- [foundational, 2020] *NeuroImage*: generating original "bi-associations" to verbal cues produced higher activation in bilateral hippocampus and inferior parietal lobe, suggesting "conceptual combination recruits episodic simulation processes" — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1a9710c4ccf44f05af6e00e4bec4bde1).
- Lai & Yu (2025, *Psychophysiology*; University of Arizona): EEG with modeling during generation of metaphors for scientific ideas; the most novel metaphors followed a sequence in which "early on, stronger alpha-band synchronization predicted greater novelty, and later, alpha desynchronization became more prominent" — [PsyPost summary, May 2025](https://www.psypost.org/brain-oscillations-reveal-dynamic-shifts-in-creative-thought-during-metaphor-generation/).
- Togashi, Shirakawa & Terai (date unconfirmed): upper-alpha EEG during description of abstract images; shape-based descriptions were largely metaphorical, and metaphor generation showed reduced upper-alpha power (especially posterior) vs literal expression; framed within conceptual blending theory — [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9ft172m6/qt9ft172m6.pdf).
- George, Christofalos & Pambuccian (2025, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 32(3):1402–1410; DOI 10.3758/s13423-024-02628-8): inducing a "relational mindset" by solving cross-domain (far) analogies (e.g., kitten:cat::spark:?) vs within-domain (near) analogies, then writing summary phrases for topics; "use of metaphoric language increased when topics contained more features, and was particularly frequent in the high relational mindset condition" — [Boise State record](https://experts.boisestate.edu/en/publications/generating-distant-analogies-increases-metaphor-production/).
- [foundational, 2015] Chan & Schunn (*Cognition*): tracking hundreds of concepts across a dozen problems on an innovation platform, direct effects of far combinations had a mean zero effect on creativity, while indirect effects (building on concepts that themselves built on far combinations) were more consistently positive — [Pitt PDF](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/Chan-Schunn2015-Cognition.pdf).
- Computational modeling: a 2023/2024 University of Milan-Bicocca PhD thesis proposes distributional-semantics models of conceptual combination and reviews its neural bases (modeling, not fMRI) — [UNIMIB thesis](https://boa.unimib.it/retrieve/ba400a66-dbdf-4437-ab36-88e0040d148c/phd_unimib_815165.pdf).
- Metaphor creativity scoring (see Q2): DiStefano, Patterson & Beaty 2024, 4,589 metaphors, fine-tuned LLM raters r = .72 vs semantic distance r = .42 — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10623591).
- Non-linear distance–creativity relation holds for metaphor generation specifically (4,376 responses; plateau at 0.9–1.0) — [Orwig et al. 2025](https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Orwig_2025_A_sweet_spot_creative_ideation_Non-linear.pdf).
- Mental imagery and semantic integration in creative writing: a Sept 2025 *Communications Biology* paper examined how mental imagery supports creative writing via semantic integration (method not confirmed from abstract) — [PubMed 41028539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41028539/).
- Semantic networks and metaphor: high-metaphor producers have more flexible/clustered/less rigid networks (Li et al. 2021) — [ECR Hub](https://ecrhub.org/publications/flexible-semantic-network-structure-supports-production-creative-metaphor) [foundational].

### Inferences
- Combining the Chan & Schunn null direct effect, the Orwig plateau, and the LLM-rater advantage: successful combination depends on integrating the two concepts into a coherent relational structure, not merely on their distance. The 2025 PB&R mindset study supports a causal role for relational (analogical) processing in producing figurative combinations.
- The EEG sequence (alpha synchronization → desynchronization) is consistent with a two-phase account: internally directed search/inhibition of dominant meanings first, then integration/elaboration.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 fMRI study of *creative* noun-noun combination was found; the neural evidence is older (2010–2020) or EEG-based.
- Quantitative effect sizes for George et al. 2025 and Lai & Yu 2025 were not retrievable.

## Key Question 4: Analogy and relational reasoning — Gentner, Holyoak, and LLM-vs-human comparisons

### Takeaway
Structure-mapping theory (Gentner & Forbus 2025 update) and Holyoak's relation-completion account (Holyoak, Ichien & Lu 2023; *The Human Edge*, 2025) frame analogy as transfer of relational systems across domains, which can generate new representations. LLM comparisons split: Webb/Holyoak/Lu (2023 NHB; 2025 PNAS Nexus) report emergent zero-shot analogy at or above human level, while Lewis & Mitchell (2024–2025 TMLR) show GPT models are brittle under counterfactual variants where humans stay robust; 2026 work finds humans benefit from cross-domain mapping at almost any distance whereas LLMs benefit only when sources are very remote, and humans transfer surface features more than LLMs do.

### Cited Findings
- Gentner & Forbus (2025, *Current Directions in Psychological Science*): review of structure-mapping theory and the Structure-Mapping Engine as psychological theory + computational model — [Northwestern PDF](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/GentnerForbus-2025-SME.pdf). Gentner (2025, *Open Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science*): analogy = "a kind of similarity in which the same system of relations holds across different sets of elements"; systematicity bias (preference for relational systems connected by higher-order constraining relations); schema abstraction via mapping makes common structure "more salient and available for transfer" — [Northwestern PDF](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/Gentner-Analogy-OECS2025.pdf).
- Holyoak, Ichien & Lu (2023, *Creativity Research Journal*, DOI 10.1080/10400419.2023.2232673), "Analogy and the generation of ideas": one contributor to creativity is "the capacity to build new mental representations of unfamiliar situations by completing a partly specified relation or analogy"; distant analogies "can encourage a creative mindset"; discusses computational mechanisms for relation-driven idea generation — [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10435749); [UCLA Reasoning Lab publications](https://reasoninglab.psych.ucla.edu/?p=528). Holyoak's book *The Human Edge: Analogy and the Roots of Creative Intelligence* (MIT Press, 2025) — [MIT Press Reader](https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/analogy-and-the-roots-of-creative-intelligence).
- Webb, Holyoak & Lu (2023, *Nature Human Behaviour* 7(9):1526–1541): GPT-3 "displayed a surprisingly strong capacity for abstract pattern induction, matching or even surpassing human capabilities in most settings" on letter-string, digit-matrix (Raven-like) and story analogies; preliminary GPT-4 tests were better — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v7y2023i9d10.1038_s41562-023-01659-w.html); [arXiv 2212.09196](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09196).
- Lewis & Mitchell (2024, arXiv 2402.08955): counterfactual letter-string analogies (permuted alphabets, non-letter symbols): humans performed well on both original and counterfactual problems; every GPT model tested degraded on counterfactuals — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08955v1). Lewis & Mitchell (2025, *TMLR*; arXiv 2411.14215), "Evaluating the Robustness of Analogical Reasoning in LLMs": on simple letter-string problems human performance "remains high" under two variant types while GPT performance "declines sharply"; on harder problems both do poorly; digit-matrix pattern similar for one variant type; on story analogies GPT models show answer-order effects and paraphrase sensitivity unlike humans; conclusion: LLMs "often lack the robustness of zero-shot human analogy-making" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14215); [TMLR listing](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2025/lewis2025tmlr-evaluating); [UvA press release, Feb 2025](https://www.uva.nl/en/content/news/press-releases/2025/02/why-gpt-cant-think-like-humans.html).
- Webb, Holyoak & Lu (2025, *PNAS Nexus* 4(5); arXiv 2404.13070), "Evidence from counterfactual tasks supports emergent analogical reasoning in LLMs": argues the critiques misunderstood the original materials; GPT-4 solved counterfactual letter-string analogies "at a roughly human level when it could run code to count" (using self-generated code only to convert letters to indices), but "significantly worse" without that — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13070); [UCLA lab news](https://reasoninglab.psych.ucla.edu/category/news).
- Liu, Dubova, Conklin, Harada & Griffiths (2026, arXiv 2603.19087, v3 Sept 2026), "Assessing the Effect of Cross-Domain Mapping on Creativity in Humans and LLMs": humans and seven LLMs designed products either drawing inspiration from a random source or addressing an unmet need. Humans "reliably benefited from cross-domain mappings"; LLMs "generated more original ideas than humans overall but showed no overall benefit from the intervention"; more distant source–target pairings produced more original ideas in both; humans benefited "at nearly any distance" whereas only the highest-rated LLMs benefited when the source was "sufficiently remote"; humans tended to transfer *surface* features, LLMs transferred *structural and functional* properties — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19087) (preprint, not peer reviewed; sample sizes not on abstract page).
- Relational mindset → metaphor: George et al. 2025 PB&R (see Q3) — [Boise State record](https://experts.boisestate.edu/en/publications/generating-distant-analogies-increases-metaphor-production/).
- Ichien, Stamenković & Holyoak (2023): GPT-4 interpretations of novel literary metaphors were rated excellent/good by a literary critic — [arXiv 2308.01497](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01497). Holyoak (2026, *Journal of Creativity*) compares human vs artificial creativity via poetry case study — [UCLA lab publications](https://reasoninglab.psych.ucla.edu/?p=528).

### Inferences
- The human mechanism highlighted by these comparisons is *robust relational abstraction*: people map structure even when surface tokens are scrambled, while LLM success is sensitive to format, order and symbol identity unless scaffolded by tools. That robustness is plausibly what lets humans harvest value from near as well as far sources (Liu et al. 2026).
- The Liu et al. finding that humans transfer surface features but still gain originality suggests human creative transfer is often *partial* or feature-level, consistent with Holyoak's "completing a partly specified relation" rather than full structural isomorphism.

### Gaps
- Human sample sizes and accuracy figures for Lewis & Mitchell and Liu et al. were not retrievable from abstracts.
- No 2023–2026 Gentner empirical paper specifically on invention/creativity was found (only theory updates).

## Key Question 5: Episodic memory, future thinking, and divergent thinking (Schacter, Addis, Madore); newest specificity-induction findings

### Takeaway
The constructive episodic simulation hypothesis predicts that flexible recombination of episodic details supports creative idea generation; TMS (2020) and fMRI conjunction work support hippocampal/core-network involvement, and divergent thinking predicts episodic detail for *novel* future events and *new* ideas. However, the specificity-induction boost to divergent thinking did not replicate across three 2023 experiments (Ahmed, Pauly-Takacs & Abraham, PLOS ONE), and no 2024–2026 peer-reviewed replication was found; newest evidence is a 2024 dissertation (hippocampal TMS) and a 2025 CNS pupillometry poster.

### Cited Findings
- [foundational] Madore, Addis & Schacter (2015, *Psychological Science*): a brief episodic specificity induction (Cognitive Interview-based training in recalling who/what/where/when details) selectively boosted AUT divergent thinking vs control, not an object-association task; no effect on convergent thinking (Exp 2) — [PMC4567456](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4567456/). Madore, Jing & Schacter (2016): effect generalized to older adults and to the Consequences task; new (and to some extent old) ideas boosted — [PMC4975991](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4975991). Abraham (2016) commentary: effects were on fluency/flexibility, not originality/elaboration — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00824/pdf).
- Ahmed, Pauly-Takacs & Abraham (2023, *PLOS ONE*): three experiments with episodic and semantic induction procedures vs control and no-induction baseline in younger and older adults; "no clear evidence for a specific role played by the induction of episodic or semantic memory processes in facilitating creative thinking across the three experiments," and induction effects "were not comparable across age groups"; higher creativity generally in older adults (60–80) — [PMC10237455](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10237455/); [Leeds Beckett eprint](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/9715).
- Thakral, Madore, Kalinowski & Schacter (2020, *PNAS*) [foundational]: fMRI-guided TMS to hippocampal networks impaired both episodic simulation and divergent thinking; "hippocampal-targeted stimulation can selectively modulate both abilities" — [Smith ScholarWorks record](https://scholarworks.smith.edu/psy_facpubs/249).
- Beaty, Thakral, Madore, Benedek & Schacter (fMRI, *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience*): triple conjunction of episodic retrieval, future simulation and divergent thinking showed common engagement of bilateral hippocampus and parahippocampal gyrus, plus IFG and middle occipital gyrus — [PSU Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/core-network-contributions-to-remembering-the-past-imagining-the-/) [foundational].
- Madore, Thakral, Beaty, Addis & Schacter: after the specificity induction, divergent thinking showed greater hippocampal activity and stronger coupling between the core (default) network and a frontoparietal control network — [PSU Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/neural-mechanisms-of-episodic-retrieval-support-divergent-creativ/) [foundational].
- Thakral, Addis & Schacter (2021): episodic detail for novel and recast *future* events was associated with AUT scores, for *new* creative ideas but not old ones; no link with detail in recalled past events — [search summary; Harvard Schacter lab](https://sites.harvard.edu/schacter-memory/?p=1459). Thakral, Devitt, Brashier & Schacter (2021): divergent thinking scores predicted false recognition and false recall — "constructive memory processes link creative thinking with the production of memory errors" — [Harvard PDF](https://sites.harvard.edu/schacter-memory/files/2022/01/thakral-2021-linking-creativity-and-false-memory.pdf).
- Kalinowski (2024, Harvard PhD dissertation, advisor Schacter), *Constructive Episodic Simulation in Brain and Cognition*: Study 3 "uses hippocampal-targeted TMS to test whether the hippocampus causally supports both creative thinking and episodic simulation"; a summary also states divergent thinking "was affected by the specificity induction" — [DASH record](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/85cc011c-62ef-4cb2-b042-a580ea731256) (results not in abstract).
- Thakral, Madore & Devitt (2025, Cognitive Neuroscience Society poster): pupil dilation tracked divergent thinking ability and number of episodic details during episodic memory and simulation, but not semantic memory — [CNS poster listing](https://cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6123).
- Fusi et al. (2026, *Applied Cognitive Psychology*), "Creative Mental Time Travelling": extends the Addis et al. (2016) logic that divergent thinking affects construction of detailed imagined future/past events — [UniBG PDF](https://aisberg.unibg.it/retrieve/19ce804e-9f47-4b17-94b9-5c399fe79a1d/Applied%20Cognitive%20Psychology%20-%202026%20-%20Fusi%20-%20Creative%20Mental%20Time%20Travelling%20%20The%20Influence%20of%20Divergent%20Thinking%20on%20the.pdf) (full findings not extracted).
- Schacter & Thakral (2024, *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 36(8)), "Constructive memory and conscious experience" — [Smith College listing](https://bouncenode02.smith.edu/people/preston-thakral).
- Meta-analytic context: Gerver, Griffin, Dennis & Beaty (2023, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 30(6):2116–2154): 525 correlations, 79 studies, 12,846 participants; overall memory–creativity r = .19; all four memory types (semantic, episodic, working, short-term) significantly correlated with creativity; semantic memory (esp. verbal fluency) drove the relationship; working memory more tied to convergent than divergent thinking; correlation larger in children than young adults — [PSU Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/memory-and-creativity-a-meta-analytic-examination-of-the-relation/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.3758/s13423-023-02303-4).
- Bi-association fMRI (2020, *NeuroImage*): original combinations recruited hippocampus and IPL, "suggesting that conceptual combination recruits episodic simulation processes" — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/1a9710c4ccf44f05af6e00e4bec4bde1) [foundational].

### Inferences
- Causal (TMS) and neural (conjunction, coupling) evidence for hippocampal/core-network involvement in divergent thinking is stronger than the behavioral induction evidence, which now looks fragile (one 2023 failure to replicate; no 2024–2026 published replication found).
- The specific contribution of episodic memory appears to be *detail recombination for novel constructions* (new ideas, novel future events), not retrieval of past episodes per se; the false-memory link shows the same flexibility has costs.

- No 2024–2026 peer-reviewed specificity-induction × creativity experiment was found; the field's current status is unresolved.
- Episodic-specific effect size from Gerver et al. 2023 not retrieved (only overall r = .19).

## Key Question 6: Cognitive models of memory search during idea generation (exploration/exploitation, semantic foraging, controlled vs spontaneous retrieval, inhibition, serial order effect)

### Takeaway
2023–2025 models cast idea generation as foraging over semantic memory under executive control: the MemiC framework (search → candidate construction → novelty evaluation → effectiveness evaluation), the entropy-modulation theory (control tunes retrieval entropy, producing faster-and-further output), and clustering/switching analyses using the Marginal Value Theorem, where slow clustering relates to divergent thinking (control/salience networks) and fast switching to remote-associate combination (default + control networks). Serial-order and inhibition studies add that suppressing dominant responses (mnemonic inhibition) matters especially late in generation.

### Cited Findings
- Benedek, Beaty, Schacter & Kenett (2023, *Nature Reviews Psychology* 2(4):246–257), "The role of memory in creative ideation": good ideas "usually come from purposeful memory retrieval rather than appearing unprompted"; creative ideation relies on controlled retrieval drawing on semantic and episodic memory and reuses memory-construction processes; proposes the MemiC framework with four stages — memory search, candidate idea construction, novelty evaluation, effectiveness evaluation — with memory systems contributing differently per stage — [BIU CRIS](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-memory-in-creative-ideation/).
- Hills & Kenett (2025, *Psychological Review* 132(1):239–251): entropy modulation theory — executive processes regulate entropy of competitive retrieval; higher activation and activation variance increase entropy and speed, reproducing faster-and-further; governs flexibility and exploration–exploitation balance — [Warwick WRAP](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/187197). A University of Tartu bachelor's thesis implemented the idea in a neural network and found higher internal entropy produced outputs that were "more diverse and generated more quickly" — [Tartu DSpace](https://dspace.ut.ee/items/6f768d5d-ab8f-431e-9173-5f6880d8296d/full) (student thesis, low evidential weight).
- Hills (2024, *Behavioral Network Science*, Cambridge, ch. 11 "Cognitive Foraging: Exploration versus Exploitation," pp. 161–183): asks "Is searching memory like searching space?" combining memory-production data with a network cognitive map from natural language — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/behavioral-network-science/cognitive-foraging-exploration-versus-exploitation/C6339CECC348298FAF3B480A9B2A1B6C). [foundational] Hills, Todd & Jones (2015, *TopiCS*): verbal fluency search resembles area-restricted foraging (local patches then switches) — [Warwick PDF](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/psych/people/thills/thills/2015hillstoddtics.pdf).
- Kenett, Baker, Hills, Hart & Beaty (2021, CogSci), "Creative Foraging": participants split by exploration/exploitation style in a creative foraging game differed in semantic network properties (animal fluency), and these related to divergent thinking — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/creative-foraging-examining-relations-between-foraging-styles-sem/) [foundational].
- Ovando-Tellez et al. (2025, *Imaging Neuroscience*, DOI 10.1162/IMAG.a.1018; Sorbonne/Paris Brain Institute with Technion, Graz, Warwick, Bordeaux), "Switching, fast and slow": associative fluency with polysemous words analyzed via clustering–switching and the Marginal Value Theorem (optimal foraging). Most participants' retrieval matched MVT predictions, some deviated; replicated in an independent sample. "Participants with more frequent slow-clustering during retrieval exhibited higher divergent thinking ability, whereas participants with more frequent fast-switching were better able to combine remote associates." Connectome-based predictive modeling: functional connectivity predicted MVT-deviant patterns and mediated the brain–creativity relation; clustering relied on control, salience and attention network interactions ("controlled processes related to persistent search"), switching on default + control network connectivity ("interactions between memory structure and control processes guiding the search") — [PMC12628016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12628016/); [Paris Brain Institute news](https://parisbraininstitute.org/news/how-do-we-explore-our-knowledge-be-creative). A follow-up FENS 2026 abstract reports fast switching linked to reduced network modularity and driving fluency–creativity covariation — [FENS abstract](https://world-wide.org/fens-26/shortcuts-semantic-memory-account-creativity-fluency-d85aa5a1).
- Nath, Dayan & Stevenson (2024, arXiv 2405.00899), "Characterising the creative process in humans and LLMs": sentence-embedding "jump profiles" on AUT and verbal fluency classify search as *persistent* (deep within few semantic spaces) vs *flexible* (broad across many). Humans show both pathways with similar creativity scores; LLMs are biased toward one pathway (varying by task), and among LLMs more flexible models score higher — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00899).
- Serial order effect and inhibition: Jędrusiak (2024, *Creativity: Theories–Research–Applications*; N = 300; SEM and multilevel models): mnemonic inhibition (thinking-induced forgetting) and attentional inhibition (Stroop) were "independently associated with originality"; mnemonic inhibition mattered more "in the later phases of generating ideas," attentional inhibition throughout; attentional inhibition "may be a prerequisite for mnemonic inhibition" — [CTRA 2024 PDF](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/ctra-2024-0012.pdf). [foundational] Wang et al. (2017, EEG): shifting and inhibition contribute to the serial order effect "because individuals suppress interference from obvious ideas and switch to new idea categories" — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02327/pdf). tDCS over DLPFC (L+R−) diminished the serial order effect with accelerated production of original ideas — [imec publication](https://imec-publications.be/bitstreams/5d27cb7e-b0a0-4ae7-93f1-04e697fb51d2/download) (year not confirmed).
- Critique: Barbot et al. (2026) argue the serial order effect's magnitude is "hardly reported" and its sensitivity to task/scoring rarely investigated — [MDPI Journal of Intelligence](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/14/6/100); a 2026 preprint reports "human-like SOEs in AUT performance across a range of LLMs" — [preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202609.2250) (not peer reviewed).
- Retrieval strategy training: a "disassembly" semantic retrieval strategy enhanced AUT idea novelty in older adults, but did not transfer to DAT — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10603994-enhancing-creative-divergent-thinking-older-adults-semantic-retrieval-strategy).
- [foundational] Hass (2018): AUT and Consequences response times fit an exponential cumulative model "consistent with studies of memory retrieval"; serial order effect in both — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02327/full).
- Spontaneous vs controlled: Baror, Aminoff & Kenett 2024 show spontaneous chained association both enhances gist memory and correlates with creativity (r = .26–.37) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-024-01672-y).

### Inferences
- A consistent two-mode picture emerges across labs: a persistent/exploitative mode (local clustering, control-network driven, linked to divergent fluency/originality) and a flexible/exploratory mode (fast switching, default-network involvement, linked to remote combination). Creativity benefits from the *alternation*, not from one mode alone (Ovando-Tellez 2025; Nath et al. 2024).
- Entropy modulation offers a single-parameter account of how control could switch between these modes, and reframes "flat hierarchies" as a possibly emergent property of high-entropy retrieval.

- Exact correlation/mediation statistics from Ovando-Tellez 2025 were not retrievable (paywall/403); only directional findings are reported.
- Sample sizes and model names in Nath et al. 2024 were not on the abstract page.

## Key Question 7: Human vs LLM creativity comparisons (2023–2026) and what they reveal about human mechanisms

### Takeaway
On short divergent-thinking tests (AUT, Consequences, DAT) GPT-4-class models match or exceed *average* humans, but large-scale comparisons (≈9–10k humans vs ≈215k LLM observations; 100k-human DAT dataset) show humans have greater variance and a higher ceiling, with top humans outscoring all models. LLM outputs are far more homogeneous across models than humans are to each other; temperature helps only up to a coherence threshold; persona prompts hurt. Process analyses show humans use both persistent and flexible search with equal success, whereas LLMs are biased toward one; humans are robust to counterfactual analogies and benefit from cross-domain mapping at any distance, where LLMs are not.

### Cited Findings
- Hubert, Awa & Zabelina (2024, *Scientific Reports*): 151 humans vs GPT-4 on AUT, Consequences and DAT; "AI was robustly more creative along each divergent thinking measurement"; controlling for fluency, AI was more original and elaborate — [PMC10858891](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10858891/).
- Huang et al. (2025, *Nature Human Behaviour*, DOI 10.1038/s41562-025-02331-1), "Human versus Machine Creativity: A Large-Scale Comparison of Divergent Thinking in Humans and LLMs": DAT with ~10,000 humans (published version reportedly 9,198) vs >200,000 LLM observations (215,542 reported). Humans scored slightly higher on average; differences "most pronounced at the extremes," with greater human variability and more originality at the upper end; top humans outscore GPT-4 and DeepSeek. Raising the LLM "creativity" (temperature) parameter improved scores up to a threshold then outputs "became nonsensical"; prompt engineering gave "mixed to negative results" — performance "dropped significantly" when models adopted creative-genius personas, and demographic-persona prompts reversed the pattern seen in human groups — [ShanghaiTech seminar page](https://www.shanghaitech.edu.cn/2025/0606/c14749a1111628/page.htm); [CAS AMSS summary](https://amss.cas.cn/kyjz1/202512/t20251229_8069181.html).
- Bellemare-Pepin, Lespinasse, … Olson, Bengio & Jerbi (arXiv 2405.13012, May 2024, rev. July 2025; 40 authors; reported as *Scientific Reports*, Jan 2026): LLMs (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini in press accounts) vs ~100,000 humans on DAT and creative writing; LLMs "can surpass average human performance on the DAT, and approach human creative writing abilities," but "fall short of the typical performance of highly creative humans" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13012); [press summary](https://siliconcanals.com/n-ai-just-beat-the-average-human-on-a-creativity-test-involving-100000-people-and-the-researchers-say-the-real-question-is-whether-the-gap-between-average-and-exceptional-will-hold/).
- Wenger & Kenett (2025, arXiv 2501.19361), "We're Different, We're the Same: Creative Homogeneity Across LLMs": across standardized creativity tests, "LLM responses are much more similar to other LLM responses than human responses are to each other," even controlling for response structure; implication: LLM creative partners "may drive all users towards a limited set of" outputs — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361).
- Orwig, Edenbaum, Greene & Schacter (2024, *Journal of Creative Behavior* 58(1):128–136), "The language of creativity: Evidence from humans and large language models" — cited in Orwig et al. 2025 abstract — [CCN 2025 PDF](https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Orwig_2025_A_sweet_spot_creative_ideation_Non-linear.pdf) (content not retrieved).
- Process-level: Nath, Dayan & Stevenson 2024 — humans use persistent and flexible pathways with similar creativity; LLMs biased to one — [arXiv 2405.00899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00899).
- Analogy robustness: Lewis & Mitchell 2025 (humans robust, GPT brittle) vs Webb et al. 2025 (GPT-4 ≈ human with code tool) — [arXiv 2411.14215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14215); [arXiv 2404.13070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13070).
- Cross-domain mapping: Liu et al. 2026 — LLMs more original overall but no benefit from inspiration prompts; humans benefit at nearly any source distance; humans transfer surface features, LLMs structural/functional ones — [arXiv 2603.19087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19087).
- Semantic-distance plateau applies to scoring LLM output too: distance beyond ~0.9–1.0 yields no gain in rated creativity, which the Huang et al. temperature finding mirrors (nonsense past a threshold) — [Orwig et al. 2025](https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Orwig_2025_A_sweet_spot_creative_ideation_Non-linear.pdf); [ShanghaiTech summary](https://www.shanghaitech.edu.cn/2025/0606/c14749a1111628/page.htm).
- Serial order effects observed in LLM AUT output (2026 preprint, not peer reviewed) — [preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202609.2250).
- Holyoak (2024, *Journal of Cognitive Psychology*), "Why I am not a Turing machine," and Holyoak (2026, *Journal of Creativity*) poetry case study — [UCLA lab](https://reasoninglab.psych.ucla.edu/?p=528).

### Inferences
- What the comparisons isolate as distinctively human: (1) *variance and tail* — human populations contain individuals whose remote-but-coherent associations exceed any sampled model, consistent with individual differences in network flexibility and control (Q1, Q6); (2) *inter-individual diversity* — human knowledge structures differ far more from one another than LLMs do, which is itself a source of population-level novelty; (3) *mode alternation* — humans flexibly switch between persistent and exploratory search; (4) *robust relational abstraction* and the ability to profit from near as well as far analogies.
- The LLM temperature ceiling and the human semantic-distance plateau both point to the same constraint on novelty: beyond a coherence boundary, remoteness stops counting as creativity — supporting two-stage (generate → evaluate) accounts like MemiC.
