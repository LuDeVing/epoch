# The Neuroscience of Creative Idea Generation: Brain Networks (DMN, ECN, Salience) and Their Interaction, with Emphasis on 2023-2026 Findings

Scope note: Compiled 2026-10-08. Priority given to peer-reviewed work from 2023-2026 plus three 2025-2026 preprints (flagged as such). Older foundational studies are labeled "[foundational, pre-2023]". Where a paper's statistics looked internally inconsistent on the page, this is flagged rather than silently resolved.

---

## Key Question 1: What is the current "dual-process / network coupling" model of creativity (DMN + ECN + salience), what evidence supports it, and what are the Beaty and Jung labs showing most recently?

### Takeaway
The model holds that idea generation draws on default mode network (DMN) regions for spontaneous/associative retrieval and executive control network (ECN) regions for goal-directed search and evaluation, with the salience network proposed as a switch between them; until 2025 all supporting evidence was correlational, but a 2025 Beaty-lab neurofeedback study and 2024 intracranial stimulation study now supply the first causal evidence, while a 2025 10-sample (N = 2,433) analysis reframes the key variable as the rate of DMN-ECN *switching* (with an inverted-U), and Jung's 2025 work (two samples, N = 236 and 234) shows that creative *achievement* tracks greater functional *differentiation* between the two networks rather than simple coupling.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational framing [pre-2023]**
- Beaty et al. 2015 (Scientific Reports) reported that during an alternate uses (divergent thinking) task, default and executive network regions coupled more strongly later in the task, interpreted as cooperation between control-related and spontaneous-thought-related networks, with focused internal attention and top-down control of spontaneous cognition — [Beaty et al. 2015, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep10964)
- The 2015 study described the posterior cingulate cortex as more strongly connected to salience regions (bilateral insula) early in the task, followed by stronger connections with executive regions (right DLPFC), the origin of the "salience network as switch" idea in creativity — [Beaty et al. 2015, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep10964)
- Beaty et al. 2018 (PNAS) built a connectome-based predictive model from 163 people doing an alternate-uses task in the scanner; key predictive regions were cortical hubs within default, salience and executive systems, and the model generalized to two independent task-fMRI samples and a large resting-state sample — [Beaty et al. 2018, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1713532115)
- Beaty and Kenett's 2023 Trends in Cognitive Sciences review ("Associative thinking at the core of creativity", vol. 27, pp. 671-683) argues idea generation depends heavily on associative thinking (linking concepts from memory), quantifiable as movement through a semantic space, drawing on both semantic and episodic memory — [Beaty & Kenett 2023, TiCS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364661323000943)

**First causal evidence for DMN-ECN coupling (Beaty lab, 2025)**
- Luchini, Zhang, White, Lührs, Ramot & Beaty (2025), Cerebral Cortex 35(4), bhaf065, published 8 April 2025: 30 right-handed university students (mean age 18.8; 20 F / 10 M), randomized 15 per condition; covert fNIRS connectivity neurofeedback (participants were told only to "reveal" a hidden picture), five 9-min blocks (45 min), two sessions 24 h apart — [Luchini et al. 2025, Cereb Cortex](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)
- Conditions: "default-executive" (train mPFC-DLPFC coupling, i.e., DMN hub + ECN hub, with SMA anticorrelated) vs "default-motor" control (train SMA-mPFC coupling, DLPFC anticorrelated) — [Luchini et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)
- Neurofeedback learning: default-executive group revealed more squares (M = 90.2 vs 66.7), t = 2.34, P = 0.026, d = 0.86 — [Luchini et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)
- Behavioral double dissociation: AUT originality showed a session x condition interaction, F = 4.15, P = 0.025; only the default-executive group improved (2.307 to 2.455, P = 0.011). Only the default-motor group improved go/no-go RT (0.372 to 0.356 s, P = 0.027, d = 0.44) — [Luchini et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)
- Neural: mPFC-DLPFC coupling during the AUT rose in the default-executive group (0.569 to 0.665, P = 0.008, |d| = 1.03); MTG-DLPFC rose (0.452 to 0.510, P = 0.039, interaction only marginal P = 0.057); mPFC-MTG higher post-training vs motor group (P = 0.013). Effects persisted ~24 h but did not transfer to resting-state connectivity — [Luchini et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)
- Authors' stated framing: before this work, "all evidence so far has been correlational"; they describe this as the first evidence that DMN-ECN coupling causally enhances creative performance — [PubMed 40197641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40197641/)
- Author-stated limitations: n = 15/group with several marginal effects; between-subjects; single neurofeedback session; fNIRS coverage limited to DLPFC for ECN, no posterior DMN, subcortical or salience regions; resting DMN-ECN coupling was already positive at baseline; only the AUT (12-s window) tested; young student sample. The fetched page also showed some inconsistently reported statistics (sign of d, df notation) — [Luchini et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)

**Largest multi-sample test: DMN-ECN switching, not static coupling (2025)**
- Chen, Kenett, Cui, Takeuchi, Fink, Benedek, Zeitlen, Zhuang, Lloyd-Cox, Kawashima, Qiu & Beaty (2025), "Dynamic switching between brain networks predicts creative ability", Communications Biology 8:54, published 15 Jan 2025 (PMID 39809882): 10 independent resting-state fMRI + divergent-thinking samples, N = 2,433, from Austria, Canada, China, Japan and the United States; the authors describe it as the largest and most ethnically diverse creativity neuroscience study to date — [Chen et al. 2025, Commun Biol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07470-9)
- Using time-resolved network analysis, creativity (but not general intelligence) was predicted by the number of DMN-ECN switches, with an inverted-U relationship between creativity and the balance of DMN-ECN switching; a separate task-fMRI validation (N = 31) showed more DMN-ECN switching during creative idea generation than a control condition — [Chen et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07470-9)
- Interpretation in the paper: high creative ability is associated with a balance between segregated and integrated network states, maintained by frequent transitions between them — [Chen et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07470-9)
- The abstract reports no effect sizes — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Dynamic%20switching%20between%20brain%20networks%20predicts%20creative%20ability%22&format=json&resultType=core)

**Generation vs evaluation: DMN + salience reconfiguration during generation (Kenett, 2025)**
- Kenett, Chrysikou, Bassett & Thompson-Schill (2025), Communications Biology 8:1726: 42 analyzed participants (of 50), two fMRI sessions one week apart; session 1 generated alternative uses (creative) or common characteristics (non-creative) for 64 objects; session 2 evaluated their own responses. Wavelet-coherence connectivity on 234 regions with multilayer dynamic community detection (reconfiguration/flexibility, integration, recruitment), Yeo 17-network partition; originality scored with the OCSAI language model — [Kenett et al. 2025, Commun Biol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09018-3)
- Reconfiguration was higher for alternate uses than common characteristics during generation (p = 0.047) but not evaluation; the effect localized to DefA, DefC and SalA subsystems. The paper states that reconfiguration within default mode and salience network regions characterizes idea generation but not evaluation; the executive network did not show this — [Kenett et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09018-3)
- Whole-brain integration was higher during evaluation than generation (p < 0.001) for both creative and non-creative responses, across eight subsystems spanning executive, default and salience networks; interpreted as top-down evaluation of novelty/appropriateness. Whole-brain reconfiguration interaction was marginal (p = 0.051); authors did not correct for multiple comparisons and call for replication — [Kenett et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09018-3)

**Rex Jung's most recent work: network differentiation (2025)**
- Sassenberg, Jung & DeYoung (2025), Cerebral Cortex 35(3), bhaf046 (preprint PsyArXiv 2024): two community resting-state fMRI samples, N = 236 and N = 234, analyzed with macroscale cortical gradients; higher creative *achievement* was associated with greater functional dissimilarity between core regions of the default and frontoparietal control networks — interpreted as the two networks carrying out more distinct cognitive roles — [Sassenberg, Jung & DeYoung 2025, Cereb Cortex (DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhaf046)](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhaf046); [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Functional%20differentiation%20of%20the%20default%20and%20frontoparietal%20control%20networks%20predicts%20individual%20differences%20in%20creative%20achievement%22&format=json&resultType=core)
- Jung's September 2025 CV lists this as his 2025 creativity paper; Jung co-edits the Cambridge Handbook of the Neuroscience of Creativity (with Vartanian; 30 essays, 45 experts) — [Jung CV 2025](https://brainandbehavioral.squarespace.com/s/jung-cv-2025.pdf); [Cambridge Handbook](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-the-neuroscience-of-creativity/CE1FF0E1875FDB0C95F26DADA507958E)

**Salience network specifically**
- A 2025 Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience review proposes the salience network acts as a mediator or switch between exploratory (DMN-driven) and focused (ECN-driven) modes; this is a theoretical proposal, and the same review notes only two studies (Beaty 2016, 2018) explicitly linked connectivity to creativity metrics in the flow literature — [Frontiers Behav Neurosci 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2025.1690499/full)
- A 2025 study of cross-domain analogical reasoning found that the functional connectome's association with creativity was mediated by analogical reasoning ability, with DMN and some salience/control-network connections preferentially involved — [Thinking Skills & Creativity 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1871187125000574)

**Review pointer**
- Luchini, Volle & Beaty (2025) published a review, "The role of the default mode network in creativity", in Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences (listed on the Beaty lab site; full text not fetched) — [Beaty Lab](https://beatylab.la.psu.edu)

### Inferences
- The 2023-2025 literature has shifted the explanatory variable from "DMN-ECN coupling strength" toward *dynamics*: switching rate (Chen 2025), transient reconfiguration during generation vs integration during evaluation (Kenett 2025), and gradient-level differentiation (Sassenberg/Jung 2025). These are compatible: well-separated networks can switch more cleanly, and an inverted-U implies both too little and too much switching hurts.
- Causal support for the coupling model is now real but thin: one n = 30 neurofeedback study (Luchini 2025) and one 9-patient stimulation series (Bartoli 2024, see Q3). The salience network remains the least directly tested component; evidence for its "switch" role is still mainly the 2015 PCC-insula timing result and the 2025 Kenett reconfiguration finding.
- The Jung result is about lifetime creative achievement, whereas most Beaty-lab results are about laboratory divergent thinking; the two are known to correlate only weakly (see Q6), so these findings may describe different phenomena.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 study directly manipulates salience-network nodes (e.g., anterior insula) during idea generation to test the switching hypothesis.
- The Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences 2025 DMN review (Luchini, Volle, Beaty) and Luchini et al. 2026 Neuropsychologia paper on IFG and category switching were identified only from listings; full text was not retrieved.
- I found no 2024-2026 Trends in Cognitive Sciences review on creativity networks; the most recent TiCS piece is Beaty & Kenett 2023.

## Key Question 2: What have large-scale or multi-site fMRI studies (connectome-based predictive modeling, "creative connectome") found since 2023?

### Takeaway
Since 2023, prediction studies have scaled up (10 samples / N = 2,433 for switching; ~17,000 participants in a 2025 ALE meta-analysis; 857 + 691 participants in a 2025 coordinate-network study), but predictive accuracy for individual creativity remains modest (typically r ≈ 0.3-0.4, R² ≈ 0.12), and the dominant conclusion is that creativity recruits the same default, control and salience hubs used by semantic cognition, episodic retrieval and executive function rather than any creativity-specific region.

### Cited Findings
- Chen et al. 2025 (Communications Biology): 10 samples, N = 2,433, five countries; number of DMN-ECN switches predicted divergent thinking but not general intelligence; inverted-U relationship; task-fMRI validation N = 31 (details in Q1) — [Chen et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07470-9)
- Liu, Zhuang, Zeitlen, Chen, Wang, Feng, Beaty & Qiu (2024), "Neural, genetic, and cognitive signatures of creativity", Communications Biology 7:1324 (15 Oct 2024; PMC11473644): multivariate pattern analysis of task fMRI from two independent samples yielded a divergent-thinking pattern with positive weights in default mode and frontoparietal control networks and negative weights in the visual network; the pattern predicted DT in separate datasets and generalized from task activity to resting-state connectivity — [Liu et al. 2024, Commun Biol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07007-6)
- The same DT pattern correlated strongly with the principal gradient of functional connectivity (sensory-to-abstract axis), and was positively correlated with dopamine-related neurotransmitter maps and with genes influencing neurotransmitter release; the fetched abstract did not state sample sizes — [Liu et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07007-6)
- Chan, Lambon Ralph & Robinson (2025, bioRxiv preprint, not peer reviewed), "The neural basis of creative thought: an ALE meta-analysis involving over 17,000 participants": 787 experiments, 10,357 foci, 17,228 healthy adults; creativity tasks activated bilateral frontal and left parieto-temporal regions (largest cluster left-lateralized: superior/middle/inferior frontal, anterior insula, anterior MTG, fusiform, inferior parietal); these overlapped heavily with "pre-existing knowledge" (semantic cognition + controlled episodic retrieval) and "executive mechanism" tasks — [Chan et al. 2025, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.19.639155v1.full)
- Only small left-lateralized clusters (inferior parietal, amygdala) were more active for creativity than for the cornerstone tasks; the authors conclude creativity is not a distinct cognitive function but arises from general-purpose knowledge and executive mechanisms interacting ("Cognitive Cornerstones Hypothesis"). Limitation: ~76% of creativity tasks were verbal — [Chan et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.19.639155v1.full)
- Kutsche et al. 2025 (JAMA Network Open): 36 fMRI studies / 415 coordinates / 857 healthy participants (discovery) and 30 studies / 383 coordinates / 691 participants (replication) mapped onto a 1,000-participant normative connectome; 86% of studies had coordinates falling in a circuit defined by negative connectivity to the right frontal pole (MNI 20, 68, -6); replication overlap stronger than chance (t = -2.75, P = 0.007) — [Kutsche et al. 2025, JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830230)
- EEG-based CPM: Chhade, Tabbal, Paban, Auffret, Hassan & Vérin (2024), Communications Biology 7:790: 90 analyzed participants (256-channel resting EEG, source-space phase-locking connectivity, SVR with leave-one-out CV) predicted Inventory of Creative Activities and Achievements total score at r = 0.36, p = 0.00045, R² = 0.12 (gamma band 30-45 Hz only); external validation in 41 independent participants r = 0.35, p = 0.02 — [Chhade et al. 2024, Commun Biol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06461-6)
- The high-creativity EEG network (8 edges) included right MTG, lingual gyrus, rostral ACC and isthmus cingulate (DMN), lateral occipital and left paracentral; the low-creativity network (26 edges) spanned DMN, somatomotor, frontoparietal, ventral attention, visual and temporal regions. Caveat: 5- and 10-fold CV did not reproduce the LOOCV significance — [Chhade et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06461-6)
- Semantic-memory mediation [foundational, 2022]: Ovando-Tellez, Volle et al. (Science Advances) found that whole-brain predictive models built on task-based functional connectivity predicted real-life creative activities and achievements, were generalizable to resting-state connectivity, and that the prediction was mediated by individual semantic memory network structure — [Ovando-Tellez et al. 2022, Sci Adv](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abl4294); [Paris Brain Institute summary](https://parisbraininstitute.org/news/how-our-knowledge-world-embedded-brain-connectivity-shapes-our-creativity)
- Weak-effect caution [foundational, 2019]: a review of large-sample neuroimaging studies of divergent thinking concluded that effect sizes of all observed correlations were weak, with globally spread weak effects in volumetry — [Takeuchi & Kawashima 2019, Curr Opin Behav Sci](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352154618301177)

### Inferences
- Across modalities (fMRI CPM 2018, EEG CPM 2024, multi-sample switching 2025) individual-level predictive accuracy for creativity has plateaued around r ≈ 0.3-0.4, similar to other individual-differences neuroimaging; the increase in N has mainly improved robustness, not effect size.
- The 2025 ALE meta-analysis and the 2025 JAMA network-mapping study converge on a distributed, left-lateralized fronto-temporo-parietal set (IFG, anterior MTG/temporal pole, IPL, insula) that is shared with semantic control; the "creative connectome" is increasingly described as a *configuration* of general-purpose networks rather than a dedicated circuit.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm any 2024-2026 creativity-network paper in Nature Human Behaviour, Nature Neuroscience, Neuron, Science, Nature or PNAS proper; the large-scale 2024-2025 work appeared in Communications Biology, Cerebral Cortex, Brain, Nature Communications and JAMA Network Open (see Q5).
- Sample sizes for the two samples in Liu et al. 2024 were not reported in the fetched abstract.
- No published pre-registered replication of the Beaty 2018 CPM in a new site was found in these searches.

## Key Question 3: What have intracranial recordings, TMS, tDCS or lesion studies shown about causal roles of specific regions in idea generation?

### Takeaway
Intracranial stimulation of DMN nodes (ACC, dlPFC/SFG, MTG) in epilepsy patients selectively lowers the originality of alternate uses without affecting fluency or mind-wandering (Brain, 2024), lesion/atrophy network mapping shows that damage to regions negatively coupled to the right frontal pole impairs creativity whereas frontopolar damage spares or increases it (JAMA Network Open, 2025), and tDCS studies (2024-2026, N = 110-114) show modest gains (d ≈ 0.35-0.6) from down-regulating left IFG and up-regulating right TPJ or from left DLPFC/right IFG montages; no 2024-2026 randomized rTMS trial on divergent thinking was found.

### Cited Findings

**Intracranial recording + direct cortical stimulation**
- Bartoli, Devara, Dang, Rabinovich, Mathura, Anand, Pascuzzi, Adkinson, Kenett, Bijanki, Sheth & Shofty (2024), "Default mode network electrophysiological dynamics and causal role in creative thinking", Brain 147(10): 3409-3425, online 18 June 2024: 13 stereo-EEG epilepsy patients, 333 DMN electrodes (148 dorsomedial, 185 lateral temporal) and 107 frontoparietal (FPN) electrodes; tasks were mind wandering (MW), alternate uses (AUT) and a sustained-attention control — [Bartoli et al. 2024, Brain](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/10/3409/7695856)
- Electrophysiology: DMN showed increased gamma (30-70 Hz) with decreased theta (4-8 Hz) relative to FPN; during AUT, DMN and FPN were similar during stimulus processing and diverged in the response stage (lower DMN theta, higher DMN gamma); the AUT showed an earlier gamma rise than MW; lateral temporal sites drove most gamma changes — [Bartoli et al. 2024](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/10/3409/7695856)
- Stimulation (9 of 13 patients; bipolar, 130 Hz, 4 mA, 3 s on/3 s off) at right ACC (3 patients), left ACC (3), right SFG/dlPFC (2), right MTG (1): AUT originality (semantic distance) fell during stimulation (median 0.97 off vs 0.94 on; W = 75, P < 0.001; before-vs-during P = 0.01, during-vs-after P = 0.02, before-vs-after P = 1); MW originality unchanged (P = 0.3); the variability proxy for fluency (5.04 vs 4.59, P = 0.65) and flexibility (P = 0.91) unchanged; no effect sizes reported — [Bartoli et al. 2024](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/10/3409/7695856)
- Author interpretation: DMN causally supports generation of original associations in divergent thinking; limitations include small heterogeneous medicated sample, uneven coverage, varying stimulation sites, and fluency measured only by a variability proxy — [Bartoli et al. 2024](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/10/3409/7695856); press summary — [Univ. of Utah Neurosurgery, Jan 2025](https://medicine.utah.edu/neurosurgery/news/2025/01/mapping-creativity-role-of-default-mode-network)
- Rabinovich, Cowan, Libowitz, Davis, Rahimpour, Smith, Beaty, Bartoli, Sheth & Shofty (2025, bioRxiv preprint, June 2025, not peer reviewed), "Human brain network control of creativity": 15 epilepsy patients with Behnke-Fried depth electrodes (single units from 10 patients, 127 cells); AUT vs basic arithmetic control; DMN high-gamma rose and theta fell during AUT but not arithmetic; a linear SVM decoded AUT vs arithmetic from DMN best (not significantly better than FPN); nonlinear CEBRA embeddings decoded originality level, with dorsal attention network best (p = 0.02 vs DMN) though DMN had the more linear creativity axis (R² ≈ 0.86 vs 0.73); ~41% of single units were trial-type selective; creativity level decodable from spiking (p = 0.007); LFP and spiking creativity embeddings shared a dominant dimension (CCA r = 0.73) — [Rabinovich et al. 2025, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.03.657648v1.full)
- The 2025 preprint cites the group's earlier stimulation finding that lateral DMN stimulation reduced idea count without changing originality, whereas medial DMN stimulation affected originality without changing fluency; no new stimulation was performed — [Rabinovich et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.03.657648v1.full)
- He, Zhang, Xiao, Hu, Wang & Liu (2025, bioRxiv preprint, July 2025, Beijing Normal University / Sanbo Brain Hospital): 40 refractory-epilepsy patients (247 hippocampal, 815 DMN, 2,612 cortical contacts); hippocampal ripple rate higher during AUT than an object-characteristics control (beta = 0.053, P < 1e-41); within AUT, ripple rate predicted semantic distance (P < 1e-6) and originality (beta = 0.162, P = 0.009), and correlated with everyday creativity (r = 0.419, P = 0.026); lower ripple-aligned mPFC high-frequency activity predicted higher originality (beta = -0.006, P_FWE = 0.001); ventral mPFC suppression partly mediated the link between category switching and originality (z = 2.08, P = 0.037) — [He et al. 2025, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.22.666046v1)

**Lesion and neurodegeneration network mapping**
- Kutsche, Taylor, Erkkinen, Akkad, Khosravani, Drew, Abraham, Ott, Wall, Cohen, Horn, Neumann, Kletenik & Fox (2025), JAMA Network Open 8(2): e2459297, 13 Feb 2025: in 56 lesion patients, lesion groups differed in circuit intersection (F = 10.25, P < .001); lateral frontal lesions (linked to impaired creativity) fell in regions negatively connected to the right frontal pole, whereas frontopolar lesions (linked to preserved or increased creativity) fell in positively connected regions — [Kutsche et al. 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830230)
- Neurodegeneration (2,262 atrophy coordinates, 4,804 patients, 7 disorders): alignment differed across disorders (F = 6.73, P < .001); semantic-variant PPA aligned most strongly (75% of atrophy coordinates in regions positively connected to the right frontal pole); FTD patients with new-onset creativity showed atrophy negatively connected to creativity foci (t = -2.14, d = -0.36, P = .04) while non-creative FTD did not (P = .86) — [Kutsche et al. 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830230)
- Interpretation: the right frontal pole may actively suppress creativity (self-monitoring / cognitive control / constraining novelty seeking), so its damage or deactivation may release creative output ("paradoxical functional facilitation"); the authors cite TMS inhibition of the right frontal pole increasing novelty seeking. Authors note the analysis is retrospective and needs prospective lesion/stimulation validation; some post hoc statistics on the page appeared internally inconsistent — [Kutsche et al. 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830230); press — [Medical Xpress, Feb 2025](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-02-mri-brain-circuit-creativity.html)
- Methodological caveat for all lesion-network-mapping results: a 2025 Nature Neuroscience paper argues that LNM "involves a repetitive sampling of one and the same FC matrix"; its December 2025 literature search identified 201 LNM studies — [Nature Neuroscience 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-02196-7)
- Hippocampal lesions [foundational, pre-2023]: patients with bilateral hippocampal damage performed significantly worse than comparison participants on verbal and figural Torrance tests, reviewed in "Are the hippocampus and its network necessary for creativity?" (2020) — [ResearchGate review 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342042935_Are_the_hippocampus_and_its_network_necessary_for_creativity)

**tDCS / tES**
- Huang, Fu, Song, Ren, Li & Zhao (2024), Cerebral Cortex 34(2), bhad531, 9 Jan 2024: three within-subject sham-controlled experiments, 38 participants each (N = 114), 1 mA; Exp 3 (cathodal left IFG + anodal right TPJ) improved AUT fluency (d = 0.42, P = 0.013), flexibility (d = 0.59, P = 0.001) and originality (d = 0.39, P = 0.015) without reducing rationality (P = 0.186); Exp 1 (cathodal left IFG + anodal right IFG) improved fluency/flexibility but not originality and lowered rationality; Exp 2 (anodal right TPJ) improved all three but lowered rationality (d = 0.45) — [Huang et al. 2024, Cereb Cortex](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/34/2/bhad531/7513583)
- Interpretation: left IFG as a top-down control/inhibitory filter (frontoparietal control network) and right TPJ as a bottom-up associative node (DMN); limitations include no neural data, the Exp-3 superiority claim rests on comparing p-values rather than a formal interaction test, a 10- vs 20-min stimulation-duration inconsistency in the Methods, and an implausible power-analysis assumption — [Huang et al. 2024](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/34/2/bhad531/7513583)
- Zhu, Bai, Xu, Xu & Nan (2026), Biological Psychology vol. 208, online 13 May 2026 (DOI 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2026.109288): randomized single-blind, 110 participants in four groups (tDCS, tRNS, tDCS+tRNS, sham), 22 min online stimulation over left DLPFC and right IFG; all active groups improved convergent thinking; tDCS increased verbal DT originality, tRNS increased figural DT fluency, and the combination did not improve DT — [Zhu et al. 2026, PubMed 42134764](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42134764/)
- A 2024 npj Science of Learning study tested tDCS over bilateral IFG on cognitive processes during creative ideation (results not extracted here) — [npj Sci Learn 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-024-00285-z)
- Li & Beaty (Creativity Research Journal 35(2), 2023, online 2022) reported that left-anodal/right-cathodal DLPFC tDCS accelerated the emergence of original ideas over the time course of divergent thinking — [Li & Beaty 2023, CRJ](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400419.2022.2068297)
- Earlier mixed results [foundational]: a 2022 double-blind study (N = 66) comparing tDCS, tRNS and sham over left DLPFC found one DT subtest significant and the other not — [Frontiers Hum Neurosci 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2022.997445/full)

### Inferences
- The causal picture now converges on a "release from control" theme from two independent methods: right frontal pole / lateral PFC lesion-network mapping (Kutsche 2025), cathodal left IFG tDCS (Huang 2024) and hippocampal-ripple-linked suppression of vmPFC schema activity (He 2025 preprint) all associate reduced prefrontal constraint with more original output, while DMN stimulation (Bartoli 2024) shows the DMN is needed to produce original associations in the first place.
- Stimulation effect sizes (d ≈ 0.35-0.6, single-session, AUT only) are small relative to the strong causal language used in some papers; blinding and sham validity were often not assessed.

### Gaps
- No randomized rTMS or theta-burst trial on divergent thinking from 2024-2026 surfaced in two searches; TMS evidence in this window appears absent or unindexed.
- No 2023-2026 study directly stimulated or lesion-mapped the temporal pole or anterior cingulate in isolation for idea generation; ACC appears only as a stimulation site pooled with other DMN nodes in Bartoli 2024.
- The He et al. 2025 and Rabinovich et al. 2025 intracranial findings are preprints; no peer-reviewed versions were found.

## Key Question 4: What role do the hippocampus and memory systems (constructive episodic simulation, semantic memory structure) play in generating novel ideas?

### Takeaway
The 2023 MemiC framework (Benedek, Beaty, Schacter, Kenett) positions goal-directed memory search and construction, not spontaneous emergence, as the engine of ideation; 2024-2026 evidence adds that hippocampal ripples and low-dimensional hippocampal representations coupled to prefrontal cortex support ideation (iEEG 2025 preprint; Nature Communications 2025), that flexible semantic memory structure predicts creativity and lowers semantic-control demands (N = 93 fMRI, 2024), and that semantic retrieval explains more variance in divergent thinking (36%) than episodic retrieval does (2026 behavioral preprint).

### Cited Findings
- Benedek, Beaty, Schacter & Kenett (2023), "The role of memory in creative ideation", Nature Reviews Psychology 2: 246-257 (Perspective): proposes the MemiC framework with four stages (memory search, candidate idea construction, novelty evaluation, effectiveness evaluation); argues creative ideas typically result from goal-directed memory processes rather than emerging spontaneously, draw on controlled retrieval, involve semantic and episodic memory, and build on memory-construction processes — [Benedek et al. 2023, Nat Rev Psychol](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-memory-in-creative-ideation/)
- Beaty, Kenett, Hass & Schacter (2023, Thinking & Reasoning 29(2): 305-339), "Semantic memory and creativity: the costs and benefits of semantic memory structure in generating original ideas" — [Beaty et al. 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10128864/)
- Herault, Ovando-Tellez, Lebuda, Kenett, Beranger, Benedek & Volle (2024), "Creative connections: the neural correlates of semantic relatedness are associated with creativity", Communications Biology 7:810, 4 July 2024: N = 93 fMRI; regions whose activity increased with judged relatedness overlapped default, control, salience, semantic-control and multiple-demand networks; default and semantic-control networks were more engaged for remote associations; more creative participants rated remote concepts as more related, showed a more flexible semantic memory organization, and showed *lower* activity in semantic-control regions when judging remote concepts (interpreted as neural efficiency); semantic network structure related to intrinsic functional connectivity — [Herault et al. 2024, Commun Biol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06493-y)
- Zhang, Li, Ding, Wu, Xie, Wu, Qin & Luo (2025), "Hippocampal-prefrontal orchestration supports higher-order learning for creative ideation", Nature Communications 17:464, online 11 Dec 2025 (PMC12800267): learning from creative exemplars improved subsequent creation; exemplar-learning trials that predicted higher subsequent creation showed reduced hippocampal representational dimensionality and increased hippocampal-ventrolateral PFC connectivity, independent of exemplar memory, for both textual and pictorial materials; vlPFC decoding of high-creation trials depended on low-dimensional hippocampal representations; the authors call this a mechanism "distinct from memory acquisition". Sample size not in the fetched abstract — [Zhang et al. 2025, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67157-x)
- He et al. 2025 (bioRxiv preprint; 40 iEEG patients): hippocampal ripple rate was higher during alternate uses than during feature listing (P < 1e-41) and predicted originality (beta = 0.162, P = 0.009); ripples were accompanied by reduced mPFC high-frequency activity and divergence of mPFC patterns from the task's schema-like pattern (P = 0.027, 7 of 8 objects); authors propose ripples "loosen" medial prefrontal schema constraints to allow access to unconventional ideas; originality inter-rater ICC = 0.68 — [He et al. 2025, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.22.666046v1)
- Thakral, Madore, Gomez & Devitt (2026, bioRxiv preprint, Feb 2026; behavioral, N = 45 undergraduates): semantic memory internal details alone explained 36% of divergent-thinking variance (beta = 0.51, p < .01) while episodic memory was not a significant predictor (beta = 0.13, BF10 = 0.31); episodic-simulation details predicted DT (24.1% of variance) but became non-significant once semantic details were added; authors endorse a "semantic scaffold" hypothesis in which DT relies mainly on semantic retrieval while future simulation draws on both — [Thakral et al. 2026, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.08.704672v1.full)
- Childhood structural evidence (NeuroImage, March 2024): divergent thinking in children was positively related to the volume of the hippocampal head and tail (not body) and to hippocampal functional connectivity — [NeuroImage 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811924000818)
- Computational context: Spens & Burgess (2024, Nature Human Behaviour) model hippocampal replay training generative (variational-autoencoder) models that reconstruct experiences from latent variables, consistent with memories being (re)constructed on shared substrates with imagination; the link to novel ideas is a hypothesis of the framework, not a tested result — [Spens & Burgess 2024, Nat Hum Behav](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v8y2024i3d10.1038_s41562-023-01799-z.html)
- Chan et al. 2025 ALE (preprint, 17,228 participants) found creativity activation overlapped with "pre-existing knowledge" tasks (semantic cognition and controlled episodic retrieval) in left middle/inferior frontal, left superior/medial frontal and left lateral temporal (BA37) clusters — [Chan et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.19.639155v1.full)

### Inferences
- Two memory-system accounts coexist and are being pulled apart: the constructive-episodic-simulation account (hippocampus recombines episodic details) and the semantic-scaffold account (flexible semantic network structure plus controlled retrieval). The 2026 Thakral preprint and the 2024 Herault fMRI study favor semantic structure as the stronger predictor of laboratory divergent thinking, while the 2025 iEEG and Nature Communications studies show hippocampal involvement that is not about episodic detail per se but about abstraction (low dimensionality) and transient release of prefrontal schema constraints.
- This suggests the hippocampus's contribution to ideation may be best described as generative recombination/abstraction over semantic-episodic content gated by hippocampal-prefrontal dynamics, rather than literal episodic recall.

### Gaps
- Sample size for Zhang et al. 2025 (Nature Communications) was not in the retrieved abstract.
- No 2023-2026 lesion study of hippocampal amnesic patients on divergent thinking was found; the lesion evidence remains pre-2020.
- Thakral et al. 2026 is behavioral only and a preprint; no fMRI test of the semantic-scaffold vs episodic-simulation accounts in the same participants was found for 2024-2026.

## Key Question 6: What are the open debates and criticisms (reproducibility, task validity of divergent thinking tests, etc.)?

### Takeaway
Three live critiques dominate: (1) divergent-thinking tasks (especially shortened in-scanner AUT versions) have weak links to real-world creative achievement and may not measure a single construct; (2) effect sizes in large-sample creativity neuroimaging are uniformly small and some results depend on the validation scheme; and (3) theorists (Dietrich 2024; Chan et al. 2025) argue there is no creativity "faculty" to localize because creativity tasks recruit the same semantic, episodic and executive networks as non-creative cognition, so network-coupling accounts may be describing general cognition.

### Cited Findings

**Task validity**
- Dietrich (2024, Frontiers in Psychology 15, 30 April 2024): argues researchers commit a "creativity faculty fallacy" by searching for a dedicated neural substrate; divergent-thinking tasks such as the AUT lack ecological validity, especially in shortened neuroimaging versions, and the creative-vs-noncreative contrast design "controls away" the very processes that constitute creativity; he also notes inconsistent ALE meta-analytic results and proposes decomposing creativity into deliberate, spontaneous and flow modes built from standard cognitive units — [Dietrich 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1373299/full)
- Historical psychometric critique: Simonton (2003) argued no creativity measure has passed the hurdles required of ability tests, with scores correlating too highly with general intelligence, weakly with each other, and weakly with overt creative behavior; Hocevar found DT tests predicted creativity no better than intelligence measures (94 students); other work found DT tests did not predict creative performance in any of three domains — [Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity and the Arts review](https://www.ovid.com/journals/paca/pdf/10.1037/1931-3896.2.2.68~assessing-creativity-with-divergent-thinking-tasks-exploring)
- The effect size of the relationship between DT performance and real-life creative achievement "tends to be weak in general", and four common DT tasks "do not measure identical constructs" — [PMC8920217, 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8920217/)
- A 2024 Creativity Research Journal article reconsiders the divergent/convergent thinking dichotomy itself — [Creativity Research Journal 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2024.2419751)
- Luchini et al. 2025 acknowledge the AUT's 12-second thinking window "may have favored evaluative over generative processes" — [Luchini et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)
- Jung's 2025 study measures creative *achievement* rather than DT, and Chhade et al. 2024 note that their EEG model was driven by creative achievements rather than activities, with self-report creativity "subject to bias" — [Sassenberg et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhaf046); [Chhade et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06461-6)

**Effect sizes and reproducibility**
- Takeuchi & Kawashima's review of large-sample DT neuroimaging found "the effect sizes of all observed correlations ... were weak", with globally spread weak volumetric effects — [Takeuchi & Kawashima 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352154618301177)
- Chhade et al. 2024: 5-fold and 10-fold cross-validation "did not reproduce the LOOCV significance", so the EEG-CPM result depends on validation scheme; R² = 0.12; external sample only 41 — [Chhade et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06461-6)
- Kenett et al. 2025: whole-brain reconfiguration effects were "weak and marginal" (p = 0.051), no correction for multiple comparisons, "replication is needed" — [Kenett et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09018-3)
- Kutsche et al. 2025 note that binarized creative vs non-creative labels oversimplify creativity, only in-scanner tasks were included, and the analysis is retrospective; the fetched page also showed internally inconsistent post hoc statistics (e.g., t = -0.32 reported with P < .001) — [Kutsche et al. 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830230)
- The lesion-network-mapping method underlying Kutsche 2025 was itself challenged in Nature Neuroscience (2025) as repeated sampling of a single normative connectivity matrix — [Nature Neuroscience 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-02196-7)
- Intracranial studies rely on 9-40 medicated epilepsy patients with clinically determined electrode placement; Bartoli 2024 used a variability proxy for fluency, and Rabinovich 2025 reports an unresolved conflict between DMN being best for decoding task type and DAN best for decoding originality — [Bartoli et al. 2024](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/10/3409/7695856); [Rabinovich et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.03.657648v1.full)
- A 2025 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience methods paper by DeYoung and colleagues (including Sassenberg), "Beyond Increasing Sample Sizes: Optimizing Effect Sizes in Neuroimaging Research on Individual Differences", addresses the small-effect problem in individual-differences neuroimaging (listing only; not retrieved) — [dblp listing](https://dblp.org/pid/89/7492)

**Is there anything creativity-specific?**
- Chan, Lambon Ralph & Robinson (2025 preprint, 17,228 participants): creativity tasks "do not uniquely engage additional brain regions but instead recruit the very same networks" as semantic cognition, episodic retrieval and executive function; only small left inferior parietal and amygdala clusters were creativity-preferential — [Chan et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.19.639155v1.full)
- Chen et al. 2025 counter that DMN-ECN switching predicted creativity but *not* general intelligence, a discriminant-validity argument for a creativity-related network property — [Chen et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07470-9)
- Liu et al. 2024 argue the DT pattern's alignment with the principal connectivity gradient "distinguishes DT from other advanced cognitive functions" — [Liu et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07007-6)

### Inferences
- The strongest current rebuttal to the "no creativity faculty" critique is not localization but dynamics and discriminant validity: creativity-specific prediction from switching that does not predict intelligence (Chen 2025), and double dissociations in neurofeedback (Luchini 2025). Both still rest on the AUT, so the task-validity critique carries over.
- The field's causal turn (iEEG, lesion mapping, neurofeedback, tDCS) has produced consistent qualitative conclusions (DMN needed for originality; prefrontal control constrains it) but every causal study is small, single-session, AUT-based, and some report statistics that do not fully cohere, so replication with pre-registration and real-world creativity outcomes remains the central open need.

### Gaps
- No pre-registered direct replication of a creativity-network fMRI finding (e.g., Beaty 2018 CPM or Beaty 2015 coupling) from 2023-2026 was found.
- No study was found that tests whether DMN-ECN coupling/switching predicts real-world creative output measured outside the lab (e.g., expert-rated products) rather than DT scores or self-reported achievement inventories.
- Effect sizes for the N = 2,433 switching result were not reported in the abstract and the full text was not retrieved.
