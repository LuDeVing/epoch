# Thought Dynamics and Mind Wandering: How Thoughts Move, and How That Relates to Incubation and Insight (emphasis 2023–2026)

Scope note: These notes cover the measurable "stream of consciousness" as a trajectory, the mechanisms proposed to steer it, and links to incubation/insight. Items marked **[foundational, pre-2023]** are included only for context. Where a source could only be read at abstract/press-release level (paywall or CAPTCHA blocked the full text), that is stated explicitly.

## Key Question 1: The Dynamic Framework of Spontaneous Thought (Christoff et al. 2016) and how it has been tested or revised since 2023

### Takeaway
The Dynamic Framework of Thought (DFT) remains the dominant organizing scheme: thought "moves" to the degree it is free of deliberate (cognitive-control) and automatic (salience/habit) constraints. Since 2023 it has been (a) empirically tested with embedding-based "semantic incoherence" across waking and dreaming (small but significant effects in the predicted direction), (b) extended to compulsive fantasy/maladaptive daydreaming, (c) sharpened philosophically to separate daydreaming from mind-wandering, and (d) criticized for underweighting goal-structured dynamics; no wholesale revision by the original authors appeared in the retrieved sources.

### Cited Findings
- **[foundational, 2016]** The DFT (Christoff, Irving, Fox, Spreng, Andrews-Hanna, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*) defines spontaneous thought as thought that "moves fairly freely" because it is weakly constrained in content and in transitions between mental states; it distinguishes flexible *deliberate* constraints implemented by cognitive control from *automatic* constraints such as emotional/sensory salience and habit, and treats mind-wandering, creative thought and dreaming as a family of spontaneous-thought phenomena — [Christoff et al. 2016 PDF](https://scottbarrykaufman.com///wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Christoff-et-al.-2016.pdf)
- A later schematic (Neuropsychopharmacology review indexed in PMC, 2021) states that automatic constraints are "a family of mechanisms that operate outside of cognitive control, including sensory or affective salience," and that thought ceases to be spontaneous at the strongest automatic-constraint levels (rumination, obsessive thought) — [PMC8616944 Fig. 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8616944/figure/Fig3)
- **Direct empirical test (2025):** Mallett, Nahas, Christoff, Paller & Mills, "Cognitive control and semantic thought variability across sleep and wakefulness," *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 6 (DOI 10.33735/phimisci.2025.10307). They operationalized DFT's "freedom of movement" as *semantic incoherence* (mean cosine distance between consecutive phrase embeddings; 300-d spaCy en_core_web_lg). Waking data: experience-sampling reports from Mills et al. (2021), up to 6×/day for one week, N = 46 (44 for on/off-task). Dream data: 1,199 authors from a public dream journal tagged lucid vs non-lucid — [Mallett et al. 2025](https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/download/10307/11817?inline=1)
- Results of that test: semantic incoherence correlated with self-rated freedom of movement, r_rm(604) = 0.13, p = 0.002; off-task thoughts were more incoherent than on-task (W = 267, p = 0.007, CLES = 0.644; medians 0.730 vs 0.696); non-lucid dream reports were more incoherent than lucid ones (W = 335,118, p = 0.040, CLES = 0.510; medians 0.513 vs 0.510). The authors interpret this as cognitive control constraining thought variability across the sleep–wake cycle; the dream effect is tiny — [Mallett et al. 2025](https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/download/10307/11817?inline=1)
- **Extension (2025):** Burrell, Lawson & Christoff Hadjiilieva, "A Dynamic Approach to Compulsive Fantasy: Constraints and Creativity in 'Maladaptive Daydreaming'," *Behavioral Sciences* 15(10):1333 (28 Sept 2025). Places fantasy, daydreaming and compulsive fantasy on DFT continua; proposes compulsive fantasy arises under strong automatic constraints plus medium-to-high deliberate constraints; separates constraints governing episode *onset* from those governing *unfolding*; argues deliberate constraints are engaged intermittently during episodes, analogous to the generation–evaluation cycle in creative thought. Evidence base is 250 Reddit posts (Feb 2025), explicitly illustrative not representative; the paper does not directly measure constraint dynamics and recommends combining self-report with neuroimaging and phenomenological interviews — [PMC12562237](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237)
- **Conceptual refinement (2024):** Lawson & Thompson, "Daydreaming as spontaneous immersive imagination: A phenomenological analysis," *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 5(1):1–34. Argues existing research either conflates daydreaming with mind-wandering or opposes them; defines daydreams as spontaneous, immersive waking imagination with a minimal sense of presence in a world and a "rolling temporality," distinct from both mind-wandering and night dreaming — [Lawson & Thompson 2024](https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/view/9913)
- **Empirical counterpart (2024):** Shimoni & Axelrod, *Scientific Reports*: planning/worrying/ruminating during another task were perceived as "mind-wandering," whereas recalling the past and fantasizing with no concurrent task were perceived as "day-dreaming," so the terms are not fully equivalent — [PMC11109208](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11109208/)
- **Earlier refinement of DFT for creativity/psychedelics (Girn et al.; date pre-2023 per the retrieved page):** updates the framework by emphasizing dynamics within and between generation and evaluation stages and places the psychedelic state as high-potential for creative generation — [Girn et al. summary page](https://www.moreblossom.com/papers/updating-the-dynamic-framework-of-thought-creativity-and-psychedelics)
- **Critique (2026 conference abstract, ESPP):** argues the DFT leaves out "goal-structured dynamics" in which cognitive control actively shapes how the stream of thought unfolds, rather than merely constraining it — [ESPP 2026 abstract](https://espp2026.sites.uu.nl/wp-content/uploads/sites/1232/2026/06/ESPP2026_File_219.pdf)
- **Theoretical complement (2025):** Corbani, Mildner, Bassett & Tamir, "Tracking the Dynamics of the Stream of Thought Reveals Its Function," *Technology, Mind, and Behavior* 6(4), DOI 10.1037/tmb0000171 (23 Oct 2025; part of a special collection on involuntary thought and affect). Argues that the amount of constraint imposed by cognitive-control and salience networks determines the dynamics of the mind, and that think-aloud plus NLP makes those dynamics quantifiable (full text was 403-blocked; claims taken from abstract/snippets) — [TMB article](https://tmb.apaopen.org/pub/jvhum0u0/release/2); [Princeton record](https://psnlab.princeton.edu/document/956)
- **Mental-health framing (2023):** Kucyi and colleagues' review in *Nature Mental Health* ("Recent advances in the neuroscience of spontaneous and off-task thought") notes people spend 30–50% of waking life thinking about something other than the current task, names hippocampal sharp-wave ripples/replay and noradrenergic/serotonergic neuromodulation as emerging mechanisms, and argues distinct brain states give rise to adaptive vs maladaptive thought — [Arizona record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/recent-advances-in-the-neuroscience-of-spontaneous-and-off-task-t/)
- **Measurement landscape (systematic review, recent):** across 555 studies of mind-wandering subtypes, questionnaires and experience sampling were the most used methods, with high ecological validity but self-report bias — [PMC12373717](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12373717/)
- **[foundational, 2018]** Seli et al.'s "family-resemblances" model (TICS) categorizes mind wandering by intentionality, content and temporal orientation; it remains the main rival framing to DFT's dynamics-first definition — [PMC12373717 summary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12373717/)
- **Forthcoming:** an Oxford Handbook chapter by Andrews-Hanna, Irving, Fox, Spreng & Christoff argues definitions of mind-wandering should incorporate the processes by which thoughts unfold over time — [PhilArchive listing](https://philarchive.org/s/Christoff%20Kalina)

### Inferences
- The DFT has moved from a conceptual proposal to a framework with an operational proxy (embedding-based semantic incoherence) that behaves as predicted in both waking and sleep data, but effect sizes are small (r ≈ 0.13; CLES ≈ 0.51–0.64), implying self-reported "freedom of movement" and text-derived variability capture overlapping but far from identical constructs.
- Since 2023 the framework's growth has been at its edges (daydreaming, compulsive fantasy, sleep) rather than at its core; the principal open theoretical challenge is whether cognitive control merely *constrains* or also *structures* the trajectory (the ESPP critique).

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 paper by Christoff herself formally revising the DFT was found; a "Christoff (2024)" on automatic constraints is cited inside Burrell et al. 2025 but could not be located or verified.
- Full text of Corbani et al. 2025 (TMB) was inaccessible; its specific empirical content (if any) is unknown.

## Key Question 2: How thought trajectories are now measured (experience sampling, think-aloud + NLP/embeddings, free-association chains, apps, fMRI/EEG decoding), and what the Smallwood / Mills / Christoff / Andrews-Hanna / Tamir / Honey–Chen groups have found

### Takeaway
The field has converged on a pipeline: record the stream (think-aloud speech, typed reports, or free-association chains), auto-transcribe (e.g., Whisper), segment into thought units, embed with sentence transformers, and compute consecutive-thought semantic distance and topic-jump boundaries, then relate these to self-report dimensions and to fMRI/EEG/intracranial signals. Key 2024–2025 findings: thoughts are clustered (consecutive thoughts far more similar than distant ones); topic jumps are marked by posterior-medial default-network responses resembling external event boundaries; episodic detail peaks right after a jump; and default-network vs medial-temporal connectivity predicts how stable vs variable a person's trajectory is.

### Cited Findings
- **Largest think-aloud dataset (2024):** Mildner & Tamir, "Why do we think? The dynamics of spontaneous thought reveal its functions," *PNAS Nexus* 3(6):pgae230. 2-minute spoken or typed think-aloud; 3,359 responses from 1,679 participants (2020–2021), 2,901 responses/1,524 participants analyzed. Audio transcribed with Whisper; text split into thought units by independent clause; hierarchical clustering identified topic jumps. Average 10.82 thoughts and 5.6 topics per 2-min report (~3.04 thoughts per topic). Episodic detail was highest in the thought immediately after a topic jump and declined within a topic; COVID-related ("current concern") content peaked *before* jumps, which the authors interpret as salient anxiety-laden content acting as an attractor. Topic-jump reliability was fair–moderate (human Fleiss' κ = 0.41; automated 0.30); effect sizes "very small" — [PMC11210302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11210302)
- **Think-aloud inside the scanner (preprint 2024; published *Nature Communications* 2025):** Su, Li, Born, Honey, Chen & Lee, "Neural dynamics of spontaneous memory recall and future thinking in the continuous flow of thoughts," *Nat Commun* 16 (11 July 2025; DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-61807-w). Participants verbalized unconstrained thought for 10 min during fMRI. 126 recruited; 118 in behavioral analyses, 75 in fMRI (motion exclusions). 15 annotators labeled sentences as current-state, semantic-world, semantic-self, episodic, future, filler, other; consecutive same-category/same-topic sentences merged into thought units; units embedded with all-mpnet-base-v2 (768-d); ~185 online coders marked boundaries ("boundary agreement") — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.02.616300v1.full); [published record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-025-61807-w.html)
- Behavioral trajectory findings from Su et al.: internally oriented thoughts (memory + future) were ~86.8% of all thoughts; current-state thoughts dominated only the first thought (57.6%); categories persisted above chance (transition-matrix diagonal); consecutive thoughts (lag ±1) were more semantically similar than distant ones (lag ±15); topic changes produced stronger perceived boundaries and lower semantic similarity than category-only changes; boundary agreement correlated negatively with consecutive similarity (mean r = −.35) — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.02.616300v1.full)
- Neural findings from Su et al.: medial/lateral parietal default-network regions were more active for internal than current-state thoughts, more so for episodic recall and future thinking than generic semantic memory; posterior medial cortex (PMC) was highest for future thinking; strong boundaries (agreement = 1) activated medial frontal and parietal default and control-network areas, with PMC peaking 4.5–10.5 s after a boundary and scaling with boundary strength; hippocampal boundary response was non-significant (p = .054); boundary patterns in PMC resembled those at movie event boundaries and were not explained by speech pauses; stronger default-network-A/control-network-B connectivity predicted more stable (clustered) thought, while stronger within-default-network-C connectivity (parahippocampal–hippocampal, r = −.42) predicted more variable thought — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.02.616300v1.full)
- **LLM-embedding "cognitive map" trajectories (2025 preprint):** Nour, McNamee, Fradkin & Dolan, "Charting trajectories of human thought using large language models," arXiv 2509.14455 (17 Sept 2025). VECTOR aligns LLM word/phrase representations with human cognitive-map organization and "casts a participant's verbal reports as a geometric trajectory through a cognitive map." Applied to narratives from 1,100 participants, trajectories predicted paralinguistic behavior (response times) and real-world communication patterns — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14455)
- **Multidimensional experience sampling (mDES) + naturalistic fMRI (2025):** Wallace … Smallwood, "Mapping patterns of thought onto brain activity during movie-watching," *eLife* 13:e97731 (Jan 2025). Because probing disrupts natural unfolding, the authors collected mDES ratings at specific film moments, derived data-driven thought patterns, and used them to analyze fMRI from movie-watching; reviewers called the approach "inventive" and the continuous thought-rating method novel. Builds on the premise that the primary dimension of experience is dependence on immediate sensory input — [eLife 97731](https://elifesciences.org/articles/97731); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/c25dc7f82d7c4c3d91663cd7ee810b4f)
- **Daily-life mDES (Smallwood ThinC lab, 2023):** Mulholland's Queen's thesis collected experience-sampling over 5 days and used PCA to identify common "patterns of thought" linked to activities; a companion preprint "Mapping cognition across lab and daily life using Experience-Sampling" exists — [Queen's thesis](https://queensu.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/faf23076-df44-4d90-a187-4ae113d26137/content)
- Smallwood's 2023 framing: the landscape of ongoing thought is heterogeneous, shaped by person and context, and distinct thought patterns map onto association cortex (fronto-parietal and default networks) — [Arizona talk abstract](https://www.cogsci.arizona.edu/events/states-mind-and-brain-jonathan-smallwood-phd-university-texas-austin)
- **Think-aloud + NLP in clinical samples (CogSci 2026):** Bonino et al., N = 67 with/without depression or anxiety, found reduced semantic diversity across speech in the clinical group — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4d78m74x)
- **Free-association chains for rumination (2025 APS poster):** a modified free-association task measuring length of consecutive negative associates found longer negative chains in people prone to rumination, especially at high depression levels — [APS 2025](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/conventions/archive/2025annual/paper/54167/)
- **[foundational, 2021]** Raffaelli, Andrews-Hanna et al. adapted a resting think-aloud paradigm to quantify content and dynamics of thought; trait brooding altered dynamics and conceptual scope (*Scientific Reports*) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/bfd87ca5f1974c3bbaf241ea896ef88f). **[foundational, 2021]** Andrews-Hanna et al. (*JEP: General*) had 94 community adults generate dynamic chains of conceptual associations to valenced seed words and rate six frequent everyday thoughts — [PDF](https://sites.dartmouth.edu/canlab/files/2021/03/2021_Andrew-Hanna_JofExpPsych.pdf)
- **EEG decoding of thought dimensions in naturalistic computer use (2025, PMC12319872):** seven thought dimensions incl. freely-moving thought; markers consistent with lab findings for internally oriented and freely-moving thoughts, novel theta/alpha/beta patterns for off-task, goal-oriented and "sticky" thoughts; deep learning decoded all seven above chance with MCC 0.22–0.43 within-participant and 0.14–0.31 across; only 7 participants / 49 datasets — [PMC12319872](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12319872/)
- **ML detection of freely-moving thought (2025):** Jothiraj, Mills, Irving, Kam et al., *Journal of Neural Engineering* 22:026021 (April 2025), used ERP and spectral EEG features plus behavior during an attention task — [UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/detection-of-freely-moving-thoughts-using-svm-and-eeg-signals/)
- **[foundational, 2021]** Kam, Irving et al. (*PNAS*): frontal alpha power higher for freely-moving than non-freely-moving thoughts; parietal P3 larger for task-related than task-unrelated thoughts — [Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2021/01/18/where-do-our-minds-wander-brain-waves-can-point-the-way/)
- **[foundational, 2020]** Tseng & Poppenk (*Nature Communications*) detected brain "meta-state transitions" without priors as implicit markers of new thoughts; transition rate was stable across contexts, predicted by narrative events during movies and by trait neuroticism at rest — [PMC7359033](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7359033)
- **[foundational, 2018]** Smith, Mills, Paxton & Christoff: 146 students rated freedom of movement of thought several times daily for 5 days; ratings fluctuated reliably across the day (lower early morning/afternoon, higher midday/evening) and diverged from task-unrelated and stimulus-independent thought — [Cognitive Research 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-018-0141-4)
- Methodological summary from the Tamir/TMB line: external raters can count "hard jumps" between thoughts, which is cumbersome but has been used to validate automated embedding tools; semantic distance between consecutive segments serves as a proxy for the size of a conceptual leap — [TMB article](https://tmb.apaopen.org/pub/jvhum0u0/release/2)

### Inferences
- Three independent labs (Tamir/Princeton; Honey–Chen–Lee/JHU; Mills–Christoff) now report the same structural fact with different pipelines: spontaneous thought is "clumpy" — runs of related thoughts punctuated by discrete jumps, roughly 3 thoughts per topic in 2-min reports.
- Su et al.'s result that internally generated topic boundaries evoke the same posterior-medial signature as externally driven movie-event boundaries suggests a common segmentation mechanism for experience whether input is perceptual or self-generated.
- Reliability ceilings matter: human agreement on where a thought ends is only κ ≈ 0.41, so any automated "transition" metric inherits substantial noise; effect sizes in this literature are correspondingly small.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve full text of Wallace et al. 2025 (eLife blocked), so its specific thought patterns and quantitative brain mappings are not reported here.
- No 2023–2026 paper by Jeffrey Wammes specifically on thought trajectories surfaced in any search; his contribution could not be characterized.
- No published 2023–2026 follow-up to Tseng & Poppenk's meta-state-transition method was found.

## Key Question 3: Semantic distance in free association — how far thoughts jump, and whether creative people take bigger/more frequent leaps (DAT, forward flow, Beaty & Kenett semantic networks)

### Takeaway
Creative people do produce more semantically distant associations (DAT, forward flow), but 2024–2025 work complicates the "bigger is better" story: (1) the distance–creativity relation appears non-linear, saturating around cosine distance 0.9–1.0; (2) memory-search dynamics show *two* creative routes — fast switching between clusters (linked to remote-associate performance) and slow, persistent within-cluster search (linked to divergent-thinking fluency/originality); (3) Hills & Kenett's 2024 entropy-modulation theory attributes "faster-and-further" idea generation to executive modulation of retrieval entropy rather than to network structure alone.

### Cited Findings
- **[foundational, 2021]** Olson et al.'s Divergent Association Task (*PNAS*): 8,914 participants named 10 maximally unrelated words; average semantic distance correlated moderately-to-strongly with the Alternative Uses Task and Bridge-the-Associative-Gap Task, at least as strongly as those measures correlate with each other; demographics explained little variance across a 98-country sample — [PMC8237676](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8237676)
- **Non-linearity (CCN 2025 abstract):** Orwig, Luchini, Beaty & Schacter: across alternate uses, question asking and metaphor generation, human creativity ratings rose with semantic distance up to a threshold (between 0.9 and 1), after which more distance did not yield more creative ideas — [CCN 2025 abstract](https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Orwig_2025_A_sweet_spot_creative_ideation_Non-linear.pdf)
- **DAT extensions:** Ishiguro et al. validated a Japanese DAT (*Cognitive Studies*, 2024/2025), motivated by immediate automatic scoring via semantic distance — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcss/32/1/32_2024.038/_article/-char/en); Haase, Hanel & Pokutta (May 2025 preprint) proposed S-DAT using multilingual LLM embeddings because the original DAT relies on English-only static embeddings — [arXiv 2505.09068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09068v1.pdf); Chen & Ding (arXiv 2023) report GPT-4 with greedy decoding outperformed 96% of humans on the DAT, with stochastic sampling/temperature raising scores for other models at a creativity–stability trade-off — [arXiv 2310.11158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11158v1)
- **Forward flow:** introduced by Gray et al. (2019) **[foundational]**; a cue word starts a chain of associations and semantic models estimate how far thought travels; Beaty et al. (2021) found forward flow positively predicted divergent thinking in two studies even controlling for intelligence — [PSU record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/forward-flow-and-creative-thought-assessing-associative-cognition/); a 2024 *Memory & Cognition* paper describes forward flow as a recently adopted measure of conceptual distance between associated concepts, with higher scores indexing broader associative thought (paywalled; abstract-level) — [Memory & Cognition 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-024-01672-y)
- A flow-state study found immersion positively associated with creativity and semantic distance, and that spontaneous flow of associations predicted originality independently of flow — [Turku repository](https://www.utupub.fi/handle/11111/60502)
- **Review (2023):** Beaty & Kenett, "Associative thinking at the core of creativity," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 27(7):671–683 (DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2023.04.004). Argues computational semantic models now allow tracking "how people move through a semantic space when forming associations," revealing search strategies tied to creativity; distinguishes free vs goal-directed association; links associative thinking to brain systems for semantic and episodic memory — [Technion record](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/associative-thinking-at-the-core-of-creativity)
- **Semantic network flexibility (2024):** Li, Beaty, Luchini, Hu & Kenett: across two studies, high crystallized-intelligence groups had more "flexible" semantic networks — less structured, more clustered, more interconnected — proposed as the link between Gc and creativity — [PSU record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/the-role-of-semantic-memory-networks-in-crystallized-intelligence/). **[foundational, 2014]** Kenett, Anaki & Faust: low-creative individuals' free-association networks were more rigid, spread out and fragmented — [Frontiers 2014](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00407/text)
- **Entropy modulation theory (2024/2025):** Hills & Kenett, "An Entropy Modulation Theory of Creative Exploration," *Psychological Review* 132(1):239–251 (online Sept 2024; DOI 10.1037/rev0000511). Starts from "faster-and-further" phenomenology (creative people produce ideas both faster and more novel); argues neither pure associative-structure nor pure executive accounts explain it; proposes executive processes modulate retrieval entropy — strength and variability of activation raise both entropy and speed — and that apparent semantic-structure differences may themselves arise from this entropy-regulating retrieval process balancing exploration vs exploitation — [Warwick WRAP](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/187197)
- **Switching dynamics and creativity (2025):** Ovando-Tellez, Vigreux, Kenett, Benedek, Hills, Beranger, Lopez-Persem, Altmayer, Bieth & Volle, "Switching, fast and slow," *Imaging Neuroscience* 3 (DOI 10.1162/IMAG.a.1018). Study 1: 86 French speakers, polysemous-word fluency task, AUT, Combination of Associates Task, executive tests, resting-state fMRI (Schaefer-200, connectome-based predictive modeling); Study 2: 92-participant behavioral replication with 49 cues. Search broadly followed the Marginal Value Theorem (switches after inter-response time reached the long-run average; those switching closer to the average produced more responses). Deviant patterns — *fast switching* and *slow clustering* — were the ones predicted by brain connectivity; fast switching related to remote-associate performance and mediated connectivity→RAT, slow clustering related to divergent-thinking fluency/originality and mediated connectivity→DT. Authors conclude the policy favoring creativity differs from the policy maximizing fluency, mapping onto flexibility vs persistence pathways — [PMC12628016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12628016/)
- **Depression as reduced "breadth":** a "facilitating thought progression" framework (Bar lab manuscript) defines breadth as the semantic distance between successive thoughts and links reduced breadth to rumination, noting depression involves reduced variability and altered transitions in large-scale networks — [Bar lab PDF](https://www.moshebar.org/_files/ugd/a77db7_80d9db72f4824f12a9352286d9b89985.pdf)
- Spontaneous-thought variability measured as consecutive-phrase cosine distance is higher off-task than on-task (medians 0.730 vs 0.696) in daily-life sampling — [Mallett et al. 2025](https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/download/10307/11817?inline=1)

### Inferences
- The most defensible 2025 statement is: creative individuals *can* reach farther in semantic space, but what distinguishes them is control over the entropy/temperature of search (Hills & Kenett) and when to switch (Ovando-Tellez), not a fixed tendency to always leap far; past ~0.9 cosine distance, extra leap size buys nothing (Orwig).
- LLMs can max out DAT-style distance metrics (GPT-4 > 96% of humans), which reinforces that raw distance is a necessary-but-insufficient proxy for creative thought.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study directly measuring leap *frequency* (jumps per minute) in creative vs non-creative people in naturalistic think-aloud was found; the evidence is from constrained association/fluency tasks.
- The 2024 *Memory & Cognition* forward-flow paper and the aging/forward-flow result could only be read at snippet level; authors and exact statistics are not confirmed.

## Key Question 4: Newest findings on incubation, sleep (N1/hypnagogia), rest, walking, showers, and proposed mechanisms

### Takeaway
The N1 "creative sweet spot" (Lacaux et al. 2021) was conceptually replicated and extended by targeted dream incubation (Horowitz et al. 2023), but a preregistered 2024/2025 nap study with a different insight task found the benefit in N2, not N1, and in the aperiodic 1/f EEG slope rather than alpha/delta oscillations. Mind-wandering-during-incubation effects remain mixed in 2025 studies: benefits appear for writing and some convergent items, but not for divergent fluency generally, and depend on awareness of wandering and task difficulty. The 2022 "shower effect" result (moderately engaging activity best) is the latest direct test of the shower/walk intuition; no 2023–2026 walking study surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- **[foundational, 2021]** Lacaux, Andrillon, Bastoul, Idir, Fonteix-Galet, Arnulf & Oudiette, *Science Advances* (8 Dec 2021): 103 adults did a number-reduction task with a hidden shortcut, then a 20-min rest with EEG. 83% of those who entered N1 (without reaching N2) found the hidden rule vs 31% who stayed awake and 14% who reached N2; as little as ~1 minute in N1 sufficed; N1 was characterized by a mix of alpha and delta activity. Mechanism proposed: a "semilucid" hybrid state where one decouples from the environment and can watch the mind wander while retaining the logic to recognize a spark — [PMC8654287](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8654287); [authors' account](https://sleepreviewmag.com/sleep-health/ideas-dozing-off-secret-unlocking-creativity/); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/sleep-creativity-neuroscience-brain)
- Caveat on the 2021 study: John Kounios noted the design cannot prove N1 caused the insight — stewing over the problem may have caused both dozing and later insight; only 38 fell asleep and 24 stayed in N1 — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/sleep-creativity-neuroscience-brain); [sleep review](https://sleepreviewmag.com/sleep-health/parameters/impact-during-wake/how-people-use-n1-sleep-to-solve-challenging-problems/)
- **Targeted dream incubation (2023):** Horowitz, Esfahany, Gálvez, Maes & Stickgold, *Scientific Reports* 13:7319 (15 May 2023; DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-31361-w). 49 participants; Dormio wearable detected sleep onset, played the cue "tree," and collected dream reports; 2×2 design (sleep vs wake × incubation vs none); three tree-themed creativity tasks. Post-N1 responses showed higher creativity and greater semantic distance than post-wake; successful incubation added benefit beyond N1 alone; per MIT's release, nappers with incubation scored 43% more creatively than nappers without and 78% more than wake-without-incubation; more tree dreams predicted more creative stories — [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/targeted-dream-incubation-at-sleep-onset-increases-post-sleep-creative-performance/); [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2023/sleep-sweet-spot-dreams-creativity-0515); [NIH Research Matters](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/quick-catnaps-may-spark-creativity)
- **Discrepant nap result (bioRxiv June 2024 → peer-reviewed 2025, "N2 sleep promotes the occurrence of 'aha' moments in a perceptual insight task"):** preregistered, 90 participants, 20-min daytime nap, perceptual insight task; N2 but not N1 increased likelihood of post-nap insight (N2 coefficient p = 0.03; N1 and wake non-significant); exploratory analysis found the 1/f aperiodic spectral slope predicted insight beyond sleep stage while classic alpha/delta band power did not; authors link this to regularization/synaptic downscaling theories — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.24.600359.full.pdf); [CCN 2024 paper](https://2024.ccneuro.org/pdf/29_Paper_authored_CCN_2024.pdf); [PMC12200826](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12200826/)
- **Ongoing (2025):** Beeman lab (Northwestern) CNS 2025 poster describes a 60-participant study with matchstick puzzles, AUT and tower-building before/after an incubation period, with a headband waking participants at N1 to report the last thought vs a video control; no results reported yet — [CNS 2025 poster](https://cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6502)
- Sleep-onset as a graded, regionally staggered transition: Andrillon, Taillard & Strauss, *Neurophysiologie Clinique* (April 2024), describe falling asleep as a progressive loss of responsiveness and reorganization of brain dynamics where regional changes take seconds but the whole period lasts minutes, requiring multidimensional (subjective + behavioral + physiological) measurement — [Bordeaux Neurocampus](https://www.bordeaux-neurocampus.fr/?p=170529)
- **Mind-wandering incubation, 2025 studies:** Du, Gordon & Tolmie (*Brain Sciences*): 85 participants, unusual-uses and remote-associates tasks with a 0-back incubation break; mind wandering had no significant effect on divergent thinking (incubation improved fluency mainly for repeated items); for convergent thinking, mind-wandering *with* awareness helped low-difficulty repeated items while mind-wandering *without* awareness hindered novel moderate-difficulty items — [PMC12190454](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12190454/). McDaniel, Habibi & Kaplan (*Scientific Reports*, preregistered): short-story writing before/after a 10-min break (0-back, 2-back, mindfulness, or none); mind wandering during incubation predicted increases in creative writing performance — [PMC12241419](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12241419/)
- Chaudhuri & Bhattacharya (*Creativity Research Journal*, in press) test poetry reading as an incubation break with 153 participants randomized to poem, poem+rating, or non-poetic text — [Goldsmiths](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/38386)
- **[foundational, 2022]** Teng & Lien (*PLOS One*) note the incubation/mind-wandering evidence is inconsistent and separate propensity to wander from diversity of content; they summarize Sio & Ormerod's meta-analysis that undemanding tasks during incubation beat demanding tasks or rest — [PMC9053813](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9053813/)
- **[foundational, 2022] "Shower effect":** Irving et al.: >300 students did an alternate-uses task, watched either a boring laundry clip or a moderately engaging *When Harry Met Sally* clip, then repeated the task; mind wandering facilitated incubation during *moderately engaging* activity, not boring ones; Irving attributes earlier replication failures (of Baird et al. 2012) to measuring distraction rather than mind-wandering — [ZME Science summary](https://zmescience.com/science/science-may-finally-explain-why-some-of-the-best-ideas-come-while-taking-a-shower)
- Mechanistic link to thought dynamics: spontaneous thought "prioritizes episodic detail," consistent with a memory-consolidation function, and salient concerns act as attractors — [Mildner & Tamir 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11210302)

### Inferences
- The two sleep-stage results are not strictly contradictory: Lacaux used a hidden-rule number task and awakened people on N2 entry; the 2024/2025 study used a perceptual insight task and let naps run 20 min. Together they suggest the relevant variable may be a neural regime (reduced constraint/aperiodic slope change) that can be reached in N1 or N2 depending on task and protocol, rather than N1 per se.
- Horowitz et al.'s finding that N1 raised *semantic distance* of outputs links the sleep literature directly to the trajectory metrics in Q2–Q3: sleep onset appears to loosen the constraints that keep waking thought clustered.
- Across incubation studies, the moderating variables now recognized (task type, awareness of wandering, item novelty, engagement level of the filler activity) explain why simple "break = insight" replications fail.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2023–2026 study on walking and creative thought trajectories surfaced; walking evidence remains the older Oppezzo & Schwartz line (not retrieved here).
- Authors and journal of the "N2 sleep promotes 'aha' moments" paper could not be confirmed from accessible sources (PMC blocked); one secondary note claims *PLOS Biology* 2025 but this is unverified.
- A rumored *Trends in Neurosciences* sleep-onset review by Lacaux/Andrillon/Oudiette (2024) could not be located.

## Key Question 5: What computational models (random walks on semantic networks, foraging/MVT, exploration–exploitation, LLM-based models) say about how thought moves

### Takeaway
The classic debate — strategic two-stage foraging (Hills) vs a single undirected random walk (Abbott/Griffiths) — has been reframed by 2024–2025 work: with rich modern embeddings, a plain similarity-driven random walk reproduces MVT-like foraging signatures (and Metropolis–Hastings "decide-to-leave" rules fit worse); human data nonetheless show systematic MVT deviations (fast switches, slow clusters) that carry the creativity signal; and Hills & Kenett's entropy-modulation theory casts the controllable parameter as the "temperature" of retrieval. LLM embeddings are now used mainly as measurement instruments (VECTOR) rather than as generative models of mind wandering.

### Cited Findings
- **[foundational, 2012/2015]** Abbott, Austerweil & Griffiths (*Psychological Review*) showed a random walk on a free-association network reproduces clustered retrieval previously taken as evidence of patch foraging; Hills et al. counter that a two-stage process using local similarity plus a global frequency cue fits better and that a pure similarity walk could not capture the foraging pattern — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/random-walks-on-semantic-networks-can-resemble-optimal-foraging/); [bioRxiv rational account](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/326397.full.pdf)
- **[foundational, 2015]** Hills, Todd & Jones: verbal-fluency search moves locally within patches then globally between them; Hills frames exploration–exploitation as fundamental across cognition — [Hills Todd TICS 2015 PDF](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/psych/people/thills/thills/2015hillstoddtics.pdf)
- **Modern-embedding test (Nov 2025 preprint):** Moore, "Optimal Foraging in Memory Retrieval: Evaluating Random Walks and Metropolis-Hastings Sampling in Modern Semantic Spaces," arXiv 2511.12759. Used OpenAI text-embedding-3-large (1536-d) on 550 animal concepts (Hills et al. 2012 fluency data, 141 participants; Troyer categories). Random walk = softmax over cosine similarity, T = 0.027, no explicit switch rule. With names only, the random walk's IRT-deviation slope was positive (0.36, p ≈ 1e-16; non-human-like); with GPT-4o-generated descriptive sentences added, the slope became −18.0 (p ≈ 7e-6), qualitatively matching Abbott et al. 2015 and MVT; Metropolis–Hastings produced no human-like IRT drop at switches. Conclusion: "appropriately structured semantic embeddings" plus fluid similarity-driven movement, not discrete accept/reject decisions, reproduce foraging dynamics — [arXiv 2511.12759](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12759v1)
- **Human MVT deviations carry creativity (2025):** Ovando-Tellez et al. found most responses obeyed MVT but fast switching (linked to remote associates) and slow clustering (linked to divergent thinking) deviated and were brain-connectivity-predictable — [PMC12628016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12628016/)
- **Entropy modulation (2024/2025):** Hills & Kenett formalize retrieval as threshold competition where executive processes raise activation strength/variability, increasing both retrieval entropy and speed to yield "faster-and-further" — [Warwick WRAP](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/187197); a 2025 Tartu thesis reports that neural-network models with elevated entropy generated more diverse representations faster and traversed more latent distance — [Tartu thesis](https://dspace.ut.ee/items/6f768d5d-ab8f-431e-9173-5f6880d8296d/full)
- Hills' chapter "Cognitive Foraging: Exploration versus Exploitation" (*Behavioral Network Science*, Cambridge UP, online 8 Nov 2024) combines fluency data with a language-derived cognitive map to ask whether memory search resembles spatial search — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/behavioral-network-science/cognitive-foraging-exploration-versus-exploitation/C6339CECC348298FAF3B480A9B2A1B6C). **[foundational, 2021]** Kenett, Baker, Hills, Hart & Beaty's "creative foraging" game grouped people by exploration/exploitation style and linked style and semantic-network structure to creative performance — [HUJI record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/creative-foraging-examining-relations-between-foraging-styles-sem/)
- **LLM embeddings as trajectory instruments (2025):** VECTOR (Nour, McNamee, Fradkin, Dolan) treats verbal reports as geometric trajectories through an LLM-derived cognitive map; in 1,100 participants, trajectory properties predicted response times and real-world communication — [arXiv 2509.14455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14455)
- Sentence-transformer embeddings (all-mpnet-base-v2) and spaCy word vectors are the de facto standards for consecutive-thought distance in 2024–2025 empirical work — [Su et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.02.616300v1.full); [Mallett et al.](https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/download/10307/11817?inline=1)
- **LLMs as generators:** GPT-4 exceeds 96% of humans on DAT distance with greedy decoding; sampling temperature trades creativity for stability — [arXiv 2310.11158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11158v1); a 2026 arXiv review of LLM creativity tests DAT robustness across embedding models and proposes a conditional DAT requiring usefulness — [arXiv 2605.13450](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13450); a NeurIPS 2025 position paper argues social simulation needs agents that simulate *thought* rather than surface text — [arXiv 2506.06958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06958v2)
- Attractor dynamics in human data: anxiety-laden current-concern content rose *before* topic jumps and peaked right after, consistent with salient topics acting as attractors in the trajectory — [Mildner & Tamir 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11210302)

### Inferences
- The convergent picture is a *temperature-controlled similarity walk over a rich semantic/episodic map*: softmax temperature (Moore), retrieval entropy (Hills & Kenett) and DFT "constraint" are three vocabularies for the same control knob; cognitive control sets the knob, salience biases the landscape (attractors).
- Because a plain random walk on good embeddings already yields MVT signatures, MVT conformity alone is weak evidence of strategic control; the diagnostic signal of strategy (and creativity) lies in deviations from MVT.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2023–2026 paper was found in which an LLM is used as a *generative* model of mind-wandering whose output trajectories are quantitatively compared to human think-aloud trajectories (jump rate, dwell time, distance distribution).
- Moore (2025) is an unreviewed preprint restricted to the animal category; generality to open-ended spontaneous thought is untested.

## Key Question 6: The neural signature of a thought transition (neural state switching, hippocampal replay, locus coeruleus/noradrenaline "reset")

### Takeaway
Three converging 2024–2025 lines: (1) intracranial recordings show hippocampal sharp-wave ripples rise during periods of vivid, task-decoupled self-generated thought (Iwata et al. 2024); (2) fMRI think-aloud shows internally generated topic boundaries evoke posterior-medial default-network responses (peaking ~4.5–10.5 s later) that resemble external event boundaries, while medial-temporal–hippocampal coupling predicts more variable trajectories (Su et al. 2025); (3) locus coeruleus arousal responses at context shifts "reset" hippocampal event representations (Clewett, Huang & Davachi 2025, *Neuron*) and tonic vs phasic LC drive shifts cortical network states (Grimm et al. 2024, *Nature Neuroscience*). Direct evidence that an LC burst triggers a *spontaneous* thought transition in humans is still missing.

### Cited Findings
- **Hippocampal ripples and ongoing thought (2024):** Iwata et al., "Hippocampal sharp-wave ripples correlate with periods of naturally occurring self-generated thoughts in humans," *Nature Communications* 15:4078 (May 2024; DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-48367-1). Hippocampal LFPs from 10 epilepsy patients for up to 15 days with experience sampling; SWR rates were higher during extended periods when thoughts were more vivid, less desirable, more imaginable, and less correlated with an external task; framed as support for self-generated thought relying on memory replay (full text blocked; abstract-level) — [PMC11111804](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11111804/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/42f322011139442b9ad0eb1fbc089991)
- **Boundary responses during spontaneous thought (2024/2025):** Su et al.: strong thought boundaries activated medial frontal and parietal default and control regions; PMC response peaked 4.5–10.5 s post-boundary and scaled with boundary strength; hippocampus showed no significant boundary response (p = .054); PMC boundary patterns resembled movie event-boundary patterns; default-A/control-B connectivity → stable thought, parahippocampal–hippocampal connectivity (r = −.42) → variable thought — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.02.616300v1.full)
- **LC "reset" at event boundaries (2025):** Clewett, Huang & Davachi, *Neuron* 113 (preprint Aug 2024: "Locus coeruleus activation 'resets' hippocampal event representations and separates adjacent memories"). Context shifts engaged pupil-linked arousal and LC activity; boundary LC activity correlated selectively with impaired order memory across (not within) events; boundaries promoted temporal pattern separation in left dentate gyrus that tracked LC responses; neuromelanin-MRI and low-frequency fluctuation markers of elevated background LC activity predicted weaker boundary responses ("hyperarousal disrupts event segmentation"); the LC did not respond at every boundary and the evidence is correlational — [Clewett lab PDF](https://clewettlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2026/01/2025_Clewett_et_al_NEURON.pdf); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.15.608148.full.pdf); [PsyPost caveats](https://www.psypost.org/scientists-uncover-brains-reset-button-for-splitting-memories-into-distinct-events/)
- **LC firing mode and cortical network states (2024):** Grimm, Zerbi et al., "Tonic and burst-like locus coeruleus stimulation distinctly shift network activity across the cortical hierarchy," *Nature Neuroscience* (Sept 2024) — title/author level only; full text blocked — [PMC11537968](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11537968/). Related: chemogenetic tonic LC stimulation strengthens the default mode network — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/40DZLXA4); a 2026 ISMRM abstract (Barcellini, Zerbi et al.) reports a cross-species fMRI signature of LC-driven brain-state transitions — [ISMRM abstract](https://echo.ismrm.org/program/abstracts/708151ac-9751-42c0-9bff-e1bb845d35c5/export/text); an ISMRM 2021 abstract reported increased pupil size during state switching when LC was up-regulated — [ISMRM 2021](https://archive.ismrm.org/2021/3151.html)
- **Clinical state-switching deficit (Dec 2024):** pilot study in *Epilepsia Open* found patients with dissociative seizures tended to remain in particular LC-dependent brain states rather than switching, proposing impaired network switching as a mechanism of dissociation — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/ccfa6bf9c7684859aad6783990657a0d)
- **[foundational, 2016]** Mittner et al. proposed an integrative model linking default-mode network dynamics to LC-noradrenaline signaling in mind wandering — [Mittner et al. PDF](https://munin.uit.no/bitstream/10037/10463/3/article.pdf)
- **Replay as mechanism (2023 commentary):** Varma & Yu (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences*) propose spontaneous neural replay in DMN and hippocampus underlies involuntary autobiographical memories, with déjà vu reflecting DMN-limited transient activation — [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/spontaneous-neural-replay-account-for-involuntary-autobiographical-memories-and-deja-vu-experiences/D2547BFF45DF24D8104447F6BFDF99F8)
- Review-level synthesis (2023): SWRs/replay and noradrenergic/serotonergic systems identified as candidate mechanisms of spontaneous thought in the *Nature Mental Health* review — [Arizona record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/recent-advances-in-the-neuroscience-of-spontaneous-and-off-task-t/)
- **[foundational, 2020]** Tseng & Poppenk's meta-state transitions as implicit markers of new thoughts, stable rate across contexts and predicted by neuroticism at rest — [PMC7359033](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7359033)
- **EEG:** freely-moving thought is marked by elevated frontal alpha **[foundational, 2021]** — [Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2021/01/18/where-do-our-minds-wander-brain-waves-can-point-the-way/); naturalistic decoding of freely-moving/sticky/goal-oriented thought from theta/alpha/beta reaches MCC 0.22–0.43 within-person (2025) — [PMC12319872](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12319872/)
- Real-time fMRI experience sampling (Mills, UBC thesis; year not shown in the retrieved listing): medial-temporal and core default-network subsystems were more engaged when thoughts were freely moving; frontoparietal control regions more engaged when thoughts were constrained/deliberate — [UBC thesis](https://open.library.ubc.ca/collections/24/items/1.0436927)
- Sleep-onset as transition regime: aperiodic 1/f slope, not oscillatory band power, predicted insight after naps — [bioRxiv 2024](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.24.600359.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- A plausible composite model from these sources: medial-temporal/hippocampal replay (SWRs) supplies candidate content and drives trajectory variability; posterior-medial default cortex registers/segments the switch (~5–10 s lag in BOLD); LC-noradrenergic phasic bursts act as a reset that separates successive mental "events," while tonic LC tone sets overall network state. This is a synthesis across studies, not a finding of any single one.
- The dissociation in Su et al. (default/control coupling → stable thought; hippocampal coupling → variable thought) is consistent with DFT's division between deliberate constraint (control network) and spontaneous content generation (medial temporal lobe).

### Gaps
- No study was found that time-locks LC/pupil bursts to *self-reported spontaneous* thought transitions in humans; Clewett et al. concerns externally imposed context shifts.
- Full text of Iwata et al. 2024 and Grimm et al. 2024 was inaccessible; SWR rate magnitudes, timing relative to probes, and the specific tonic-vs-burst network effects are not reported here.
- The O'Callaghan, Walpola & Shine (2021) neuromodulatory-transition review was referenced in search results but a verified URL could not be obtained, so it is not cited above.
