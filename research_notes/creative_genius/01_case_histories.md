# Case histories of breakthrough ideas: how the idea arose, in retrospect

*2026-10-13. Brief 01 of creative_genius; extends idea_generation/00_SYNTHESIS.md. Grades: **A** historian or cognitive-scientist primary-source study, or edited manuscript; **B** participant's own words, read verbatim (retrospective unless dated); **C** journalism, interview relay, encyclopedia; **D** popular retelling or anecdote, treated as myth unless a higher grade confirms it. Bracket numbers are Sources.*

## 1. Cases

*Mix* = recombined; *Dropped* = assumption; *Rep* = representation; also *Before*, *Prep*, *Check*.

**Darwin, selection (1837-38). A, B.** *Mix:* breeders' selection [1] plus Malthus's geometric-ratio sentence, which the Darwin Online editors identify in Notebook D (28 Sep 1838), in an entry that also cites De Candolle's "warring of the species" [2]. *Dropped:* that adaptation needs design or will [1]. *Rep:* "a force like a hundred thousand wedges" [2]. *Before:* 14 months of transmutation notebooks (from July 1837); Malthus read "for amusement" [1]. *Prep:* 21 years to the Origin; a 35-page sketch only in 1842 [1]. Gruber: a "network of enterprise" [4]. **Myths:** the Galapagos-finch epiphany is a 20th-century legend [3]; the Autobiography's "Baconian... without any theory" self-portrait is retrospective [1][2].

**Einstein, 1905. A, B.** *Mix:* the magnet-conductor asymmetry ("unbearable for me"), Lorentz's local time, the relativity principle [5]. *Dropped:* absolute simultaneity, after years on a failed emission theory [5]. *Rep:* time defined by signals; a postulate-based theory [5]. *Before:* crisis, then Besso, about 5 weeks before the paper was finished [5]. *Prep:* the beam puzzle at 16; "seven and more years"; paper written in 5-6 weeks [5]. *Check:* Fizeau and aberration, not Michelson-Morley [5]. Norton: "virtually no primary sources", and the beam story shifts between tellings [5][6]; Einstein denied suddenness: "by steps" (via [9]).

**Einstein, equivalence to general relativity (1907-15). A.** *Mix:* free fall (the "happiest thought", in a 1907 review for Stark, recalled in 1922) [8], special relativity, tensor calculus. The Zurich Notebook holds candidate correct equations from 1912-13, dropped for want of coordinate restrictions giving the Newtonian limit and conservation; non-covariant "Entwurf" equations followed; in Nov 1915 he returned by choosing Christoffel symbols as the field [7]. *Check:* Newtonian limit; Mercury's perihelion [7]. *Prep:* 8 years, about 3 lost to a mistaken obstacle; the reconstruction is "strong but largely circumstantial" [7].

**Poincare, Fuchsian functions (c. 1880-81), and Hadamard. B.** *Before:* 15 days trying to prove such functions impossible, then a black-coffee night when ideas "collide until pairs interlocked"; analogy with elliptic functions guided the next step [10]. *Dropped:* that they cannot exist. *Rep:* on an omnibus step the link to non-Euclidean transformations came with nothing "to have paved the way", verified later "for conscience' sake"; "often this feeling deceives us" [10]; told in 1908 [9]. Hadamard (1945) printed Einstein's reply: thought is visual and muscular "associative play"; words come "only in a secondary stage" (via [9]; book not opened).

**Kekule, benzene (1865). A; the dream is D.** A constraint, not a picture: tetravalent carbon allows over 200 C6H6 isomers, so the task was one structure that fits data. False isomer reports in 1861-62 made a ring unthinkable; 1863-64 corrections left one mono- and three di-substituted benzenes, which a ring explains [11]. *Mix:* the 1858 chain theory plus cleaned counts; Couper, Loschmidt, Laurent had ring-like ideas he knew. *Rep:* a closed ring, later with oscillating bonds. *Prep:* 7 years. *Check:* his lab ran "facts to prove or disprove"; the ortho objection led to the 1872 oscillation [11]. The snake reverie surfaced in 1890, undocumented; Rocke trusts the sincerity, not the memory [11].

**Shannon, 1948. B.** *Mix:* Hartley's log measure [13] and thermodynamic entropy [12]. *Dropped:* meaning ("semantic aspects... irrelevant to the engineering problem"). *Rep:* a message as one selection from a set, with a probabilistic source [13]. *Prep:* Hartley read in the 1930s; probabilistic model "1941", reached via a coin, a loaded coin, a die; cryptography and information theory "stimulate each other"; 2-3 years' work by 1943-44 [12]. His 1952 talk lists simplification, solved analogues, restating, generalizing, steps, inversion [14]. *Check:* theorems; hand-built Markov texts [13]. **Myth:** the von Neumann "entropy" story, which Shannon disowned [12].

**Watson and Crick (1951-53). B, C; credit contested.** *Mix:* Chargaff's ratios, King's College X-ray results, Pauling-style model building, Donohue's keto-form correction [15][16]. *Dropped:* phosphates inside (first model, rejected by Franklin) and wrongly drawn base forms [16][17]. *Rep:* cardboard cut-outs of the bases; with the right forms A-T and G-C fit by hydrogen bonds [16]. *Before:* Pauling's wrong triple helix; a "four-week frenzy" [15]. *Prep:* about 18 months from the Nov 1951 King's meeting [17]. *Check:* Chargaff's ratios fell out; "roughly compatible... unproved until it has been checked against more exact results" [15]. Cobb and Comfort (2023): Franklin an equal member of a quartet [17].

**Feynman diagrams (c. 1939-48). B, A.** *Dropped:* the field's own degrees of freedom and self-action; its fault (radiation resistance) he met later [18]. *Mix:* Wheeler's half-advanced, half-retarded action; Dirac's remark that a kernel was "analogous" to the exponential of the action: "let's see what happens if we make them equal", and Schroedinger's equation fell out [18]. *Rep:* path sums, then diagrams as bookkeeping [19]. *Before:* stuck, at a beer party. *Prep:* "about eight years". *Check:* every known case and limit; one evening matched a six-month calculation (Slotnick) [18]. Pocono 1948: "no one seemed able to follow" [19].

**Kalman filter (1958-60). B, C; origin thin.** Wiener's problem "re-examined using the Bode-Shannon representation of random processes and the 'state-transition' method"; stationarity and infinite memory dropped; filtering dual to the regulator; *Check:* "two well-known problems, confirming and extending earlier results" [20]. *Before:* not documented. Adoption: NASA Ames, holding an unsolved nonlinear Apollo navigation problem, heard Kalman in fall 1960 and it "hit a responsive chord"; Schmidt joined it to in-house linear-perturbation methods, relinearized about the current estimate (the extended filter); an accidental mis-initialized run showed convergence [21].

**Backpropagation (1960-86). B, C.** Many arrivals: control theory, Linnainmaa 1970, Werbos, Parker, Rumelhart [24]. Hinton: "early 1982... mainly [Rumelhart's] idea"; "using the chain rule to get derivatives was not a novel idea"; *Check:* hidden units learned word representations from family-tree triples, which won Nature (1986) acceptance [22]. *Dropped:* threshold units; Rosenblatt had the error-correction idea, but discrete outputs have zero derivative [24]. "The ability to create useful new features distinguishes" it from the perceptron procedure [23]. Schmidhuber's chronology is partisan [24].

**Attention (2014) and the Transformer (2017). B, C.** Problem: the fixed-length-vector bottleneck [25]. Bahdanau: a two-cursor scheme was too complex and a hard-coded diagonal one lacked elegance; then "learn to search where to put the cursor", from his own translation exercises, as softmax-weighted averaging, which "worked great from the very first try" and beat the basic encoder-decoder, most on long sentences [25][26]. Transformer: Uszkoreit proposed self-attention about 2014; a design doc; a first model only level with LSTMs; Shazeer's rewrite and a sprint to the 19 May 2017 deadline, with ablations, gave record BLEU [28]; contributions are itemized in the paper [27]. *Dropped:* the fixed vector, then recurrence. *Prep:* months; about 3 years from idea to paper.

**CRISPR (1987-2013). B, C.** Repeats first seen in 1987; the immunity reading came in 2003-05. Makarova et al.: spacers homologous to phage and plasmid genes "make us abandon" the DNA-repair hypothesis for an RNAi-like defense [29]. *Rep:* spacers as a memory of past invaders. *Check:* adding or deleting spacers changed phage resistance (2007) [31]. The RNAi analogy was right on function, wrong on target (these systems cut DNA) [31]. *Engineering step:* from the tracrRNA:crRNA secondary structure Jinek et al. "suggested the possibility" of one chimeric guide, "appealing... utility for programmed DNA cleavage and genome editing" [30]; human cells 2013 [31]. *Prep:* 25 years.

**Two shorter cases.** *Maxwell (1861-62; B, A via summaries):* a vortex and idle-wheel model offered as "provisional and temporary"; wave speed from electrical data "agrees so exactly" with light's that "we can scarcely avoid the inference" [32]; Nersessian: analogy, imagery, thought experiments and limiting cases recur in conceptual change [33]. *Wright brothers (1899-1903; C):* a 1901 glider gave one-third of predicted lift; they doubted the inherited Smeaton constant (0.0054; their glider data implied about 0.0033) and built a tunnel [34].

## 2. Regularities

- **Time:** months (attention) to 25 years (CRISPR); most cases ran 3-8 years. The last step took days to weeks once the representation was found (Besso to paper 5-6 weeks; DNA 4); checking is slower.
- **Shape:** 2-4 existing parts, one dropped assumption, one changed representation; arrivals are often multiple (backprop; Darwin and Wallace).
- **Tellings drift** (Darwin's month, Einstein's beam, Kekule after 25 years, Poincare after 27): log reasons at proposal time.
- **Myths (D):** flagged in the entries above; also Newton's apple, told in 1726, the real bottleneck a 20-year proof [37].
- **Seen live (Dunbar):** 18 of 70 results unexpected; 8 of 10 inconsistent ones yielded hypotheses; 97 of 99 analogies stayed in biology [36].

## 3. Patterns and what transfers to an LLM idea lane

Inferences from mechanism in retrospective, survivorship-biased cases, not tested interventions. An agent lacks years of immersion, so each move starts from the recipe, ledger and diagnostics. Run each as a long-shot-lane card, A/B against the plain arm, outputting its instance and a falsifying number.

1. **Literalize a loose analogy** [1, 18, 29, 32]. *Do:* find "behaves like / analogous to" in recipe comments, ledger notes or a paper; rewrite it as an equality of two named quantities; derive one consequence the source did not state; name the code change that tests it. Feynman set Dirac's "analogous" to "equal"; Darwin carried breeders' selection into nature. *Fails when* the identity holds only in a limit: RNAi was right on function, wrong on target [31]; "often this feeling deceives us" [10].
2. **Delete a load-bearing part** [5, 18, 20, 25, 27]. *Do:* list five things every run inherits (tied embeddings, one schedule, a fixed state vector, a hard discrete choice); imagine each false; keep one that a logged diagnostic already charges a cost to; write the replacement, softening a discrete choice into a softmax-weighted mixture, as Bahdanau did [26]. *Fails when* no measured cost backs it, or the obvious counterexample was skipped: Feynman's idea had a "glaringly obvious fault" [18].
3. **Anomaly first; then audit what forbids the idea** [7, 11, 29, 35]. *Do:* take the most surprising number in the diagnostics; write "if real, also X"; check X in existing logs. For one killed ledger idea, name the measurement that killed it: single-seed, untuned, buggy? Re-run once. Kekule's ring was unthinkable until false isomer reports were corrected; Einstein's right equations sat on paper 3 years; ResNet began from "unexpectedly... not caused by overfitting" [35]. *Fails when* the anomaly is noise or every dead idea is revived: replicate on a second seed first.
4. **Re-measure one inherited constant** [34]. *Do:* pick a value the recipe took from elsewhere (LR rule, warmup, init std, clip); sweep 5-7 points with only that knob moving at toy scale; if the optimum is over 25% off, propose the correction. The Wrights' rigs showed an inherited constant about 40% off. *Fails when* the optimum is scale-dependent: repeat at two widths.
5. **Re-represent until the constraint is computable** [10, 16, 18, 20]. *Do:* redraw the pathology as another object: a per-layer table of update norms, a shape-annotated signal-flow diagram, a toy (2 layers, 1 head, 64 wide) solvable by hand; read off what is forced; state the idea as that object's missing entry. *Fails when* chosen for elegance: Poincare found false ideas also gratify the sense of beauty [10]; demand one number the old view could not give.
6. **Scaffold with a pre-registered number; then drop the story** [32]. *Do:* ship each long shot as story, one surprising numeric prediction, and a kill threshold fixed before running; if the number fails, discard the story (Maxwell's "provisional and temporary" model). *Fails when* a coincidence passes for confirmation (Maxwell's match was rare).
7. **Shrink, solve, generalize, invert** [12, 14]. *Do:* cut the change to a one-minute reproducer where the pathology survives; solve there; widen one axis at a time; ask what recipe would produce the bad number. *Fails when* the reduction strips the phenomenon (Shannon: "you may have stripped away the problem") [14]: check at two sizes.
8. **Keep open problems with blockers; re-screen when a blocker moves** [21, 26]. *Do:* hold 5-8 unsolved pathologies with exact specs and what blocks each (compute, memory, a missing op); read every new paper or ledger result against all of them ("applies / blocked by X"); when a constraint moves, reopen what it killed. NASA Ames had the spec when Kalman arrived; Bahdanau judged attention inevitable, awaiting GPUs. *Fails when* fits are forced: demand a named blocker.
9. **Known-answer test before the novelty claim** [18, 20, 28]. *Do:* state the neutral setting where the new component must reproduce the baseline exactly, and one old result it must recover as a limit; then the novel prediction. Feynman matched Slotnick's limit; Kalman "confirmed" known results; the Transformer first matched LSTMs. *Fails when* parity hides a no-op: log the effective value of every knob.
10. **Port by adapting at the violated assumption** [21, 24, 36]. *Do:* state the problem solver-neutrally (state, inputs, constraint, objective); name the source solution; list which of its assumptions your setting breaks; write the adapter (linearize, relax, soften); derive one prediction. The extended Kalman filter was a linear filter plus relinearization; backprop needed differentiable units. *Fails when* similarity is only surface (live analogies stayed near [36]).
