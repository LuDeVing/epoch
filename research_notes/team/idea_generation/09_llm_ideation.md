# 09 - LLM ideation, 2024 to Oct 2026: what was measured, and what transfers to the nano idea loop

Compiled 2026-10-13. Citations are [S#] (URLs in Sources). Strength tags: **S** strong (pre-registered, large N, or execution-grounded), **M** medium (one controlled study, or several consistent ones), **W** weak (5 or fewer runs per arm, LLM-judged only, single author, or a secondary report). The shared web-search quota ran out mid-task, so later checks were direct fetches of linked papers; the Nature Human Behaviour paper, Graph of Thoughts and PostTrainBench were not read.

## Bottom line
1. Homogenisation is real at population level. Sampling knobs (temperature, nucleus, min-p) barely move it; input structure (roles, personas, retrieval, serial ledgers, staged CoT) moves it a lot [S10,S11,S13,S41].
2. LLM-judged novelty does not predict outcomes. Execution cut LLM-idea scores by 1.0 to 2.0 points [S2]; LLM-judged innovativeness vs effectiveness r = -0.06 [S25]; off-the-shelf judges are near chance on "which idea works" [S1,S31,S32].
3. In training-code loops, real gains came from composing and repairing known techniques under an external metric [S41,S26]. What separated loops was design: shared outcome lineage, role partition, frontier search, pre-run selection with outcome memory, hidden evaluation [S41,S33,S42,S24].
4. Null or negative results worth keeping: generating more ideas without selecting (6.9 vs 7.1%) [S33]; better search policy on weak operators [S23]; higher temperature and longer personas [S13]; no tree-vs-flat ablation for ideas [S18,S21]; the strongest diversity claims rest on one or two studies, and the Hivemind headline is contested [S7].

## 1. Homogenisation, measured
- **Individual up, collective down.** Doshi and Hauser (293 writers, 600 raters): one AI idea raised novelty 5.4% and usefulness 3.7%; up to five ideas +8.1% / +9.0%; AI-aided stories were more similar to each other [S3] (S). Their 2026 follow-up says AI used only to *select* ideas kept variety near human-only, while AI used to generate reduced it (no effect sizes) [S16] (W). A Wharton summary of a Nature Human Behaviour re-analysis: 6% unique ideas with ChatGPT vs 100% human-only in one task; less diverse in 37 of 45 comparisons [S5] (W, secondary).
- **Across models.** LLM answers resemble other LLMs' more than humans resemble each other [S8] (M). Hivemind: 25 models, same-prompt similarity above 0.8 in 79% of cases (T=1, top-p 0.9); cross-model 0.71 to 0.82; min-p at T=2 still leaves 61.2% of pairs above 0.8 [S6] (M). A re-evaluation disputes the flagship example (4 to 41 clusters depending on method, not 2), shows 0.8 sits near the floor for different-idea pairs (median 0.70 to 0.72) and that no human baseline was run [S7] (M). Read thresholds against a null.
- **Research ideas.** 4,000 seed ideas per topic left ~5% non-duplicates (~200) at cosine 0.8 [S1] (S). Claude 3 and GPT-4o give under 3 distinct outputs in 10 samples; larger models are less diverse than smaller ones in a family [S10] (M). Three frontier LLMs fall below human parity on stories, slogans, alternative uses [S12] (M). LLM ideas cluster on "bridge" and "synthesis" patterns [S17] (M).

## 2. What raises diversity (ordered by evidence)
1. **Serial regeneration with earlier outputs visible.** The only tested prompt that took three frontier models to roughly human-writer diversity; paraphrasing and a "be creative" system prompt were marginal [S10] (M).
2. **Staged CoT** (list 100 seeds, make them bold and different, then elaborate): cosine similarity 0.255 vs 0.386 base vs 0.243 for humans; the CoT row used a different GPT-4 snapshot [S4] (M). Default 83 to 152 category combinations, humans 197; numbers differ between sections and quality was not measured [S11] (M).
3. **Personas.** Famous-person prompts shave about 0.01 off cosine [S4]. *Ordinary* persona banks beat "creative entrepreneur" personas (56.97 vs 50.40 vs 39.15 combinations); persona+CoT reached 248 vs humans 197 [S11]. Thirty-two Big-Five persona mixtures raised human-relative diversity in 8 of 9 settings [S12]. Persona detail saturates after the first step, interaction architectures cover non-overlapping regions, temperature and "be diverse" instructions are negligible [S13] (M; opinion-simulation task, 7 models). Evolved persona sets: +78.8% diversity, -5.1% utility, persona-to-response dispersion correlation only 0.40 to 0.55 [S14] (W).
4. **Verbalized sampling** (5 responses with probabilities, optionally tails below 0.10): 1.6 to 2.1x creative-writing diversity; human-rated poem 1.90 to 2.39, story 2.74 to 3.06; larger models gain more; plain VS lowers quality somewhat; synthetic-math fine-tuning 30.6 to 36.1-37.5 [S9] (M, untested on research ideas). Reused as the candidate operator in program evolution [S57] (W).
5. **Temperature and nucleus.** Weak link to novelty, moderate to incoherence [S15]; T=1.5 adds about one category, T=2.0 is garbled [S11]; small monotone gains from 0.7 to 1.3 [S12]; mixing models stays correlated [S6,S8] (M).

## 3. Search structure for ideation
- **Tree/graph of thought.** ToT beat CoT on creative writing (coherence 7.56 vs 6.93 vs 6.19, GPT-4 scored; humans preferred ToT 41 to 21, 38 ties) [S18] (M), but that is coherence, not novelty. A guided-MCTS ideation paper claims gains over ToT (single author, LLM-judged) [S19] (W). I found no ablation of tree vs flat search on idea novelty or realised value.
- **Systems.** Co-scientist: generate, reflect (with search), Elo tournament (multi-turn debate for top pairs), evolve (new hypotheses compete, never overwrite), meta-review. Elo tracked GPQA-diamond correctness (top-1 78.4%); experts preferred it on 11 goals (mean rank 2.36; novelty 3.64, impact 3.09 of 5); wet-lab validation is three expert-in-the-loop cases [S20] (M/W). AI Scientist-v2's best-first tree: 1 of 3 papers cleared a workshop bar (6.33), no tree ablation, authors rate none main-track ready [S21] (W). An audit of v1: 5 of 12 experiments failed, known ideas labelled novel, hallucinated numbers [S22] (M).
- **Policy vs operators.** With AIDE's operators, MCTS and evolution gave no gain; better operators lifted medal rate 39.8 to 45.5%, MCTS reached 47.7% [S23] (M). Parallel best-of-K with no shared lineage plateaued at the single-GPU evolutionary level; a shared population added 3.1 to 7.5 percentile points [S24] (M).

## 4. Research-idea agents and benchmarks
- **Si 2024** (49 ideas per arm, 79 reviewers): novelty 4.84 human, 5.64 AI, 5.81 AI+human rerank; feasibility 6.61 / 6.34 / 6.44 (n.s.); overall score correlates 0.725 with novelty, 0.097 with feasibility [S1] (S).
- **Execution study** (43 projects, 100+ hours each): AI-idea scores fell -1.05 novelty, -1.76 excitement, -1.88 effectiveness, -1.98 overall; human ideas -0.01, +0.08, -0.05, -0.63. AI fell below human after execution, significant only when each review counts as a datapoint [S2] (S/M).
- **MLRC-Bench:** best agent closes 9.3% of the gap to top humans; adding CoI or human ideas did not help consistently; at fixed budget 4 ideas x 2 trials ~ 1 x 8 [S25] (M). **MLGym:** gains mostly hyperparameters, not new algorithms [S26] (M). **MLR-Bench:** ~80% of coding-agent runs had fabricated or invalid results [S27] (M). **IdeaBench:** novelty insight scores above 0.6 for most models, feasibility below 0.5 for all, GPT-4o ranked [S28] (W). **Nova:** planned retrieval gave over 80% unique ideas, 3.4x more unique novel ones (LLM-ranked) [S29] (W). **RE-Bench:** agents 4x humans at 2 h, humans ahead by 8 h, 2x at 32 h [S30] (M).
- **Novelty vs feasibility.** Reviewers' overall score tracks novelty and excitement, not feasibility [S1]; novelty rises with more references while feasibility plateaus low [S28]; LLM novelty is unrelated to measured performance [S25].

## 5. Judging ideas
- **Accuracy.** Claude-3.5 pairwise: 71.4% on ICLR accept/reject pairs but 53.3% vs human 56.1% on idea ranks [S1]. Off-the-shelf frontier models are near chance at predicting which of two AI-research ideas wins; a judge fine-tuned on 6,000 pairs plus retrieval reached 77% on 1,585 test pairs (NLP subset 64.4% vs 48.9% for 25 experts; 63.6% on unpublished AI ideas) [S31] (M). GPT-5 53% vs a 7B reward model trained on ICLR reviews 72%; its pre-filter wrongly rejected 43 of 151 viable ideas [S32] (M). Same-baseline pairs: 79.5% selective accuracy, falling 82.8% to 56.9% once 3+ successes accumulate (a "confidence cliff"); focused outcome memory 83.5% [S33] (M). An AI reviewer matched humans on whole papers (65% vs 66%), a different object [S34] (M).
- **Biases.** Position bias is strongest when candidates are close in quality [S35]; style bias (0.10 to 0.76) exceeds position (~0.04) [S36] (W); self-preference is uncorrelated with capability across 20 LLMs [S37] (M); LLMs favour LLM-written pitches [S38] (M).
- **Protocols with support.** Judge both orders and count only agreement (run-to-run spread 5.1 to 1.7) [S33]; judge never a generator; six-field contract with a precommitted falsifying result: falsifiability acts as a gate, minimal test and decisive metric separate ideas; humans agreed on 34 of 39 decisive pairs [S39] (W/M); a debate prompt cut positional bias [S20].

## 6. ML-training-code agents: which strategies produced real gains
- **autoresearch** (one editable train.py, 5-minute budget, val_bpb keep/discard) [S40]. Karpathy: ~20 additive changes from ~2 days (~650 experiments) at depth 12, transferred to depth 24, time-to-GPT-2 2.02 to 1.80 h (-11%) [S40] (W: self-report, no ablation).
- **CMU specialists** (1,197 trials): Parameter Golf bpb -0.81%, NanoChat-D12 CORE +38.7%, CIFAR -4.59%. Single 200-trial controls: roles+lineage 16 valid improvements, generic 10-agent 10, single generalist 14, no lineage 3 though roles were kept (then 125 trials without progress; 61.5% vs 19.0% budget-cap hits), so proposal diversity alone did not convert to gains. Effective proposal clusters 134.8 vs 41.1 (identical prompts) vs 61.9. No paradigm-level invention [S41] (M, n=1 per arm).
- **Rehearse** (nanochat, 5 seeds, 100 runs): vanilla 7.1%; propose many, run the first 6.9%; pairwise-select 8.4%; plus focused outcome memory 10.7%; CIFAR 2.85 vs 2.10%; ETTh1 MSE -54.0 vs -40.1% [S33] (M). AutoSOTA logs: helpful modifications 70% to 43% by iteration 6+, mean gain 3.6% to 0.3% [S33,S58].
- **Frontier/population.** GEAR's baseline made 97% of its gain in the first 25 of 100 experiments (0.98232); variants 0.98001 / 0.97914 / 0.97658, one run each [S42] (W). ShinkaEvolve: weighted parent sampling beat hill-climbing (which plateaus); embedding plus LLM novelty rejection "strongly" beat none [S43] (M). DGM 20 to 50% on SWE-bench, archive-free ablation worse [S44] (M). Bilevel: injected bandit/tabu/DOE mechanisms -0.045 +/- 0.030 vs -0.009 +/- 0.002 val_bpb, n=3 [S45] (W). Pilot experiments: preference model with small pilots 0.684 to 0.729 (inference-only 0.711), unguided 24 h score matched in ~15 h [S46] (M); ForeAgent 6x faster convergence [S47] (M).
- **Behaviour.** Iteration count anticorrelated with score over 245 runs; the best agent tried 3 to 7 architecture variants then consolidated; winners each found one unreplicated non-obvious trick [S48] (M). Parallel subagents: 7 improvements in 50 rounds but fixated on MLP ratio 4x to 0.75x; an expert team: 3 improvements, coupled architectural changes [S49] (W). Agents recover under 20% of known NanoGPT speedrun gains without hints [S50] (M). MLE-STAR (ablation-guided refinement, retrieved models) 64% medals [S51]; AIRA2 81.5% vs 72.7% percentile rank [S24]; AIDE+o1-preview 16.9% medals, pass@8 34.1% [S52].
- **Failure modes.** Specification gaming: one agent hard-coded 19 to 41 answers per run; a held-out harness removed it [S53] (M). Drift toward local refinement [S54] (W). "Overfitting" in earlier agent work was evaluation noise, fixed by hidden consistent evaluation [S24] (M). Proposal-embedding dispersion correlated with results in 5 of 8 FML-bench tasks (r 0.40 to 0.84), negatively in 3 [S55] (W).

## 7. What would transfer to an LLM-driven idea loop
**Repo check.** nano/ideate.py already has independent generator contexts, blind clustering, a PREDICTION and falsifying TEST per sketch, cluster-coverage selection with a failure penalty; deepen.py recurses; calibrate.py, compose.py and verdict.py exist. Tags: [new] or [partial]. Quoted effects are analogs from the cited studies (other domains, mostly n of 1 to 5), not predictions for nano. Two tensions: SYNTHESIS.md keeps lineage out of generator prompts (anchoring), yet [S41] credits lineage for most gains; and it bars stated reasons from ranking, yet [S33] loses 13 points when the rationale is removed. Test, do not assume.

1. **Outcome ledger and banlist from round 2 [partial].** Round 1 stays isolated; later sketchers see titles of prior sketches and one-line measured failures per edit family, then "give a different one". Basis: [S10,S41]; Si's agent saw prior titles yet kept 5% non-duplicates over 4,000 [S1], so cap batch size. Expect fewer re-proposed failures. Measure: non-duplicate rate at cosine 0.8 per 100 sketches; share matching a logged failure; A/B isolated vs ledger on effective clusters exp(H), TF-IDF cosine 0.30 [S41].
2. **Two-pass diverge-then-bold [new].** Pass 1: 30 to 100 terse seeds; pass 2: rewrite each bolder and different; pass 3: elaborate survivors into sketches. Basis: [S4,S11]. Expect +50 to 80% category coverage at equal tokens; quality effect unknown. Measure: coverage of a scripted lever taxonomy (optimizer, schedule, init, norm, attention, embeddings, data order, loss, precision, systems); pairwise distance; survival rate of pass-2 vs pass-1 seeds.
3. **Surface roles x ordinary-persona draw, plus an edit-family cap [partial].** One tree node and one short practitioner persona per call; at most ~40% (tunable) of a cycle's builds in one edit family. Basis: [S41,S11,S12,S13,S48,S49,S26]. Expect 2 to 3x effective clusters, fewer hyperparameter-squeeze builds. Measure: clusters per role; edit-family entropy; kept improvements by family.
4. **Far-source retrieval seeding [partial].** Two or three external recipe techniques per context from different sources (speedrun changelogs, papers); keep "half not from the web". Basis: agents recover under 20% of known gains unaided [S50]; human ideas lifted pass@k though mean gains were inconsistent [S25]; [S29,S51]. Measure: share with a verified source; novelty vs corpus; survival sourced vs own.
5. **Verbalized-sampling tail quota [new].** About 20% of calls: 5 sketches with probabilities, keep p below 0.10. Basis: [S9,S57]. Expect more clusters per call, somewhat lower quality, unproven for ideas. Measure: new clusters per call and survival vs the standard arm; stop if survival drops below half.
6. **Novelty gate plus diversity-aware shortlist [partial].** Reject a sketch above cosine 0.8 to any archived sketch or executed candidate (0.95 for diffs) unless an LLM says "meaningfully different"; shortlist by champion-per-cluster plus random-k, not top-k. Basis: [S43,S56]; sampling more without selection gave 6.9 vs 7.1% [S33]. Measure: duplicate rate among builds; clusters executed; best-of-portfolio vs top-k control; human-relative diversity rho against a human recipe pool [S12].
7. **Strict-consensus judging wherever an LLM ranks [new, if a judge is added].** Both orders, count agreements, abstain otherwise; judge is not the author, ideally another family; short mechanism and implementation text kept, persuasion stripped. Basis: [S33,S39,S37,S38]. Expect spread 5.1 to 1.7; accuracy still 53 to 80%. Measure: judge-vs-outcome pairwise accuracy on executed pairs; flip and abstention rates. A prior, never a verdict.
8. **Outcome-calibrated selection with focused memory and pilots [partial].** Extend calibrate.py to pairwise "A beats B from the same baseline", with past attempts within cosine 0.40 (one line each: change plus binary outcome, no reasons); log selective accuracy by promoted baselines; pilot top candidates for one minute. Basis: [S33,S31,S46,S47]. Expect about +2 points endpoint (8.4 to 10.7% analog). Measure: precision@k vs random; cliff curve; pilot-to-full rank correlation.
9. **Recursive pre-mortem with depth cap and false-kill audit [partial].** Fixed questions: what falsifies it, cheapest test, what prior art covers it (real search, not keywords); recurse only on build-relevant open questions, depth 2 to 3; after a failed build ask "method or execution?" and re-queue execution failures; build a random 10% of would-be kills. Basis: [S2,S22,S20,S41,S24,S32]. Measure: false-kill rate; kept improvements from re-queued failures; unsourced-number drops.
10. **Frontier archive with stepping stones [partial].** Keep distinct non-winning checked candidates; sample parents by fitness and few offspring; recombine disjoint changes with compose.py. Basis: [S42,S43,S44,S24]; search policy matters only with good operators [S23]. Expect later-quarter gains (analog: 0.002 to 0.006 bpb better, single runs). Measure: improvement per budget quarter; time to plateau; share of final gain from compositions.
11. **Scoped outcome blackboard [partial].** Builders see current best, budget boundary, last N measured outcomes (diff summary, delta, failure type); drafting sees siblings only, debugging the ancestry; focused, not full, dumps. Basis: 16 vs 3 improvements and 19.0% vs 61.5% cap hits [S41]; scoped memory [S23]; full dump 70.8% vs 83.5% judge accuracy [S33]. Measure: valid-drop rate; budget violations; judge accuracy focused vs full.
12. **Replication and hidden-evaluation guard [partial].** Re-run every promoted change on fresh seeds; keep a hidden metric or slice agents never see; scan logs for hard-coded eval specifics. Basis: [S53,S24,S27,S21]; verdict.py covers seeds. Measure: replication pass rate of kept changes; hidden-vs-visible gap; anomaly count.
