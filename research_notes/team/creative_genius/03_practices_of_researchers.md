# 03. Practices of exceptionally creative researchers: executable moves, evidence, failure modes (2026-10-13)

*For the ambitious lane of the nano loop. Seventeen practices, each reduced to an operational form with grade and failure mode; then 12 operators. Grades follow the series: **H** large controlled or replicated; **M** peer-reviewed data, one study or small n; **L** self-report, essay, case. None reaches H. Tags: [P] primary text opened; [S] abstract or page opened; [R] reported only. The search quota ran out partway; later sources were fetched directly. "Brief NN" = research_notes/team/creative_genius; "note NN" = research_notes/team/idea_generation.*

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Almost everything is survivor self-report (L).** At best M, beyond anecdote: a subtraction cue, surprise handling in labs, failure-tolerant funding, co-location, heuristic and TRIZ training (weak, mixed).
2. **The numbers.** A cue that subtraction was allowed lifted the share listing a subtractive idea from 21% to 48% (n = 369) [14]. HHMI-style "people, not projects" funding went with +39% output, about +96% top-percentile papers and +35% flops, versus early-career prize winners [24]. Co-location made joint work about 3.5x likelier [25].
3. **The stories compress to a few moves:** generalise or specialise; restate; invert or subtract; keep rivals; keep a problem list; measure what nobody measured.
4. **"Ambitious" has a usable definition.** Hamming: important means "you have a reasonable attack", not a big prize [1]. Pólya: the ambitious plan wins "provided it is not based on mere pretension but on some vision of the things beyond" [4]. Each lane idea ships with its attack (rung-3 test, target cell) and is a generalisation, not a larger magnitude.
5. **Expect failure.** Feynman: most ideas of the research "were not ultimately used in the final result" [3]. Darwin: bar Coral Reefs, no first hypothesis stayed unchanged [12]. Track lane yield separately (brief 04).
6. **LLM caveat.** Untested in an LLM loop; plain "wild, outside the usual categories" prompts matched TRIZ, C-K and SCAMPER (note 03). So an operator is an input bundle plus output schema, never a persona, A/B'd against a plain control.

## 2. Practices

| 1 | Standing problem list, Great Thoughts Time (Hamming [1]; Feynman via Rota [2]) | Hold 10-20 problems "for which they are looking for an attack" (Feynman: ~12, dormant); test each new trick or result against each; give Friday afternoons (~10% of time) to what matters. Rota: even Hilbert "had only a few tricks"; name and reuse yours. | L, second-hand for Feynman | Hindsight defines "important"; hits remembered, misses uncounted. |
| 2 | Open door, mixed groups (Hamming; Bell Labs under Kelly [29]) | Work where you meet people; Kelly paired solid-state physicists with chemists, metallurgists, engineers. | L ("I cannot prove the cause and effect"). Proxy M: Jussieu natural experiment, co-location made joint work ~3.5x likelier, better and worse projects [25]. | Co-located labs converge [25]; early peer exposure narrows LLM ideas (note 07). |
| 3 | Reframe (Hamming [1]) | Resolved to "never again solve an isolated problem except as characteristic of a class"; ask what a defect is good for. | L | Generalising before one instance works. |
| 4 | Re-derive, reformulate (Feynman [3]) | Told Dirac's expressions were "analogous", asked "let's see what happens if we make them equal" and found the path-integral link. Keeps several formulations of a theory: "not psychologically identical" for guessing. | L | Equation guessing is "very easy to go off in wildly incorrect and impossible directions". |
| 5 | Heuristics (Pólya [4]) | Restate; solve a related, more general, more special or analogous problem; keep part of the condition; test extreme cases; examine the weakest point; inventor's paradox. | M-L: Schoenfeld 1979, trained group beat controls, small [5]. | Too vague to follow unprompted; generalising can make a problem harder. |
| 6 | Rising sea (Grothendieck [6]) | Do not hammer the nut: build theory until the theorem is "submerged and dissolved" (McLarty: find the problem's natural world, restate it there, let its cohomology solve it). | L; one celebrated case (Weil conjectures) | A few pages became 102; "disproportionate" to many; no intermediate test. |
| 7 | Dumb questions (Tao, after Halmos [7]) | Is each hypothesis necessary? Converse true? Special and degenerate cases? Where is each hypothesis used? Delete one; strengthen the conclusion; try another method. | L | Mostly confirms conventional wisdom (Tao). |
| 8 | Birds and frogs (Dyson [8]) | Birds "bring together diverse problems"; frogs "solve problems one at a time"; "mathematics needs both". Alternate a unifying pass and a detail pass. | L | Types, not moves. |
| 9 | Take the mathematics seriously (Dirac [9]); thought experiments (Einstein; SEP [10]) | Dirac: "perfect and generalise the mathematical formalism", then "interpret the new mathematical features in terms of physical entities". Einstein: imagine an idealised case (sealed elevator: gravity or acceleration?) and look for a contradiction with a principle. | L; Dirac's "holes" first read as protons, then as the anti-electron (positron, 1932); his monopole is unobserved, I believe. Norton: (disguised) arguments. | Right structure, wrong reading; hidden scale effects; intuition taken for evidence. |
| 10 | Notebooks and rates (Edison [11], Darwin [12]) | Edison: "a minor invention every six weeks and a major invention every six months". Darwin: log any fact "opposed to my general results" "without fail and at once". | L, retrospective | Collecting without a hypothesis is slow (15 months to Malthus). |
| 11 | Contrarian truth (Thiel [R]) | "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" State it; build on it. | None | Contrarian is not correct. |
| 12 | Inversion (Jacobi, Munger [13]) | "Man muss immer umkehren": ask for the opposite of the goal; assume failure, list reasons. | L; proxy M: prospective hindsight raised reasons found ~30% (reported by Klein [15]). | Failure lists with no action. |
| 13 | First principles (Aristotle, Musk, Wrights [16][17]) | Musk: "reason from first principles rather than by analogy"; Aristotle: "the first basis from which a thing is known". The Wrights doubted the inherited lift constant (0.0054; theirs ~0.0033) and built a wind tunnel. | L; one case | Discards tacit knowledge; label overstretched. |
| 14 | TRIZ (Altshuller) | Name the contradiction (improve A, worsen B), apply a separation principle, aim at the ideal final result. | M-L: 3 universities, novelty p = .0004, .0145, .58; quantity lower, n.s. [18]. fNIRS N = 32, fluency fell; ideality untested (note 03). | Fewer ideas; the contradiction is the weak step in LLM pipelines. |
| 15 | Strong inference (Platt [19]) | Alternative hypotheses; a crucial experiment excluding some; clean result; recycle; 30-60 minutes daily on the written logical tree. Ask "what experiment could disprove your hypothesis?" | L: no outcome data. Only 21 of 100 sampled studies tested more than one hypothesis [20]. | Rivals cannot always be listed; the "ruling theory". |
| 16 | Compression progress (Schmidhuber [22]) | Seek data whose regularity is unknown but learnable; interest is the steepness of the learning curve. | M: infants (N = 42, 30) looked away from very predictable and very surprising events [23]. | Noise reads as surprise (brief 07). |
| 17 | HHMI, ARPA, PARC | "People, not projects", tolerate early failure [24]; ARPA program directors choose and manage projects [26]; Heilmeier's questions end in "midterm and final exams" [27]; PARC Dealer: argue "to illuminate rather than merely to win" [28]. | M for HHMI (matched, difference-in-differences). ARPA paper: no effectiveness evidence; Heilmeier attribution unconfirmed; Dealer is Kay's recollection. | Stars are selected; failure tolerance buys flops. |

## 3. Operators for an LLM idea lane

Inputs: **SCRIPT** = train.py, the part's region and assumptions. **DIAG** = the measured one-pager (loss curve, loss by position and token frequency, per-layer gradient, activation, attention sharpness, noise floor). **BRIEF** = the part's research brief. **RULES** = fixed rules (same data and budget, +10% size, 2x step time, one GPU). **LEDGER** = past builds, predictions versus outcomes. Each operator is a card: addendum plus context bundle. Ranked evidence first, fit second. Tail tags: evidence grade; fit; reach (my inference); loop status against nano/loop.py cards.

1. **Subtract first** [14][7][16]. *Do:* for each component of your region write "remove it, or freeze it at identity or zero"; say what DIAG shows it buys; keep the two removals you can defend and spend the freed parameters or steps on one named alternative. *In:* SCRIPT, DIAG, RULES. *Out:* ranked deletions with predicted cell change; one delete-and-reinvest sketch. M; fit high; reach far if structural; loop new.
2. **Surprise triage** [21][19]. *Do:* for each LEDGER row whose outcome misses its prediction by more than the noise floor, and each DIAG cell no idea explains, write three rival explanations, the 45-step run that separates them, and the idea that follows if the least popular rival is true; replicate a z > 2 surprise first. *In:* LEDGER, DIAG. *Out:* rival table, run, derived sketch. M; high; near-mid; new (mechanisms in brief 07).
3. **Class, then special case** [1][4][6]. *Do:* state the idea as one member of a family (scalar, per-layer vector, learned function of a diagnostic; one layer, all layers; one token class, frequency-conditioned) with identity at initialisation; name the cheapest member to build and one nobody has listed. *In:* SCRIPT, RULES. *Out:* family axes, build member, queued member. M-L; high; mid; new (identity-at-init only an arm).
4. **Invert** [13][15]. *Do:* write the recipe that guarantees the worst validation loss at this budget; for each of its five ingredients name the nearest one in SCRIPT and propose the negation; then assume a verified winner and name the baseline assumption it broke. *In:* SCRIPT, DIAG. *Out:* five negations as sketches. L, proxy M; medium; far; partial (transform card flips one assumption; pre-mortem runs after selection).
5. **Separate the trade-off, make the defect an asset** [18][1]. *Do:* take one measured trade-off (loss down, but time, memory or variance up; or one layer's gradient far from the rest); keep the gain and drop the cost by separating in time (schedule), space (layers, heads) or condition (token class, phase); then ask what the defect is good for. *In:* LEDGER, DIAG. *Out:* three separation sketches, one defect-as-feature sketch. M-L; high; mid; have (contradiction card, rounds 2+; diagnosis card, round 1); add the defect-as-asset question.
6. **Problems-by-tricks grid** [2][1]. *Do:* keep at most 12 problems (worst or oddest DIAG cells) and 10 tricks (winners in LEDGER or BRIEF); test each BRIEF technique against all 12; emit the three best-mechanism pairs, adapted to RULES (zero-init, last-k layers, gate), preferring techniques from another part or field. *In:* DIAG, BRIEF, LEDGER. *Out:* (technique, problem, mechanism, adaptation) sketches. L; ports and recombinations dominate real gains (note 00); high; near; partial (adapt card: three techniques, no grid).
7. **Rivals and a contradiction log** [19][12]. *Do:* list three mechanisms that would produce your predicted gain, the diagnostic that separates them and the outcome that kills each; log the strongest fact in BRIEF or LEDGER against your favourite. *In:* BRIEF, LEDGER, DIAG. *Out:* rival table, one-line contradiction log in the sketch. L; high; near (sharpens, no ambition gain); partial.
8. **Re-represent** [3][4]. *Do:* rewrite the training step in three equivalent forms (optimiser as steepest descent under a norm; attention as a kernel smoother; residual stream as an ODE; loss as code length); per form list one edit natural there and awkward elsewhere; where a source says X "resembles" Y, set them equal and propose the missing term. *In:* SCRIPT, BRIEF. *Out:* forms, edits to named script lines, one residual. L, proxy M (comparing two analogues doubles transfer, note 02); medium; far; new.
9. **Re-measure an inherited constant** [17][16]. *Do:* list the hand-set constants in your region (rates, betas, warmup, init scale, clipping, softcap, rotary base); for the two inherited from another scale, derive or measure the value at ours from DIAG and propose a schedule or learned replacement. *In:* SCRIPT, DIAG. *Out:* provenance list, measurement, replacement. L; high; near (tag "ground"); new.
10. **Gate card** [27][28][24]. *Do:* before a build, answer Heilmeier's questions (goal in plain words; state of the art and its limit; what is new; risks and payoffs; cost and time; midterm and final exams as rung-3 and rung-4 predictions with numbers); a second agent sees only the card and attacks it. *In:* sketch, RULES, BRIEF. *Out:* card, attack, build, revise or drop. L-M; high; a gate, not a generator; partial.
11. **Limit case** [10][9][4]. *Do:* push one equation in your region to a limit (width to infinity, depth 1, one-token context, learning rate 0, vocabulary 2) or follow an "unphysical" consequence; say what breaks first and which DIAG cell shows it; if the baseline already shows the symptom, propose the fix. *In:* SCRIPT, DIAG. *Out:* argument, predicted cell, sketch. L; medium; far; new.
12. **One anchored contrarian claim** [R]. *Do:* state one belief about this recipe most practitioners reject; name the one DIAG or LEDGER number that supports it; derive the change; drop it if no number exists. *In:* DIAG, BRIEF. *Out:* claim, anchor, change. None; low-medium; far; new.

**Use.** Default lane: operators 1-6, each agent taking two plus one of 8, 11, 12 (far-reach quota); 7, 9, 10 are hygiene on every sketch. Hamming's open door becomes late exposure to LEDGER and BRIEF, not peers' sketches (independent generation beats interaction, note 00). Score operators by new-cluster share, rung-3 pass rate and verified wins per build; retire one after 8 builds with no hit (note 03).
