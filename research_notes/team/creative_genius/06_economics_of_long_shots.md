## Bottom line

1. **The value sits in the tail.** In eight datasets of inventions the top 10% of cases took 48-93% of returns [1]; Bing's top 2% of ideas gave 74.8% of gains, tail index 1.31 [2]; over half of even the best VCs' investments fail while a few produce most returns [3]; 4.3% of US stocks explain all net wealth creation [4]. Citations are thinner: lognormal-like, power-law index 3.2 above 160 citations [5][6]. Count hits, maxima and upper quantiles, never mean yield.
2. **Tolerance for failure buys tail and flops together.** HHMI appointees against early-career prize winners: +96% papers in the top percentile, +35% flops [7].
3. **Novelty loses at the cheapest gate and wins late.** One SD more novelty cut a proposal's rank by 4.5 percentile points [8], and the ERC penalty arose at the first screen [9]; highly novel papers trail for 3 years, then lead (+39.8% odds of top 1% at 14 years) [10].
4. **No evidence fixes a share.** The 70/20/10 rule is traced to a consultancy study [21], not an experiment. Sizing follows from success probability p, payoff V and cost c (Weitzman index V - c/p) [11] and from sampling arithmetic: at p = 0.05, three lane builds (one run) give a 14% chance of one hit, 45 give 90%.
5. **"Conventional core plus atypical link" raises the hit rate but is not where most hits come from:** 6.7% of papers, about 12% of top-5% papers (my arithmetic from [12]).
6. Judge the lane separately, by counts. Kill ideas on mechanism evidence and budget, not the lane on a failure streak: one failure moves the odds by only 1.07x.

## 1. Evidence

**Tails.** Citations rescale to one lognormal, variance 1.3, so the top 10% of papers hold about 44% of citations (arithmetic mine) [6]; above 160 citations a power law with index 3.2 fits, and a lognormal fits as well [5]. Innovation values are heavier: about two thirds of 21,000+ financings returned less than capital and about 4% returned 10x or more [16]; Bing's index of 1.31 means infinite variance [2]. Our only domain distribution, accepted speedrun records, has median 1.1%, mean 1.9%, maximum 10.4% of wall time (brief 12), roughly lognormal with sigma near 1: a long-shot win is worth perhaps 3-10x a median win (inference; survivors only).

**Careers: equal odds and streaks.** The top paper falls at a uniformly random position (random-impact rule); impact is Q x p, with Q stable per scientist (lognormal, variance 0.46) and luck p (variance 0.95), and "increased productivity alone cannot substantially enhance the chance of a very high impact work" [13] (H for the rule, M for Q). Simonton's equal-odds baseline is the same claim [28]. Hits still cluster: 82-91% of careers hold a hot streak, typically 3.7-5.7 years, without higher output [14], preceded by exploration followed by exploitation, not by either alone [15] (M).

**Uzzi's pattern.** In 17.9M papers the best cell (conventional core, atypical tail) hit 9.11 per 100 against 5.82 for conventional only, 1.57x; it holds 6.7% of papers, so about 12% of hits, and most hits sit elsewhere (cell shares from the Fig. 2 caption, arithmetic mine) [12] (H as association).

**Failure tolerance.** HHMI funds people, tolerates early failure and rewards long-run success. Against matched early-career prize winners (73 investigators) the effect grows with the citation quantile: +39% papers, +55% top-5%, +96% top-1%, and +35% papers below the investigator's own least-cited earlier work, with more new keywords [7] (M; selection not fully excluded). A lab experiment found tolerance plus long-run reward raised exploration and discovery of a novel strategy against fixed pay or pay-for-performance [17] (M). Innovative papers hit more often, but the extra reward does not offset the risk of failing to publish [18]: risk pays in the tail, roughly neutral in the mean.

**The novelty paradox.** For 2001 papers over 14 years, highly novel papers had 39.8% higher odds of top 1% (moderately novel 11.2%), 16.8% higher odds of the top decile and 16.8% higher odds of the bottom decile: both tails fatten [10]. They trail for 3 years, sit in lower-impact journals, and at 15 years are nearly twice as likely to be top 1% [9]. Reviewers (142 evaluators, 150 proposals, 2,130 randomised pairs) ranked proposals 4.5 percentile points lower per SD of novelty, an information effect rather than a strategic one, and also scored proposals near their own expertise lower [8] (M-H). ERC applicants with highly novel records were less likely to be funded, mostly at the stage-one screen [9]. In 9,661 contests more rivals cut each one's effort but raise the chance of an extreme solution, and for uncertain problems the net effect is positive [19]: breadth beats depth for long shots (inference).

**Organisations and portfolio rules.** March: improving exploitation faster than exploration pays now and turns self-destructive later [20]. Firm surveys are reported to link balanced ambidexterity to growth (He and Wong: exploratory-exploitative interaction raises sales growth, imbalance lowers it; Junni et al.'s meta-analysis is also positive) [22] (L: cross-sectional, recalled from abstracts, not opened). I found no outcome study of any fixed share; 70/20/10 (core/adjacent/transformational, often attributed to Google) is traced to Nagji and Tuff's study of outperformers, known to me only secondhand [21]. ARPA rules are staging, not shares: 3-5-year managers with discretion; about half of ARPA-E's projects were funded from below the score cutoff a pure ranking would use; milestones expand good projects and terminate misses; "many failures can be justified by a single success", and one-in-a-thousand ideas cannot be measured on a political timescale [23] (L-M). NSF's SGER let officers fund risky work without review and they used a small share of the funds [9]: optional risk pots go unspent. Cheaper tests raise the long-shot share: after cloud computing cut start-up costs, VCs made more, smaller bets, with more failures and 15-20% larger step-ups [24]. Biomedical chemists' strategy is over 3x costlier than the most efficient one, and the efficient one turns riskier as a field matures [25].

**Theory (L).** Weitzman: the index s solves E[(X - s)+] = c; open boxes in descending index; stop when the best prize in hand beats every remaining index [11]. At equal mean and cost, more spread means a higher index, so riskier boxes go first. My algebra: a long shot (p, V, c) has s = V - c/p, a safe win g at cost c has s = g - c, so the long shot goes first only if V > g + c(1/p - 1). At p = 0.05 and a safe win worth 3 builds, V must exceed 22 build-costs (7x a safe win); at p = 0.10, 4x. At Bing, with index below 3, the best policy tests many ideas lightly and shrinks small t-statistics hard [2]. With only (1+e) moments, the pulls needed to separate arms at gap D scale as D^-(1+e)/e, unavoidably [26]: D^-4 or steeper at Bing's index. Kelly: f* = p - (1-p)/(W-1) gives 1.7% (p .05, W 30) to 5.3% (p .10, W 20) per independent shot [27]; it maximises log wealth, not best-of-N, so read it as diversification arithmetic: a 20% lane is 4-12 independent mechanisms, not variants of one.

## 2. Sizing and judging the ambitious lane

Loop today: about 30 agents write 5-8 sketches each; round one has 10 builds including a 20% lane (2 builds) with 3x looser rung-3 tolerance; rung 4 takes 5 minutes, an overnight run 31.

**R1. Quota per agent: about a quarter of each agent's 5-8 sketches, never fewer than 2, tagged AMBITIOUS; the rest SAFE.** AMBITIOUS: drops or flips a named assumption, states p (at most 0.2) and upside (at least 3x a typical safe win), and passes p x V > c. SAFE: a port or adaptation with p of at least 0.3. *Evidence:* bias against risk enters at every layer [8][9][10] and optional risk pots go unspent [9], so the floor must be mandatory. Equal odds [13][28] means expected hits grow linearly with attempts and a dry spell does not lower the next attempt's odds, so hold the quota through failures; but Q multiplies, so attempts cannot replace implementation skill (fidelity checks, repair). LLM samples collapse (200 unique ideas in 4,000, brief 11), so the lane needs several times more distinct clusters than slots, which a quota above the 20% build share supplies. *Uncertainty:* no study tests quotas; the quarter is judgement between the lane's 20% and the 10% rule of thumb; stated p is a label only (self-confidence predicts failure at about chance, thinking_parts/00_IDEAS.md).

**R2. Build slots: keep the 20% lane but make 3 lane builds per run a floor (today 2 of 10 in round one and 1 of 4 in round two, which rounding can cut to 0); reserve 1 in 5 overnight slots for it; move within 10-35% on pooled yields.** *Evidence:* with illustrative p 0.04, V 7 safe wins, cost 1.5 for the lane against p 0.15, V 1, cost 1 for safe, expected value per cost is 0.19 against 0.15 (inference): no dominance, so the share buys tail insurance and information, not expected value. Three lane builds give a 14% chance of a hit at p 0.05, so the lane is an option pooled across runs. The scarce stage is overnight (a verified win costs at least 93 GPU-minutes), so the share applies there. The optimum rises as safe yield decays [25] and as tests get cheaper [24]: add 5 points when safe yield per GPU-minute halves. *Uncertainty:* p and V for the lane are unmeasured; nothing separates 20% from 10% or 30%.

**R3. Gate leniency: for lane builds replace the rung-3 gap test (tolerance 3x today) with "it runs, loss falls, the named diagnostic moves"; run rung 4 on every lane survivor; send the best lane candidate overnight even if it ranks below safe ones (the reserved slot).** *Evidence:* a gate pays only if minutes saved exceed false-reject rate x p x V. If it rejects 60% of lane builds it saves about 3 minutes per build; with p x V = 0.28 safe wins, a 10% false-reject rate costs 0.028 safe wins, worth more than 3 minutes whenever a safe win is worth over about 100 GPU-minutes, and verifying one takes at least 93 (inference). False rejects of novelty are documented: delayed recognition [10], penalty at the cheapest screen [9], ARPA-E's half below cutoff [23]; staging suits tests that are cheap and quickly updated [9]. *Uncertainty:* nobody has measured rung-to-final rank correlation for novel ideas; the hedge lane (brief 11) does, and if it shows under 10% false rejects, tighten. Neither the current 3x tolerance nor its removal has direct evidence.

**R4. Yield accounting: a separate ledger, counts and quantiles, never means.** Columns: verified wins, maximum gain, stackable and near-miss counts, mechanism-valid rate (the diagnostic moved the named quantity), distinct clusters, GPU-minutes. On a one-day horizon expect 0-1 lane hits, so report the lane by near-misses and mechanism-valid builds. Compare lanes on continuous rung-4 extrapolated z (upper quartile, 95th percentile, bootstrap), since binary outcomes need about 190 builds per arm (80% power) to separate 10% from 3%; log selection propensities. Do not rank agents by hits: at p 0.04 an agent with 10 attempts expects 0.4 hits and 66% see none [13]; rank on process metrics. *Evidence:* failures must not count against a program [1]; rare outcomes cannot be measured in time [23]; sample means mislead at an index near 1.3 [26]. *Uncertainty:* moderate; the near-miss definition is mine.

**R5. Stop rule.** (a) Idea: one build plus one repair, then kill unless the diagnostic shows the mechanism inactive (a bug, not a verdict); 42% of AI-scientist experiments failed on coding errors (brief 05). Retire a cluster after two independent implementations both fail rung 4 with a flat or falling trend, as ARPA terminates on missed milestones [23]. (b) Lane: never stop on a streak; between p 0.02 and 0.08, 11 failures are needed to move the odds 2x, and zero hits in 30 builds only bounds p below 10% (100 builds: 3%). Review at 40+ pooled lane builds: shrink to 10% if the mechanism-valid rate is under 50% or near-misses run under a third of the safe lane's; cap at 35% of GPU-minutes. (c) Night: stop adding lane candidates once the best verified gain exceeds the largest remaining V - c/p [11]. (d) After a lane win, give its cluster 2 follow-up builds outside the quota: exploration then exploitation precedes streaks [15] (analogy). *Uncertainty:* thresholds are judgement; Pandora assumes independent boxes and known distributions [11].

## 3. Caveats

Designs are observational and survivor-filtered; the step from science funding to a training loop is analogy; no study measures LLM-agent long-shot rates; p and V for the lane are unmeasured, so every loop number above is a starting value to be revised by the ledger.
