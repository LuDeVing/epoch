## evaluation

| ID | Mechanism | Claim | Evidence | Maps to | Agent translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EVL-01 | ANTI: Accept ideas on felt certainty | The Aha feeling can be induced for wrong answers by semantic priming plus visual similarity (N=300). It inflates truth ratings of unrelated false statements (d=0.63, preregistered N=300), and susceptibility is unrelated to cognitive-style traits (N=200). Simple warnings do not reduce false insights, and a detailed explanation of the manipulation gives only a small reduction (Grimmer 2023). In MEG data, high confidence amplified processing of confirmatory post-decision evidence and suppressed disconfirmatory evidence, so changes of mind became less likely (Rollwage 2020). | replicated | insight-check | /insight-check treats high evaluator confidence as a trigger for an external test (code execution, counterexample search, python simulation), never as a pass. The safeguard is built into the structure, not into prompt warnings: SKILL.md lines such as 'beware of plausible but wrong ideas' are not counted as protection. A falsifier subagent receives the candidate with no confidence score and no evaluator rationale. It must list concrete evidence or test cases against the idea before giving any judgement, and the number of required counterexample attempts scales with the evaluator's confidence. Prime check: if an idea closely resembles a cue in problem.md or a seed, by words or by embedding, it is flagged as a possible primed false insight and needs an independent verifier that never saw that seed. All pass/fail outcomes go to insight.log. Harness ablation: warning-only vs tool-verified, compared on false-insight rate. |
| EVL-02 | ANTI: Trusting imagined simulation as verification | Human mental simulation is incomplete and not probabilistically coherent. People commit conjunction fallacies on collision outcomes, miss changes to blocks after a tower collapses, and judge arrival order below chance in 2-second videos; about 40-50% hold persistent physics misconceptions. Theory from active inference adds that understanding requires testing models through action whose outcomes can be observed. The best tests are the ones whose outcomes most sharply discriminate between rival models (Friston 2025). Where no test can be run, a claim can be partly justified by showing it is derivable from established results (Nickles). | replicated | insight-check | /insight-check never accepts a subagent's narrated 'I simulated it and it works' as verification. It requires an external result: executed python, a solver, a unit test, a fact found on the web, or an explicit counterexample search. insight.log records the verification type. When two or more shortlisted ideas compete, a subagent designs the cheapest concrete test whose predicted outcome differs most between them and runs it. For ideas with no executable check, a fresh subagent tries to reconstruct the idea step by step as a derivation from accepted facts in problem.md and from cited sources, and each step is checked with tools or sources. If neither route succeeds, the idea is marked 'unverified' and cannot reach 'solved'. /evaluate ranks ideas whose predictions were checked against tool output above ideas judged only by reading. Use weak appraisal during forage loops and strong testing only at the end. |
| EVL-03 | ANTI: Authors self-review or self-score their ideas | Deliberate reasoning shows strong myside bias and is lazy when it judges its own reasons: 'by and large, solitary reasoning doesn't correct mistaken intuitions', though it evaluates other people's arguments much better (Mercier & Sperber). Self-assessed creativity correlates only about 0.1-0.3 with rated performance (N=425; preregistered N=317). The apparent Dunning-Kruger pattern is mostly regression to the mean, and people underestimate their own originality and are swayed by serial position and fluency. | replicated | evaluate | Generators never score or 'double-check' their own ideas as a quality gate, and ideas.jsonl gets no generator-confidence field. Critique always runs in a fresh subagent that receives the ideas as unattributed text, consistent with P1. Before evaluation, a script shuffles idea order and rewrites each idea to one sentence, removing serial-position and fluency cues. Harness ablation: same-context self-critique vs fresh-context critique, to measure the gap for the model. |
| EVL-04 | Distance and abstract construal improve idea selection | Creators forecast the success of others' novel ideas more accurately than managers did, but not of their own (339 circus professionals, checked against 13,248 audience members). Greater psychological distance raised selection of optimal ideas via construal level (four studies), and unaided selection is often near chance. Across five experiments, people under-ranked their highest-potential initial idea: the one they ranked second was often best after development. Forecasting at a more abstract construal level improved identification of the best idea. | replicated | evaluate | Cross-evaluation: each forage batch is scored by a different fresh subagent that has also generated on the same problem, never by its own author, after a script strips seed and mode labels. The judging prompt sets abstract construal ('state the general principle the idea embodies, then judge whether it would work for this goal in general'), not a persona. /evaluate does not pick one winner from raw ideas. It returns the top 3, /think spawns one development subagent per idea (elaborate, make concrete, design a test), and the final selection sees only the developed versions. Harness ablations: cross-scoring vs one neutral evaluator, and select-then-develop vs develop-top-3-then-select. |
| EVL-05 | Causal judgment via counterfactual simulation | Causal judgments are predicted by comparing the actual outcome with the outcome simulated under an intervention that removes the candidate cause. Across 18 collision animations, causal and counterfactual ratings correlated at r = 0.96, and causal questions alone triggered counterfactual eye movements. | replicated | evaluate | In /evaluate, a fresh subagent names each shortlisted idea's key causal component. It then states the outcome with that component removed, and with it replaced by the default-schema alternative. If removing the component does not change the outcome, the idea is scored as decoration, not cause. Where possible the counterfactual runs as a python script instead of being imagined (see EVL-02). Output: a counterfactual_delta field in evaluations.jsonl. |
| EVL-06 | Conventional core plus atypical combination | In 17.9M papers, the highest-impact work combined highly conventional pairings of prior work with an intrusion of unusual pairings, and such papers were twice as likely to be highly cited. Teams were 37.7% likelier to insert novel combinations. Surprising combinations of content and context predicted top-10% impact. | replicated | evaluate | /evaluate breaks each idea into its component concepts and scores each pair by embedding similarity, with high similarity counting as conventional. It prefers ideas whose pairs are mostly conventional plus one or two rare pairs, flags all-conventional ideas as obvious, and flags all-rare ideas as unintegrated. This combination profile complements the single distance window of EVL-09 (P4). |
| EVL-07 | Insight-labelled solutions are more often correct | Self-reported insight solutions are more accurate than analytic ones: 78% vs 42% correct on fragmented pictures (Salvi 2016). In Becker 2025, 65.3% of correct Mooney solutions were high-insight vs 34.7% low-insight, and this replicated in a preregistered control. | replicated | insight-check | In /evaluate, record for each idea a suddenness flag (no earlier idea in its cluster led to it) and the evaluator's confidence. The combined flag is used only as a prior for verification order: flagged ideas are verified first, and verification is never skipped (EVL-01). The harness logs whether flagged ideas pass verification more often than unflagged ones, and the flag is dropped if they do not. |
| EVL-08 | Calibrated rubric judge over raw distance | On 4,589 metaphors from 1,546 participants, fine-tuned RoBERTa (r=.72) and GPT-2 (r=.70) predicted held-out human creativity ratings far better than semantic distance did (r=.42), and they generalized to untrained prompts. Remoteness is necessary but not sufficient. Boden argues that AI will find evaluating ideas harder than generating them. Runco et al. found that people who evaluate ideas well also tend to generate them well. | single-study | evaluate | Spend the build effort on /evaluate first, and never rank on embedding distance alone. A fresh evaluator subagent scores each idea against a rubric with anchored examples. Distance stays as a gate (EVL-09), and the rubric score drives the ranking. Before any generator is tuned, the harness checks evaluator scores against human ratings on a held-out public dataset (AUT or metaphor ratings) and on a rubric-scored subset of the custom benchmark, and reports the rank correlation. Whenever possible, evaluator scores are backed by executable checks rather than LLM judgement alone. |
| EVL-09 | Semantic distance sweet spot, calibrated per family | In 3,448 AUT, 2,430 question-asking and 4,376 metaphor responses, generalized additive models showed that human creativity ratings rise with cue-response semantic distance only up to about cosine distance 0.9-1.0. Past that point more distance adds nothing, plausibly because ideas lose coherence or relevance. A rational analysis of curiosity argues that the best level of uncertainty depends on the environment's causal structure, so the window should not be fixed globally. | single-study | evaluate | /evaluate embeds the reference (the problem cue and the default-schema solutions) and each idea with one fixed embedding model, computes 1-cosine in python, and stores it in evaluations.jsonl as a gating feature, not a target to maximise. The distance reward is capped at the calibrated ceiling. Ideas past the ceiling get no distance credit and must pass an explicit integration check against the constraints in problem.md (EVL-11). Windows are stored per problem family in memory/semantic/window.json. The harness fits a GAM of rubric score on distance for each embedding model and benchmark (MacGyver, BRAINTEASER, design set), and /encode loads the window for the matched family. |
| EVL-10 | Source and reality monitoring of premises | Imagined and perceived signals intermix on a single axis. A reality judgment depends on whether the combined signal crosses a threshold, so strong enough imagery becomes indistinguishable from reality (Dijkstra & Fleming 2023). Divergent-thinking scores predict false recognition and false recall, because the same constructive recombination that produces ideas also produces confabulated details (Thakral 2021). Higher-order theories (Lau's Perceptual Reality Monitoring; Shea & Frith) hold that representations need metacognitive source and confidence tags for flexible control. | single-study | insight-check | Every claim and idea written to the workspace carries tags: source (generated, inferred, retrieved from memory/semantic, problem statement, or web), verification status (naming the check), and confidence (0-1 with a justification). /insight-check extracts each shortlisted idea's factual premises (material properties, precedents, cited mechanisms). A fresh subagent with web and verification tools sorts them into verified, unverified or likely confabulated, and searches for prior art. It writes {premise_status, prior_art_url_or_none} to insight.log, logging confabulation separately from false insight. A python check blocks a 'solved' verdict if the winning idea rests on any untagged or merely generated premise. High-novelty, low-confidence ideas go to verification instead of the shortlist. The harness checks the confidence tags against outcomes. |
| EVL-11 | Integration: judge ideas against the whole problem | In 42 participants, whole-brain integration was higher during evaluation than during generation (p < 0.001) across executive, default and salience networks. Network reconfiguration was higher for creative generation (p = 0.047) and was absent during evaluation. On high-insight trials, an 18-node solution network became more integrated (connectivity p-FWE=0.009, higher clustering, shorter path length). Boden argues that a combination must have a point, and the PET FISH problem shows that combined concepts have emergent properties that neither part has. | single-study | evaluate | Keep generators narrow (one seed, a fresh context each), and give the evaluator the integrated view: problem.md, every constraint and every generator's ideas in a single context. For each idea, evaluations.jsonl lists the constraints and resources it engages. Integration score = fraction of hard constraints addressed, plus a bonus when the idea connects previously disconnected constraint clusters in an idea graph built with networkx, where nodes are ideas and constraints and edges are evaluator-asserted links with stated reasons. For combination ideas, the generator must state the point (which constraint the combination resolves that neither part resolves alone) and list positive and negative emergent properties. Combinations with no stated point are rejected, and negative emergent properties are checked against the hard constraints. |
| EVL-12 | Empowerment: prefer ideas that open more options | In Little Alchemy 2 (N=29,493 players), people were intrinsically motivated to create objects that let them create more objects. The drive disappeared in a variant without recognisable semantics, so it depends on world knowledge of what combinations afford. | single-study | evaluate | Add an 'options opened' score to /evaluate. For each idea, a fresh subagent lists the concrete follow-up ideas or sub-solutions it makes possible, and a python script counts the distinct ones after embedding dedup (similarity >0.85). On a 'promising' verdict, /think seeds the next /forage pass from high-empowerment ideas as well as top-effectiveness ones. |
| EVL-13 | Domain expertise grounds effectiveness judgments | In a comparison of 115 mathematics experts with 109 matched novices, experts had higher mathematical creativity and better memory for structured domain material, but no higher domain-general creativity. In another study, only experts could predict a creator's creativity from the product, while beauty judgments did not differ between groups. | single-study | evaluate | Give /evaluate domain-grounding material: web-fetched references, known solutions and domain checks gathered by /encode into runs/<id>/domain.md. Effectiveness scoring then uses domain knowledge rather than generic plausibility. Keep the domain material away from the persistent /forage agents' first half (P5), so knowledge supports judging more than it narrows generation. |
| EVL-14 | Smooth fluency trades originality for quantity | In N=400 on the AUT, the smooth-performance component of flow increased idea count but lowered creativity, especially when conventional associations had to be inhibited. The absorption component was linked to higher creativity and greater semantic distance. | single-study | evaluate | /evaluate and the harness never reward idea count. A python step computes each generator's fluency (ideas per 1k tokens) and mean embedding distance from the default-schema solutions. Generators with high count and low distance are flagged 'smooth-conventional', and their share of the shortlist is capped. The persistent /forage mode stands in for absorption. |
| EVL-15 | Bad lot: rank plus absolute adequacy | Inference to the best explanation can return 'the best of a bad lot'. Whether the best explanation is among the hypotheses considered is a question about generation, and the winner must also be 'good enough' (Lipton 1993). Adding a catch-all negation does not help. | theory | evaluate | /evaluate scores each idea against an absolute bar written in problem.md before generation starts (the minimum that would count as solving the problem), not only relative to the other ideas. If no idea clears the bar, the verdict is 'impasse: bad lot' even if one idea ranks highest. The orchestrator then returns to generation or /restructure instead of polishing the top idea. |
| EVL-16 | Stated reasons are post hoc rationalisations | Mercier & Sperber, against Boghossian's Taking Condition: representing the premises as good reasons is usually a result of moving to the conclusion, often a rationalisation, rather than its cause. Reasons are generated quickly, with no insight into why they were chosen. | theory | evaluate | Treat any rationale a generator attaches to an idea as non-evidential. Strip ideas.jsonl entries down to {id, text, testable_claim} before /evaluate sees them. /insight-check verifies the idea's claim directly (a test, a simulation, a counterexample search) and never grades how persuasive the generator's explanation is. |
| EVL-17 | MemiC: novelty gate then effectiveness | The MemiC framework splits ideation into goal-directed memory search, candidate construction, novelty evaluation, then effectiveness evaluation. It argues creative ideas usually come from controlled retrieval and construction over semantic and episodic memory, not from spontaneous emergence. | theory | evaluate | Run /evaluate as two gated passes, each in a fresh subagent. Pass 1 scores novelty only (embedding distance within the EVL-09 window plus a novelty judgment) and drops ideas outside the window. Pass 2 scores effectiveness and testability on the survivors only, without seeing the novelty scores. Optionally, a construction step before evaluation combines raw fragments in ideas.jsonl into complete candidates. |
| EVL-18 | Robustness under problem perturbation | In a computational model, NREM-like replay of single episodes with occlusion improved representational robustness. Removing NREM lowered linear separability on occluded inputs, while performance on unoccluded inputs stayed the same or improved. | theory | evaluate | Add a robustness check to /evaluate. A python script makes 3 perturbed variants of problem.md: one constraint dropped, one resource removed, one set of quantities changed. A fresh subagent checks each shortlisted idea against every variant. Ideas that work only for the exact original wording are flagged as brittle in evaluations.jsonl. |

### Sources and caveats

- **EVL-01**
  - key_sources: Grimmer 2022, https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/eliciting-false-insights-with-semantic-priming/; Laukkonen 2020, https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-dark-side-of-Eureka-Artificially/991013062313802368; Grimmer 2023, https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-illusion-of-insight-detailed-warnings/991013093873602368; Rollwage et al. 2020, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7250867/
  - caveats: Human anagram and perceptual paradigms; false insights felt weaker than true ones. The warnings result comes from one paper, and the Rollwage result from one perceptual MEG study. In related diagnostic work, falsification framing had tiny effects on confidence (Kojima 2025: 5.20 to 4.98 on a 10-point scale) and changed next steps rather than verdicts (Lenz 2026). LLM overconfidence and LLM responses to instructions come from different mechanisms, so the harness should test warnings directly. The design conclusion (verify externally) still holds.
  - merged_from: ANTI: Accept ideas on felt certainty [insight_and_aha_moments]; ANTI: Prompt warnings to prevent false insights [insight_and_aha_moments]; High confidence suppresses disconfirming evidence [reasoning_metacognition_and_working_memory]

The simulation errors themselves are replicated, but defenders read them as efficient partial simulation, so the dispute is over interpretation rather than the data. The intervention, discriminating-test and derivation parts are theory (an arXiv toy simulation and philosophical arguments). LLM derivations can look plausible and still be wrong. Many open-ended design problems have no executable check. The rule that every /think pass ends in an observable action is a control decision, not an evaluation one.
