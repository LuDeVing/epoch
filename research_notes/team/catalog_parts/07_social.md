## social

| ID | Mechanism | Claim | Evidence | Maps to | Agent translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOC-01 | Generate independently, then pool (nominal groups) | Interacting brainstorming groups produce fewer and worse ideas than nominal groups (individuals working alone, outputs pooled), and the loss grows with group size (Mullen 1991 meta-analysis). Electronic brainstorming reverses this only for large groups, with a crossover near 10 members (DeRosa 2007). | meta-analysis | forage | /forage subagents run in parallel with fresh contexts and write to separate files (ideas/<agent>.jsonl). None reads another's output until /evaluate merges them. Only if fan-out exceeds about 10 agents should the harness test a shared, append-only ideas.jsonl that later agents may read between rounds. Ablation: isolated vs shared-visibility generators at fan-out 4 and 12. |
| SOC-02 | Deep-level diversity matters, demographic diversity does not | Team diversity has only a small ('insubstantial') average link to performance, larger for creative and complex tasks (registered-report meta-analysis). Demographic diversity was unrelated to team creativity across 134 field studies. Deep-level, cognitive and experiential diversity is positively related to team creativity. | meta-analysis | forage | Make subagents differ in what they work with, not in a label. Each forage agent gets a different retrieved knowledge pack (web or memory documents from a distinct field), a different method (constraint inversion, morphological matrix, analogy) or a different representation of the problem. /think writes the seed-method-knowledge triple to state.json and checks the triples are pairwise distinct by embedding. Never assign identity labels or personas. |
| SOC-03 | Partial connectivity (intermediate coupling) favors cumulative innovation; hubs suppress it | Across four chimpanzee subspecies, limited connectivity between groups favored the few cases of cumulative culture, which arose through transmission between populations, incremental change and repurposing; a model found algorithmic mediation helps most in less dense networks. Convergently, connectome models show metastability peaks at intermediate global coupling, and removing high-eigenvector-centrality hubs increases it, with clustering and small-worldness correlating positively. | single-study | forage | Run /forage as two or three islands of subagents, each with its own seed set and its own ideas file. Every k rounds, /think migrates the top two ideas from each island into the others (or, as a lighter variant, each agent reads 2-3 random ideas sampled from the other islands). To prevent hubs, cap how many next-round seeds any single shortlist idea or semantic-memory item can supply. The harness compares isolated, island-with-migration (and random-sample partial coupling), and fully connected setups on pairwise idea diversity, best score and solve rate. |
| SOC-04 | Larger populations need a scored record and payoff filtering | In arrowhead-technology transmission experiments (combined N=941), the benefit of population size depended on attention filtering toward high-payoff solutions. Larger populations improved cumulative culture when learners could use an external record that supported comparing payoffs and recombining solutions. | single-study | forage | Increase /forage fan-out only together with a shared, scored record. Each later-round generator reads a scored table derived from ideas.jsonl and evaluations.jsonl, sorted by score, with components broken out so it can recombine them. The harness tests fan-out of 2, 4 and 8 agents, with and without the scored record, to check that the size benefit depends on the record. |
| SOC-05 | Selective learning preserves complex solutions | In 20 populations over 12 generations (N=3,450) learning to sort, known algorithms emerged. When learners could choose whom to learn from, complex high-performing algorithms persisted. Without that choice, populations converged on simpler, more transmissible ones. | single-study | forage | In multi-round /forage, each new-generation subagent gets a list of idea lineages with their evaluator scores and chooses which lineage to extend. It is not simply handed the most recent output, which would make a transmission chain. Record lineage ids in ideas.jsonl. The harness compares chosen-parent and assigned-parent conditions on final complexity and score. |
| SOC-06 | Conceptual work needs shared context | Across about 20M articles and 4M patents over 50 years, remote teams were less likely than on-site teams to produce disruptive work, in every field, period and team size. Remote members mostly handled late-stage, codified technical tasks rather than tacit conceptual work. | single-study | think | Split work by phase. Conceptual steps (/encode, /restructure, choosing the framing) run in one agent or one context holding the full problem.md, trajectory and evidence. Codified late-stage steps (scoring, verification code, writing up) go to fresh subagents with compact specs. Do not fragment framing across many agents that only see summaries. |
| SOC-07 | Cross-model LLM outputs are homogeneous | Across standardized creativity tests, LLM responses are much more similar to other LLMs' responses than human responses are to each other, even after controlling for response structure. LLM creative partners may push users toward a limited set of outputs. | preprint | harness | Parallel subagents running the same model and prompt will converge, so they do not supply diversity on their own. Inject it from outside: random concept lists drawn with python, retrieved web documents, varied constraint sets. The harness measures mean pairwise embedding distance among ideas from different subagents within a run and across 10 runs. A seed scheme stays only if it raises that distance without lowering judge-rated quality. |
| SOC-08 | Attention schemas improve multi-agent mutual prediction | Agents with an attention schema judged other agents' attention more accurately, were easier for others to read, and did better on a joint scene-painting task. The gains were specific to attention-judgment tasks and were not explained by added complexity (Farrell, Ziman & Graziano). | preprint | forage | Before generating, each parallel /forage subagent writes a two-line focus.md: the cluster it targets, its angle, and what it will avoid. The orchestrator embeds these focus statements, detects overlap by cosine similarity, and reassigns seeds before full generation, so parallel agents cover distinct regions. Measure pairwise idea diversity with and without this focus exchange. |
| SOC-09 | Argument exchange needs evaluators with divergent priors | In the argumentative theory, reasoning works through producing and evaluating reasons between people, so idea quality should improve under adversarial exchange. Its stated limit: it 'does not work when we reason alone or with people who share our beliefs'. | theory | evaluate | For the top 3 shortlisted ideas, run an exchange in files. A critic subagent writes objections to objections.md. A fresh defender subagent, given the idea and the objections, writes rebuttals or revisions. A third fresh subagent judges which objections still stand. Critics get different evidence seeds (different retrieved documents, a counterexample-search brief, a test-writing brief), not personas, so their priors genuinely differ. |

### Sources and caveats

- **SOC-01**: Key sources: Mullen, Johnson & Salas 1991, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15324834basp1201_1; DeRosa, Smith & Hantula 2007, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563205000506. Caveats: These are human group findings, with the losses driven by production blocking and evaluation apprehension. LLM subagents fail differently, mainly through anchoring. Both meta-analyses predate 2023. Within a single generation round this is consistent with SOC-03 (islands with migration), which concerns coupling across rounds, but the two should be tested in the same ablation grid. Merged from: Generate independently, then pool (nominal groups) [modulators_and_practical_levers].
- **SOC-02**: Key sources: Wallrich et al. 2024, https://gwern.net/doc/economics/2024-wallrich.pdf; Byron et al. 2023, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/peps.12501; Wang et al. 2019, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331401613_Team_creativityinnovation_in_culturally_diverse_teams_A_meta-analysis. Caveats: These are human team studies with mostly organizational outcomes. The effects are small and depend on context (psychological safety, collectivism). How well an LLM's 'knowledge pack' corresponds to human expertise is unknown. Pairs with SOC-07: same-model agents are homogeneous by default, so this input-level diversity is the main available lever. Merged from: Deep-level diversity matters, demographic diversity does not [modulators_and_practical_levers].
- **SOC-03**: Key sources: Gunasekaram et al. 2024, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk3381; Czaplicka et al. 2025, https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2024.0686; Hancock et al. 2025, https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf. Caveats: Evidence is chimpanzee genetic and cultural network data, one simulation model, and connectome model results reported in a review; no direct human-group or LLM multi-agent test. The best migration rate or coupling level is unknown. The connectome entry was tagged social but comes from brain dynamics (closer to state); it is merged here because its agent translation targets inter-agent coupling. Tension with SOC-01 (full isolation) to be resolved by ablation. Merged from: Partial connectivity favors cumulative innovation [development_language_and_culture]; Intermediate coupling maximizes metastability; hubs suppress it [brain_dynamics_and_neuromodulation].
- **SOC-04**: Key sources: Walker & Fay 2026, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40973-x; Lupyan et al. 2026, https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916251398539. Caveats: One paper, two experiments, on a virtual lab task. Human attention limits differ from an LLM's context limits. The filtering may also amplify evaluator bias. Related to SOC-05 (selective learning) but distinct: this is about population size and an external record, SOC-05 about choosing whom to learn from. Merged from: Larger populations need records and filtering [development_language_and_culture].
- **SOC-05**: Key sources: Thompson et al. 2022, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abn0915. Caveats: One large experiment on an algorithmic task. Choice depends on the scores being meaningful. LLM summaries passed between generations may simplify ideas regardless of selection. Merged from: Selective learning preserves complex solutions [development_language_and_culture].
- **SOC-06**: Key sources: Lin, Frey & Wu 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06767-1. Caveats: Observational; full text not accessed. 'Co-location' maps only loosely onto shared context windows. This is in tension with P1's fresh-evaluator rule, which applies to evaluation, not framing. Arguably a control (work-allocation) mechanism as much as social. Merged from: Conceptual work needs shared context [development_language_and_culture].
- **SOC-07**: Key sources: Wenger & Kenett 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361. Caveats: Preprint, and the tests are short divergent tasks. Whether seed injection actually reduces homogeneity has not been shown. Partly a measurement finding (population-level diversity metric); kept here because it constrains multi-agent design. Merged from: Cross-model LLM outputs are homogeneous [memory_semantic_networks_and_combination].
- **SOC-08**: Key sources: Farrell, Ziman & Graziano 2024-2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00983. Caveats: Preprint, with results only qualitative on the abstract page. The agents were trained, not prompted LLMs, and using this for overlap avoidance is an extrapolation. Exchanging focus statements is a light form of coupling; check it does not cause the anchoring SOC-01 warns about. Merged from: Attention schemas improve multi-agent mutual prediction [consciousness_and_global_workspace].
- **SOC-09**: Key sources: Mercier & Sperber 2020, https://www.dan.sperber.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020_mercier_sperber_bounded-rationality-in-a-social-world.pdf; Informal Logic reply, https://doaj.org/article/7d6ee10a9a814469bf31d273c6580e9b. Caveats: The notes did not retrieve the quantitative group-reasoning results. Copies of the same model may share beliefs, which is exactly the theory's failure condition (see SOC-07). Divergence has to come from inputs and tools. Functionally this is an evaluation mechanism realized socially; kept under social but could be cross-listed in evaluation. Merged from: Argument exchange needs evaluators with divergent priors [philosophy_of_thought_and_creativity].
