## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Independent human replication of the Hernandez novelty result: no data since 2013, and effect sizes were never reported. Unblock by finding the full text of Hernandez 2013 and Chang 2016, or run a small human study.
- The 2025 to 2026 Springer IFIP TRIZ-with-LLM chapters (ARIZ, contradiction processing, cognitive skills): pages blocked. Unblock with institutional access or author preprints.
- Relational-reasoning moderation (Dumas and Schmidt 2015) and principle abstraction level (Livotov 2019): abstracts only. Need the full text for N and effect sizes.
- Contamination: whether TRIZBench cases and textbook cases sit in model training data. Needs a post-cutoff case set; TRIZ-GPT's 10 post-April-2023 cases are one start.
- Blind human or model-free novelty rating of TRIZ outputs. Our bench has no rater panel; G8 says to judge novelty by running, which needs open items that have a runnable check.
- Behaviour of 2026 frontier models: most TRIZ-LLM numbers use GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Qwen3-8B and similar. Unblock by running T1 and T3 on our current model.
- Whether TRIZ matrices fit non-engineering problem classes. We would need to build a table per class, and nobody has shown that this works.

## Order to run
1. **E07.03-U1 together with U2 (one run, five arms):** it is the decisive experiment nobody has run, and it separates typed content from restatement and random stimulus.
2. **E07.03-U3:** cheapest and most likely to matter. It tests whether check-gated triggering removes the over-firing that GPT-4 showed (86 of 100).
3. **E07.03-U11 then U5:** a code-only baseline run on existing TRIZBench data that tells us whether any model step in principle selection is worth its tokens.
