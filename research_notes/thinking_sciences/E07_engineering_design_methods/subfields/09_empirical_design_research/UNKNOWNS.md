## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Human-side baselines (Shah 2000, Nelson 2009, Sio 2015 meta-analysis, Girotra 2010, Linsey 2011) are paywalled and unread. We would need the PDFs, or a human pilot, to know how well "more ideas, better best idea" and example-fixation hold for people.
- Engineering artefact tests: nothing on our bench builds and tests a physical or simulated design. We would need simulation-checkable design items (for example parametric design tasks with a simulator) to test whether ideation variety predicts tested performance.
- A trustworthy "same idea" gold set: we need 300+ idea pairs labelled by 3 or more engineers to calibrate any embedding threshold. Costly.
- The cause of cross-model sameness (shared or synthetic training data) cannot be tested without model access beyond prompting.
- LLM-judge ensembles and pairwise-with-reference novelty judging have no published test; we could build one, but it needs the human consensus set above.
- Aggregation of per-idea Shah scores into a set score (Sorensen et al. 2026, unopened). Read the paper first.
- The Shah et al. 2000 method-comparison numbers: no access.

## Order to run
1. **U1 with U2 together (same items, same seeds).** Both tell us whether S3 open mode earns its tokens against plain sampling, and what to pass to each fresh attempt. Cheapest high-value test; it decides the core generation design.
2. **U3 then U4 (small human labelling job).** Every later test needs a diversity metric we can trust. 100 pairs plus 20 sub-sets is a one-day annotation task and removes the largest measurement doubt.
3. **U9 (spread vs executed success on checkable items).** It guards against optimising a metric that does not pay. The literature (N=43 and 3,222 runs) says the link is weak, so a quick local answer decides whether spread is ever a selection signal or stays a diagnostic.
