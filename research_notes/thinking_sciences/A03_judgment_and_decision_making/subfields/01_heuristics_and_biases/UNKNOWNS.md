# Heuristics and biases: what we don't know and should test
*Candidates for the schema that are not established. Nothing here goes into SCHEMA.md until its test passes. Source: DEEP.md, claims.jsonl, critique.md in this folder.*

Test defaults: 40 or more fresh items per arm, 3 samples per item, each in a fresh context, option order randomised, setup tokens counted (G7). "Equal tokens" means total tokens per item, including any strip, rewrite or check step.

## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Late instruction vs shallow-layer anchor (K27).** No paper tests it, and our bench measures behaviour, not layers. U1 and U4 give the behavioural answer; the mechanism stays unknown. Unblock: a small open-weight model with attribution tooling.
- **Exact 1974 calibration design (K30).** The almanac, 98 percent design has never been run on an LLM. Unblock: a hand-built almanac set of about 100 questions; this is a research replication, not a schema need, so low priority.
- **Whether anchoring susceptibility tracks model confidence (d18, abstract only).** Would need token log-probabilities, which the agent's API may not expose. Unblock: log-prob access or a sampling-based confidence proxy.
- **Human-like vs not-human-like errors (K19).** Needs matched human controls at the original N. We have no human arm. Unblock: use published human baselines only as reference, never as a pass criterion.
- **Whether results for 7 to 8B open models (d07, d09) carry to the agent's model.** Needs the agent's actual model under the same protocol; the test list above does that.
- **Long agent contexts with many tool results and earlier guesses.** AnchorBench's History and Tool pathways have format confounds (d06). Unblock: our own long-context bench with matched fresh and running arms (U3).
- **Interaction of anchoring with the other subfields in this run (Bias and debiasing, Outside view and base rates, Diagnosticity of evidence, Value of information).** Results in those documents may supersede U6 and U7. Unblock: read their DEEP.md files when written, and merge the tests that overlap.
- **Unverified sources.** Kahneman and Frederick (2002), Gigerenzer (1991) with the 1996 replies, Epley and Gilovich (2006), Bar-Hillel and Koehler on causal base rates, Schwarz et al. (1991) on experienced ease were not opened. Unblock: open them before citing specifics.
- **Items from 2026 preprints read only via a fetch summary (d08, d09, d12, d16, d21) or as an abstract (d18).** Unblock: re-open the full PDFs.

## Order to run

1. **U1 (strip vs budget on anchored numbers).** It is the central S1 question, is cheap (one item set, four arms), and also tests the claim in G11 that more thinking is not the fix.
2. **U3 (fresh context vs running context for repeated estimates).** Nearly free, no model changes, and the largest measured pathway (own earlier answer, 0.34) has never been tested against a fresh-context arm.
3. **U8 (arithmetic in code vs in the model).** Largest reported effect (GPT-4 18 to 86-97 percent) in the folder, simple to build, and the result decides whether U6 and U7 are worth running at all.
