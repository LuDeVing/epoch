## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Peer-reviewed frontier evidence for choice-price reversal.** The only source is a preregistered blog post with stateless calls. Unblock: a same-session replication on our bench (U4) and, ideally, a published study.
- **Human data on whether a neutral restatement helps people.** None found. Unblock: a human arm is not available; use the LLM bench only and keep the claim LLM-specific.
- **Whether the reasoning-model invariance (Ge et al., 3 prospects) generalises.** Item coverage is thin. Unblock: build 100+ prospects with a structured generator (U6).
- **Frame as a real information signal in LLMs.** No LLM test of a speaker reference point exists. Unblock: construct items where the frame implies a typical value (U5).
- **Primary-source details still unresolved.** Payne 2,550 vs 2,150 decisions; Kuhberger h=0.612 attribution; Mandel 2014 not opened; Payne-Bettman-Johnson primary papers not opened. Unblock: open the primary papers; none of these should drive a design choice.
- **Hidden-test generalisation.** All published LLM framing results use authored items. Unblock: check that our bench items are not in training data (fresh numbers and domains).
- **Multi-turn amplification.** d13 shows worse agreement in chats, single-source. Unblock: multi-step bench items with a framed first turn (U12).
- **Effect of accuracy targets on the "right" answer.** Reasoning moves choice toward expected value (d18, partly read). Where the correct choice is not the expected-value one, we have no data. Unblock: items with a stated risk preference.

## Order to run

1. **U1 (accuracy kept).** It decides whether S1 is free. Cheap, uses existing items with gold answers, and every other S1 test depends on its answer.
2. **U7 (valence, order, negation, cue drift).** Cheap factorial run on forced-choice items. It tells us which normalisation steps are worth keeping and shapes U2 and U3.
3. **U2 (code render vs model rewrite).** Biggest expected gain: if code rendering matches model rewriting at lower tokens, S1 becomes nearly free and removes the overshoot risk seen with GPT-3.5.
