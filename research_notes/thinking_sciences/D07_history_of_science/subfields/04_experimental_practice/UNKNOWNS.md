## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Original text of Hacking, Galison and Gooding. Claims K01-K03 and K22 rest on the encyclopedia summary. Unblock: read the books or a second secondary source.
- Whether POPPER-style control holds on non-permutation nulls (confounded or irrelevant real data). Unblock: build such nulls; needs a dataset with known confounds.
- Post-saturation PaperBench and EXP-Bench successors with newer models. Unblock: find or build them; needs running costs we may not have.
- Preregistering for the next LLM. An agent cannot access a later model. Unblock: a proxy using a different model family.
- Whether there is a measurable experimenter's regress in LLM test design (K03). Unblock: tasks with no independent criterion and a hidden truth.
- Why BED-LLM's abstract (+37.4 pp) and its table (+40.4 pp) differ. Unblock: ask authors or recompute from released code.
- Instrument-level tests: nothing known on LLMs doing Hacking-style intervention or calibration (c26; limited search). Unblock: wider literature search.
- Exploratory experimentation (K23) and trading zones: no agent-facing measure yet.

## Order to run
1. D07.04-U1 (EIG ranking at equal tokens): the largest measured effect so far (+37-40 pp unmatched) and the one with the biggest open budget question; cheap on closed items.
2. D07.04-U2 (run-and-check): attacks the biggest failure share (execution, 40-50%), needs only a code runner, and a cited-output rule is likely to be adopted if it works.
3. D07.04-U3 (fixed criterion): cheap to build, tests the contested regress claim directly, and sets the false-confirmation baseline that U4, U6 and U7 reuse.
