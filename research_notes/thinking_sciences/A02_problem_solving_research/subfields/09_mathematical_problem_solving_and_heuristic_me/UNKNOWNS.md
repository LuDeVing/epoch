## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Primary human data: Schoenfeld 1985, Hembree 1992 and IMPROVE effect sizes were never read. Unblock: open the papers or a later review and record N and effect sizes. Until then no pooled human effect may be quoted.
- Schoenfeld's 60% to 20% drop is second-hand. Unblock: read Schoenfeld 1992 or an equivalent primary report.
- Whether Gandhi-type behaviours carry to maths word problems and proofs, and whether scaffolding alone can induce them. Unblock: needs RL or fine-tuning access, which our prompt-only bench lacks.
- Per-heuristic effect in humans. Schoenfeld 1979 trained five heuristics together. Unblock: none cheap; a new human study.
- Reasoning-model behaviour on Polya-style prompts. Gains reported on PaLM-2L, davinci and GPT-3.5 may be gone. Unblock: run T1 to T4 on a reasoning model before trusting any number from the papers.
- Hanoi cliff vs output limit (Shojaee vs Lawsen). Unblock: a rerun with output budgets set high enough, and numbers published.
- Proof-level tasks. USAMO grading is human and LLM graders did not reproduce human grades (c16). Unblock: a reliable proof grader or a set of proof-like items with code checks.
- Problem posing for frozen agents. Unblock: a set of open tasks with a running check.

## Order to run
1. U1 (conditional juncture monitor): cheapest and highest stakes, since S6 control is the core claim from this literature and always-on already looks harmful.
2. U4 (specialise operator on find-the-formula items): the largest reported gain (about +30 points) and the check is code, so noise is low.
3. U6 (restate-and-strip vs ignore sentence): brittleness is the best-established failure and the fix is one cheap call; the result also tells us whether S1 input hygiene needs a model-specific setting.
