## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Current frontier reasoning models.** All effective-vote measurements are on 0.5B to 32B models. Frontier data: one NLI panel (d17), GPQA agreement (d18), Gemini-2.5 near ceiling (d16). Unblock: run U1 on our own reasoning model; it needs no new data.
- **Jaworski and Alam 2026 (500 draws, 7 to 35 percent).** SSRN returned 403. The sample-count explanation for the 1.6 vs 7 to 35 percent gap cannot be settled without the paper. Unblock: a different copy of the paper, or U7 with 500 draws on a small numeric set.
- **Herzog and Hertwig 2009 and 2014, Rauhut and Lorenz 2011, Litvinova, van Dolder.** Paywalled or unopened. Claims that rest on them (K03, K23) stay single-source. Unblock: institutional access or author copies.
- **Per-item n_eff.** Existing measures are global (c across items), which mix item difficulty with true dependence. Per-item dependence needs many samples per item. Unblock: a 30-item set with 50 samples each, per-item success rate spread.
- **Sequential vs parallel at equal tokens for reasoning models.** Contested; the one positive study has a table/text mismatch. Unblock: U6, plus an independent replication of Sharma and Chopra on a larger problem set.
- **Does a human-style delay have any LLM counterpart?** Fresh context removes the first answer completely. No test of "time" exists. Unblock: none needed; U4 covers the closest thing.
- **LLM version of the expertise boundary.** Needs a way to make the model "less expert" on the same items (e.g. smaller model on the same set) to see the gain return. Unblock: U14 with two model sizes.
- **Shared bias.** Averaging cannot remove it. The only remedies seen are outside signals (a different source, a check, retrieved evidence). A test needs items where the model's modal answer is wrong plus an external source. Unblock: U10 on a hard set, then a retrieved-evidence arm.
- **Human-subject questions** (culture, time pressure, incentives): not tested in any source. No agent analogue; not worth pursuing.

## Order to run

1. **U1 (n_eff and k cap) together with U3 (zero-spread share).** One data collection (5 to 16 fresh samples per item) answers both. It sets the base numbers every other test depends on.
2. **U2 (2-sample disagreement gate).** Cheapest large saving, built on the same samples as U1. If it passes, G12 gets a concrete rule.
3. **U4 (fresh vs continued context) and U5 (diverse vs temperature samples).** Both use the same sample runs with different prompts. They decide what the S3 attempts should look like.
