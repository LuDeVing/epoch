## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Real judicial opinions with extracted factors: we have no labelled set of case pairs with expert distinctions. Needs a small annotated set (50 to 100 pairs) from a law-trained annotator.
- Human lawyer and judge behaviour (anchoring on the first cited case, framing): no human study was opened. Needs the human-decision literature (Guthrie et al. 2001; Englich et al. 2006) read in full; cannot be run on our bench at all.
- Whether models causally use decodable factor structure: needs open-weight models and steering or ablation tools. Our bench is prompt-level.
- Original IBP, CATO and Aleven accuracy claims (K20): need the papers (paywalled). Until then no symbolic-baseline comparison.
- Independent replication of the Zhang et al. benchmarks: nobody outside the group has published; we would have to build the benchmark ourselves from CATO.
- Best K for legal reasoning (not retrieval): needs a legal reasoning set with correctness labels, which we do not have.
- 2020-2026 papers possibly missed because search quota ran out: re-run discovery when quota resets.

## Order to run

1. E11.05-U1 (decompose comparison with code-computed differences): largest expected gain, cheap, and it directly tests the one finding that matters most (chained steps collapse).
2. E11.05-U3 (read the text, then check support for the cited holding): established risk, cheap to grade, and it protects every other step that reuses a prior case.
3. E11.05-U6 (relative length re-check trigger): nearly free to implement, evidence from two independent sources, and it applies to every item type, not only legal ones.
