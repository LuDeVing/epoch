## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Current Claude and GPT-5-class results on Belief-R and the Kirkpatrick-Sterken set are missing. Unblock: obtain the public sets and run them on our deployed model (cheap; part of U1, U2).
- Human item-level data for the suppression task. No large registered replication was found, and the item-level link to LLM error is unknown. Unblock: raw data from a Many-Labs style replication, or run our own human sample.
- Original Elio and Pelletier / Hewson and Vogel default-inheritance data. Behind paywalls. Unblock: library access or author request.
- Reiter 1980 and Gottlob 1992 first-hand. Not opened, so complexity and fixed-point details come through reviews. Unblock: institutional PDF access.
- Token cost of a solver tool call. Not measured anywhere. Unblock: own run in U6.
- Independence of the 2026 results. DeReLab and DeFAb come from one group; the Aethis paper is a vendor paper. Unblock: an independent re-run of a subset, or wait for peer review.
- Drift. Opus 4.6 moved from 89.7% to 98.5% on the same prompt between March and April. Unblock: log model snapshot ids and re-run the anchor set at each major test.
- Floating conclusions and rational closure drowning in LLM behaviour. Nothing found. Unblock: design a small probe set after U5.

## Order to run

1. U1 (gated revise vs re-check-always). It is the central question for G1 and S5, uses one item set, and also yields the stale / over-flip log for every other test.
2. U9 (question order). Almost free, reuses U1 items, and may remove a large artefact before we judge anything else.
3. U3 (exception field in memory). Directly tests the one real gap in our rule store: the ExpeL-style count cannot write exceptions; cheap to build and measure.
