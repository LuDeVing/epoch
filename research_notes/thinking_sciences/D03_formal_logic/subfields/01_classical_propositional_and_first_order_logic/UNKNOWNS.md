## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- **Realistic rule sets with known contradictions.** No benchmark of natural-language specs (policies, schemas) with ground-truth inconsistency exists; SATBench is random CNF. Unblock: write about 100 seeded policy documents with a gold conflict set, or mine edit histories of real rule files.
- **Soundness judging at scale.** Chain soundness needs a judge or formal checker. The GPT-judged soundness in d19 is itself unvalidated. Unblock: a small human-labelled set of chains to calibrate any judge.
- **Real solver access.** Solver-based claims (K09, K13, K27) need Z3 or Prover9 in the harness. Unblock: add a sandboxed solver tool. Until then only prompt-only variants can run.
- **Current-model baselines.** Premise-order and rule-type results come from 2023-24 models. Unblock: rerun a permutation test on our own models first; this is U3's baseline.
- **Belief bias in models as response bias vs sensitivity.** Needs graded confidence or ROC-style data from a model. Dube et al. 2010 and Heit and Rotello 2014 not opened. Unblock: elicit confidence ratings and fit slopes.
- **First-order undecidability in practice.** How often real checks hit a budget. Unblock: log timeouts in any solver trial.
- **Equal-token accounting for hidden reasoning.** Reasoning models use about 10x hidden tokens (c26). Unblock: the harness must record total thinking plus output tokens per arm.

## Order to run
1. **U3 (restate in order, drop distractors):** cheapest, needs no solver, and gives the premise-order baseline for our own models, including reasoning models.
2. **U1 with U2 (clause-by-clause assignment, abstain outputs):** serves the S4/S8 "do the rules contradict" use case directly, which no published study measures.
3. **U9 (step source tags):** cheap, and tests the label-vs-trace gap that makes every other accuracy number untrustworthy.
