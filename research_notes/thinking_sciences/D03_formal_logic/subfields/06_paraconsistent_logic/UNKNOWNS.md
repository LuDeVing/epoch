## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **A reliable gold set of real impasse contradictions.** We only have synthetic injections. Unblock: log real S6 events from bench runs and label which involved a genuine conflict (the schema notes impasse may be about 8% of problems, which caps the gain).
- **Whether paraconsistent scaffolds for LLMs exist in literature we missed.** arXiv, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429. Unblock: re-run the queries for LP, FDE, LFI and Belnap with LLM terms when the APIs answer.
- **Whether glut rates predict wrong answers.** Allen et al. report rates, not error links. Unblock: measure on our own claim sets with known truth (U9).
- **Behaviour of current frontier models.** Most conflict benchmarks used 2024-era or small open models. Unblock: run U2 and U13 on the models we actually use.
- **Why reasoning models notice and then drop a conflict.** Only case studies (PCBench R1). Unblock: log traces in U2 and compare thinking text with the final output.
- **Human data to agent mapping.** The 7-66% human glut acceptance has no task-performance link. Probably not worth testing.
- **Original sources (Priest 1979, Belnap 1977, da Costa 1974) were not opened.** Unblock: open them only if a formal detail starts to matter.

## Order to run

1. **D03.06-U13 (trace self-contradiction pass) with D03.06-U2 (conflict register):** cheapest, built on established findings (reporting 4-40% to 54-78%), and both go in the same S6 entry step.
2. **D03.06-U1 (keep vs retract vs halt):** the core S6 decision and the largest gap in the literature; the answer decides whether paraconsistent handling belongs in the schema at all.
3. **D03.06-U3 (bilateral vs matched unilateral):** the only direct LLM paraconsistent result is confounded with abstention; matched arms settle whether the structure matters before we pay 2x tokens.
