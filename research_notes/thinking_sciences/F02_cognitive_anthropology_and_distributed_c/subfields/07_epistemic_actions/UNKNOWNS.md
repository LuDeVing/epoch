## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Independent replication of the human effects.** Preview priming (N=30 and N=15, one lab, odd statistics in the 2000 paper) and the skill direction (N=2 vs 59 players, log-rule classification) cannot be settled with LLM runs. Unblock: a human Tetris study with intent coding or eye tracking. Not needed for the agent decisions above.
- **Steering vs selecting on real repositories.** Our bench may lack bug-fix items with executable tests at the 80-item scale. Unblock: build or import a small SWE-style set.
- **Hidden-state tasks with a cost per probe.** We need environments where probes cost something and the world can lie. Unblock: build the U13 game and a probe-cost wrapper in the harness.
- **Frontier-model behaviour.** Most evidence on misleading probes, tool overuse and self-repair is on 2023 to 2024 models. Unblock: rerun U9 and U2 on the current model before trusting the older numbers.
- **Matched-token accounting.** Probe cost includes history resending (11 to 12x if uncached, c21). Unblock: the harness must log cached and uncached tokens separately.
- **Papers never opened.** Neth and Payne 2002, Gray and Fu 2000, Kirlik 1998, Maglio et al. 1999, the IBM "self priming" paper. Unblock: obtain full texts; low priority for the agent.
- **Probe cost in context vs reasoning.** Whether a long chain of thought is cheaper than a tool round trip depends on model and tool latency. Unblock: measure per class in the first U1 run.

## Order to run

1. **U1 (probe-first vs attempt-first).** It is the main open question (gap 1), nothing matched exists, and the other tests depend on its answer.
2. **U9 (control against misleading probes).** Harm from self-written probes is established, the fix is cheap (one control run), and it protects every other test.
3. **U3 (steer vs select) with U2 (gate) run on the same items.** Both reuse the U1 item set, so they add little cost, and they decide where probe output goes and when to probe.
