## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Real GPS protocol numbers (Newell and Simon 1961/1972, Ernst and Newell 1969): need the books or a library copy. Not needed for design.
- Effect sizes and N for the goal-free studies (Sweller and Levine 1982, Owen and Sweller 1985, Ayres 1993, Vollmeyer 1996): need full text. Human-only; low value for the schema.
- Whether any high-quality study supports the MEA teaching meta-analyses (1.431, 0.920): dropped unless full text shows rigour.
- Why GPT-5.4 does not gain and Claude and Gemini do: needs more models and thinking on/off in the same suite; partly covered by U2.
- Whether MEDAKA transfers to discrete and open-ended domains such as code: needs a discrete validator suite; partly covered by U1 and U4.
- Probe and steering results (internal state decoded but unused): need open weights. Unblock by running a small open model, but it does not change a scaffold on an API model.
- Independent replication of MEDAKA, ADaPT and MAP: wait for other groups; our bench runs are the replication.
- Scaffold-vs-plain on renamed Hanoi (memory vs reasoning): covered by U15 once the twin set exists.

## Order to run
1. **U1 with U2 (gap step vs vote, thinking off and on):** the only direct MEA-for-LLM claim; one run settles whether O5 keeps its gap template and for which modes.
2. **U6 (exact check vs soft check vs more samples):** the most solid pattern in the evidence and cheap to build; likely the biggest gain.
3. **U5 (protect achieved goals) then U3/U4 (state restatement and gap-choice ablations):** U5 fixes the oldest known failure of MEA; U3/U4 show which part of the MEDAKA recipe to keep.
