## Contradictions between sources

- Continue vs restart: Snell (sequential revision wins on easy), Backtracking paper (task dependent), ParaThinker and Hassid (parallel short wins), an inverse-entropy snippet (sequential wins). Direction depends on difficulty, model type (trained revision model vs prompted) and whether a verifier exists. Claim K11 is marked contested.
- Brown et al. show vote/reward-model accuracy plateauing; Chen et al. show vote accuracy falling for hard queries. A plateau and a decline are different shapes; neither tested the other's setting.
- Seeding restarts with prior best heuristics: lower best, higher median (K20). One campaign each, so no conclusion.
- Restarts-as-help (K12, parallel short chains) vs underthinking (K14, too many switches harm). They are consistent only if the restart is at an attempt boundary after a check; no study tests that directly.
- BATS uses a model-made PIVOT/CONTINUE verdict; our schema says code decides. The gain may not survive replacing the verifier by rules.

## Weak spots

- Almost all classic evidence (Gomes, Luby, Kautz) is SAT/CSP with a checkable answer and independent runs. LLM runs are not independent given shared prompts and temperature, and no paper measured an LLM heavy-tail or hazard curve directly. The one LLM restart study is a single domain (steel rolling), one model, one real campaign, one preprint.
- Most "established" claims have two or more sources but from the same group (Gomes/Selman/Kautz) or the same style of benchmark. Kautz 2002 dynamic policies, Gomes-Selman portfolios, Truchet speed-up, BATS, Zhang anytime index, Conformal Thinking: each is single-source.
- Cutoffs in Gomes 1998 were tuned on the very instances reported (selection effect).
- Huang 2007 is the best independent restart evidence in a modern solver, but it is one solver and I could not re-open it. Restarts help there partly because learned clauses persist, so it does not show that amnesiac restarts help.
- Many LLM results use math or code with verifiers; agent tasks without a verifier (open reasoning, ARC-style) are barely covered.

## GAPS

1. Is the finishing-time distribution of an LLM attempt (same prompt, temperature, different seeds) heavy-tailed or has a falling hazard on our task families? Nobody has measured it; it decides restart vs continue. Needs 30+ attempts per item with a verifier.
2. At equal total tokens, how do (a) one long attempt, (b) k fresh short attempts and (c) Luby-scheduled attempts compare on ARC-style and BrainTeaser items with code-checkable answers? Which unit size works?
3. When a verifier is absent or weak, is any restart schedule better than continuing? What is the best code-only stop signal (repeated tool error, no new fact for N steps, answer unchanged across restarts)?
4. What should cross a restart: nothing, a verified-facts note, or the prior best answer? Does a note cause anchoring that kills the diversity restarts buy (the seeded vs unseeded signal in K20)?
5. Can early-signal prediction (Kautz 2002 style) work on LLM attempts, using features from the first few hundred tokens, and how much does it beat Luby at equal tokens?
6. Does the continue-vs-restart rule flip with first-attempt difficulty, and can a cheap pilot attempt classify difficulty well enough to route (Snell's difficulty bins used pass@1 from many samples, which we cannot afford)?
7. Does external-feedback refinement (tool result, test failure) beat fresh restart at equal tokens, and by how much, for our model class? Self-critique alone is shown not to help; the externally-signalled case is only supported by oracle-label tables.
8. How large is the gain of a K=2 to 4 portfolio of restart candidates picked by a cheap verifier, and does it hold on held-out items rather than the pool-selection set?
9. Is the budget-visibility effect (BATS tracker) real for a scaffold with explicit attempt counters, and does it interact with restarts (agent gives up too early)?
10. Open the unread foundations (Zilberstein 1996, Dean and Boddy 1988, Korf 1985, Luby 1993 theorems, Huang 2007 tables) to fix the anytime property list and confirm the Luby constants before they go into the schema text.

## Gap search results

Done 2026-10-26. Evidence in `evidence_d.jsonl` (d01 to d20). Search limits: WebSearch quota was exhausted, and the arXiv, Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex search APIs returned 429 or quota errors. Sources were therefore found by direct PDF fetch of papers already known by id, plus IJCAI and AAAI pages. New claims K24 to K31 added; K04, K10, K11, K12, K13, K15, K16, K20 and K23 got extra evidence and text.

Summary: no gap was fully closed. Gaps 7 and 10 (Huang part) are closed or mostly closed; gaps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 9 got partial evidence; gaps 6 and 8 got nothing new; gap 10 stays open for Zilberstein, Dean and Boddy, Korf, Luby 1993.

1. **Heavy tail or falling hazard of an LLM attempt: still open, partial.** Nobody measured finishing-time distributions. New indirect facts: per item, failure falls exponentially with attempts, and the heavy tail is in the spread of per-item pass@1 (d01, K24); within an attempt, correct solutions are shorter than wrong ones and length-capped sampling shows inverse scaling (d12, d13, K25). That suggests a falling hazard of success per token, but it is not a hazard curve. The 30+ attempts per item experiment is still needed.
2. **One long vs k short vs Luby at equal tokens: open, sharpened.** Counting total tokens changes the answer: sequential refinement with a check stays on the Pareto frontier and parallel drafts buy latency (d08: o3-mini 76.9 / 81.5 / 86.7 at matched sequential budget only), while Wait-extension without a check loses to parallel paths (d11, d12). Marked contested (K30). No Luby arm on ARC or BrainTeaser exists.
3. **No or weak verifier: partly closed for the stop signal.** Answer agreement across samples is a validated stop rule (K28, three independent sources, established): up to 7.9x fewer samples at under 0.1 point loss (d15), 33.8 to 84.2 percent fewer (d16), 52.8 percent fewer tokens (d14). Repeated tool error and no-new-fact stops have no evidence. These results are about how many samples to draw, not about restart beating continue. Setlur (d18) argues no verifier-free method scales as well.
4. **What crosses a restart: partly answered, still one source per signal.** Restart without memory cost timeouts in a CSP solver (365 to 378) and recorded nogoods reversed it (337) (d06, K26). Wrong candidates in a summary sharply lowered accuracy (d09, K27). Prior answer passed on gave +1 to 4 points but with no equal-token control (d10). Working rule: carry verified failures, not unverified answers. Still no direct anchoring-versus-diversity test.
5. **Early-signal prediction on LLM attempts: partly closed.** DeepConf (d14, K29) is an LLM analogue: windowed confidence aborts bad traces, 43 to 79 percent fewer tokens at about equal accuracy. It uses 2,048-token windows, needs logprobs, was not tested on the first few hundred tokens, and was never compared with Luby. Single source.
6. **Difficulty flip and pilot routing: no new evidence found.** Zeng (d12) and Olausson (d03, success by difficulty) are consistent with a flip but do not test routing from a cheap pilot.
7. **External-feedback refinement vs fresh restart: mostly closed for 2023-era models.** Test failure plus self-explanation did not beat resampling at equal budget (GPT-4 APPS 1.05x with 10 drafts + 1 repair, 0.97x with 2 drafts + 10 repairs); only a stronger critic or a human gave a clear gain (33.3 to 52.6 percent repair success) (d02, d03, folded into K12). Not tested on reasoning models, and not tested with a tool result other than unit tests.
8. **Portfolio of 2 to 4 candidates, held-out gain: no new evidence.**
9. **Budget visibility: partly addressed, contradicted.** Token elasticity shows a too-small stated budget can raise token use (d17, K31). BATS positive result stands (K16). No study of attempt counters with restarts, so the early-give-up risk is untested.
10. **Foundations: half closed.** Huang 2007 tables read from the primary PDF (d04, d05): solved of 251 = 127 none, 164 geometric (100,1.5), 166, 168, 174 geometric (32,1.1), 178 fixed 16000, 180 Luby unit 32; days 11.28 to 7.68. Luby sequence 1,1,2,1,1,2,4 confirmed from Huang's definition and the theorem statement (log-factor of optimal; no universal policy better by more than a constant), but Luby 1993 itself was not opened. Zilberstein 1996: landing page and abstract only, PDF returned an empty body (d19). Dean and Boddy 1988 and Korf 1985 not opened. A Wikipedia check (d20) names only measurable quality and interruptibility. The schema text should say "anytime properties from memory" until the primary text is read. Luby edge over a good fixed cutoff is small (2 instances of 251).

Corrections to earlier claims: K15 quotes s1 "50.0 to 57.6 with two Waits"; the s1 appendix table reads 50.0, 53.3, 53.3, 56.7 for 0, 1, 2, 4 Waits (single model, 30 items, one item = 3.3 points). One-item differences should not be read as effects.

Biggest remaining need: the 30+ attempts per item run with a verifier on ARC and BrainTeaser items, logging tokens to first correct, with arms for no carry-over, verified-facts note and prior answer at equal total tokens. That single experiment would settle gaps 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6.
