# Training: what we don't know and should test

*Candidates for the schema that are not established. Nothing here goes into SCHEMA.md until its test passes. Source: DEEP.md, claims.jsonl, critique.md in this folder.*

## Test before adding

All tests: arms at equal total tokens (setup counted), at least 5 runs per item, frozen retrieval, same items across arms, paired bootstrap 95% interval. Always include a plain arm and a constant base-rate forecast.

## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Human replication of the one-hour module by an independent group.** Needs new human data, or a read of Sedlmeier and Gigerenzer 2001 and Karimi Motahhar and Gruca 2025 (both blocked). Unblock by institutional access.
- **Persistence without feedback.** Needs a delayed retest of the same people with no Brier feedback. No such dataset found. The GJP archive might allow an analysis by someone with access.
- **Exact numbers in Lichtenstein and Fischhoff 1980 and Stone and Opel 2000.** Originals unread. Unblock by fetching the papers.
- **Which year's training content mattered.** Four content versions confounded with years. Needs the OSF material (osf.io/ecmk6) and a re-analysis.
- **Weight training vs scaffold at equal tokens.** We cannot fine-tune on our bench. Needs a small open model and a resolved-question set of 10,000 or more (Turtel used 10,000 plus 100,000 synthetic).
- **Newest reasoning models (2025 to 2026) with training-style prompts.** Needs a frozen-retrieval set that resolves after the model's cutoff.
- **Leakage-free large question sets.** Paleka's pitfalls and FutureSearch's pastcasting variance mean our results are only as good as the freeze. Needs a frozen pastcasting corpus or a live set resolved after data cut.
- **Human baseline on our bench items.** Without one, we compare only against plain and constant arms.

## Order to run

1. **U1 (reference-class step vs filler at equal tokens).** It is the best-supported human component (Brier 0.17 vs 0.42 among principle users) and the cheapest, and the LLM prompt analogue is the unresolved gap.
2. **U2 (calibration shrink, per task type).** Human gain is mostly lower confidence, so a code-side shrink is the likeliest real transfer; it also tests the risk of flipping to underconfidence.
3. **U4 (class record with lesson after the first attempt).** It is the only weight-free analogue of practice with feedback, which carried the largest share of human and RL gains (RL -0.025).
