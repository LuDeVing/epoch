# Science of science: subfields

Science of science uses large datasets of papers, patents and careers to study how science works. The 2018 review in Science (14 coauthors, including Fortunato, Bergstrom, Börner) covers six topics: knowledge networks, problem selection, novelty, career dynamics, team science and citation dynamics. The Wikipedia page on metascience adds methods, reporting, reproducibility, evaluation and incentives, and the evolution of science. A parallel strand, the psychology and philosophy of scientific discovery, studies one thinker. Most opened findings are correlational and describe populations, so few speak to a single agent.

## Dig into next

1. **Scientific discovery as reasoning (Klahr and Dunbar).** Questions: Does the two-space model (hypotheses, experiments) map onto S3 and S4? What did their studies find about confirming versus disconfirming tests, and does it support S7 counterexample search? Evidence needed: the 1988 paper and Dunbar's lab studies (neither opened).
2. **Novelty and atypicality.** Questions: Is a mix of mostly conventional work with a small atypical share a usable rule for attempts in open mode? How does the atypicality versus disruption split guide which attempts to favour? Evidence needed: the full 2025 paper and Uzzi et al.
3. **Reproducibility practices.** Question: Which practices (preregistration, held-out checks) map to G3? Evidence needed: meta-research reviews with measured effects.

## Does not transfer

Funding, peer review, citation economics, hiring, prestige and team structure are institutional. Large-team and mentorship findings concern groups and people. Publication data describe what gets published, not how people thought. Incentive problems such as p-hacking have no counterpart in a program.
