# Cognitive neuroscience of creativity and insight: subfields

This field uses imaging, electrophysiology and behaviour to ask what happens when people produce new ideas or suddenly see a solution. It splits by task (divergent thinking, remote associates, insight problems, improvisation), by level (regions, large networks, the structure of semantic memory) and by phase (generating, evaluating, incubating). Most results are descriptive: they say where and when. A few imply procedures: generation and evaluation are separable phases, associative distance matters, and impasse followed by a change of representation is the core of insight.

## Dig into next

**1. Generation versus evaluation.**
- Does a separate-call judge or a code check, applied after fresh generation, raise final-pick quality at equal tokens?
- Does mixed generate-and-judge in one call reduce novelty?
- Evidence needed: Ellamil et al. (not opened), NoveltyBench A/B.

**2. Impasse, conflict and representational change.**
- Can code detect "conflict" (output contradicts a stated expectation) and trigger O6 only then?
- Does the wiki's claim that people cannot sense nearness to a solution hold for models, justifying code-only triggers?
- Evidence needed: the 2019 Neuroscience review and Metcalfe and Wiebe, plus our impasse logs.

**3. Semantic distance.**
- Is there an optimal embedding distance for seeds in open mode?
- Evidence needed: Kenett network studies (effects small), distance sweep on our tasks.

## Does not transfer

Regions, networks, EEG bands, hemispheric findings, mood, personality and stimulation results are about brains. The felt "aha" must not be used as a signal: it is a human feeling and its report is not a record of cause (G10). Incubation breaks assume a continuing brain. The Wikipedia page notes that nearly 20% of one sample never reported insight, and that the evidence base is mostly small lab samples.
