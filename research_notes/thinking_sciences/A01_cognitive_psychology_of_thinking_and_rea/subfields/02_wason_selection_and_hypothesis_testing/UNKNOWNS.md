# Wason selection task and hypothesis testing: what we don't know and should test

*Candidates for the schema that are not established. Nothing here goes into SCHEMA.md until its test passes. Source: DEEP.md, claims.jsonl, critique.md in this folder.*

## Test before adding

Common setup for every test: hidden-rule discovery suite with a code oracle (agent proposes inputs, gets yes/no, then a final rule is scored on held-out inputs). Include items whose first natural guess is a strict subset of the truth. Equal total tokens per arm, setup and all samples counted (G7). Fresh context per attempt (G4). Run one thinking and one non-thinking model. "100 items" means 100 episodes per arm per model; the published effects are small (+0.06 to +0.15), so use at least 80.

## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Does thinking in opposites work for people outside the one Italian student sample? Needs an independent human replication. Unblock: search for replications, or ignore and rely on U3 and U4 on models.
- Do Ragni's dependence results say anything about resampling attempts? The human result is about one person's card picks and is disputed. Unblock: only the sample-correlation log in U11 can say.
- Is Wason's wish-to-verify the cause in models, or matching and drift into disjoint guesses? Needs token-level traces from models, not only tests. Unblock: store full reasoning and hypothesis per round.
- Do falsifying-probe results hold on ARC grids? No study exists and we lack a grid oracle that answers single probe inputs. Unblock: a harness oracle that returns the output for a proposed grid under each candidate transformation.
- Does a statistical falsification gate (POPPER-style) help open scientific hypotheses (K16)? Needs data-driven hypotheses and permutation nulls; our items have none. Unblock: a data-analysis item set.
- Real size of the information-gain model advantage in humans (Kellen and Klauer dispute). Needs the raw human data. Not needed for the agent.
- Originals for Rossi 2001 and Gale and Ball 2012 contrast figures, and the Jhaveri per-model token and success cells (garbled). Unblock: obtain the PDFs or the tables; U3 reruns the question anyway.
- Selection task as a discriminating test: reasoning models score 98 to 100 percent on deontic items, so it cannot separate strong models. Unblock: harder, multi-step items.

## Order to run

1. U1 (code-gated falsification vs prompt-only): highest expected gain, nobody has tested it, and the gate is cheap to build.
2. U3 (Think-in-Opposites at matched tokens): the best-supported human and model idea, and the matched-token control costs one extra arm on the same suite.
3. U7 (code-scored information gain, closed and self-made sets): large published gain on games, and one run settles where it applies.

U2 is a free log on the same runs, so collect it alongside U1.
