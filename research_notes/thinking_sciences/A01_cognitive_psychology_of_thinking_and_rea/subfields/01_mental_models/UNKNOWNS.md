## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Is the "model method" (95% vs 63%) real? The source is an unpublished 1999 thesis and the author's reports disagree. Unblock: Bell's original data or an independent replication. Do not build on it.
- Independent human replication of illusions with pragmatic and item-order controls (Rips, Tabossi, Walsh, Koralus originals not read in full). Unblock: the full papers. It matters for item design, not for our bench.
- Girotto and Gonzalez 53% vs 8% and other second-hand figures. Unblock: open the originals.
- Why did the best of 38 models reach 91.7% when the mean was 40.6%? Unblock: per-model outputs from d01, or our own sweep over several models.
- Is the LLM illusion surface matching or a representation effect (d25)? Unblock: item sets that pull the two apart, and a way to see the model's representation (hidden states or probes we lack).
- Johnson-Laird's own item lists. Unblock: the original lists. Until then we write our own and note the difference.
- Fine-tuning and steering-vector fixes (NVC, content bias). Out of scope: no training or hidden-state access.

## Order to run

1. U1 (illusions vs controls, plain/warning/rubric): cheap, and it decides whether the whole illusion branch (U2, U3, U5) is needed for our model.
2. U4 (NVC option plus code counterexample search on 64 syllogisms): the gap is established, the fix is untested, and the code search is a cheap, strong operator.
3. U2 then U3 on the U1 items (code case table, then code trigger): the largest possible gain; run only if U1 shows a gap, and keep the table gated until its control cost is measured.
