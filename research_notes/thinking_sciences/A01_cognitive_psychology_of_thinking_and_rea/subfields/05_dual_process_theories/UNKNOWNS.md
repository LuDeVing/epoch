## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Real fast-correct share (K04): the 75-88% figure from Bago and De Neys 2017 is unverified. Unblock: open the paper or re-measure on our own families (U6).
- Human conflict detection (K03, K14): needs human timing or mouse data. A model has no response time. Unblock: use token entropy or sample disagreement as a stand-in (U2), and treat the human result as background only.
- Which theory is right on time order (fast-then-slow vs parallel): untestable in a model without a clean split of its processes. Unblock: test order-free vs fast-then-check as a design choice (U4), not as theory.
- 2026 frontier models on trap items: no published result. Unblock: our own run of U1 on the model we ship.
- The single-process critics' reply (Pennycook and colleagues to Melnikoff and Bargh) and Osman 2004 follow-ups were never read. Unblock: read them; this changes only U12.
- Shojaee 2025 three-regime claim is disputed (token limits, unsolvable instances). Unblock: read the replies, or run U4 with solvable items and generous caps.
- Methods that need weight training (Dualformer, ACPO, distillation): the scaffold cannot train. Unblock: a fine-tuning setup, or skip.
- Whether Liu 2025 tasks match our task classes (K26): needs a task-by-task map. Unblock: label each bench family against the six tasks; feeds U14.

## Order to run

1. U1 (principle-changed traps): cheapest and the biggest risk. Tells us if the base model falls for lures and if an S4 check is worth building.
2. U3 (structural gate on a mixed set): the S0 gate is the core of the design and K22 only covers maths. Includes the self-rated-difficulty arm.
3. U2 (sample disagreement vs class pass rate): reuses the U1 and U3 items and samples, so it is nearly free, and it decides whether S6 gets a signal at all.
