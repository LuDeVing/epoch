## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Does the Heit-Rotello effect hold for long, multi-premise, real-world arguments? Search stopped early. Unblock: natural-predicate arguments with human ratings, and the full text of Heit and Rotello (2010).
- Which human numbers are right? Rips (2001), Stephens, Dunn and Hayes (2018) and Singmann and Klauer (2011) are known only second-hand. Unblock: full-text reads.
- Trippas, Thompson and Handley (2017) sample sizes and conflict costs differ across two extractions. Unblock: one clean read.
- Cheng et al. Table 13 row and column mapping is garbled. Unblock: the original PDF table.
- Can any experiment separate one latent scale with two cutoffs from two latent scales? Ashby says state-trace analysis cannot count systems. Unblock: a better method. This is theory work, not a bench test.
- Why does written reasoning cut the content effect while the internal validity and plausibility signals stay aligned? Needs white-box access (steering, probes). A closed API agent cannot do this. Unblock: an open-weight copy.
- Is the plausibility pull a training-text artefact? Needs training data. Not testable here.
- Our bench may lack believability-matched item sets and human-rated induction-strength items. Unblock: build them from the U1 design, with many more believable-conclusion items (the 14-model study had only 38 of 160).

## Order to run

1. U1 (wording, 48 items): measures the agent's own cutoff, yes-bias and sensitivity first. U2, U3 and U4 reuse its data, and every later test needs this baseline.
2. U5 (form-first or assume-true vs plain): the biggest likely gain per token, and it decides whether O3 gets a new step.
3. U6 (short derivation vs bare verdict vs votes): settles the contested chain-of-thought result at equal tokens on a closed model, and checks G11. Add U10 (one worked example) to the same item pool, since it costs almost nothing.
