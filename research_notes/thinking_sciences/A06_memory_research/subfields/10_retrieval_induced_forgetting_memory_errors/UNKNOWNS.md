## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- A frontier or Claude-family RIF measurement: needs a model run budget and a lesson set with exact-match facts. Unblocked by building the U1 synthetic set.
- The full Li and Wang paper (no arXiv version). Need to read the method: item types, judge prompt, token cost. Unblock: ask the authors or find the OpenReview text.
- A post-2014 human RIF meta-analysis (not found; search was rate-limited). Needed only to size the human prior.
- Whether LLM RIF is attention dilution or context contamination (Li and Wang report 0.42x late-layer attention). Needs open-weight models with attention access; our main model is not.
- Interaction of sibling damage with retrieval top-k in our real memory store. Needs a store with usage logs over many runs, which we do not have yet.
- Whether the human closure effect has a prompt-level analogue. Needs a retention probe design that does not itself act as rehearsal.
- Judge-free scoring for elaboration tasks. Li and Wang is judge-scored; the bench needs an exact-match design to avoid judge bias (guardrail G5).

## Order to run
1. **U1 (sibling damage), with U13 on a second family.** Cheapest and decides whether anything else in this subfield matters; no schema step is needed if damage is under 5 pp.
2. **U2 (list-all fix after work).** Only if U1 passes; the one tested cure, one extra turn, so a cost check is trivial.
3. **U4 (source plus conclusion in lessons).** Independent of U1, cheap, and attacks the most reliable harm found (stale and lossy memory, K16, K19, K22).
