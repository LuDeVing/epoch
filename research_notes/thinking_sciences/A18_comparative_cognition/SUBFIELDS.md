# Comparative cognition: subfields

Comparative cognition studies thinking in non-human animals. Wikipedia lists its areas as neurobiology, learning and conditioning, insight and problem solving, communication, tool use and memory, and the evolution of cognition. It carries an old methods debate: anthropomorphism against parsimony (Morgan's canon). Emery and Clayton argue that corvids and apes share a "tool kit" of causal reasoning, flexibility, imagination and prospection. For us the methods debate is the most useful part, since it shows how to tell flexible reasoning from trained habit.

## Does not transfer

Species differences, ecology, brains, sociality and the evolutionary story are about animals. Animal tasks are physical, slow and small-sample. The value for us is method (controls), not findings about thinking operators.
