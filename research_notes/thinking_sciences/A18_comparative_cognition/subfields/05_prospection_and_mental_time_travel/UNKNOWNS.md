## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Raby 2007 and Correia 2007 primary counts: need a human with a browser and library access. Until then no jay effect size is usable.
- A pre-registered, larger-N western scrub-jay replication: no lab houses the species now (Farrar 2020). Cannot be unblocked by us.
- Full text of the two EFT meta-analyses: the OSF PDF was image-only. Needs OCR or a publisher copy to read moderator tables.
- Token cost of a trailing reminder on recent frontier models: no published measurement; our own run in U3/U4 is the only way.
- Whether TriggerBench scores are biased by its GPT-4o judge and LLM-written scenarios: needs a human-scored sample.
- Whether PM benchmark gains from ProMemAgent, PIS and Pro-Memory hold with other authors and on benchmarks they did not build: needs independent runs.
- Whether other frontier models match Claude on the CheeseBench radial-arm maze with more than 10 trials per cell: needs the benchmark code and budget.
- Whether the spoon-test result survives a training-history control: needs new crow data.
- Whether an LLM can be given a real "need state" rather than a persona prompt (the delay-of-gratification benchmark only role-plays): needs a design where the state changes the reward, not the instructions.

## Order to run

1. **U4, then U3.** Cheapest: it measures our baseline compliance on format constraints and decides whether reminders are worth testing at all. It also checks the reminder cost found in Mittal.
2. **U1 with U2 (ledger with and without evidence link).** Largest expected gain (up to 60+ points for small models, 10-20 for Claude models) and the only item with four independent preprints behind it. The evidence-link arm costs almost nothing extra.
3. **U12, then U11.** Both use the same synthetic items as U1. They show whether our agent has the context cliff and whether plain retrieval hurts, which sets how hard U1 should push.
