## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Soar's learned-rule match cost (Rete cost) has no counterpart. We would need a store of thousands of entries to see context or retrieval cost; our benches are small. Unblock: a synthetic stream of 1000 items from a generator.
- Whether chunking reduces the impasse rate itself (the uniform learning hypothesis). Needs impasse-class logging over a long stream with repeating classes. Unblock: a stream generator with controlled class recurrence.
- Power versus exponential form of learning curves needs per-run fits over many items; the human full texts (Heathcote 2000, Evans 2018, Rickard 1997) are unread.
- Natural staleness of entries (not injected errors). Needs a stream where facts change. Unblock: a drifting-rule item generator.
- Real Soar rule-level learning from an LLM (CogRec, NL2GenSym style). Needs a Soar runtime; only one puzzle family and the authors' data exist.
- Generalisation to reasoning tasks of per-subgoal grain findings that come from code and tool environments.
- Reasoning items lack cheap execution checks for many classes; the admission rule for no-check classes is untestable until we have an independent outcome check there.

## Order to run
1. C02.03-U5 (does a store help our model at all, with same-instruction empty and random arms): cheapest, and every later test is wasted if the empty arm captures the gain (K23).
2. C02.03-U2 (admission rule, with U4 token logging in the same run): decides what to write, and gives the net-token cost for free.
3. C02.03-U3 (precondition check against wrong application): the main risk of any store, and no study has measured the fix; run on the same stream with seeded bad entries.

U1 follows once U5 shows a gain; it is the Soar-specific idea and the highest-value open comparison.
