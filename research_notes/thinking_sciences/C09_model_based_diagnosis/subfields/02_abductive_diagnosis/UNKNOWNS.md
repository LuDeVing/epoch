## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Classic papers (Reiter 1987, de Kleer-Williams 1987, Console-Torasso 1991) not read in print; unblock by getting full text from a library or open copies, then confirm K01-K03 formal statements.
- Physical, circuit or mechanical domains with executable models: the bench has no simulator items yet; unblock by building a small deterministic fault-injection generator (Elenchos style) with known ground truth.
- Interacting faults: no known method; needs a model of interaction (not additive) and ground truth; unblock by generating puzzle families with controlled interaction.
- Leakage-controlled medical diagnosis sets: unblock with held-out case sets and candidate-space isolation (b23).
- Newest frontier models: most evidence is from 2024-2026 preprints with different model lists; unblock by running our own model on matched items.
- Whether the detect-vs-attribute gap holds for our specific agent: needs the two-score item format of U1-U4.
- Whether enforced code checks outperform the same rule stated as text (no clean matched test exists, K11): unblock by U8's four-arm design.
- Whether the Ab representation matters beyond structure: compare Ab table against another equal-structure table (hypothesis, evidence for, evidence against).

## Order to run
1. U3 (code simulator/diff vs prose simulation): cheapest, largest known effect on easy cases, and it decides whether the rest of the loop lives in code.
2. U1 (Ab table vs prose): no direct study exists, is nearly free to run, and defines the hypothesis record all later tests build on.
3. U2 (forced rival step vs best-of-2): addresses the best-documented failure (premature commitment) and its result decides whether U4 and U5 are worth the cost.
