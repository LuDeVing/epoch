## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **de Kleer and Williams 1987 and the ATMS papers are unread.** All our statements on priors, Bayes updating and entropy probe choice are second-hand (K25). What would unblock: a library copy of the Artificial Intelligence 32 papers (the publisher returned 403 and the mirror 404). Until then, do not cite GDE for numbers.
- **No end-to-end study of conflict plus hitting set over several failed attempts of one model exists** (K23). Our U2 would be the first. What would unblock a comparison: our own bench with logged attempts and a code checker.
- **Independent check of the corrected hitting-set algorithms.** Pill et al. (2011, 2012, 2016) were not opened (the 2016 PDF was damaged). What would unblock: a working download, or our own brute-force property test (cheap, do it anyway).
- **A noisy-oracle version of QXR with current models.** He et al. flag it as open. We can test it (U9), but the result will only cover the models we can afford.
- **Real-trace labels.** Every number about caps, empties and ranking is from our toy simulation (d16, d17). It needs hand labels of true error steps on at least 100 traces. What would unblock: a labelling pass (cost is human time).
- **Why high effort collapses some models** (K35). Needs per-model logs separating overflow, parse failures and wrong answers. Blocked on U10.
- **Replication of every 2026 LLM-plus-conflict result.** Sarkar (n=14 infeasible items), He (3 strong models), Sztyber-Betley (hand-made examples, large sd), SemLoc (250 programs), Karimi (12 domains). None replicated. We can partly replicate on our bench (U3, U9).
- **Counterfactual toggles on prose.** About 14,400 rollouts per trace; cannot run at equal tokens on long chains. What would unblock: a cheap proxy, such as coarse steps (5 to 10) with 3 to 5 rollouts each.
- **Delta debugging and spectrum baselines on reasoning text.** No head-to-head found. What would unblock: our own implementation of Ochiai over a check-by-step matrix (cheap), as an extra arm in U2.
- **GPT-4-era evidence against the schema bet** (Stechly: sampling matches feedback loops). It is not known whether this holds for 2026 models. U2 is the test.
- **Whether prose steps have any "normal behaviour" model.** Without one, consistency-based diagnosis reduces to hitting sets over check supports. We may be doing "spectrum-style blame" and calling it diagnosis. U2's B2 arm will show whether the diagnosis part adds anything.
- **VERGE ablation row for AR-LSAT (91.7 vs 83.0)** was not re-read. Direction is unclear. Re-open the paper before using U14.

## Order to run

1. **C09.01-U1 (empty and size-1 rates on 100 real traces).** Cheapest, log-only, and it decides whether anything else in this file can work. If conflicts are mostly empty, build step-level checks first.
2. **C09.01-U2 (hitting-set blame vs model blame vs cheap rule vs best-of-N).** The whole bet. Include the "last step of smallest failed check" arm, because the toy says it may tie, and G7 says we keep the cheaper element.
3. **C09.01-U4 (cap recall on the same traces).** Reuses U1 data, and sets the default cap and the raise rule that U2 needs. Run U4 inside the U1/U2 pass so the labelling cost is paid once.

Always run the brute-force property test of the hitting-set code (DEEP.md section 8, E11) before any of the three.
