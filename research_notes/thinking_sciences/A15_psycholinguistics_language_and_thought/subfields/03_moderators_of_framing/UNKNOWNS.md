## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Independent human replication of Druckman 2001 (credible advice). None found. Needs a new human study or a pre-existing dataset; we cannot run it. Until then the credible-advice claim stays single-source and U2 stands on analogy.
- Real stakes. All LLM framing tests are stake-free or hypothetical. Unblocked by a benchmark where the answer changes a scored outcome the model sees.
- Mechanism of human time pressure (attention vs non-engagement). Needs eye-tracking data; for the LLM side we only have an indirect analogue (token cap, verbal "hurry" line).
- Whether alignment training or maths-reasoning training causes the reverse or null direction. Needs paired base/instruct/reasoning checkpoints; only open models allow it.
- Whether attribute, goal and emphasis frames behave like probability frames under canonicalisation. No test exists; needs new item sets.
- Per-language frame gaps in LLMs. BiasLab (d09) reports no per-language numbers; full text and data may unblock this.
- Human age and numeracy moderation (b19, b20 are abstract-level and conflict). Low value for the agent.
- Unopened items (Macmillan-Scott, Nguyen, Liu ethics 2026, MedMisBench full text, Keysar 2012 full text, Wharton persona study). Opening them could change K08, K19 and U12.

## Order to run
1. **U8 (model-class gap check).** Cheapest: one plain run on 60 numeric pairs and 30 vignettes tells us whether any mitigation is needed at all, and it fixes the noise floor for every later test.
2. **U1 (canonicalise vs warning vs reframe-compare at equal tokens).** Highest expected gain: the only large, code-side effect found (36.81% to 6.68%), and it decides whether S1 gets an input-hygiene step.
3. **U3 (label and authority strip rule), run together with U5 (blind vs named).** Both reuse the U1 item set, both cost little, and they decide whether the "strip labels and user beliefs" rule becomes a hard guardrail and whether our frame benchmarks are valid.
