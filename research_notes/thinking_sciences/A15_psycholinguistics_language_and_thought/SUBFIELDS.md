# Psycholinguistics, language and thought: subfields

This field asks how language and thought affect each other. One branch tests whether the language you speak shapes cognition (linguistic relativity). Another studies how wording changes judgement within one language (framing, question wording). A third studies how sentences are understood. Casasanto divides relativity work into effects of thinking for speaking and effects beyond language use, which he calls Whorfian psychophysics. The current view, per the Wikipedia summary, is that the strong form is rejected and a weaker, domain-specific form has experimental support. For us, wording effects matter most, because a model only reads words.

## Dig into next

**1. Framing and wording effects on a model.**
- Questions: Which wording changes (option order, loaded verbs, gain or loss) move a model's answers on our classes? Can code build a neutral second wording and treat disagreement between wordings as an impasse signal (G1)?
- Evidence needed: paired item sets; our paired runs at equal tokens. Wikipedia reports that credible information removes framing in people; test whether it does in models.

**2. Presupposition and suggestive cues.**
- Questions: Does moving a stated guess or leading phrase to a marked block cut anchoring? Which cue types matter most?
- Evidence needed: Loftus-type studies (to be opened); our planted-hint tests.

**3. Pragmatic misreading.**
- Questions: Which misreadings are common (nearby question, missed negation)? Does code-checked restatement catch them?
- Evidence needed: Ferreira on shallow processing (to be opened); our error logs.

## Does not transfer

Claims that speakers of different languages see colour, space or time differently depend on lifetime learning of one language. A model has seen many languages at once. Cross-language effects are modest even in people. Developmental language acquisition and bilingual emotional distance do not apply.
