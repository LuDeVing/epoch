# Cognitive linguistics: subfields

Cognitive linguistics holds that language reflects how people structure concepts, not a separate module. Wikipedia divides it into two areas: cognitive semantics (meaning as conceptual structure) and cognitive approaches to grammar (the language system). Its best-known ideas are conceptual metaphor and conceptual blending. Blending is described as a set of steps, which makes it the most procedural part. Claims that thought is grounded in the body are weaker, and the main critics say blending lacks testable hypotheses.

## Does not transfer

The Wikipedia overview notes that image schemas and usage-based models were barely explained there. Blending examples are hand-picked riddles and phrases, not tests of reasoning gains.
