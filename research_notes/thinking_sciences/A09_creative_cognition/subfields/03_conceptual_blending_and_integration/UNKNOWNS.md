## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- **Human gold for blend quality.** KOMBINE's judge check used one rater. We need at least two human raters on 100 blends for U2 and U4. Unblock by recruiting two raters or by using the gold from a published set.
- **Original numbers for the distance effect.** Estes and Ward (2002) and Wilkenfeld and Ward (2001) were abstract-only. Unblock by getting the full papers (library or author copy).
- **Gibbs (2000), Coulson and Matlock (2001), McCubbins and Turner (2013), Eppe (2018).** Paywalled. They may hold experiments on which blending patterns are easier, which would feed U11.
- **Optimality principles (topology, unpacking) as an automatic score.** Only a poster without results exists (d18). We would first have to build the scorer and test it against human value judgments.
- **Expert cases with an explicit generic-space step.** None found. It belongs to the discovery case studies subfield.
- **Hampton replications since 2010.** None found, in humans or LLMs. A small human study would be needed.
- **Why the generic-space step fails.** KOMBINE's explanation (lack of experiential knowledge) is untested. A test would give the model retrieved domain facts for each input before the frame step.
- **Text-modality evidence for order and dominance.** Image results only (U10 tests it, but that is a first look).

## Order to run
1. **U1** (structured vs plain at equal tokens). It is the central missing arm. If structure does not win, most of the rest of this list is moot (G7).
2. **U2 with U4** (code frame check and emergent-claim check, with human gold). Both are cheap and give the guards the schema needs under G2 and G5. They also provide the scoring used in all later tests.
3. **U3** (best-of-5 bridges with a code filter). The best-of-N gain is the largest effect in the evidence (+17 points), and it tells us whether the budget should go to sampling frames rather than refining.
