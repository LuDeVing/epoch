## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- CaRT replication on a second base model and domain: needs a fine-tuning setup and a success-rate oracle. We have neither. Unblock: a small open model plus a cheap rollout oracle on one of our checked task families.
- Probe-based stopping compared with prompted-judge stopping: needs hidden-state access. Unblock: run an open-weight model locally and train a linear probe on its intermediate-answer positions.
- RL on the stop decision with a token-cost-discounted reward: needs training. Unblock: only if the project later fine-tunes anything.
- Joint learning of what to ask and when to stop (the CaRT authors' own claim): needs a trainable asker and a simulated answerer.
- False-stop rate of confidence, probe and judge rules measured on one shared benchmark: needs the open-weight setup above.
- PeerConf and MARS numbers: unopened (NeurIPS page returned 404); needs the paper to appear on arXiv.
- Forward citations of CaRT: APIs were rate-limited; recheck Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex and Google Scholar when quotas return.
- Human optimal-stopping data to compare with LLMs: behavioural journals were not searched; needs a targeted search outside arXiv.
- CaRT's own test-set mismatch (100/200 in the text vs 121/93 in the appendix): needs the authors' code or a reply.

## Order to run

1. C12.09-U4 (false-stop rate by difficulty): it is a pure logging and analysis job on runs we already make, and it decides how aggressive every other stop rule may be.
2. C12.09-U3 (window or Bayesian agreement stop vs fixed N): the best-supported rule, cheap to code, and it gives the first equal-token result on our own items with correlated samples.
3. C12.09-U1 (forced answer after k no-gain steps): the cheapest harness change with a pre-registered replication behind it, and it directly targets the failure of agents that keep going on useless evidence.
