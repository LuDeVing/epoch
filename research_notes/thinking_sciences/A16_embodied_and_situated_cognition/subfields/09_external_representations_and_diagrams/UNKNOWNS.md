## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- True representational isomorphs of one puzzle (cups vs pegs) for an LLM: no item set exists. We would need generators for three surface forms and a validator proving equivalence (K23).
- Larkin and Simon (1987) full text, and Zhang and Norman exact means or effect sizes: need library access or an author copy. Until then no human effect size can be quoted.
- Human replications or failed replications of both founding papers: none found in 800 to 1,000 scanned citing titles. Needs an abstract-level screen of the full citation lists.
- VoT, WoT or Sketchpad on a 2025-26 reasoning model: needs a rerun of those protocols on our model, native reasoning on and off.
- Token cost of a code state check: no source measured it. Our harness must log it.
- Why LLMs fail on scattered facts (retrieval in context or reasoning): needs attention or probing access we do not have.
- Image arms need a vision channel in the bench. If the target model is text-only, U13 and the image arms of U3 are out of scope.

## Order to run

1. A16.09-U1 (checker vs rules in prompt): cheapest, the clearest human result (zero errors), and the harness can already check puzzle moves in code.
2. A16.09-U4 (redraw plus code check): the most-cited method, but its drawn states were right only 24-51% of the time, so the check decides whether it is usable.
3. A16.09-U3 (text vs drawn crossover by size): sets the O6 routing rule and tells us whether U5, U6 and U9 are worth running.
