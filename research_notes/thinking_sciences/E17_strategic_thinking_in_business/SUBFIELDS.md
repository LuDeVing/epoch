# Strategic thinking in business: subfields

This field studies how organisations and leaders decide where to compete and how. It divides into strategy-formation process research (Mintzberg's ten schools), scenario planning, planning and its measured effect, managerial cognition, decision processes, and analysis tools. The best evidence found is a 2019 meta-analysis of strategic planning (about 9,000 organisations) reported on Wikipedia. It found a positive effect on performance, and that benefits depend on formal analysis, comprehensive option generation and careful stakeholder management, not on having a plan alone. For scenario planning the opened page says evidence is largely anecdotal. I could not run the extra searches for controlled studies because the search budget ran out, so that gap is open.

## Dig into next

1. **Planning-performance research.** Questions: Which of the moderators (formal analysis, comprehensive option generation, stakeholder management) have a single-agent analogue, probably option generation? Does the effect hold for comprehensive option generation specifically? Evidence needed: the meta-analysis itself (authors and effect size not on the opened page), then S3 tests of more versus fewer options.
2. **Scenario planning.** Questions: Which step creates the benefit: cutting 7 to 9 stories to 2 or 3 distinct ones? Can code check distinctness? Evidence needed: controlled studies of scenario effects on judgement, which I could not search for.
3. **Assumption testing.** Question: Can a plan become a list of assumptions ranked by test cost and tested in order? Evidence needed: McGrath and MacMillan; cases with outcomes.

## Does not transfer

Positioning, stakeholder power, culture, resource allocation and board politics are organisational. Strategy research relies on cases and surveys with survivor bias. The de Geus audit suggests scenarios mainly changed conversations, a social benefit a single agent lacks. Scenarios can be self-fulfilling.
