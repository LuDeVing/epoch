## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Real instruments with noisy readings. Almost every result uses a simulated answerer and a closed list. Unblock: a seeded simulator with noise levels, then a real tool set (code repos with injected faults).
- Real human efficiency of half-split. Goldbeck 1957 and Gugerty 2007 were not reachable; Schaafstal 2000 is abstract only. Unblock: obtain full text through a library; or accept machine-side evidence only.
- Whether the model's simulated outcome tables are good enough. Needs ground-truth likelihoods on a real system first.
- Multiple simultaneous faults. All our tests assume a single fault; multiple-fault diagnosis is super-exponential. Unblock: pair with the consistency-based diagnosis subfield.
- Intermittent and flaky tests. Bisect and greedy gain break under flakiness (c18). Unblock: a flaky-test generator.
- Per-step vs end-to-end accounting. The 94.3% per step figure is our own arithmetic. Unblock: instrument per-step verdicts in T10.
- Token cost of the selector. BED-style scoring is 25 to 36 times slower in wall clock; tokens not reported. Unblock: count tokens for each arm in T1.
- Dollar prices to token/time prices transfer. Needs T7.

## Order to run
1. E08.04-U1 (core test): everything else depends on whether code-chosen tests beat model-chosen tests at equal tokens; no study has measured this.
2. E08.04-U2 (filter vs scoring): cheapest form of the gain. If the filter alone works, we add it with almost no token cost.
3. E08.04-U4 (no-fault exit): false faults of 11 to 24 percent are a measured, expensive failure with an easy fix to try; it needs only a checklist.
