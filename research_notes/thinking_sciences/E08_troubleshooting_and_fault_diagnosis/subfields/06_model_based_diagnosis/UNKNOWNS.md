## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Real device or service models: our bench has puzzles, not systems with a structural model. Unblock: build a small generated circuit and rule-base suite (U1) and one real tool task with a known dependency graph.
- Dynamic and sequence faults (Grastien-style hypothesis spaces): no bench items. Unblock: add event-sequence diagnosis items.
- Human oracle error rates and LLM oracle error rates on probe queries: needs a probe-answering model with logged mistakes. Unblock: run U6 with a real LLM answerer, not injected errors.
- Original papers not read in full (Davis, Genesereth, Bylander, de Kleer 1992): abduction hardness stays secondary-sourced. Unblock: obtain the PDFs.
- Leak-free fault-localisation data for 2026 models: Defects4J results risk contamination. Unblock: new bugs written after the model cutoff.
- Direct equal-token literature test of LLM + hitting set: none exists; our U1 is the first.
- Whether 2026 reasoning models close the gap with exact solvers at 5 to 20 diagnoses (d01 used o3-mini): rerun the d01 steps with our current model.

## Order to run
1. U1 (code hitting set vs LLM-only): it decides whether the code-run reasoner is worth building at all, and its circuit set serves U2, U4, U6, U7.
2. U3 (gated acceptance): cheapest test, reuses existing puzzle and ARC items, and could change G2 for the whole agent.
3. U2 (code entropy probes): largest measured effect in the literature (+35 points) and it reuses the U1 candidate sets.
