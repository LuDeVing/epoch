## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- A direct test of Roediger-style test vs restudy inside an LLM context: needs a protocol where the model truly loses access to the text (context reset or a truncated note) and a held-out question set. Unblocked once U1 is built.
- Large pre-registered multi-site replication of Roediger and Karpicke 2006: not found. Needs a human lab; we cannot run it. Treat the effect as well supported but not formally replicated at scale (K35).
- Publication-bias-corrected Rowland 2014 and Adesope 2017 estimates: full texts were not opened for bias analysis. Unblocked by access to the papers or a re-analysis.
- Whether PIT and Self-Tuning gains hold on 2025-2026 frontier models. Needs a fine-tuning setup our scaffold does not use. Also not separable from format matching (d12).
- Whether the human fluency illusion appears in LLM forecasts of their own accuracy (partly U3).
- Feedback alone vs retrieval alone: the no-study-with-feedback preprint is abstract-only (d19), and Rowland's delayed-feedback cell is k=6 (b02).
- Randomised evidence that LLM-written quizzes match instructor quizzes on learning (U11). Needs human subjects.
- The two lines of evidence that retrieval changes forgetting rate (d10) were not followed up.

## Order to run
1. **U1 (reconstruct vs re-read after a context reset).** It is the one direct test of the effect in a setting where an agent can actually "forget", and it is cheap. A null here lets us close the topic.
2. **U9 (gated lesson rules with a leak-free split).** The largest reported gain (+10) and the most useful if real. It also guards against the leakage artifact that faked a memory effect (d04).
3. **U7 (success-bin analysis of self-made checks).** Reuses runs from U1 and U9 at almost no extra cost, and it tests whether the one robust human moderator (initial success) shows up in an LLM.
