# Cross-cultural cognition: subfields

Cross-cultural cognition compares how people from different societies perceive, categorise, reason and explain. Its best-known programme contrasts holistic thought (context, relations, change, dialectical reasoning) with analytic thought (objects, categories, rules, formal logic), mostly East Asian against Western samples. A second strand asks why the differences arise, and the social orientation hypothesis ties them to independent versus interdependent social life. Critics point to confounds and weak individual-level links. Direct transfer to a model is limited, but the contrast supplies candidate alternative framings to test.

## Does not transfer

Cultural values, self-construal, social norms, child rearing and group identity are about people. A model has no culture, only text from many. A claim that one style is best in general conflicts with our data that problem class decides. Within a culture, style barely predicts individual cognition, so even for people it is a weak guide.
