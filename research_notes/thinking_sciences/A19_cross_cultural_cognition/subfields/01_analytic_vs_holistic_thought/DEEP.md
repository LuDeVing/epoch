# Analytic vs holistic thought: deep document

*Subfield A19.01 of cross-cultural cognition. Built from evidence_a-d.jsonl (a01-a31, b01-b26, c01-c28, d01-d19), claims.jsonl (K01-K22) and critique.md in this folder. Marks: [V] opened by a research agent, [M] snippet or abstract only. Status: [E] established, [S] single-source, [C] contested.*

## 1. What it is

Analytic vs holistic thought is a theory of cultural difference in how people attend, categorise, explain and argue. It comes from Nisbett, Peng, Choi and Norenzayan (2001, Psychological Review 108, 291-310) [V, a01].

The two poles, in the authors' words:

- **Holistic thought**: orientation to the whole field and to relations between object and field. It relies on experience-based knowledge. It is dialectical: it expects change, accepts contradiction, and looks for a Middle Way.
- **Analytic thought**: detaches the object from its context. It assigns the object to a category. It uses rules and formal logic and tries to avoid contradiction.

The claim is that East Asians lean holistic and Westerners lean analytic. The authors say the difference is in kind, not only in degree (b01). It covers both perception (what you look at) and reasoning (how you argue).

The paper is a narrative review. It has no pooled effect size. It mostly summarises small student samples from two or three countries. It says it "will not provide details about samples" (a01). The authors also say North Americans may differ from East Asians more than Europeans or Latin Americans do.

Why it matters for our agent: it supplies two named framings of a problem, for use as alternative framings in O6 (change representation) and O8 (reframe the goal).

- Analytic framing: object + category + rule.
- Holistic framing: field + relation + context + change.

The honest summary: the human evidence is real but weaker and less coherent than its fame suggests. The LLM evidence is contradictory. No study shows that switching between the two framings improves reasoning accuracy. So the framings are a candidate to test, not an element to add.

## 2. History and theories in depth

### 2.1 Roots

- **Field dependence**: Witkin and Berry (1975) found farmers more field dependent on the rod-and-frame test than hunters, herders and industrial peoples. Orthodox Jewish boys were more field dependent than secular Jewish boys, with IQ controlled (a02). Only Westerners and migratory foragers sit consistently at the field-independent end (a19).
- **Categorisation**: Chiu (1972) gave triads to children. Chinese children grouped by relation ("the mother takes care of the baby"). American children grouped by category ("both are adults"). Ji and Nisbett repeated it with adults in their own languages (a09, b03).
- **Attribution**: Choi and Nisbett used the Jones-Harris attitude paradigm. Koreans who had written a forced-side essay inferred weaker attitudes in the target than Koreans in the standard condition. Americans inferred equally strong attitudes in both (a06). Morris and Peng found Americans explained mass murders by dispositions and Chinese by situations and relations.
- **Dialectics**: Peng and Nisbett (1999). Chinese gave Middle Way answers to mother-daughter conflicts. Americans picked a side (a11).

### 2.2 The 2001 theory

The paper proposes a causal chain (a02):

1. Social organisation: personal agency (Greece) vs collective harmony (China).
2. What people attend to.
3. Metaphysics: discrete objects vs a continuous field.
4. Tacit epistemology.
5. Cognitive process.

The origin is "distally materialistic but proximally social": Chinese rice-farming cooperation and a centralised state, against Greek herding, trade and debate. The authors call the origin story speculative. It rests on secondary historians.

### 2.3 Environment and "point of view" (2003)

Nisbett and Masuda (2003, PNAS 100, 11163) [V, a13-a16] added the physical environment.

- Miyamoto and Nisbett photographed post offices, schools and hotels in New York and Tokyo, Ann Arbor and Hikone, and Chelsea and Torahime. Japanese scenes had 32 percent more objects (edge counts) than American scenes at every town size. Students of both nations rated Japanese scenes as more cluttered and less clearly bounded.
- East Asian students drew landscapes with a higher horizon and more objects.
- Japanese portraits show the person small in the frame. Americans take close-ups.
- Ji et al.: Chinese expected more change in a described state (for example a romance) than Americans.

### 2.4 Competing and later explanations

- **WEIRD critique** (Henrich, Heine, Norenzayan 2010) [V, a19, a20]: Arabs, Malaysians, Russians, Turkish herders vs farmers, Mapuche and Sangu farmers also look holistic. So the contrast may be West vs most of the world, with Westerners at the extreme. Regional gaps inside countries (Northern vs Southern Italy, Hokkaido vs mainland Japan) run the same way.
- **Rice vs wheat** (Talhelm et al. 2014, Science) [a21]: within China, rice provinces were more holistic and interdependent than wheat provinces, even across neighbouring counties on the border. N=1162 Han Chinese undergraduates. Climate, modernisation and pathogen theories did not fit.
- **Relational mobility** (San Martin, Schug, Maddux 2019, six studies) [M, d13]: low relational mobility predicts more holistic attention, mediated by external locus of control. Study 4 manipulated it. A socio-ecological rival to rice farming.
- **Self-construal priming** (Oyserman and Lee 2008, 104 experiments) [M, d11]: priming independence or interdependence moves cognition. Effects moderate for relationality and cognition, small for self-concept and values.
- **Language** (Ji, Zhang, Nisbett 2004 [M, c25]; Rhode et al. 2016 [V, a23]): the testing language shifts triad choices and picture description.

### 2.5 The theory's own repair (2010-2020)

Na et al. (2010) [V, b05, b06] showed that ten cognitive-style measures barely correlate within a group. Na et al. (2020) [V, d08], from the same school, accept this and argue for stable individual profiles across measures rather than one trait. That is a retreat from "a thinking mode" to "a bundle of separate habits".

### 2.6 Measurement tools

- **Analysis-Holism Scale (AHS)**, Choi, Koo, Choi (2007) [M, a31]: 24 self-report items, four factors: attention, causality, change, contradiction.
- **Triadic Categorization Task (TCT)**: relation vs category pairing.
- **Dialectical Self Scale (DSS)** and **Self-Construal Scale (SCS)**.
- Performance tasks: rod-and-frame, framed line, Navon, embedded figures, change blindness, eye tracking, fish-scene recall and recognition.
- **AHS-R** (Ayturk et al. 2026) [M, d14]: splits Midway and Contradiction and drops Perception of Change.

## 4. Mechanisms and models

Each is described so an engineer could turn it into a prompt step or a code check. None is tested as an accuracy gain for an LLM.

### 4.1 Attention allocation (focal vs field)

Model: a scene has a focal object and a context. A reader spends attention on both. Analytic readers front-load the object; holistic readers add the field later. Chua et al. found a time course: Americans favoured the object at 420-1100 ms, Chinese the background at 1100-3000 ms (a17). Even this has a failed replication (b07).

Implementation for a text agent:
1. List the entities (object).
2. List the conditions, background facts and relations between entities (field).
3. Ask which of the two lists the question depends on.

Code can check coverage: every sentence of the prompt should map to a listed entity or relation. That is a checklist, not a style claim.

### 4.2 Categorisation: category vs relation

The triad: given three items, pair two. Analytic pairs by shared category rule. Holistic pairs by relation (monkey-banana). Model: two matching functions over the same items, similarity-by-feature vs association-by-link.

Implementation: for any grouping question, compute both groupings and compare. If they agree, the question is not framing sensitive. If they disagree, that is a signal (G1) to look closer. Items come from Ji et al. 2004; Jin et al. used a 20-item version with 10 real items (c02).

### 4.3 Rule-based vs experience-based reasoning

Norenzayan et al.: rule-based learners fail when rule and similarity conflict; exemplar-based learners do not (a10). Both groups were equally accurate on abstract logic, so this is a default, not an ability (a10; b09 shows Asian samples even more accurate on abstract syllogisms).

Implementation: two solvers. One states a rule and applies it. One retrieves the nearest solved cases and adapts. Code compares outputs. This resembles S2 (candidate rules) plus a case memory.

### 4.4 Causal attribution and field-based explanation

Holistic attribution adds situational and relational causes. Boundary: the effect shows for ambiguous events and vanishes for clear mechanical ones (a07). Koreans responded to a salient situational constraint (a06).

Implementation: when a problem has an ambiguous cause, ask for a second explanation list that names only context and interactions. For clear mechanical problems skip it.

### 4.5 Dialectical reasoning (change, contradiction, Middle Way)

Three components: expect change, tolerate contradiction, seek a middle. The AHS-R separates Midway and Contradiction and drops Perception of Change (d14). Spencer-Rodgers et al. confirm the construct at country level (d15).

Implementation: a contradiction-tolerant pass. Hold two conflicting claims, state what each is true of, and test whether both can hold under a narrower reading. This is separate from context attention (K21). Note the cost recorded by Nisbett: holism makes any outcome seem explainable, so hindsight surprise falls (a08). Risk for an agent: excusing a real failed check. G2 and G3 must still bind.

### 4.6 Why the two systems may not be one thing

Within-person r=0.038 means "holistic" is a bundle of separate habits (b05, d08). A model with one switch is wrong. A better model: independent per-task framing choices, selected by task features (K22, d09, d19).

### 4.7 Mechanisms of cultural cause (context only)

Social orientation (a02), rice labour (a21), relational mobility with external locus of control (d13), language (a23, d12), the visual environment (a13). None dominates. Priming evidence is mixed (b13 vs d11).

## 5. What holds up

### 5.1 Replicates

- **Group differences on some tasks.** Picture description by language (d=-2.15, a23), fish-scene style, triad choice in many studies, the DSS at country level (d15), environment scene counts (a13).
- **Weak individual coherence.** Found three ways: Na 2010 (r=0.038), Klein 2009 (r=-.14, -.11), Na 2020, Lacko 2023 and 2025 [E, K04].
- **Not just East vs West.** Other groups differ too; Westerners are the extreme (a19) [S].
- **Boundary conditions.** Effects shrink when the feature is made salient (a10, b04), when events are clear and mechanical (a07), and when task goals matter (d09).

### 5.2 Failed or weakened

- **Eye tracking**: Evans 2009 null (b07). Cenek 2020 partial (d07). No large preregistered replication exists (K03).
- **Change detection**: Miller 2025 null for culture (d09).
- **Priming**: Magid 2017 null with larger n than most priming studies; face-scan priming null (b13, b14, d10).
- **Preregistered tests**: Lacko 2025 (N=993) and Lacko 2023 (N=392) do not support the theory as measured (d05, d06).
- **Multi-country battery**: Rosetta Phase I found mixed results with two reversals (b08, b09).
- **Rice theory**: mixed. Holism survives re-analysis; individualism does not; Galton's problem stands (b11, b12).
- **Publication bias is untested.** Published d=0.62 may be inflated. No bias-corrected re-analysis of Oyserman and Lee was found (d11).
- **Language effect on description may be about speech, not seeing.** Description differed, recall did not (a23). Word order may drive "mentioned first" (a28).

### 5.3 Moderators

Task salience, event ambiguity, goal relevance (d09), stimulus composition (Goh, Tan, Park 2009, quoted in b07), sample type (students, graduate students living abroad), social class (b06), region, relational mobility (d13), language of testing (a23).

### 5.4 Interventions

- **Worked (small, human)**: verbal primes shifted gaze and description in children (d12). Language of testing shifts description (a23).
- **Did not work**: activity priming (b13, d10), individualism/collectivism priming on face scanning (b14). Oyserman and Lee report priming effects in 104 experiments, but the larger recent tests find none.

### 5.5 Overall verdict

Weak but real group differences on certain tasks. No single trait. Big claims about causes and about eye movement are unsettled. The self-report AHS is not a safe probe (d06). Task-based probes (Navon, embedded figures) are better but still modest.

### 6.2 What these studies show together

- The same paradigm gives opposite verdicts: Jin (holistic), Atari (analytic, WEIRD), Hu (US model more relational on triads). Items, prompts, models and scoring all differ and none were controlled against each other (c27).
- Prompt-language effects are small and unstable (Sun and Wang vs Lu). Lu's items were withheld.
- Direction depends on task within one model pair (Hu). That fits "task and prompt matter more than model origin" but does not prove it.
- Self-report scales measure what the model says it is like, not how it reasons. GPT-3.5 answered "strongly agree" to almost everything (acquiescence), which inflated both SCS subscales at once; GPT-4 sat near human norms (c03).
- Jin's tiny SDs (0.10) come from 10 runs at temp 1 compared with a human population SD; the t-test overstates certainty (c01).
- Explanations offered: next-token prediction uses global context (Jin, a25); RLHF flattens variance into a helper persona (Hu, c17); English-heavy training (Atari). No ablation separates them.
- Cultural prompting shifts values but not to zero, and it can hurt some countries (c18).
- Image content mattered more than prompt language in captions (b18). The Japanese-trained VILA-JP was not more holistic than GPT-4o-JP (a28).
- Hu et al. include an author from the original theory's line. Effects are on generated counts at temp 1 with LLM judges; robustness to wording is untested.

### 6.3 Using the framing to improve reasoning

- No study was found that uses an analytic/holistic switch to raise accuracy on a reasoning benchmark (K17, c28). The search was limited, so this is absence in a limited search.
- Nearest analogue: step-back abstraction prompting (Zheng et al. 2024, d19). PaLM-2L MMLU Physics 66.4 to 73.2, Chemistry 70.9 to 81.8, GPT-4 Physics 69.4 to 84.5 (CoT 82.9), TimeQA 41.5 to 66.0, MuSiQue 35.5 to 42.6, StrategyQA 82.8 to 82.7. Gains are on principle-heavy tasks. No equal-token control against self-consistency.
- Multi-agent debate: no MAD method had a win rate above 20% against CoT in 36 scenarios. GPT-4o-mini MMLU: CoT 80.73, SC 82.13, SoM 74.73, Multi-Persona 75.47. Multi-Persona win rate 0%. Model heterogeneity helped (+6.4%, +8.2%). (Zhang et al. 2025, d17.) Smit et al. 2024: Multi-Persona about 7% worse in the default setup (d18). This binds G9.

| E3 Contradiction pass | Items with inconsistent premises or two plausible claims | plain; contradiction-tolerant pass; second sample | Accuracy and rate of wrongly excusing a failed check | Risk of excusing errors; keep only if accuracy rises and the false-pass rate does not |
| E4 Field-list step | Word problems with distractors or hidden conditions | plain; plain + entity list; plain + entity + field list | Accuracy; missed-condition rate | May help hidden-condition items; may hurt on distractor items (G11) |

Rules: pre-register the analysis. Use at least 3 prompt wordings per condition, because the LLM literature shows wording flips the verdict.

## 9. What does not transfer, and why

- **Culture itself.** The theory is about socialised humans. A model has no childhood in a rice or wheat society. Asking it to "be holistic" produces a style, not a thinking mode (K14, c17).
- **Eye movements and perception.** Text agents have no gaze. Attention allocation has no direct analogue.
- **Social causes** (rice, relational mobility, self-construal priming). No analogue, and priming itself is contested (b13).
- **Self-report scales.** They measure what a model says. Acquiescence was clear in GPT-3.5 (c03). The AHS is unreliable even in humans (d06).
- **Group comparison.** The human literature compares group means. Our agent needs a per-problem accuracy gain, which no human study measures.
- **Cultural "debiasing" by persona debate.** Gains are on bias scores judged by LLMs (c21), not on accuracy.
- **Both framings as a general upgrade.** The theory itself says each framing has costs: holism makes any outcome seem explainable (a08); the analytic stance gives no guarantee against bad inference (a11, weak counter-arguments made Americans more confident).
- **East-West labels.** Westerners are the outlier (a19). A reframing built on "Eastern" thinking imports a stereotype.
- **Large LLM effect sizes** such as d=2.42 are on generated counts and reflect model style, not culture (d02).

## 10. Open questions

1. Does a forced second framing raise accuracy at equal tokens over a second sample of the same framing? No study tests this.
2. Which task types favour a holistic framing? Only the analytic side has a nearest analogue (step-back).
3. Is LLM "cultural style" more variable across wordings than across models? Not run.
4. Is the Evans null on eye movements resolved by a large preregistered replication? None found. A direct large replication of Masuda-Nisbett recognition was also not found.
5. Do Oyserman and Lee priming effects survive bias correction? No re-analysis found. Does a prompt prime behave like a human prime in an LLM?
6. Is the cultural-persona debate gain from diversity or from extra samples? It lacks an equal-token control; generic debate does not beat sampling.
7. Does the dialectical component predict reasoning accuracy? Replicated as a construct (d14, d15) but never linked to accuracy.
8. Are the numbers of Lu et al., Sun and Wang and Sabir stable? They were carried over from earlier agents. Hu was re-checked (d01-d04).
9. What are the reliabilities (alpha) of the Navon and embedded-figure tasks? Not extracted.
10. Do the original Masuda and Nisbett 2001 N and effect sizes match the review percentages? Paper not read.
