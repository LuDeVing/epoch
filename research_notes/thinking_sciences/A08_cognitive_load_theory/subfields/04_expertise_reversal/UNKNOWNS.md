## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Whether the founding human effect sizes hold outside the Sweller network. No registered replication exists. We cannot run human studies. The 2025 meta-analysis is the best prior. Unblock: a community call for unpublished work, or a second meta-analysis with more databases.
- Whether element interactivity or knowledge is the true moderator in humans. Needs the 2017 Chen paper text and a meta-analysis that codes interactivity. Both are unavailable (paywall, not coded).
- Why SkillsBench tasks got worse. Per-task baseline versus delta is only in heat maps; three trials make small negatives noise. Unblock: the authors' per-task table, or rerun on our bench with more trials.
- A clean crossover study of scaffold strength by model strength at matched tokens. None exists in the literature. U1 and U3 are our version.
- Why self-written help fails (unused, displaced solving, locked-in errors). The audit covers 12 trajectories chosen for extreme gaps. Unblock: trajectory logs from our own runs (U6).
- Whether training-time hint fading (QuestA, Scaf-GRPO, HATCH) says anything about prompt-time fading. We do not train. Unblock: none needed unless we fine-tune.
- Whether LLM-simulated learners predict real learner results. They score with an LLM judge. Unblock: human data on the same tasks.
- Few-shot hurting reasoning models: the vendor gave no table. Unblock: our own U7.
- Calibration of "equal tokens" when a creator step or a shorter prompt is involved. Our proposal: total tokens per item, plus accuracy per token. Not validated by any source.

## Order to run

1. U1 (effect by pass-rate tercile): cheap, uses data we mostly have, and tells us whether there is any reversal to design for.
2. U8 (leave-one-out ablation at equal tokens): cheap, enforces G7 directly, and the only test here that needs no new design.
3. U2 (pass-rate-triggered fade vs fixed): the S0 rule that follows if U1 shows a gradient; needs 20+ tasks per class so batch it with U1's runs.
