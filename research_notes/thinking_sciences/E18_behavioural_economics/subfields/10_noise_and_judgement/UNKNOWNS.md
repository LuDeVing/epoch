## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Noise audits of LLMs in professional tasks (medicine, law, finance, underwriting) against human spread. No such study found. Needs human-expert rating sets for the same cases; unblock by finding a public dataset with multiple expert ratings per case.
- Primary numbers for the human noise claims (Noise book audits, Goldberg 1970, Grove full text, Vul and Pashler 2008, Galton). Needs the book or paper access; they matter only for motivation, not for the schema.
- Cause of hosted-API non-determinism (batching, co-mingled inputs). Needs provider-side access; we can only measure the floor, not fix it.
- Prompt-format sensitivity on Claude 4/5-class and GPT-5-class models. Existing numbers are old and mostly open models; unblock with U1 on our own stack.
- Equal-token tests on closed frontier models from other labs. Needs API budget and the same harness; unblock by running U3 on more than one model family.
- A principled rule for sample size per item and for an agreement threshold. Needs the U8 and U9 data across difficulty levels.
- Whether coded debiasing removes shared bias (acquiescence, order, verbosity) in averaged LLM judgements. Needs a bias-labelled item set.
- Conflict between Chiang and Lee 2023 (stable ratings) and Stureborg and Rating Roulette (low self-agreement). Needs the Chiang and Lee numbers; they were abstract-only.

## Order to run
1. U1 (noise audit): cheapest, and it tells us whether occasion, pattern or level noise is the one to fix before we buy any of the others.
2. U2 (both-order judging): cheap, established direction, and decides whether pairwise model verdicts stay in the loop at all.
3. U3 (vote vs long chain vs verifier at equal tokens): the biggest budget question and the largest gap in the literature; its result sets the default split for G11 and G12.
