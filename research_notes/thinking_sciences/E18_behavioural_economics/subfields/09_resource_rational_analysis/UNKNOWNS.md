## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Independent replication of the Lieder/Griffiths cost-sensitivity and anchoring results. Needs human participants; not our work. It would tell us how far to trust the human side. Unblock by checking for new replications each batch and treating the human side as single-lab until then.
- Independent measurement of time and error cost for LLM tasks (a price we can pre-register). Partial: tokens are a clean price, but the price of an error is a free choice. Unblock by fixing one error price per benchmark before any run.
- Source of the "86% / 55%" planning figures. The primary paper (d07) does not contain them. Unblock by finding the secondary source or dropping the figures.
- Sample sizes and full texts of Lieder et al. 2018 (d13) and Vul et al. 2014 (not read beyond abstracts). Unblock by opening the PDFs.
- Open-ended and agentic evidence for early exit (few results beyond SWE-bench, BrowseComp and chat). Unblock by building a 20+ task agentic bench with logged first-correct stage.
- Models with built-in budget awareness. Whether frontier models of late 2026 still follow presentation order. Unblock by repeating Fan et al. style exams with our model.
- Whether R3-Bench's offline oracle is a realistic live ceiling. Unblock by a live-policy oracle on our own sets.
- Training-based methods (RaM, L1, AdaptThink, ROI-Reasoning). They need weights. Unblock only if the project ever allows fine-tuning; until then use them as design hints.

## Order to run

1. E18.09-U1 (cheap difficulty gate for S0): S0 is the central design question of this subfield, and every other test needs a difficulty estimate, so its overhead and accuracy must be known first.
2. E18.09-U2 (stopping rules at equal tokens): the largest established savings are here, no source compares the rules, and the bench items needed are the same as U1.
3. E18.09-U3 (code-side shared-budget allocator): the failure of prompting is the best-established LLM finding of this subfield (71 of 72 cells, two independent sources), and a code allocator is cheap to build.
