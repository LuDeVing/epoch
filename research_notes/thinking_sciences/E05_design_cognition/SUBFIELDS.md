# Design cognition: subfields

Design cognition studies how designers think: how they frame ill-defined problems, generate and judge ideas, and move between problem and solution. Cross's work traces it to 1960s protocol studies of simulated computer-aided design. A useful split is three paradigms: Simon's problem solving, Schön's reflective practice, and situated cognition. Design problems have no single answer and no clean check, so evidence is weaker than in E01 to E03, but this field is the main source of the reframing and co-evolution ideas in our schema.

## Dig into next

**1. Framing and reframing.**
- What steps make a reframe (find the paradox, choose an analogy, state a frame, test its consequences)?
- Can a frame be a typed record that code compares with the old one?
- Evidence: Dorst's cases (not opened), plus the null result for LLM reframing already in the schema; needs a controlled test with a code check.

**2. Co-evolution versus problem-space-first.**
- Kokotovich argues for mapping the problem space first. Which order does better, and for what kind of task?
- Does restating the problem after a failed attempt beat restating before?
- Evidence: Maher and Poon, Dorst and Cross protocols, equal-token test.

**3. Fixation and analogy.**
- Which example types cause LLM fixation and which break it?
- Evidence: Jansson and Smith, Linsey, schema F05 sources.

## Does not transfer

Client relations, aesthetics, hand sketching, studio culture and team conflict are about people and practice. "Good design" is a judgement without one check. Co-evolution claims come mostly from case studies without controls. I could not open Cross's review (certificate error), so claims about it rest on the encyclopedia entry and the search snippet.
