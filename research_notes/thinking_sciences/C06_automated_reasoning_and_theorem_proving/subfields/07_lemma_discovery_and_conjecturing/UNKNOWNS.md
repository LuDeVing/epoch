## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- **Real hit rates of Graffiti and HR.** Only second-hand numbers ("about 80 papers", "20 OEIS sequences"). Would need the original papers or OEIS records.
- **Lemma truth rate in non-numeric plain text** (logic, geometry, combinatorics) where a small-case computation is not obvious. Would need items with machine-checkable small cases, or a formal checker.
- **Whether Seed-Prover and Hilbert gains come from lemmas or from stronger models.** Needs a lab that can hold prover and total tokens fixed. We cannot run these systems.
- **Judge calibration for "interesting" or "new" conjectures.** No gold labels. Would need human-rated conjectures in our domains.
- **Contamination.** We cannot tell if a lemma is derived or recalled in Mathlib-like domains. Would need freshly formalised or invented domains (the Lemmanaid AFP-2026 idea).
- **Why proofs in the conjecturing loop get longer** (harder or just wordy). Would need a difficulty measure independent of length.
- **Cross-problem transfer at scale.** Needs a long stream of related items and per-lemma logging that does not exist yet (add the funnel log first).
- **TheSy vs Hipster vs CVC4 conjecturing on one benchmark.** Tool-level question; low value for the agent.

## Order to run
1. **U2 (small-case check before relying on a lemma).** Cheapest. The formal evidence for filtering is the strongest (K02), and it produces the lemma funnel log every other test needs.
2. **U1 (lemma-first vs longer direct attempt, equal tokens).** The central open gap (K18). The answer decides whether any lemma operator is worth keeping.
3. **U3 (impasse-only vs always-on lemma step) together with U7 (cross-class transfer).** U3 protects G1 and G12 and is cheap once U1 exists. U7 decides whether the planned subgoal library is worth building.
