## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Formal conflict derivation for free-form language tasks.** We have no way to name the minimal set of responsible choices when the attempt is prose. Unblock: restrict to tasks with typed, replayable steps (code, grid transforms, plans), as in U4 and U5.
- **Cross-problem transfer of no-goods.** CDCL no-goods live inside one problem. Our S9 lessons are meant to travel across problems. Nothing in the SAT work tests that. Unblock: a class with many related items and a held-out split, after U1 passes inside one problem.
- **Effect on the strongest reasoning models.** Gains shrink with model strength (about 1 point for think-enabled models in one preprint). Unblock: run U1 and U3 on our final model before any other model, and report with intervals.
- **Subsumption and completeness of an LLM-side store.** SAT clauses subsume each other exactly. Language triggers do not. Unblock: define the trigger as a set of code-checkable features and test subsumption on those features only.
- **Heavy-tailed attempt lengths for language models.** Not measured by anyone. Unblock: log attempt length and success on our existing runs (free; part of U6).
- **Why learning loses in some SAT families** (sverif, termrw, crypto-type). Katebi's authors could not explain it. Unblock: instance-level study; low priority for our purposes since U2 is the practical gate.
- **Sources still unopened:** Pipatsrisawat and Darwiche 2011, Dechter and Frost on dead-end learning, ASP lazy nogood generation, AgentDebug full text. They might add numbers but are unlikely to change the tests above.

## Order to run

1. **C06.03-U2 (shortening ratio per class).** It is cheap, runs on failures we already have, and tells us where U1 is worth its cost.
2. **C06.03-U9 (exact-repeat blocker) with the heavy-tail logging of U6.** Near-zero cost, uses runs we already make, and sizes the repeat problem on our models.
3. **C06.03-U1 (coded no-goods vs free text vs resample) on the classes that pass U2, with U3's gate arm included.** It is the one missing experiment in the whole literature, and its answer decides whether the rest (U4, U5, U8, U11) is worth building.
