## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Human argument-mapping training effect (0.34 vs 0.70 vs 0.8 SD).** We need the primary papers (Twardy 2004, Alvarez 2007, van Gelder 2015); two were blocked or not retrieved. It tells us little about the agent, so low value.
- **A gold standard for free-form agent text.** Public corpora grade exact spans in essays or abstracts. Agent restatements have no gold span. We need a small hand-built set with human-marked premises and hidden premises (about 100 items, two annotators, kappa reported).
- **Semantic quality of hidden premises.** Gold warrants are rated acceptable only 45.7% of the time with kappa .18 to .31. We need a rating scheme with better agreement (for example forced pairwise choice) before we can score hidden premises.
- **Real linked versus convergent ground truth.** Theory itself disputes the label (Freeman, Yanal, Conway). We would need a task where the label has a checkable consequence, for instance premise removal changing a computed answer.
- **Founding-theory claims** (Toulmin 1958, Freeman 2011, Johnson and Blair) were not opened. They matter only for definitions, not for accuracy.
- **Independent replication of the 2026 preprints** (Pirozelli, Feng and Hunter, Ryu, Kim and Yang). Nothing to run; we can only rerun on our own items, which U1 to U5 do.
- **Significance of the gated-pass result.** Needs more attack items than UKP essays offers (42). Unblocked by building a larger attack set in U4.

## Order to run

1. **D04.01-U1 (restate-with-grounds vs plain vs token-matched vote).** It is the one question the whole subfield turns on, and the literature says "no accuracy gain" more often than yes. A null result saves us a slot in S1/S2.
2. **D04.01-U5 (code checks on restatements).** Cheap, deterministic, and it makes any restatement step safe whether or not U1 passes.
3. **D04.01-U3 (adversarial ARCT).** One zero-shot run each; tells us whether any hidden-premise number we use as evidence can be trusted before we build on U2 and U7.
