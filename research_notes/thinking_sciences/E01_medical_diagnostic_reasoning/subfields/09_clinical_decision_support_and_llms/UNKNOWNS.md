## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Human-in-the-loop effects (Goh, Bean, automation bias).** Needs physicians or lay users. Unblock with a small human pilot, or skip; the agent has no human consumer.
- **Real-patient, live-care performance.** All strong results use written cases. Needs real records with outcomes and clinical sign-off. Hager is the only real-record study and used 2023 open models.
- **Replication of Goh 2025 and of the Goh 2024 null.** Needs a physician trial with predefined prompts and trained users. Out of reach.
- **MAI-DxO ablation by role in the original setting.** Needs SDBench plus the gatekeeper and a published or rebuilt panel. Partly covered by U5 and U6 on our own cases.
- **Pre/post-cutoff accuracy of reasoning models on real, unpublished records.** Needs a private post-cutoff case set. Build one from cases published after the base model's cutoff.
- **Simulated patient validity.** Bean showed a simulator barely predicts human results. Needs human-played checks (B3 in DEEP.md section 8) before any ranking claim based on a simulator.
- **Calibration on open diagnosis.** Needs a large CPC set with graded top-1 and repeated samples. Covered in part by U8.
- **False-positive and healthy-case behaviour.** SDBench has no benign cases. Needs a mixed set with negatives.

## Order to run

1. **U3 (elicitation drop for the current base model).** Cheap (plain model only), and it decides whether U2, U9 and the whole interactive branch are worth the tokens.
2. **U4 (rerank stage for top-1 vs top-10).** The most consistent finding in the literature (top-1 well below top-10), cheap to build in code, and a direct fit to S7.
3. **U1 (evidence-tied challenger vs plain and vs free challenger).** The main orchestration claim in the field has no equal-token control, and the free-challenger risk (d05) means the answer is not obvious; run U7 on the same planted-hypothesis cases at the same time.
