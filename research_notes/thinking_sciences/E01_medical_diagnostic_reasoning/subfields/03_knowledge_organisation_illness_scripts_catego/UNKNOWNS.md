## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **A verified class library at scale.** We have no checked set of script-form entries (with frequencies and sensitivities). Unblock: build 100-200 entries from a licensed reference, with a clinician review of a sample.
- **Held-out cases that are free of leakage.** NEJM and case reports are public and may be in training data (leakage cut Recall@1 from 54.3% to 40.3%, d25). Unblock: cases written after the model cutoff, or a private set from a clinical partner.
- **Expert panels for SCT.** A reliable SCT needs about 100 items and 10-20 experts (b15). We have no panel. Unblock: recruit clinicians, or use published SCTs with released expert keys.
- **Real messy records.** Most LLM results are on clean vignettes; real-record results are worse (c05, d16). Unblock: access to de-identified notes (MIMIC-IV or ER-Reason style data).
- **Original numbers of the founding human studies.** Schmidt and Boshuizen 1993, Custers 1996, Brooks 1991 are abstract-only. Unblock: full-text access; matters only for the human side.
- **Third-party reruns of vendor scaffolds** (MAI-DxO, K17) and of Savage's prompts on current models. Unblock: run our own versions under U7 and U9.
- **Effect of scripts on human hand-off.** Goh and Healy disagree (K18). Not needed for the agent loop; would need clinician users.
- **Whether the agent should use the human intermediate effect at all.** No LLM analogue is known (U10 is the first test).

## Order to run

1. **U1 (class library vs free CoT at equal tokens).** Biggest expected gain and the central open gap; its design (held-out items, token matching) is reused by every other test.
2. **U3 (contrast entries vs plain entries), with U2 (exemplar, script, both) on the same items.** Cheap once U1 exists; the contrast field is the best-supported way to organise the library, and the exemplar arm answers gap 2 for free.
3. **U4 (graded prevalence prior) and U5 (hold step).** Both are code-side, cheap, and target documented failures (Safranek's 79.3% to 30.4% swing; 65.6% of wrong first diagnoses staying wrong).
