## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- **Printed Cognition 2008 text.** Publisher returned 403. Bilalic numbers rest on the in-press draft. Unblock: a library copy or an open version.
- **Luchins 1942 original and Saariluoma 1990.** Not opened. Unblock: scans or the secondary reviews with tables.
- **Lovett and Anderson 1996 (ACT-R).** Not opened; the model could give a way to simulate set strength from past success counts. Unblock: read it and implement a small version.
- **True masters at scale.** Sheridan and Reingold used lower-rated experts and found no gap; Bilalic found a big one. Needs a new human study; out of reach.
- **Attention in a model.** No way to see "gaze". Attention-weight or hidden-state probes need an open model with access; our bench uses an API model. Unblock: one open model run for the signal tests.
- **Contamination of public benchmarks.** mARC items are now public (a26); the 2026 gain may be partly memory. Unblock: write fresh trap/control items for our own bench.
- **MedEinst design from one group.** Benchmark and method share authors (c16). Unblock: replicate on our own twins (U6).
- **Real agent runs with accumulating memory.** Compliance data are injected memories on web tasks (d11). Unblock: a long-run bench with real retrieval.
- **Time and token cost of the set.** Some human results show cost only in time (b14, a21). Our bench logs tokens, so this can be checked in U1; it needs a per-item token record.

## Order to run
1. **U1 (in-context Luchins test).** It decides whether Einstellung from examples exists for our model at all, and gives the effect size that every other test is powered on.
2. **U3 (early signals, using U1 traces).** It costs only logging, and a signal that needs no reference solution is what lets G1 fire only when needed.
3. **U2 (which de-fixation step).** It uses the U1 items and the U3 trigger, and answers the contested question of what to do once a signal fires; U4 (retrieved memory) follows as the next most valuable test.
