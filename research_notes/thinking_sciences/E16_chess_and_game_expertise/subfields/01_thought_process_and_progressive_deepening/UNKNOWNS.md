## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- **Humans on hard positions.** The depth slope of 2.4 plies per 200 Elo is N=4. Connors et al. (2011) has 22 players but is paywalled. Unblock: get the full text through a library, or run no test (it does not change the schema directly).
- **Returns against quality in humans.** No study links immediate or delayed returns to move quality after controlling for skill. Needs raw protocol data (not available) or a new think-aloud study.
- **Verifier against different path against different representation.** ThinkRetrieve varied retrieval content only, in math. A clean three-way test needs a task with a cheap exact checker, a representation switch we can code, and a sampler. Partly testable once B1 and B2 run; the three-way design needs an item type where all three can be applied.
- **Strong-model Einstellung.** mARC shows high-effort models resist traps, but the miss set came from the same physicians. A fair test needs items built so the familiar answer is wrong by construction, with the model's own first answer as the anchor. We need to author such items.
- **Chess as a test domain.** Chess benchmarks score the first move on short puzzles. A deliberation test with a live engine as ground truth is possible but needs an engine on the bench machine.
- **Replicating the 32.0% against 1.2% branch ablation.** Needs trees extracted from traces by something other than one GPT-5 judge, and a game with a cheap solver. Unblock: build a small four-in-a-row harness, or use U8 on our own tasks as a proxy.
- **Whether gains from 2026 preprints hold.** ThinkRetrieve, Reasoning Memory, OTBench and the Nguyen allocation paper are single-lab and often use 30-problem sets. Unblock: our own runs at 100+ items.
- **Estimating difficulty without cost.** Snell's estimate used 2048 samples per question. The 4-sample disagreement works only for answers that can be compared. Open answers need another signal.
- **Token-exact matching.** Most LLM studies match on sample counts, not tokens. Our bench must count tokens including retrieved text; published numbers cannot be trusted at equal tokens.
- **Time pressure for humans.** The Kasparov and Chabris–Hearst readings stay opposed. Only our own budget sweep (U9) answers the question for a model.

## Order to run
1. **E16.01-U8 (branch and revisit ablation on logged traces).** Cheapest: it reuses traces we already have, and it tells us which revisit steps are worth anything before we spend on new arms.
2. **E16.01-U4 (difficulty-scaled budget with a floor).** Established in sampling, cheap (reuses first passes), and it sets the budget every later arm needs.
3. **E16.01-U1 and U2 together (revisit payload: retrieved case; forced different second pass).** The largest expected gains and the central missing link from the critique: what makes a revisit useful. U3 follows once U1 and U2 show which revisit types are worth comparing.
