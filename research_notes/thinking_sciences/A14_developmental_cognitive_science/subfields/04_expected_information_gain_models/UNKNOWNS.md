## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Real human answerers. Almost every EIG result uses an LLM as answerer. We need a small human-in-the-loop set, or logged real user clarification turns, to see how noise and effort change the gain.
- Cook 2011 replication. No direct replication or failed replication was found. It needs a developmental lab, not our bench. It matters little for the agent; treat as a caution.
- Nelson 2014 primary numbers (paywalled). Not needed for the schema; read when access appears.
- Models newer than 2024 on question asking (o-series, extended-thinking Claude, Gemini 2.5). Published data are missing. Our own run (U1, U4) would produce it.
- Token cost of EIG. No paper reports tokens for BED-LLM. We must count tokens in our own harness first (calls O(N+M+NM) for LLM-answered tests).
- Open-task hypothesis spaces (research questions, proofs). No benchmark with a known hypothesis list exists; we would have to build one from solved tasks.
- Search retry. Gaps 1, 6, 7 and 9 had the thinnest search because quotas ran out; retry with a working search tool.
- Why higher EIG does not always raise success (BigBench). We need item-level logs from our own runs.

## Order to run

1. A14.04-U2 (code ranks vs model picks): cheapest, same candidates, and it checks the best-established claim in our own harness before anything else is built.
2. A14.04-U1 and U4 together (equal tokens against best-of-N and thinking mode, plus the share of turns where the thinking model already picks the best test): this decides whether the step is worth any tokens at all on a current model.
3. A14.04-U7 (ask-or-answer gate and question cap): the evidence says gates are weak and wasted questions cost points, so the decision when to ask is where this component can lose, and it is cheap to measure on a mixed set of underspecified and fully specified items.
