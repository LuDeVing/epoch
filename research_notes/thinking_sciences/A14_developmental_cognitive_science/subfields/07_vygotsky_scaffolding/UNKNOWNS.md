## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Human-learner effects (fading, handover, persistence).** The human literature (Belland 2014 vs 2017 on fading; Kestin; Bastani) needs real learners. Unblock: none for our agent; use only as design rationale.
- **The 1976 and 1975 founding numbers.** Wood, Bruner and Ross and Wood and Middleton could not be opened (403). Unblock: a library copy or open-access mirror, then re-check the 10/50/75% and the contingency finding.
- **Three-size solver ladder for hints up to frontier models.** HintMR covers 4B-8B only. Unblock: access to a frontier helper and several solver sizes on our bench.
- **Cui and Sachan figures.** Not confirmed by any API; gains are only in figures. Unblock: open the PDF figures or ask for the data.
- **The Bastani design critique (SSRN 4898213).** Not read. Unblock: read it and decide if class-level assignment weakens the -17% estimate.
- **Token-matched equal-compute baselines.** No published paper has them for lessons. Unblock: our own bench is the first test (U1).
- **Grader realism.** LLM judges are near chance on pedagogy and LLM students over-perform. Unblock: use code-checkable items only.
- **Writer strength vs experience.** ExpeL confounds them. Unblock: a design that holds the writer fixed and varies only the source of experience (U7).

## Order to run

1. **U2 with U1.** The empty-store control costs one extra arm, and without it no memory result can be read; it tells us whether the store matters at all.
2. **U3.** Wrong lessons are the main harm risk (HintMR -21.6, Bastani -17%), and the gate is cheap to build and test on the same sequence.
3. **U4.** Contingent escalation is the central claim of the scaffolding literature, has no LLM test, and fits the existing impasse step at low cost.
