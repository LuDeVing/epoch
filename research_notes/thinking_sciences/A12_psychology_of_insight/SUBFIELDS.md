# Psychology of insight: subfields

Insight research studies the moment a stuck solver sees a problem differently and the solution follows. Knoblich et al. describe the sequence as exploration, impasse, insight, execution, first noted in scientific discovery (Helmholtz, Poincare) and brought into the lab by Gestalt psychologists (Duncker, Kohler, Wertheimer). The field divides mainly by theory: representational change, progress monitoring, dual-process and three-process accounts, plus method and neuroscience strands. It is close to our S6 IMPASSE and operators O4 and O6.

## Dig into next

**1. Constraint relaxation and chunk decomposition.**
- Questions: What constraints does a model assume without being told ("only add digits", "objects stay put")? Can code list stated constraints and offer one to drop at a time, and does that beat free reframing? Which are hardest for a model to drop?
- Evidence needed: the Knoblich problem sets graded by constraint type; our puzzles tagged by the blocking constraint.

**2. Progress monitoring as an impasse detector.**
- Questions: Which cheap counters (moves used, repeated errors, no change in best score) predict a stall? What should change when the detector fires?
- Evidence needed: MacGregor et al. and Jones move-count data at switch points; our run logs with trigger times.

**3. How common is true restructuring?**
- Questions: In our task classes, what share of items needs a restructure rather than more search?
- Evidence needed: Ash et al. 2009 (to be opened); our own tagging.

## Does not transfer

The felt aha, its speed, brain signature, sleep and incubation, and mood effects are human experience and physiology. Self-reported suddenness is unreliable even for people.
