# Dual-process and three-process accounts: deep document

Scope: analytic plus intuitive processes in insight; the Davidson and Sternberg three-process view (selective encoding, selective combination, selective comparison). Rating for our agent: background only. The usable part is the dual-process engineering evidence and the LLM insight results.

Evidence ids (a01-a28, b01-b26, c01-c25, d01-d17) and claim ids (A12.07-K01 to K24) refer to the files in this folder. Honest limit up front: the founding 1984 paper was never read beyond its abstract, so this document has no founding numbers for it. No study found tests the three-process account itself in a language model.

## 1. What it is

Four families of account compete to explain insight.

- Special-process view. Insight needs a distinct restructuring process (Gestalt tradition).
- Business-as-usual view. Insight uses the same processes as ordinary problem solving (Weisberg; Chuderski and Jastrzebski).
- Three-process view (Davidson and Sternberg 1984; Davidson 1995, 2003). Insight is the skilled use of three selective processes [a01, a02, a16, b19, b20]:
  - Selective encoding: separate relevant from irrelevant information. Stock example is the lily-pad problem (the lake is covered on day 60; which day is it half covered? Day 59). People fixate on 60 and "half" [a16].
  - Selective combination: join separate pieces into a new whole.
  - Selective comparison: link new information to old knowledge and analogues.
- Dual-process view. A fast, intuitive, autonomous system (Type 1) and a slower, working-memory-dependent analytic system (Type 2). For insight, intuition proposes, analysis checks [a03, a12, a13]. Evans and Stanovich (2013) define the split by autonomy versus working-memory need. Speed, effort and accuracy are correlates, not definitions.
- A newer position (Macchi 2025): insight rests on unconscious analytic thought [a10, a11, b05].

The three-process view sits nearer the special-process family but allows gradual training [a27]. Davidson and Sternberg cite an intervention study showing insight can be trained [d01]. The three processes need not be sudden or unconscious; the sudden Aha follows a good selective step plus a restructuring [b20, not opened].

## 2. History and theories in depth

- Metcalfe and Wiebe (1987). Warmth ratings climb steadily on algebra problems but stay flat then jump on insight problems. People cannot predict nearness to a solution [a08, second-hand].
- Davidson and Sternberg (1984), Gifted Child Quarterly 28(2):58-64. Abstract only: three processes, ways to assess insight, a training study [d01]. Secondary sources say gifted children did better on insight items and benefited from help with the three processes [b19, not verified]. No N or effect sizes available.
- Davidson (1995), "The suddenness of insight", in Sternberg and Davidson (eds), The Nature of Insight. Found 12-13% of problems classed as non-insight still showed the insight warmth jump [a05, d03, second-hand via Webb 2016]. So class is set by solution pattern, not by problem label.
- Gilhooly and Murphy (2005): cluster analysis gave only partial support for a clean insight/non-insight split [b22, not opened].
- Weisberg and Fleck (2004, 2013, 2015): many insight-problem solutions come by plain analysis; argue for one set of ordinary processes [b21, not opened].
- Helie and Sun (2010): Explicit-Implicit Interaction theory, CLARION model. Five principles: coexisting explicit and implicit knowledge, simultaneous processing, redundant representation, integration of results, iterative bidirectional processing. Simulates human incubation and insight data [a14, abstract only].
- Evans and Stanovich (2013): the current defence of dual-process theory [a13].
- Chuderski and Jastrzebski (2018): insight strongly related to working memory and reasoning ability, so little beyond ordinary reasoning. A search summary gives 51.8% of variance and 84.6% of the shared link; both unverified [b23, d16]. DeCaro et al. (2017) say the divergence comes from whether participants who already knew the solutions were excluded [d16].
- Zander, Oellinger and Volz (2016): theory review. A discontinuity model of insight resembles dual-process accounts of judgment: non-conscious restructuring needed to overcome a biased intuitive impression [a12].
- Salmon-Mordekovich and Leikin (2023), N=115: "not that special, but business not quite as usual" [d15].
- Macchi, Poli and Caravona (2025): double dissociation with verbalisation; the "unconscious analytic thought" view [a10].

Links to other subfields run in parallel (not covered here): Stage model and incubation, Insight neural signatures, Constraint satisfaction, Process stage models.

## 4. Mechanisms and models

Each mechanism is written so it can be built.

1. Three-process checklist (framing stage).
   - Encode: list the givens. Mark each as relevant, irrelevant or unknown. Drop the irrelevant ones. Keep the original text beside the filtered text.
   - Combine: propose 1 to 3 ways to join the kept facts into one structure.
   - Compare: retrieve 1 to 3 analogues from memory (solved problems of the same class) and note what maps and what does not.
   - Status: untested for LLMs. Nearest proxy is System 2 Attention (below).
2. System 2 Attention as selective encoding [c21]. Call 1: "Rewrite the context, keeping only text relevant to the question, removing opinions." Call 2: answer from the rewrite only. Cost is one extra generation. Fails if the rewrite drops a needed fact, so keep the original available for a fallback.
3. Fast/slow routing (SwiftSage) [c18]. A small fast model acts. A code-side rule detects trouble (repeated errors, no progress, invalid action). On trouble, the large model plans and sets subgoals. The switch is code, not the model.
4. SOFAI metacognitive switch [c17]. A metacognitive module compares the fast solver's confidence and past experience with the cost of the slow solver, then chooses. Proposal paper only, no benchmark numbers.
5. Entropy-gated forced reconsideration [c06, d10]. Compute sequence entropy of the first pass. If it is in the top 20%, append "Wait, we need to reconsider. Let's think this through step by step." and sample again. Works on math; near zero on crosswords and Rush Hour.
6. Candidate retention (proposal from [c03]). Code extracts every candidate answer that appears in the thought log. Dropping one needs a stated reason. Final stage: endorse or reject each retained candidate by a check, not by the model's feeling. Not yet tested.
7. Help-via-examples as trained intuition [c10]. Store solved same-type items. Put 2 to 7 in the prompt. Works for same-form items; may be surface rule learning.
8. Distillation [c19]. Fine-tune direct answers on outputs of a slow method. Works when the slow output is learnable (last letter, coin flip). Fails on step-by-step arithmetic. Not available to a scaffold-only agent.
9. EII/CLARION [a14]. Two coupled stores, explicit rules and implicit associations, run in parallel and integrate results iteratively. Incubation is modelled as implicit processing continuing while explicit processing is idle. Per-task fit numbers not read.
10. Warmth as a monitor. Humans cannot read nearness on insight problems. Analogue: token-confidence trajectories. Untested.

## 5. What holds up

Holds up.
- Insight and non-insight labels are not clean process classes: Aha higher for insight items, accuracy similar, 12-13% of non-insight problems with the insight pattern [K02, three independent sources].
- Aha predicts correctness, yet false insights are common (37%; primed d about 1.5) [K03]. Felt certainty is cheap to induce.
- Early GPT-3 imitates human intuitive CRT errors; chat models do not; fresh items restore the gap [K09, K10].
- CoT is a System 2 override for weak models and adds little for strong ones [K10].
- Spontaneous "wait" shifts are rare and do not help [K15].
- CoT can hurt on pattern, face and exception-learning tasks (3 of 6) [K22].
- A long prescribed step list does not automatically beat direct answering on lateral puzzles [K20].
- Fast/slow agents beat single-process agents on ScienceWorld [K17].

Failures, conflicts and moderators.
- Warmth pattern: Metcalfe and Wiebe versus Hedne 2016 (no difference) [K04].
- Verbalisation and insight: Schooler (1993) found impairment; Fleck and Weisberg (2004, 2013) and Gilhooly et al. (2010) found none; Ball et al. (2015) found thinking aloud similar to silence. Earlier studies used 2-3 insight problems. Macchi 2025 is the same lab as Macchi and Bagassi 2012 [d13]. No outside replication [K24].
- False-insight replication is in-lab only (smaller in Exp2); Ammalainen and Moroshkina (2021) is broadly consistent [d14].
- Special process versus business as usual: unresolved; Chuderski numbers unverified [K07, K08].
- Entropy-gated cue: +8.41 pp math, +0.45 crosswords, about 0 Rush Hour. Base math accuracy is low, so gains may be correction of sloppy passes. Mostly a 1.5B model [d10].
- Spontaneous shift sign flips by domain: harmful in math at all four temperatures, not significant in crosswords (p=.26) [d09].
- Distillation does not work for CoT arithmetic [c19].
- Distractor tasks: longer reasoning can lower accuracy [d12].

Interventions that worked: CoT on weak models; examples; S2A rewrite; fast/slow routing; entropy-gated cue on math; explanatory few-shot on GPT-4 (76.7 to 90.0).
Interventions that did not: step-structured CoT on GPT-3.5 (10 to 13 points below direct); spontaneous reflection; CoT where verbal thinking hurts.

Evidence quality warnings.
- Many LLM papers are 2025-2026 preprints.
- Orru: 30 items, 20 humans. Speed: 5 insight items. Pupil study: 11 solvers.
- Corrections found in critique: the a23 author list is Yax, Anllo and Palminteri (a23 and c11 are one study). The false-insight paper is Grimmer et al. 2022 (not Laukkonen). N=723,200 in d'Aliberti is the entropy regression, not the shifted/non-shifted contrast.
- Independent-source counts collapse duplicates: Hagendorff (b11-13, c08-10), Mizumoto (a20, a21, c01-04), Webb (a04, b01, b02).

## 6. Language models

How models behave.
- Older GPT-3: intuitive-prone, like humans. davinci-003 80% intuitive, 5% correct on CRT [c08]. More refined GPT-3 variants were more intuition-prone on fresh items, not less [c11].
- Chat models: ChatGPT-3.5 59% correct, GPT-4 96%, 0% intuitive on the original items [c08]. Likely partly memorisation, since fresh items cut older-model scores [c11]. No fresh-item test of a GPT-4-class model was found.
- Verbal insight items: ChatGPT at modal human score (12/30 pooled) [b08]; GPT-4o beats humans on rebus [a19]. Representational change: humans ahead by .31 to .46 [a19].
- Japanese riddles: 7.6% (non-reasoning), 17.6% (reasoning), human 52.9% [c01]. GPT-5 inside the human CI. Parameter size does not predict accuracy; release date does [c02].
- BRAINTEASER: ChatGPT 53/63 vs human 92; rewording drops accuracy; tuned systems reach 98-100% [c14, c15, d05].
- Interactive lateral puzzles: GPT-4 best, AC 34.2 [c16].
- Reasoning traces: rigid and sequential on ill-structured problems; self-awareness in only 16% of reasoning papers [a22].

What changed performance and by how much.
- CoT (weak model): correct 5 to 28%, intuitive 80 to 29% [c09].
- Examples: 2 to 92% with 2 examples (type 2) [c10]. Explanatory few-shot on GPT-4: 76.7 to 90.0 [d05]. Word puzzles five-shot: 53.5 to 84.4, likely format learning [d06].
- Reasoning mode: OR 2.29 on riddles [c02].
- Few-shot with reasoning demonstrations on riddles: better in all 4 models, significant in 2, not in the top model [d08].
- S2A rewrite: +17.5 and +9.6 points [c21].
- Forced reconsideration: +8.41 pp math [c06].
- Step-structured prompt: 10 to 13 points below direct on GPT-3.5 [d04].
- Retrieval of 3 shots: direct 82.5-85.0 on the GPT-3.5 sentence set [d04].
- Test-time scaffolding of cognitive structures: up to +66.7% on complex problems [a22].

Contamination: no uncontaminated lateral set exists. The NazoNazo authors argue memorisation does not explain low scores (familiar answers should be easier to endorse) and propose a rotating private set [d08].

## 8. Concrete tests on our bench

All arms at equal total tokens (setup counted, G7). Fresh items written after the model cutoff where possible.

1. Three-step framing vs plain CoT vs direct. Items: BRAINTEASER sentence and word (original plus semantic and context rewrites) and the NazoNazo blind set (159 items). Metric: group-based accuracy. Expected: at best a small gain on a strong model; a loss if the prompt is long.
2. Candidate retention. Items: NazoNazo, riddle sets. Metric: accuracy and verification-failure rate (correct string in log, wrong final). Expected: failure rate falls; accuracy gain smaller than the failure share allows.
3. Entropy-gated reconsideration vs spontaneous vs none. Items: lateral/riddle set plus MATH-500 as a positive control. Metric: accuracy, wins minus losses. Expected: about +8 pp on the math control, near 0 on lateral.
4. S2A rewrite before solving. Items: distractor-laden word problems and puzzles with irrelevant clauses. Metric: accuracy and token cost. Expected: +5 to +10 points if items have distractors; zero otherwise.
5. Short direct vs long CoT by item type. Items: insight riddles, pattern/perceptual items, algebra. Metric: accuracy by type. Expected: crossing pattern (long CoT helps algebra, not riddles).
6. Class-routed vs uniform schema on a mixed set. Metric: accuracy. Expected: no gain from routing.
7. Incubation analogue: unrelated task between two attempts vs two attempts back to back. Metric: accuracy of resample, diversity. Expected: null in a stateless model unless the task changes sampling context.
8. Fresh-item CRT check on the agent's base model: original vs rewritten trap items. Metric: accuracy drop (memorisation gap).

## 9. What does not transfer, and why

- The special-process versus business-as-usual debate. A model has no unconscious stream, so there is nothing to settle for our purposes.
- Aha as a feeling. Models write "aha" from text patterns. Markers were rare (6.31%) and uninformative on accuracy [c05, b15].
- Warmth ratings. Humans give a rating; a model's analogue is a token probability, which is a different thing.
- Pupil dilation, EEG, incubation sleep effects. No physiological channel.
- Giftedness training effects (Davidson and Sternberg). Children, one training study, numbers unknown.
- Human verbalisation costs (verbal overshadowing). For humans the cost falls on attention and implicit knowledge. For models verbalisation is the computation, so the human result is at best an analogy. LLM evidence is only from non-insight tasks [d11].
- Distillation (Yu 2024) needs fine-tuning, which a scaffold-only agent cannot do.
- SwiftSage's trained small model is not available; only the code-side switch transfers.
- Human-label classes (insight versus analytic). Labels fail even in humans (12-13%).

## 10. Open questions

- What did Davidson and Sternberg 1984 and Davidson 1995 measure (N, effect sizes, training gains)? Full text needed.
- Does an explicit encode/combine/compare step beat plain CoT at equal tokens? No one has tested it.
- Are BRAINTEASER-type gains real reframing or format learning and contamination? No uncontaminated set exists.
- Does a forced reconsideration cue help on riddles and lateral items?
- Does a candidate-retention rule remove verification failures?
- Does long CoT hurt LLMs on insight items as it may hurt humans?
- Is any "wait" marker calibrated on riddles, and does it depend on temperature?
- What do Chuderski and Jastrzebski 2018, Weisberg 2015 and Gilhooly and Murphy 2005 report in full?
- Has anyone outside the original labs replicated Macchi 2025 or Grimmer 2022?
- Do current large models show intuitive errors on fresh CRT items? The only fresh-item data are the GPT-3 family.
