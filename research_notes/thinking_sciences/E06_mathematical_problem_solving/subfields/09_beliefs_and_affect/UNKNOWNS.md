## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Wild goose chase effect size with independent coding: needs human protocols or a coded trace corpus with two raters; our bench has traces but no validated episode coder (Read, Explore, Plan, Verify). Unblock by building a trace coder and checking inter-rater agreement.
- Direct LLM test of Schoenfeld's belief list: no instrument exists. Unblock by writing a short effort-belief probe (how long should a typical problem take; when is it impossible) and checking whether answers predict give-up.
- Growth-mindset and math-anxiety moderators (Burnette, Tipton, Rege, Hecht): human only; reading them would not change the schema.
- 1985 book, 1988 and 1989 Schoenfeld papers, DeBellis and Goldin, Garofalo and Lester: not opened; reading them could add belief-measurement details but not schema changes yet.
- Decoding-time thought-switch penalty (TIP): needs logit access, which API models lack. Unblock by running an open reasoning model locally.
- Training-side fixes (self-training against overthinking, fine-tuning against flip-flopping): need weights.
- Equal-token comparison across published stop-rule papers: they use different models and token accounting; only our own bench can settle it.

## Order to run

1. **U13 then U2.** Cheapest: the baseline flip rate on our current model decides whether a verify-before-flip rule is worth building, and it is the best-evidenced failure.
2. **U3.** The equal-token three-way (fixed cutoff, Wait forcing, stability stop) settles the over/underthinking contradiction and the schema's G11 and S6 design in one run.
3. **U1.** Cheap and controlled input-side fix with the strongest LLM evidence in the subfield; one extra restate step, easy to measure on reworded items.
