## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Vessey's finding on expert hypothesis handling has no replication. We need new human think-aloud or eye-tracking data on current code. Our bench cannot produce it.
- Parnin and Orso vs Xia et al.: full texts were not re-opened. A reading of both (tool accuracy, participants, bug size) would tell whether a list's accuracy threshold exists. Unblock by fetching both PDFs.
- Break-even location accuracy: needs items with controllable location error and a large N. Build a seeded-location set (U2) first.
- Whether the "right location, wrong patch" share (60.8-69.1%) holds with a stricter location test. Needs the raw trajectories of Coherence Collapse or our own runs.
- debug-gym's Llama-3.3-70B text-vs-table conflict and the Mini-nightmare rows. Needs the released logs.
- Reasoning models' own internal debugging: we cannot see inside hidden reasoning, so cannot tell whether an explicit cause step duplicates what the model already does. Needs visible reasoning traces or a controlled prompt ablation (U1).
- Whether the 2026 preprints replicate (Shi, Loc2Repair, RGFL, SGAgent, Coherence Collapse). Needs independent reruns; several have internal number errors.
- Token-cost accounting in published studies: most do not report equal-token baselines. Our bench is the only place to get this.

## Order to run

1. A20.01-U10 (diagnose-and-repair vs resample at equal tokens): it decides how big S5 should be at all, and every other test depends on that budget.
2. A20.01-U1 (cause statement before patch) with U2 as an arm: the best-supported unknown (three indirect sources) and cheap to run, and it settles whether S5's location step needs a guard.
3. A20.01-U6 (automatic revert to the best patch): the cheapest to implement, uses only existing checks, and targets the largest failure bucket (right location, wrong or overwritten patch).
