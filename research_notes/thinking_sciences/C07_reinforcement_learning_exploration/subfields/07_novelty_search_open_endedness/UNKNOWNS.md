## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **A neutral behaviour space for text.** No low-dimensional descriptor exists for free text. Needs: a labelled set of answer approaches per item, so approach labels can be scored by code.
- **Noise from judges inside a long archive loop.** Needs thousands of rounds and an external judge. Our bench runs short episodes. Unblock with a multi-round harness.
- **Independent replication of Voyager, OMNI and DGM headline numbers.** Needs outside papers or compute we do not have. Treat as lab-reported.
- **Why the smallest models are the most diverse (NoveltyBench).** Needs several tuned and untuned checkpoints. We cannot control training.
- **Equal-token curves for Tree of Thoughts and PlanSearch.** Published papers report equal attempts. We must run our own.
- **Deceptive items.** We need a set of items with a known tempting wrong first answer (tagged), to split gains by deception.
- **Pure novelty with no goal.** Only relevant to true open-ended modes; our bench always has a scored goal.

## Order to run

1. **U1 (difference pass vs independent attempts).** It is the direct gap in the literature, cheap (one prompt change), and a null result would remove most of the other candidates.
2. **U3 (descriptor).** The descriptor drives results in every source; it decides what U1, U2 and U7 should record, and it is cheap on the same items.
3. **U6 (resume vs restart).** The strongest single-source gain (74% vs 49% on Game of 24) for reasoning, but it costs more tokens, so only an equal-token test shows whether it is real.
