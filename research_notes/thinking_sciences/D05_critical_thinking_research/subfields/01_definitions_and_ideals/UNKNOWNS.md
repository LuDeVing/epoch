## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Certainty-gated Huang re-run on 2025 to 2026 reasoning models.** Needs a stable certainty signal (agreement across short attempts) and enough items per class. Unblocks U3.
- **Token cost of critique and premise check on frontier models.** The literature does not report it; our harness logging has to supply it first.
- **Prompt-only manipulation of dispositions** (open-mindedness, suspending judgment). Needs a validated disposition scale for models. None exists.
- **Interpretation and explanation benchmarks.** No LLM benchmark covers these two Delphi skills; we would have to build items with a known intended reading.
- **Human-comparison claims** (o1 vs students). Would need matched cohorts and 10+ runs. Not worth building; use the bench as the reference.
- **Verifying Abrami 2015 (g+ 0.30) and Huber and Kuncel (percentile) from full text.** Background only; no schema effect.
- **Whether any LLM test measures Dewey, Ennis or Facione directly.** No such test found; the whole literature measures critique.

## Order to run

1. **U3 gated critique.** It protects against the largest measured loss (Huang: CSQA 75.8 to 38.1) and reuses the check logging we already have.
2. **U2 stance hold.** The biggest missing disposition; every model flips; cheap to probe with an "are you sure?" turn on items we already solve.
3. **U1 premise check.** Cheapest to run and the largest unprompted gap (under 15% to 0.81 with a prompt); the two fixes (U1, U2) are separate, so run them as separate arms.
