## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- **Human Gick and Holyoak numbers.** Only second-hand (29% vs 50%, Exp 2). Needs the primary papers. Does not block any test above.
- **Abrami control-type and quality moderators.** Primary paper returned 403. Needs full text via another route. Only matters for human claims.
- **Frontier reasoning models (R1, o1 class) and curated memory.** Early evidence says minimal or inconsistent gain (d12). Needs a bench arm with such a model at equal tokens; check what the bench budget allows.
- **Reasoning-task distance curves in the literature.** Search was cut short (quota out, APIs returned 429). A fresh search could find papers; absence is not proven.
- **Whole-task success in memory studies.** Published gains are step-level with whole-task success 1-5%. Needs a bench with whole-task scoring and enough items; our reasoning items may solve this.
- **Illusion of Thinking rebuttals.** Output-length and puzzle-design critiques not searched. Needs a search pass before U14 is trusted.
- **Class definitions.** Every keyed-memory test depends on how classes are defined and detected (S0). Needs the S0 gate research; until then use hand-labelled classes.
- **Training-route effects (RL vs SFT, breadth vs maths-only).** Model-level; cannot be tested with a prompt scaffold. Only relevant to model choice.

## Order to run

1. **U1 (keyed vs shared memory).** It is the McPeck question for our agent, no paper answers it, and the answer decides how the whole memory store is built.
2. **U6 (three-arm placebo control).** It is cheap, it sets the control for every other test, and it checks whether the scaffold gain is real before we spend on the rest.
3. **U5 (scaffold on perturbed variants).** Gaps 3 and 4 in the critique; uses items we can generate by code, and tests the guardrail and input-hygiene parts of the schema with a frontier model.
