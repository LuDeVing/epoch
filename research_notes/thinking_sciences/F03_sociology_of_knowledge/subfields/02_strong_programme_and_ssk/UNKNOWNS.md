## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Direct test of Bloor's symmetry or interests on an LLM: no design exists in the literature; our U5 is only a proxy.
- Collins 1985, Franklin 1990, Collins 1974 (TEA laser) and the Collins 2017 imitation-game numbers: need the primary texts (publisher paywall or 403). Unblock by getting the PDFs or the books.
- Chuang false-framing asymmetry on current models: needs a population-simulation harness and many agents; our bench is single-agent.
- Imitation game with an LLM as the interactional-expert candidate: needs a panel of domain experts as blind judges.
- Whether peer-review noise findings (ICC .34-.65) apply to LLM judges: needs human-labelled gold sets for our item classes.
- Effect of source prestige on LLM-written reviews at venue scale: needs real review data (ICLR-style) and a way to blind it.
- Macro-social claims (interests, group stakes): out of scope for a single run.
- Population conventions and minority flips (2% to 67%): need a multi-agent harness, which G9 and G4 tell us not to build.
- Whether training-time fixes (synthetic-data finetune) would add anything beyond the scaffold: needs a trainable model.

## Order to run

1. **U1 with U2 together** (hold position unless new evidence, with a valid-correction check): the largest expected gain, the problem is measured on every model family, and one item bank serves both.
2. **U4** (blind judging): cheap code-side change, applies to every judge call in the schema, and source and persona effects are measured on 2025 models.
3. **U11** (concrete wrong suggestion defence): the one social-pressure attack that still works on frontier models, so a test here tells us if the scaffold helps where the model alone does not.
