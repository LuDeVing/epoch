## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- A direct LLM version of Chase-Simon recall and of chunked span. No published study found (search was partial). We can only build our own probe (U11).
- Independent replication of the Pleiss et al. familiarity gradient outside chess. Needs another lab or non-chess domains with a familiarity measure. We can run a proxy by plain-model pass rate.
- Staleness over time (drift in what a good entry is). Needs a long stream of tasks with a changing distribution. Our bench has fixed splits.
- Whether the human template theory (slots, defaults, links) is right, since all data come from one lab. Human data from a second lab would unblock it. The LLM test (U5) tests only the engineering value.
- Scale: human experts hold 50,000 to over 300,000 chunks. Our library will hold tens to hundreds. We cannot test large-library behaviour except by synthetic entries.
- Cross-model portability of a library (SkillWeaver shows cross-agent gain, abstract only). Needs runs on a second model family.

## Order to run
1. **U1 (library vs no library with a reuse-blocked arm).** Everything else is moot if the library gain comes from self-correction (Berlot-Attwell). It is cheap and decides the whole memory design.
2. **U2 (gate vs add-all) together with U3 (k sweep).** Same items, the largest published effects (up to 22 points and 5 points). They set the write and read rules at once.
3. **U4 (budget by hit vs miss).** Reuses the U1 data. A positive result gives a direct token saving under G11 and G12.
