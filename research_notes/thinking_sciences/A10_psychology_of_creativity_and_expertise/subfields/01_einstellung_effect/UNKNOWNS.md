## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- Real Luchins data. The 1942 monograph and thesis tables were not opened. Unblock: obtain the monograph or the OSF data from Binz and Schulz, re-read the tables, confirm the Elo-odds figures (3.67 and 1.82 per 100 Elo).
- Private trap sets. M-ARC items are public, so contamination is possible. Unblock: write fresh hand-made trap items and keep them out of shared context.
- Gaze analogue. We have no attention measure for a hosted model. Unblock: attempt-structure features from traces (U6), or attention entropy on a small open-weight model.
- Age and IQ effect (K11). No analogue. Unblock: none planned; compare model sizes only (U13).
- Which account (schema attention, prior cost, success-ratio choice) fits LLMs. Unblock: needs data from U1 and U4 first.
- Independent run of the MedEinst audit system. Unblock: wait for code release or build a minimal version (U10).
- Long agent runs where a set builds over many turns. Unblock: a logged long-run bench with route labels per turn.
- Luchins' own sources (1942 monograph, Luchins and Luchins 1959, Ross 1952) were never opened; the IQ claim and the interleaving result (16% vs 80%) rest on one re-analysis. Unblock: obtain the monograph or the Binz and Schulz OSF data.
- Frontier-agent behaviour on contextual drag and repeated failed calls. The small-model result covers 135M to 1.7B only; the drag result is mostly open models on math. Unblock: run U5 and U14 on our own agent.
- Whether the Illusion of Thinking trace shapes hold once its published critiques (token limits, infeasible instances) are read. Unblock: open the critiques before building the U6 detector.

## Order to run

1. A10.01-U1: does the set exist in our agent. Cheapest, and it gates everything else; if blocked examples do not raise the long-method share, most rows lose their target.
2. A10.01-U4: hint line vs forced alternatives vs plain reflection. Decides whether to add a G1/S3 line, and reuses the U1 items so it adds almost no cost.
3. A10.01-U3: interleaved vs blocked examples. The largest human effect (16% vs 80%) and the most direct rule for how memory lessons and few-shot examples are built.
