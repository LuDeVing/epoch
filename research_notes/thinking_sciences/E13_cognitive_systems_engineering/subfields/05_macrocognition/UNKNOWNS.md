## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet

- RPD share and replication quality: needs the 1986 Klein, Calderwood and Clinton-Cirocco report and the true replications (DTIC blocked; paywalled journals). Would unblock: whether the 80-90% claim is worth any weight.
- Kopka and Feufel appendix: temperature-0 tables (S1, S2, S4) and the reasoning versus non-reasoning table conflict. Would unblock: whether the gain is stable and whether U1 is likely to fail.
- Triage data: we have no clinical-style vignettes with class labels. Would unblock: direct replication of the Kopka design on Claude models (U1 uses a synthetic proxy meanwhile).
- Human-AI loss after bias correction (Vaccaro): needs the supplement or a re-analysis. Would unblock: whether any human-in-loop analogue (self-review step) should be penalised.
- Whether Claude and Gemini models already do RPD-like steps internally: no published data; needs our own runs with thinking on and off (U5).
- Multi-agent coordination results (Buchler, Porat) are about human teams; we have no multi-agent bench with shared-state load. Would unblock: U7.
- Plausibility-transition predictions: the model has never made testable predictions; a coded trace corpus of agent runs with anomaly annotations would be needed.

## Order to run

1. **U1** (RPD prompt vs threshold shift vs length-matched prompt): cheapest, directly checks the only positive LLM result and the main confound.
2. **U3** (anomaly log then reframe on a planted contradiction): the largest untested idea in the field and the clearest known LLM failure (stuck on early frames, -39% multi-turn).
3. **U2** (first-option-then-check vs enumerate): cheap with a code check, and it decides whether the RPD route saves tokens under G7.
