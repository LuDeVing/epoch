## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Primary Hollnagel and Woods texts (1983, 2005, 2006), Klein et al. 2004 and Sarter et al. 1997 were not opened. What exactly they claim on team-player requirements is second-hand. Unblock: read the books or open-access chapters.
- Stanton et al. (2001) COCOM test: only an abstract was seen. Unblock: obtain the full text for sample size and coding agreement.
- Interpredictability and directability as LLM measures: no measured analogue found. Unblock: define a metric (does the model state what it will do next and does it obey a redirect), then test.
- Live-user collaboration (Collaborative Gym, Magentic-UI): our bench has no human or reliable simulated user. Unblock: a scripted user with side information.
- Human-final vs program-final pairs: over 95% of meta-analysis systems gave the human the final say. Unblock: our own U3 results.
- Vaccaro coverage ends mid-2023. Unblock: a newer meta-analysis, or our own task-type split (U4).
- Whether the 45% threshold and the amplification result hold for program-loop scaffolds. Unblock: U13 on two benchmarks.
- Hand-back studies with LLM agents (Bainbridge take-over analogue): only Laban's multi-turn drop is close. Unblock: U7.

## Order to run
1. E13.01-U14 (token cost of verify vs plain): cheapest, needs only logging, and it feeds every other test's equal-token budget.
2. E13.01-U3 (per-class final say, model vs program): highest expected gain, uses data we already collect, and it decides where a program veto is worth it.
3. E13.01-U6 (hardest-tier loss): one binned analysis of existing results; if the scaffold loses on the top tier, we can route those items to plain at no cost.
