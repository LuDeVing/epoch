## Open questions we cannot test on our bench yet
- Test-time training on the task's own examples (K13): needs weight updates. Unblock: a small open model we may fine-tune per task.
- Pure MDL at test time (CompressARC, K15, K23): needs a GPU and 20 minutes per puzzle. Unblock: a GPU job queue; only worth it if U1 shows length carries signal.
- Whether pure MDL scales (ARC-AGI-2: 4%): no scaling study exists in the literature. Unblock: new research or a scaling experiment.
- Human-simplicity results (Feldman 2000, Planton 2021, Lake 2015): abstracts or second-hand only, no replications found. Unblock: open the full papers and look for replications.
- 2025-2026 reasoning-model behaviour on compression tasks (o3-class, Claude and Gemini thinking models): no published numbers found. Unblock: run the probe in U8.
- ARC Prize 2025 follow-ups and Poetiq's own write-up: unopened. Unblock: read the write-up and any refinement-harness ablations.
- English vs Python hypothesis spaces on a large, non-human-fit task set: only one 8-person study. Unblock: U9.
- Learned-library benefit for agent memory with tasks that share only part of their subroutines: break-even unknown. Unblock: U4 with a graded sharing level.

## Order to run
1. U1 (shortest vs vote vs first-fit): it is the core S7 question, no paper answers it, and it reuses the sample pools we already generate, so it is cheap.
2. U2 (mutated-input check): it directly fixes the known false-pass rate and decides G3/S8 wording; one extra execution step per item.
3. U3 (program-first router with direct fallback): if it holds, it is a simple S3/S7 rule with a G6 fallback; both arms are already built by the first two tests.
