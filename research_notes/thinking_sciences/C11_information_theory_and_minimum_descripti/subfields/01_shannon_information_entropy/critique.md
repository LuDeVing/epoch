## Contradictions between sources
- UoT main vs appendix numbers (item 4).
- Kuhn 92.7/95.5 vs 95.3 (item 6).
- Shannon 1948 (50% redundancy) vs 1951 (about 75%) is not a contradiction: different context lengths (8 vs 100 letters).
- Human information search: EIG vs probability gain is contested (K23); BED-LLM shows EIG beats raw entropy, but nobody in the LLM papers tests probability gain.

## Weak spots
- Independence is low. All semantic-entropy results (K06-K09) come from one group (Kuhn, Farquhar, Gal, Kossen). I marked K06 single-source although four evidence lines exist.
- All EIG-for-LLM wins are on closed, enumerable hypothesis sets (20 Questions, diagnosis, board games). Nothing tests open-ended reasoning or ARC-style puzzles. The hypothesis set is generated by the LLM, and its quality is the largest factor (K13: 93 down to 25 without filtering).
- EIG methods use extra LLM calls. Most reported comparisons are not at equal tokens (UoT beats CoT-SC at its own budget, pruned UoT is closer). Our rule needs an equal-token test.
- BED-LLM baselines: authors note their Prompt-Only is weaker than in some earlier work; the "doubling" depends on that baseline.
- Success on 20 Questions has a ceiling effect (Animals 95), so differences among strong methods are within se.

## GAPS (most decision-relevant first)
1. At equal total tokens, does EIG-over-hypotheses probe choice beat plain self-consistency or a single good prompt on open tasks (ARC items, BrainTeaser) where the hypothesis set must be invented?
2. How good must the hypothesis list be? Is there a minimum size or coverage below which EIG scoring is worse than prompt-only (the filtering ablation suggests a cliff)?
3. Does probability gain (expected drop in error) beat Shannon EIG as the probe score for an LLM agent, as it did for humans under experience-based learning?
4. Does semantic entropy (or cluster share) work as a gate on multi-step reasoning and ARC, not only factual QA, and how often are confident-wrong answers passed?
5. What stop rule is best: entropy threshold, EIG threshold, or fixed budget? Evidence shows LLMs keep asking after the answer is known (ChatGPT 7.24 questions on 8 candidates), but no study compares stop rules.
6. Can answer-likelihood (the LLM's p(answer|hypothesis)) be estimated cheaply and accurately enough, or does noise in it erase the EIG advantage (the ice-cream question case)?
7. Does the Shannon-style redundancy argument (more context lowers uncertainty) predict how much carrying S-state history into the probe prompt helps, versus hurts through length?
8. Is per-item log-loss or compression a usable proxy for correctness of the agent's own answers (Huang link is model-level, not item-level)?
9. Are results from Cover-King gambling and Oaksford-Chater (not opened) consistent with the figures quoted, and do any failed replications exist for the rarity-assumption information-gain model?
10. Units and floor: what is the Hoffmann irreducible loss E in bits per token, and what does it imply for how much any probe can gain on text-like tasks?


## Gap search results

Evidence in evidence_d.jsonl (d01-d16, 15 opened PDFs or pages, plus one failed-access line). The WebSearch budget was exhausted, so sources were picked from known arXiv ids and opened as PDFs. The arXiv, Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex search APIs were rate limited (HTTP 429).

1. Equal-token EIG vs self-consistency on open tasks: PARTLY ANSWERED, still open. No paper compares them at equal tokens on ARC or BrainTeaser. Hypothesis Search (d04) is the closest ARC result: 17% direct, 23% programs only (64 programs), 30% with hypotheses. Compute is not matched. QuestBench (d10) shows unaided question choice is weak on logic and planning (40-50%). The cheap option "LLM picks the best of candidate questions from a filtered belief set" beat Prompt-Only in BED-LLM (d08) and should be the first equal-token test.
2. Minimum hypothesis-list quality: PARTLY CLOSED. On ARC the list contained a correct hypothesis for only 49 of 100 tasks and 28 of 30 solved tasks had one (d04, K28). So coverage sets a ceiling. No ablation of list size against EIG vs prompt-only exists. The cliff in K14 stays a single 20 Questions result.
3. Probability gain vs EIG for LLM agents: OPEN. No LLM study found. Piriyakulkij (d06) shows expected entropy and expected model change are equivalent there, which does not address probability gain.
4. Semantic entropy as a gate on multi-step reasoning: PARTLY CLOSED. Answer agreement tracks accuracy on GSM8K (d01) and confidence filtering lifts AIME accuracy (d14), but semantic entropy proper was not tested on reasoning in anything opened. Confident-wrong passes are reported (d14; K27).
5. Best stop rule: PARTLY CLOSED for answer sampling. Beta stability beats entropy threshold and majority threshold at equal cost (d02); entropy-zero windows save 34-84% (d03); DeepConf up to 84.7% (d14). For EIG probe stopping, OPEN.
6. Cost and noise of answer-likelihood: PARTLY CLOSED. The known failure is noisy answers (ice-cream example, d09), fixed by subtracting expected likelihood entropy. Estimation error of p(y|h) is not measured in any opened paper. Verbalised probabilities beat logprobs for chat models on factual QA (d05).
7. Context history in the probe prompt: ANSWERED for one setting. Updating the likelihood with history hurt in 13 of 15 cells (d07, K29). Not tested for belief-set history.
8. Item-level log-loss as correctness proxy: OPEN. Only model-level evidence (Huang) and item-level agreement or verbalised confidence (d01, d05, d15).
9. Cover-King and Oaksford-Chater checks, failed replications: OPEN. Pages could not be opened (d16). K25 is flagged unverified.
10. Hoffmann E units: CLOSED by derivation. E = 1.69 is nats per token, about 2.44 bits per token; unit is inferred, not stated (d12, K32).
