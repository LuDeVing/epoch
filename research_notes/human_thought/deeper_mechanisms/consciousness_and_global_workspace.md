# Consciousness and the Global Workspace: what consciousness science says about how thoughts become conscious, and what conscious access does for reasoning and idea combination

Scope note: compiled 2026-10-10. Emphasis on 2023–2026 work; older items are labeled "(foundational)". An earlier round already covered creativity networks, mind wandering, insight/aha, semantic memory and practical levers; those are not re-researched here. Where a claim is a theory's assertion rather than an empirical result, it is labeled as such.

## Key Question 1: Current Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT) — what conscious access is claimed to do for flexible thought, serial reasoning and cross-module combination, and the "ignition" evidence

### Takeaway
GNWT (Dehaene, Changeux, Mashour, Roelfsema) holds that a content becomes conscious when it triggers "ignition" — a sudden, nonlinear, all-or-none, self-sustaining activation of long-range workspace neurons (dense in prefrontal and parietal cortex) ~200–300 ms after stimulus onset — and that the function of this access is to make one selected content globally available to otherwise encapsulated specialist processors, allowing it to be maintained, amplified, combined with other contents, and used as a step in a sequence of mental operations. Ignition is empirically well supported as a correlate of reportable perception (human EEG/MEG/iEEG, monkey PFC recordings); the claims about serial, multi-step reasoning rest on behavioral dual-task and "chaining" experiments from the Dehaene lab, most of which are older (2008–2012).

### Cited Findings
- Core statement of the theory (2020 review, still the reference version): ignition is a "sudden, coherent, and exclusive activation" of workspace neurons coding the current content while the rest of the workspace is inhibited; on conscious trials activity diverges nonlinearly from unconscious trials ~200–300 ms post-stimulus and spreads to prefrontal and parietal cortex; the late correlate is the P3/P300 wave; the GNW assigns a key role to large layer II/III pyramidal neurons with long-range axons (plus layer V), most dense in PFC, but "the GNW is not localizationist." — [Mashour, Roelfsema, Changeux & Dehaene, Neuron 2020 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/)
- Functions GNWT assigns to conscious access, per the same review: (i) global routing/broadcasting so information becomes "widely accessible" to local processors; (ii) amplification and sustained maintenance via recurrent loops (basis of working memory); (iii) binding — attention and conscious access co-select features of one object represented in different areas; (iv) mental operations — an active content can be transformed (e.g., mental rotation) and a sequence of workspace states supports "successive mental steps required to solve a task"; (v) combining information — the review's Raven's-matrices example describes conscious contents being exchanged between processors to solve the problem. — [Mashour et al., Neuron 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/)
- Timing is flexible, not fixed: under dual-task load ignition can be delayed well beyond 300 ms, and a retro-cue up to 900 ms after a stimulus can still trigger conscious access; in infants the late slow wave appears at ~900 ms at 5 months and ~750 ms at older ages versus ~300 ms in adults (Kouider et al. 2013). — [Mashour et al., Neuron 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/); [Kouider et al., Science 2013](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1232509)
- Ignition evidence in monkeys: van Vugt et al. (2018) recorded V1, V4 and dorsolateral PFC while monkeys reported near-threshold stimuli; reported stimuli produced a late, nonlinear PFC response ("ignition") whereas unreported stimuli evoked early visual activity that failed to propagate; the signal-detection threshold mapped onto the ignition threshold. — [van Vugt et al., Science 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aar7186); summarized in [Mashour et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/)
- Ignition evidence in humans (foundational): all-or-none bimodal visibility distributions in the attentional blink (Sergent & Dehaene 2004); a nonlinear late (>270 ms) divergence of EEG activity at the masking threshold (Del Cul, Baillet & Dehaene 2007); late (>200 ms) ignition as the common signature across sensory modalities (Noel et al. 2018; Sanchez et al. 2019, as cited in Mashour et al. 2020). — [Sergent & Dehaene, Psychological Science 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2004.00748.x); [Del Cul et al., PLoS Biology 2007](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.0050260); [Mashour et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/)
- What GNWT says unconscious processing can do: the first ~200 ms of feedforward activity can be "fully preserved" without conscious perception; local prediction-error responses (auditory mismatch) persist in coma, sleep and inattention; early local recurrent loops occur in lower areas; subliminal stimuli produce slowly decaying activity rather than ignition; items outside the focus of attention can be held by weaker local persistent firing or "activity-silent" synaptic storage. — [Mashour et al., Neuron 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/)
- Original computational model (foundational): Dehaene, Kerszberg & Changeux (1998) simulated a network of workspace neurons with long-range connections plus specialized processors and showed effortful tasks (Stroop) require the workspace while routine tasks do not; Dehaene & Changeux (2011) consolidated the "ignition" signatures (late amplification, long-distance synchrony, P3b). — [Dehaene, Kerszberg & Changeux, PNAS 1998](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.95.24.14529); [Dehaene & Changeux, Neuron 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.018)
- The "human Turing machine" framing (foundational, Dehaene lab): Zylberberg, Dehaene, Roelfsema & Sigman (2011) proposed that the workspace implements a serial production system: each conscious state selects one operation whose result is broadcast and becomes the input for the next, giving humans flexible but slow, serial, multi-step mental programs. — [Zylberberg et al., Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2011.05.007)
- Chaining requires consciousness (foundational): Sackur & Dehaene (2009) asked participants to apply two successive arithmetic operations (e.g., add 2, then compare to 5) to masked or visible digits; single operations could be partially performed on subliminal digits, but chaining two operations only occurred for consciously perceived digits. — [Sackur & Dehaene, Cognition 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2009.01.003)
- GNWT's own 2017 taxonomy distinguishes C1 (global availability / workspace broadcast) from C2 (self-monitoring / metacognition) and argues both are computations that current AI lacks. — [Dehaene, Lau & Kouider, Science 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871)
- October 2025 restatement by GNWT proponents in response to Cogitate: conscious content is explicitly encoded in GNW activity and this encoding should be continuous while the content remains conscious; ignition after stimulus onset in a 200–800 ms window is the core prediction; spontaneous activity grounded in molecular mechanisms (receptor allosteric transitions, E/I balance, AMPA/NMDA ratio) is expected to play a role in ignition. — [Naccache, Sergent, Dehaene, Wang, Farisco & Changeux, Neuroscience of Consciousness 2025](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147)

### Inferences
- GNWT's account of thinking is essentially "one broadcast at a time": the workspace is a serial, capacity-limited channel whose job is to route a selected representation to many processors. On this view, combining two ideas requires that both be (sequentially or jointly) in the workspace; combination of two never-conscious representations across modules is predicted to be impossible or very weak. This is the main point of contact with idea generation.
- The theory predicts that novel multi-step operations (chaining) are the hallmark of conscious processing, while well-practiced single operations can run unconsciously; Sackur & Dehaene (2009) is the clearest direct test and still stands as foundational support, but I found no modern preregistered re-test.
- The flexibility in ignition timing (delayed under load; retro-cues; 900 ms in infants) implies that "becoming conscious" is a dynamical threshold event rather than a fixed latency, consistent with a competitive-selection bottleneck.

### Gaps
- I did not find a 2023–2026 paper by Dehaene or Mashour that revises the ignition mechanism itself; the 2020 Neuron review plus the 2025 Naccache et al. commentary remain the current statements.
- The Mashour et al. 2020 review does not itself discuss the psychological refractory period or explicitly claim "one conscious content at a time"; those claims come from older Dehaene-lab papers (see Key Question 5).
- No recent direct neural evidence that cross-module combination (as opposed to broadcast of a single content) requires ignition was found.

## Key Question 2: What the Cogitate adversarial collaboration (IIT vs GNWT, Nature 2025) and other Templeton adversarial collaborations found, and what this means for workspace-based accounts of thinking

### Takeaway
Cogitate (n = 256; fMRI, MEG, iEEG; preregistered) found that conscious content was decodable and sustained in posterior cortex, that PFC showed only a brief onset "ignition" carrying category but not identity or orientation information, and that neither the IIT-predicted sustained posterior synchrony nor the GNWT-predicted offset ignition appeared; the authors say the data "substantially challenge key tenets of both theories." For workspace accounts of thinking the main lesson is that PFC broadcast looks transient and coarse rather than a continuous high-fidelity copy of conscious content; GNWT proponents reply that offset ignition was never a core prediction and that PFC's mixed selectivity makes decoding hard. The other Templeton collaborations (INTREPID: IIT vs predictive processing; ETHOS: higher-order theories) have published no final results as of October 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Design: 256 participants (fMRI 120, of whom 73 in searchlight decoding and 70 in gPPI connectivity; MEG 102, of whom 65 in decoding/connectivity; iEEG 34 patients, 29 in decoding, 31 in activation models, 28 in RSA) viewed suprathreshold faces, objects, letters and false fonts (20 identities each, 3 orientations) for 0.5, 1.0 or 1.5 s while detecting rare targets; orientation and duration were always task-irrelevant and most analyses used task-irrelevant trials. Three predictions were preregistered for each theory with pass/fail criteria. — [Cogitate Consortium, Ferrante, Gorska-Klimowska et al., Nature 642:133–142 (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1); full text at [PMC12137136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136)
- Preregistered protocol (2023) describing the experiments and the ex-ante pass/fail criteria. — [Melloni et al., PLOS ONE 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0268577)
- Prediction 1 (decoding of content): iEEG posterior face-vs-object decoding exceeded 95% for roughly the stimulus duration; PFC decoding reached ~70% but only ~0.2–0.4 s post-onset; fMRI searchlight decoding ~75% posterior vs ~60% PFC (middle/inferior frontal). Orientation decoded posteriorly (~95% iEEG, ~75% MEG, ~45% fMRI) but was absent in PFC with iEEG and fMRI (MEG ~35% in PFC, possibly leakage). Adding PFC to posterior decoders did not improve and sometimes reduced accuracy (Bayesian evidence against improvement). — [Cogitate 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136)
- Prediction 2 (maintenance over time): in posterior iEEG, 25 of 657 electrodes tracked stimulus duration in a sustained way (12 category-general, mostly early visual; 13 category-selective, mostly faces in ventral temporal cortex); only 8 of 53 face-selective electrodes (~15%) showed sustained tracking. In PFC, 99 electrodes showed non-selective onset responses and 24 category-selective onset responses, but none of 655 PFC electrodes showed the GNWT-predicted onset+offset pattern (the same analysis detected that pattern in 10 visual-cortex electrodes, so it was not a sensitivity failure). RSA: posterior face/object representations were sustained (fit IIT better); PFC representations were transient at onset only. Identity was sustained posteriorly for objects, letters and false fonts (not faces) and absent in PFC. — [Cogitate 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136)
- Prediction 3 (connectivity): iEEG phase consistency showed only brief low-frequency (2–25 Hz) V1/V2 synchrony largely explained by the evoked response and no content-selective PFC synchrony (BF01 1.15–5.32 favoring the null); fMRI gPPI from FFA showed content-selective connectivity with V1/V2, IFG and IPS but no selective object–PFC connectivity, with 62–94% of prefrontal voxels showing evidence for the null; gamma connectivity between category-selective electrodes and PFC did appear within the GNWT window in iEEG dynamic functional connectivity. — [Cogitate 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136)
- Authors' verdict: IIT passed on sustained content-specific posterior activity but failed on sustained posterior synchronization ("the most direct challenge"); GNWT was challenged by the absence of offset ignition and by PFC's failure to represent identity or orientation, and partly supported by task-independent category representation and brief onset ignition in PFC. The results "align with some predictions of IIT and GNWT, while substantially challenging key tenets of both." The PFC findings are also said to challenge higher-order theories that place conscious content in PFC. — [Cogitate 2025, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1)
- Press and judging: the study was run by six independent labs; at the 2023 preliminary release, three pre-appointed judges concluded IIT fared slightly better than GNWT on the agreed-upon tests. — [Science news 2023](https://www.science.org/content/article/search-neural-basis-consciousness-yields-first-results); [Quanta 2023](https://www.quantamagazine.org/what-a-contest-of-consciousness-theories-really-proved-20230824/)
- GNWT proponents' rebuttal (Oct 2025): (1) offset ignition was never a core prediction and should occur only if participants attend to the offset; Cogitate's stimuli were task-irrelevant with 500/1000/1500 ms durations and gave no reason to attend to offset, and no GNW simulation ever predicted a definite offset ignition; (2) PFC lacks columnar organization and shows mixed selectivity, so poor decoding there is expected, and visual-area decodability (which also occurs unconsciously) is not strong evidence for IIT; (3) "silent states" are not mandatory for GNWT; (4) Cogitate tested only visible stimuli, so it could not address the central conscious-vs-nonconscious contrast; they also note Cogitate validated transient onset ignition in the predicted 200–800 ms window. — [Naccache et al., Neuroscience of Consciousness 2025](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147)
- IIT proponents' framing: an IIT-aligned summary states two of IIT's three preregistered predictions were supported and the third is contested, with the authors' response in the Nature supplementary information. — [IIT Wiki "Empirical validation"](https://www.iit.wiki/validation) (proponent source; weigh accordingly)
- Aftermath: after the 2023 preliminary release an open letter signed by over 100 researchers called IIT "pseudoscience"; a 2025 Nature commentary cited this as a breakdown of collegiality while arguing adversarial collaboration should be more widely adopted. — [Nature news 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/d41586-023-02971-1); [Nature commentary 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-01379-3)
- INTREPID (IIT vs two predictive-processing variants: Pennartz's neurorepresentationalism and Friston's active inference; Tononi leads IIT): three experiments across ten institutions on three continents; an "adversarial collaborative review" preprint (Sept 2025) by Corcoran, Tononi, Friston, Pennartz et al. lays out contrasting predictions and a scheme for pooling evidence across experiments but reports no final results; a July 2025 symposium was meant to unveil findings. — [Templeton INTREPID page](https://www.templetonworldcharity.org/blog/integrated-information-theory-iit-and-predictive-processing-accounts-of-consciousness); [Corcoran et al., arXiv 2509.00555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00555); [INTREPID symposium](https://arc-intrepid.com/symposium/)
- ETHOS (higher-order theories): led by Stephen Fleming (UCL) and Axel Cleeremans (ULB), who contributed theories but recused themselves from running experiments; launched summer 2024 with designs due July 2025; three experiment lines: psychophysical adaptation (Denison, Peters) to distinguish "rich" vs "sparse" higher-order representations; imaging of perception vs imagination (Dijkstra, Morales); hypnotic suggestion (Dienes) to test whether higher-order states can misrepresent first-order content (e.g., seeing a red apple as green). No results reported. — [Templeton ETHOS page](https://www.templetonworldcharity.org/blog/how-ethos-empirical-tests-of-higher-order-theories-of-consciousness-is-accelerating-research-on-consciousness)
- Why no GNWT-vs-HOT head-to-head: reporting from the original Templeton workshop states participants could not derive predictions that distinguished GNWT from HOT sharply enough, so HOT was spun out into a separate collaboration. — [Quanta 2023](https://www.quantamagazine.org/what-a-contest-of-consciousness-theories-really-proved-20230824/)
- Context: the Templeton "Accelerating Research on Consciousness" program funds five structured adversarial collaborations in total. — [Templeton blog](https://www.templetonworldcharity.org/blog/adversarial-collaboration-drives-breakthroughs-understanding-human-consciousness)
- Reference theory statements for the two tested theories: IIT 4.0 (Albantakis et al. 2023) and the Seth & Bayne 2022 taxonomy of theories. — [Albantakis et al., PLOS Computational Biology 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465); [Seth & Bayne, Nature Reviews Neuroscience 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-022-00587-4)

### Inferences
- For thinking: the data support the existence of a brief frontal ignition that carries a coarse, categorical summary of a percept (~70% iEEG decoding for 200–400 ms, then gone) while detailed content stays posterior. If one extrapolates to thought, the workspace may broadcast a compressed pointer or label rather than a full copy; detailed content would have to be re-read from posterior/specialist stores. This is compatible with GNWT's broadcasting function but weakens the picture of PFC as a continuous high-fidelity stage.
- Cogitate measured passive viewing of task-irrelevant stimuli; it did not test the workspace's claimed functions for reasoning (maintenance under task demand, chaining, combination). GNWT's proponents are right that the conscious-vs-unconscious contrast was not tested, so the collaboration is largely silent on what consciousness does for cognition.
- The result is interpreted asymmetrically by the two camps; the authors' own text is the most reliable guide and it declares no winner.

### Gaps
- No published final results from INTREPID or ETHOS as of this search; both are expected 2025–2026.
- I did not read the second half of the Nature full text (supplementary analyses, e.g., MEG activation models in detail), so some secondary numbers may be missing.

## Key Question 3: Higher-order theories (Lau, Brown, Rosenthal, LeDoux, Fleming, Cleeremans) and Attention Schema Theory (Graziano) on thinking, metacognition and self-monitoring of thoughts

### Takeaway
Higher-order theories locate consciousness not in broadcast but in a (probably prefrontal) meta-representation that re-represents, monitors or "reality-checks" first-order states; the computational versions (Lau's perceptual reality monitoring, Fleming's higher-order state space) make consciousness a metacognitive inference about the reliability/source of a first-order signal, so they tie conscious thought to confidence, source monitoring and imagination-vs-perception discrimination rather than to combination of information. Attention Schema Theory treats awareness as a simplified internal model of attention used to control attention and to model others' attention; 2021–2025 neural-network tests show adding such a schema improves attention control and multi-agent prediction. Cogitate's weak PFC content representation is a problem for "rich" HOTs; the ETHOS collaboration is testing rich-vs-sparse and misrepresentation questions but has no results yet.

### Cited Findings
- Reference statement of modern HOT (foundational): Brown, Lau & LeDoux (2019) define the approach (a first-order state is conscious only when targeted by a suitable higher-order representation), discuss the role of PFC and defend against lesion-based objections. — [Brown, Lau & LeDoux, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2019.06.009)
- LeDoux & Brown (2017) extend HOT to emotion, arguing fear is a higher-order cognitive construct assembled in cortical "general networks of cognition" (working memory/PFC), not a subcortical circuit output. — [LeDoux & Brown, PNAS 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1619316114)
- Fleming's computational HOT: "awareness as inference in a higher-order state space" (HOSS) models consciousness as a higher-order generative model's inference that a first-order content is present ("something vs nothing"), linking awareness reports to metacognitive confidence and to PFC generative models; conscious access becomes a self-monitoring computation. — [Fleming, Neuroscience of Consciousness 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niz020)
- Lau's Perceptual Reality Monitoring theory (book-length statement, 2022): consciousness arises when a higher-order mechanism (prefrontal) tags a first-order representation as reflecting current reality rather than noise, memory or imagination; the theory uses the analogy of a GAN discriminator and connects consciousness to metacognition and to distinguishing perception from imagery. — [Lau, In Consciousness We Trust, OUP 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198856771.001.0001)
- The ETHOS collaboration organizes HOT variants on two axes: "rich" (higher-order states carry content as detailed as perception) vs "sparse" (higher-order states only track the reliability, strength or precision of first-order states), and "relational" (first-order states supply content; strong misrepresentation precluded) vs "non-relational" (experience follows the higher-order state even if it mismatches first-order processing); the re-representation is "probably located in frontal cortex, though nobody is certain." — [Templeton ETHOS page](https://www.templetonworldcharity.org/blog/how-ethos-empirical-tests-of-higher-order-theories-of-consciousness-is-accelerating-research-on-consciousness)
- Four named computational HOT variants being contrasted: HOROR (Brown 2015), Perceptual Reality Monitoring (Lau 2019), the Self-Organising Meta-representational Account (SOMA; Cleeremans et al. 2020) and Higher-Order State theory. — reported via search snippet of a review on philarchive ([philarchive FLECHT](https://philarchive.org/archive/FLECHT)); I could not fetch the full text (HTTP 403), so treat the variant list as reported, not verified in the primary text
- Bridge between GWT and metacognition: Shea & Frith (2019) argue the global workspace "needs metacognition": representations in the workspace carry metacognitive tags (confidence, source) that are required for the workspace to do its job of flexible, system-level control of cognition, so broadcast and self-monitoring are complementary rather than rival accounts. — [Shea & Frith, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2019.04.007)
- GNWT's own C1/C2 split concedes the point: C2 (self-monitoring, confidence, error detection, knowing what one does not know) is listed as a separate computation from C1 (global availability), with both argued to be needed for human-like flexible cognition. — [Dehaene, Lau & Kouider, Science 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871)
- Cogitate's bearing on HOT: the authors state the limited PFC representation of conscious dimensions (identity, orientation absent; category only transiently) also challenges higher-order theories that place conscious content in PFC. — [Cogitate 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136)
- Attention Schema Theory (foundational statement): the brain builds a simplified, schematic model of its own attention (the "attention schema"), analogous to the body schema; this model is used to control attention, is attributed to others for social cognition, and is the basis for the claim of subjective awareness; Graziano argues all three functions stem from the same source. — [Graziano & Webb, Frontiers in Psychology 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00500); [Graziano, PNAS 2022 "A conceptual framework for consciousness"](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2116933119)
- AST in neural networks (2021): Wilterson & Graziano trained a reinforcement-learning agent on a ball-catching task with a limited attention window; agents given an attention schema (a model of their own attention) learned to control attention and performed better, and performance dropped when the schema was removed. — [Wilterson & Graziano, PNAS 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2102421118)
- AST in multi-agent networks (2024–2025): Farrell, Ziman & Graziano trained transformer-style agents to categorize other agents' attention states, judge how categorizable their own attention is, and predict each other in a joint scene-painting task; agents with an attention schema categorized others' attention more accurately, were themselves easier to categorize, and improved on the joint task; gains were specific to attention-judgment tasks and not explained by added complexity (results qualitative on the abstract page). — [Farrell, Ziman & Graziano, arXiv 2411.00983 (rev. Aug 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00983)
- AST applied to transformers (2025): "Attention Schema-based Attention Control (ASAC)" adds an explicit attention-schema module for attention management in transformers, citing Goyal et al. and VanRullen & Kanai. — [arXiv 2509.16058](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16058v1)

### Inferences
- For thinking and idea generation, HOT's distinctive contribution is the claim that what makes a thought conscious is a self-monitoring judgment about it (its reliability, source, or reality status), not its broadcast. This predicts that metacognitive signals (confidence that an idea is promising; discrimination of "remembered" from "generated") are constitutive of conscious ideation rather than add-ons, and that impaired reality monitoring would blur imagination and perception (relevant to the perception-vs-imagination ETHOS experiments).
- AST implies a specific mechanism for self-monitoring of thoughts: an internal model of what one is currently attending to. In machine implementations the schema's measured benefit is better control of attention and better prediction of other agents, suggesting that in humans the schema's role in thinking is primarily attentional control and social modeling rather than content generation.
- The sparse-vs-rich HOT axis matters for workspace accounts: Cogitate's coarse, transient PFC code is more compatible with sparse HOTs (and with GNWT broadcasting a pointer) than with rich HOTs.

### Gaps
- I could not access the computational-HOT review on philarchive (403) and did not retrieve quantitative lesion/TMS metacognition data (e.g., effect sizes of prefrontal disruption on metacognitive efficiency) within the call budget.
- No 2023–2026 primary paper from Rosenthal on thinking was found; no HOT or AST paper specifically about idea generation or creative thought was found.
- ETHOS has reported no empirical results.

## Key Question 4: State of evidence on unconscious thought and problem solving — Unconscious Thought Theory and its replication record; unconscious priming of solutions; what unconscious processing provably can and cannot do

### Takeaway
The Unconscious Thought Advantage (Dijksterhuis's "deliberation without attention") has not survived scrutiny: a 2015 meta-analysis plus a large preregistered replication found no reliable effect, with earlier positive results confined to small-sample studies, and a medical-decision meta-analysis reached the same null. The broader literature (Newell & Shanks 2014; Mudrik & Deouell 2022; Tal & Mudrik 2024) converges on a "limited scope" view: unconscious processing can perform single, well-learned, local operations (feedforward categorization, single-word semantics, simple priming, possibly subliminal hints toward a solution) but reliably fails at flexible initiation of a novel operation, integration across elements (object–scene relations, sentence meaning, arithmetic chaining) and following instructions.

### Cited Findings
- Original claims (foundational): Dijksterhuis (2004) and Dijksterhuis et al. (2006, Science) reported that after a distraction period participants chose better among complex multi-attribute options (e.g., cars with 12 attributes) than after conscious deliberation, proposing Unconscious Thought Theory. — [Dijksterhuis, JPSP 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.5.586); [Dijksterhuis et al., Science 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1121629)
- Early supportive meta-analyses reported g = 0.251 (Acker 2008, UT vs conscious thought) and g = 0.402 (Strick et al. 2009, UT vs conscious thought and immediate decision). — summarized in [Nieuwenstein et al., Judgment and Decision Making 2015](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/on-making-the-right-choice-a-metaanalysis-and-largescale-replication-attempt-of-the-unconscious-thought-advantage/A31405A97BC221C10153E9CF78A94DB6)
- Definitive meta-analysis and large replication: Nieuwenstein, Wierenga, Morey, Wicherts, Blom, Wagenmakers & van Rijn (2015) found the published literature on multi-attribute UTA contained predominantly non-significant effects (N = 45) and only 16 significant effects, 12 in the UTA direction; prior UTA reports were confined to underpowered studies; their large-scale replication yielded no evidence for the UTA; they conclude "there exists no reliable support for the claim that a momentary diversion of thought leads to better decision making than a period of deliberation," attributing the asymmetric funnel to publication bias against small studies finding a conscious-thought advantage. — [Nieuwenstein et al., JDM 2015](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/on-making-the-right-choice-a-metaanalysis-and-largescale-replication-attempt-of-the-unconscious-thought-advantage/A31405A97BC221C10153E9CF78A94DB6)
- Earlier failed replications: Calvillo & Penaloza (2009) found decisions were consistently better than chance when made immediately after encoding and no UT advantage; Nieuwenstein & van Rijn (2012) failed to replicate within-subjects even when the task met every meta-analytically identified facilitating condition, and suggested sample characteristics (e.g., gender) rather than task features drove earlier results. — [Calvillo & Penaloza, JDM 2009](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/are-complex-decisions-better-left-to-the-unconscious-further-failed-replications-of-the-deliberationwithoutattention-effect/D2F0A25CA1F498259B90C3289FA54342); [Nieuwenstein & van Rijn, JDM 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/876C6081F70DCDBABAF789080FBEFE2C/S1930297500003338a.pdf/the-unconscious-thought-advantage-further-replication-failures-from-a-search-for-confirmatory-evidence.pdf)
- Medical decision making: a critical review and meta-analysis concluded the UT advantage in medical decisions is "an unreliable effect," that the many non-replications reflect a genuine null, and that insufficient power or moderators are unlikely explanations. — [Vadillo, Kostopoulou & Shanks 2015 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4436803/)
- Broad critical review (foundational): Newell & Shanks (2014, BBS target article) reviewed unconscious influences on decision making (UTT, subliminal priming, deliberation-without-attention, intuition) and concluded evidence for unconscious influences is weak once awareness is measured properly. — [Newell & Shanks, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X12003214)
- Unconscious arithmetic claims and their collapse: Sklar et al. (2012) reported solving masked arithmetic under continuous flash suppression; Ric & Muller (2012) reported unconscious initiation and following of an addition instruction. Tal & Mudrik (2024, JEP: General 153(10):2470–2486) ran two "highly powered" preregistered experiments with tighter control of awareness of both instruction and digits and failed to replicate Ric & Muller, concluding "the current evidence does not support the claim that arithmetic operations can be flexibly initiated unconsciously" and that consciousness "may indeed be necessary for tasks that require integrating different kinds of information in a complex and flexible manner." — [Sklar et al., PNAS 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1211645109); [Tal & Mudrik 2024 record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/no-evidence-for-unconscious-initiation-and-following-of-arithmeti/); [Tal & Mudrik 2024 PDF](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/mudriklab/files/2024/09/TalMudrik_2024.pdf); replication-atlas entry: [FORRT](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1037/a0024608/)
- A separate direct replication of Sklar et al.'s unconscious arithmetic was retracted after a data-processing error; the corrected analysis found no evidence of unconscious arithmetic. — [Retraction Watch 2017](https://retractionwatch.com/2017/05/08/can-math-unconsciously-replicators-prominent-2012-study-doubts)
- Unconscious integration: Mudrik et al. (2011) originally reported integration of an object into a scene without awareness; preregistered follow-ups by Biderman & Mudrik (2018) found evidence for implicit but not unconscious processing of object–scene relations and noted this "is in line with both IIT and GNW theories." — [Mudrik et al., Psychological Science 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797611408736); [Biderman & Mudrik, Psychological Science 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617735745)
- Unconscious sentence comprehension: Rabagliati, Robertson & Carmel (2018) failed to replicate CFS-based claims that multi-word meaning is processed without awareness, concluding awareness is important for language understanding beyond single words. — [Rabagliati et al., JEP: General 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000348)
- Review of neuroscientific evidence (2022): Mudrik & Deouell survey processing without awareness and argue the robust findings are low-level and single-item while high-level integration claims are fragile. The "integration window" framing: higher-level integration needs more processing resources and a longer temporal window, typically available only with awareness. — [Mudrik & Deouell, Annual Review of Neuroscience 2022](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-110920-033151); integration-window framing as cited in [a 2026 metaphorical-priming paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810026000437)
- Methods consensus: a 2024 multi-author paper, "Studying unconscious processing: towards a consensus on best practices," recommends preregistered analysis plans and proper awareness measures and acknowledges that publication bias and selective reporting have accumulated false positives. — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381901870_Studying_unconscious_processing_towards_a_consensus_on_best_practices)
- What unconscious processing can do for problem solving (positive, older evidence): subliminally presented hints improved solving of insight problems in Hattori, Sloman & Orita (2013); a solution-relevant prime shown below awareness increased solution rates without participants reporting the hint. — [Hattori, Sloman & Orita, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0389-0)
- GNWT's own list of unconscious competences (feedforward categorization in the first ~200 ms, local prediction errors, single-operation priming, activity-silent storage) matches the empirical "limited scope" picture. — [Mashour et al., Neuron 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/)

### Inferences
- The evidence pattern is consistent across paradigms: unconscious processing is good at activating stored associations (priming, single-word semantics, learned category mappings) and at passively letting a solution-relevant representation accumulate activation, but bad at operations on representations: selecting a novel rule, applying it, chaining steps, or binding two items into a new relational structure. For idea generation this suggests unconscious processes can bias which elements become available (spreading activation, hints) but the combinatorial act itself is a conscious, workspace-dependent event.
- Because the UTA literature's positive findings cluster in small samples with evidence of publication bias, practical advice to "let the unconscious decide" is unsupported; incubation effects (covered in the earlier round) are better interpreted via forgetting of fixation and renewed conscious search than via productive unconscious deliberation.

### Gaps
- Sample sizes and effect sizes of Tal & Mudrik's two experiments were not extracted (the PDF did not parse); only "highly powered" is confirmed from the abstract.
- I did not find a 2023–2026 meta-analysis of subliminal solution priming; Hattori et al. 2013 is the main positive result and I found no preregistered replication of it.
- Proponents' responses to Nieuwenstein et al. 2015 (if any) were not retrieved.

## Key Question 5: Capacity and serial nature of conscious thought — one thought at a time, ~4-item working memory, the central bottleneck — and how this constrains idea combination

### Takeaway
Multiple independent lines converge on conscious cognition being a low-throughput serial channel: a ~4-chunk working-memory limit with a single-item focus of attention (Cowan; Oberauer), a central processing bottleneck that forces two decisions to be made one after another (psychological refractory period, attentional blink), which the Dehaene lab showed share a common late frontoparietal stage, and Zheng & Meister's 2024 estimate that behavioral information throughput is ~10 bits/s against ~10^9 bits/s of sensory input. The implication for idea combination is that only a handful of elements can be simultaneously active in the workspace, so combinations must be built pairwise and sequentially, with intermediate results stored and re-read.

### Cited Findings
- Working-memory capacity ~4 chunks (foundational): Cowan's 2001 BBS review argued the capacity of the focus of attention is about four items when rehearsal and chunking are prevented. — [Cowan, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922)
- Single-item focus (foundational): Oberauer (2002) proposed a concentric model in which only one item occupies the focus of attention at a time, with a few more in a directly accessible region. — [Oberauer, JEP: Learning, Memory & Cognition 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.28.3.411)
- GNWT's working-memory picture matches this: a focus of attention holding the most active (conscious) item plus a limited-capacity store of a few others held by weaker local firing or activity-silent synapses. — [Mashour et al., Neuron 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/)
- Central bottleneck (foundational): in the psychological refractory period (PRP) paradigm both stimuli are processed but the second response is delayed by a central bottleneck (Pashler 1994 review); Sigman & Dehaene (2008) showed with fMRI/EEG that perceptual stages run in parallel while a central decision stage is serial; Marti, Sigman & Dehaene (2012) found the attentional blink and PRP share a late (>300 ms) frontoparietal bottleneck stage in MEG. — [Pashler review (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC546336/); [Sigman & Dehaene, Journal of Neuroscience 2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0948-08.2008); [Marti, Sigman & Dehaene, NeuroImage 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2011.09.063)
- A unified attentional bottleneck: fMRI shows a common frontoparietal "multiple-demand" system is the site of the severe capacity limit in implementing more than one task set at a time. — [Tombu et al., PNAS 2011](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1103583108)
- Chaining is serial and conscious: Sackur & Dehaene (2009) found that two successive mental operations cannot be chained on subliminal input; chaining requires conscious perception of the operand. — [Sackur & Dehaene, Cognition 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2009.01.003)
- Throughput estimate (2024): Zheng & Meister (Caltech; arXiv Aug 2024, accepted at Neuron) compute human behavioral information throughput at ~10 bits/s across tasks versus ~10^9 bits/s gathered by sensory systems, and propose an "outer brain" handling fast high-dimensional sensorimotor signals and an "inner brain" that processes the few bits controlling behavior; they explicitly pose "why can we only think about one thing at a time?" as unexplained and link the limit to a serial, one-path-at-a-time organization. — [Zheng & Meister, arXiv 2408.10234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10234); press summary: [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/neuroscience/news/caltech-scientists-have-quantified-the-speed-of-human-thought-394395)
- Theoretical 2026 preprint: conscious access framed as a structure-preserving translation from a continuous sensorimotor regime ("System C") to a centralized engine of discrete symbols ("System D"), with the serial bottleneck (PRP, attentional blink, working-memory limits) treated as a constitutive feature of symbolic operation rather than an incidental limit; predicts a measurable collapse from graded similarity structure to low-dimensional categorical equivalence classes at access. No data. — [Yang, arXiv 2608.20723 (Aug 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20723)
- 2026 accumulation-to-threshold model of the stream of thought (PMC), found by search but not read; listed for the writer's awareness only. — [PMC13474264](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13474264/)

### Inferences
- Combining the ~4-chunk limit, the single-item focus and the serial central stage yields a concrete constraint on idea combination: at most a few candidate elements can be co-active, only one can be operated on at a time, and each operation costs on the order of hundreds of milliseconds. Rich combinations therefore depend on chunking (compressing a sub-combination into a single workspace token) and on offloading intermediate results (to memory or external media), which is why externalization and notation plausibly expand combinatorial reach.
- Cogitate's finding that PFC holds a coarse categorical code is consistent with the workspace trafficking in compressed chunks/pointers rather than full content, which is what a ~10 bits/s channel would require.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 experimental paper that directly measures how many ideas or candidate solutions can be simultaneously entertained during ideation; constraints are extrapolated from perception/WM/dual-task paradigms.
- Zheng & Meister's per-task bits/s values (typing, speech, memory sports) were not extracted from the abstract page.

## Key Question 6: 2023–2026 computational implementations of global-workspace ideas in AI and what they showed about flexible problem solving

### Takeaway
A steady line of work (VanRullen & Kanai 2021 blueprint; Goyal et al. ICLR 2022 shared workspace; Juliani et al. 2022; Butlin et al. 2023 indicator properties; Devillers/Maytié/VanRullen 2024; Dossa et al. 2024; Chateau-Laurent & VanRullen 2025) implements a bandwidth-limited shared latent space through which specialist modules compete and broadcast. Reported benefits are consistent in direction: better compositional/out-of-distribution generalization, zero-shot cross-modal transfer with 4–7x less paired data, robustness at small memory sizes, and improved multi-step "chaining" of operations versus LSTMs/Transformers, with a sharp optimum in workspace capacity (e.g., 5 slots, ARI 0.915 vs 0.154 at 2 slots and 0.351 at 10). None of these systems is claimed to be conscious; they are evidence that a serial, capacity-limited broadcast channel is computationally useful for flexible, multi-step problem solving.

### Cited Findings
- Blueprint (foundational, 2021): VanRullen & Kanai propose a "Global Latent Workspace": independent specialized modules each with a high-level latent space; an intermediate shared latent space trained by unsupervised neural translation (cycle-consistency) among module latents; attention selects which inputs enter; selected content is copied into the workspace and immediately broadcast back to all modules. — [VanRullen & Kanai, Trends in Neurosciences 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2021.04.006)
- Bengio's "consciousness prior" (foundational, 2017): the hypothesis that a low-dimensional, sparse conscious state (a few variables selected by attention) is a useful inductive bias for learning high-level, compositional representations and causal structure. — [Bengio, arXiv 1709.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08568)
- Goyal et al. (ICLR 2022 oral): "Coordination Among Neural Modules Through a Shared Global Workspace": specialists (Transformer heads / RIMs) communicate only through a limited-slot shared workspace for which they compete; the authors argue capacity limits "encourage specialization and compositionality" and "facilitate the synchronization of otherwise independent specialists"; on the bouncing-balls task performance peaked at 5 memory slots (ARI 0.915) and fell with 2 slots (ARI 0.154) or 10 slots (ARI 0.351). — [Goyal et al., ICLR 2022 / arXiv 2103.01197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01197); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=XzTtHjgPDsT); slot numbers via [review summary](https://liner.com/review/coordination-among-neural-modules-through-a-shared-global-workspace)
- Juliani, Arulkumaran, Sasai & Kanai (2022) argue the Perceiver architecture's latent bottleneck already approximates a global workspace and link conscious function to general intelligence in machines. — [Juliani et al., arXiv 2204.05133 / TMLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05133)
- Butlin, Long, Elmoznino, Bengio, Birch, Constant, Deane, Fleming, Frith, Ji, Kanai, Klein, Lindsay, Michel, Mudrik, Peters, Schwitzgebel, Simon & VanRullen (2023) derive "indicator properties" for AI consciousness from GWT (GWT-1: multiple specialized modules operating in parallel; GWT-2: limited-capacity workspace entailing a bottleneck and selective attention; GWT-3: global broadcast to all modules; GWT-4: state-dependent attention allowing the workspace to query modules for complex, successive tasks), from HOT, AST and others, and conclude no current system satisfies all indicators but there are no obvious technical barriers. — [Butlin et al., arXiv 2308.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)
- Devillers, Maytié & VanRullen (IEEE TNNLS 36(5):7843–7857, 2024): a two-modality (vision + language) Global Workspace with frozen pretrained unimodal encoders, trained with cross-modal translation plus self-supervised cycle-consistency; aligned and translated between modalities with "4 to 7 times less" matched data than a fully supervised approach, and the workspace representation improved downstream classification and robust transfer learning. — [Devillers et al., arXiv 2306.15711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15711)
- Maytié et al. (2024): zero-shot cross-modal transfer of reinforcement-learning policies through a Global Workspace; a policy trained on one modality transfers to another via the shared latent. — [arXiv 2403.04588](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04588)
- Dossa, Arulkumaran, Juliani, Sasai & Kanai (Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience 18:1352685, June 2024; Araya Inc. and Microsoft Research): an embodied agent built to the Butlin et al. GWT indicator properties, navigating 3D environments with audiovisual input; the GW architecture "performs better and more robustly at smaller working memory sizes" than a standard recurrent architecture, with task complexity and regularization "essential for feature learning and the development of meaningful attentional patterns within the workspace." — [Dossa et al. 2024 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11211627/); [Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2024.1352685/text)
- Chateau-Laurent & VanRullen (Feb/Mar 2025): "Learning to Chain Operations by Routing Information Through a Global Workspace": an LSTM controller gates information between Input, Increment and Output modules via a shared workspace to perform multi-step addition (both with hand-designed one-hot modules and with learned MNIST modules), explicitly framed as System-2 reasoning; with fewer parameters the GW model "outperforms LSTMs and Transformers when tested on unseen addition operations" (interpolation and extrapolation). — [arXiv 2503.01906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01906)
- Other 2025–2026 GW-architecture papers (found, not read in full): "Multimodal Dreaming: a Global Workspace approach to world-model-based RL" ([arXiv 2502.21142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21142)); "Hypothesis on the functional advantages of the selection-broadcast cycle structure: GWT and dealing with a real-time world" ([arXiv 2505.13969](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13969)); "An attention mechanism for robust multimodal integration in a Global Workspace architecture" ([arXiv 2602.08597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08597), Feb 2026).
- Philosophical application to LLM agents: Goldstein & Kirk-Giannini (Oct 2024) use GWT to assess whether language agents could be conscious, noting that VanRullen & Kanai do not require processing inside the workspace whereas Juliani et al. do. — [arXiv 2410.11407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11407)
- Measuring "ignition" in LLMs (2026): Rahbar's "Ignition Index" fits a 4-parameter sigmoid to per-layer probe accuracy vs input signal strength across 11 models in five families; feedforward transformers exceeded state-space models (Mamba) by 89% in aggregate steepness (p < 1e-13, d = 0.52); Huginn-3.5B showed 2.12x higher ignition along its recurrence axis than its depth axis; Pythia-410M showed a +67% phase transition at training step 256 preceding induction-head formation; shuffled-label controls gave 9.6-fold selectivity (p < 0.001). The author does not link ignition to reasoning ability. Single-author preprint; unreviewed. — [Rahbar, arXiv 2608.05160 (May 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05160)
- A 2026 preprint on the "serial-depth bottleneck" in LLM chain-of-thought cites Chen et al. (2024) proving an L-layer decoder-only transformer fails at L-sequential function composition; an analogy, not human data. — [arXiv 2608.09942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09942)

### Inferences
- The machine results converge on the same three claims GNWT makes about the human workspace: (1) a narrow bottleneck forces modules to specialize and representations to become compositional; (2) a shared latent lets information learned in one module be used by another (zero-shot transfer is "combining information across modules"); (3) a controller that routes contents through the workspace step by step generalizes to longer/unseen chains better than monolithic networks. This is the strongest available evidence that seriality and capacity limits are features for flexible problem solving, not merely costs.
- The sharp optimum in workspace capacity (Goyal et al.) and the robustness advantage at small memory sizes (Dossa et al.) suggest an intermediate bottleneck is best, consistent with the human ~4-chunk limit being functionally tuned rather than merely a constraint.
- None of these systems implements metacognition/self-monitoring (C2/HOT) or an attention schema beyond Graziano's small-scale demonstrations; combined C1+C2 architectures remain a gap.

### Gaps
- Quantitative accuracies for Chateau-Laurent & VanRullen 2025 and Dossa et al. 2024 were not extracted (abstract-level fetches only).
- No 2023–2026 paper directly benchmarks a GW architecture on open-ended idea generation or creativity tasks; all tasks are perception, control or arithmetic chaining.
- Bengio-group 2023–2026 follow-ups to the consciousness prior (beyond the 2022 shared workspace) were not surfaced by my searches.
