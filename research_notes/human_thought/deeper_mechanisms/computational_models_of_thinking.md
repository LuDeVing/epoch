## Key Question 1: Mental simulation and intuitive physics/psychology ("game engine in the head"), counterfactual simulation, and the 2020-2026 critiques

### Takeaway
The "intuitive physics engine" (IPE) program remains the only quantitative account that predicts graded human physical and causal judgments across many scenes (correlations of ~0.9+ with counterfactual judgments; eye-movements that trace counterfactual trajectories), but 2020-2023 work shows people systematically violate the "complete, noisy, probabilistically coherent simulation" assumptions (conjunction fallacies, below-chance temporal-order judgments, change blindness after collapse). The field's current synthesis is *partial / selective simulation*: people simulate only the parts of a scene deemed relevant, which both explains the fallacies and is the most implementable version of the theory.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational IPE claim (pre-2023, context only)**
- Battaglia, Hamrick & Tenenbaum (2013, PNAS) proposed that human physical scene understanding rests on an "intuitive physics engine" — approximate, probabilistic simulation akin to a video-game engine — and fit graded judgments about block towers falling/direction of fall across hundreds of scenes; the model and its successors are the baseline all later critiques target — [Battaglia et al. 2013, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3831455)
- Ullman, Spelke, Battaglia & Tenenbaum (2017, TICS, "Mind Games: Game Engines as an Architecture for Intuitive Physics") extended the proposal: game-engine architecture (object representations, approximate rigid-body dynamics, "sleeping" of irrelevant objects, level-of-detail control) as a model of the human physical reasoning system — [Ullman et al. 2017 TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2017.05.012) [foundational; DOI from memory, not re-verified this session]

**Counterfactual Simulation Model (CSM) for causal judgment**
- Gerstenberg, Goodman, Lagnado & Tenenbaum (2021, Psychological Review) introduced the CSM: causal judgments about dynamic collisions are predicted by comparing what actually happened to what the simulated world would have done under an intervention (removing the candidate cause); the model decomposes causation into *whether*, *how*, *sufficiency* and *robustness* aspects; a preliminary study established a "very close quantitative mapping between causal and counterfactual judgments"; Experiment 1 showed scene pairs with identical actual outcomes but different counterfactual outcomes yield very different causal judgments; the CSM fit judgments better than a heuristic model based on features of what actually happened — [Gerstenberg et al. 2021, Stanford CICL](https://cicl.stanford.edu/publication/gerstenberg2021csm)
- Gerstenberg (2024, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 28(10):924-, "Counterfactual simulation in causal cognition") reviews the CSM: three ingredients are needed — "a generative mental model of the world, the ability to perform interventions on that model, and the capacity to simulate the consequences of these interventions"; across 18 collision animations, a group asked "Did ball A cause ball B to go through the gate?" and a separate group asked "Would ball B have missed the gate if ball A had not been present?" gave answers that aligned with r = 0.96 — [Gerstenberg 2024 TICS, DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2024.04.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.04.012); [PDF](https://cicl.stanford.edu/papers/gerstenberg2024counterfactual.pdf)
- Eye-tracking (Gerstenberg et al. 2017 Psych Sci, summarized in the 2024 review): saccade endpoints during the pre-collision interval were classified as "counterfactual saccades" if they landed near the path ball B would have taken had A been absent; participants asked causal questions produced counterfactual saccades nearly as often as those asked counterfactual questions, and far more than those asked only about the actual outcome — "when people are asked a causal question about what happened, they spontaneously simulate counterfactual outcomes" — [Gerstenberg 2024 TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.04.012)
- Three-ball scenarios show judgments track *how* as well as *whether*: in a causal chain, ball B is judged somewhat responsible even though participants agree E would have gone through without B; in double prevention B gets little responsibility even though E would have missed without B — [Gerstenberg 2024 TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.04.012)
- The CSM has been extended to omissions as causes, physical support/stability, causal-verb semantics, and to the social domain (responsibility in helping/hindering scenarios where the counterfactual is "how the agent would have acted") — [Gerstenberg 2024 TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.04.012)
- Gerstenberg's own open questions (2024): "What determines what counterfactuals come to mind"; "How do people carry out counterfactual simulations in their mind? What aspects of the world do they choose to simulate, what aspects do they ignore?"; how do people pick the level of abstraction (actions vs mental states vs traits) for counterfactuals — [Gerstenberg 2024 TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.04.012)

**Critiques: the "broken physics engine" line**
- Ludwin-Peery, Bramley, Davis & Gureckis (2020, Psychological Science 31(12):1602-1611) reported a conjunction fallacy in intuitive physics: people judge a conjunction of two physical outcomes (e.g., in a two-ball collision) more likely than one of its components, which a coherent probabilistic simulator should never do — reported via [Vicovaro 2023 review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10607174/) and [Bass et al. 2021 NeurIPS workshop page](https://nips.cc/virtual/2021/34418)
- Ludwin-Peery, Bramley, Davis & Gureckis (2021, Cognitive Psychology, "Limits on simulation approaches in intuitive physics") identify three "core tenets" a viable simulation theory must hold and argue each conflicts with general cognitive findings; three experiments: (1) *complete simulation* — participants were poor at detecting changes in the number and shape of blocks in a tower after it collapsed; (2) *temporal order* — with two spheres rolling down ramps with obstacles, participants often reversed arrival order, and in a 2-second video version performance was *below chance*; (3) *normative probability* — the conjunction fallacy in collision outcomes — [Gureckis lab page](https://www.gureckislab.org/papers/ref/ludwin2021limits); experiment details via [Vicovaro 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10607174/); PDF on E. Davis's site [cs.nyu.edu CogPsychFinal.pdf](https://cs.nyu.edu/~ed1/papers/CogPsychFinal.pdf)
- Vicovaro (2023, Journal of Intelligence 11(10):187, "Grounding Intuitive Physics in Perceptual Experience") catalogues persistent misconceptions that Bayesian IPE models do not predict under realistic conditions: ~half of participants predict straight-down fall of a ball released by a moving carrier (McCloskey et al. 1983, including in a real walking task); the "heavier falls faster" belief appears by ages 5-6 and persists; Hecht (2015) rods task: just over 40% predicted the heavier rod falls faster and just over 70% judged it harder to balance (difficulty is actually equal); Smith et al. (2018) found large errors on static pendulum-path predictions but near-physical responses in an action-oriented catching task. He proposes a domain-general heuristic — overgeneralization of perceptual-motor experience — and a dual-system view (cognitive heuristics vs accurate, cognitively impenetrable motor representations) — [Vicovaro 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10607174/)

**Defense: partial / selective simulation and generalization tests**
- Bass, Smith, Bonawitz & Ullman (2021, NeurIPS Physical Reasoning workshop, Spotlight; "Efficient Partial Simulation Quantitatively Explains Deviations from Optimal Physical Predictions") propose *partial simulation*: "moves forward in time only parts of the world deemed relevant"; they show the Ludwin-Peery conjunction fallacy "is hard to explain with full simulation but can be accounted for quantitatively with partial simulation", with the direction of the effect depending on how close the sphere starts to the hole; the fallacy is framed as an efficient shortcut, "not a fallacy per se" — [NeurIPS 2021 page](https://nips.cc/virtual/2021/34418); talk framing in [Ullman, Collège de France seminar 2024 (PDF)](https://www.college-de-france.fr/sites/default/files/media/document/2024-02/Dehaene_2023-2024_Seminaire_26-janvier_Ullman.pdf)
- Wang, Jedoui, Venkatesh, Binder, Tenenbaum, Fan, Yamins & Smith (CogSci 2024, "Probabilistic simulation supports generalizable intuitive physics"): two human studies over seven physical scenarios (predict whether/where two objects contact); humans were highly accurate and consistent with each other; a noisy-physics IPE generalized in human-like ways to unseen stimuli and to a different query type, whereas state-of-the-art learned dynamics models and scene-feature models explained human predictions less well; conclusion: learned dynamics models "need more structure" — [Smith lab page](https://www.mit.edu/~k2smith/publication/physion_sim)
- Vicovaro's own survey notes the simulationists' response options: partial simulation (Bass et al. 2021, drawing on "sleeping elements" from Ullman et al. 2017) and the dual-system account — [Vicovaro 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10607174/)

**Intuitive psychology / theory-based models as simulators**
- Tsividis, Loula, Burga, Foss, Campero, Pouncy, Gershman & Tenenbaum (2021, arXiv) "Theory-Based Reinforcement Learning": the EMPA agent (Exploring, Modeling, Planning Agent) learns probabilistic generative models *written as programs for a game-engine simulator* via Bayesian inference, explores by simulating, and plans with heuristic search on the learned simulator; on 90 Atari-style games it "closely matches human learning efficiency", learning in minutes of play, and "reproduces fine-grained patterns in people's exploration trajectories and learning dynamics" — [arXiv 2107.12544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12544)

### Inferences
- The simulation-vs-heuristics debate has effectively converged on a resource-bounded simulator: the CSM/IPE wins on *graded, scene-specific* prediction and on process evidence (eye-movements), while the Ludwin-Peery/Vicovaro findings show the simulator is incomplete, selectively attends, and is not probabilistically coherent across conjoined events. Partial simulation is a direct bridge to Key Question 4 (resource rationality): what gets simulated is itself a controlled, cost-sensitive decision.
- Implementable procedure (from CSM + partial simulation): (1) build a generative scene model; (2) select a candidate cause and a counterfactual intervention (remove/replace); (3) sample N noisy forward rollouts of only the "awake" objects on the causal path; (4) estimate P(outcome differs | intervention) and separate "how" vs "whether" contrasts; (5) output graded causal/responsibility judgment. Gerstenberg's open question — "what determines which counterfactuals come to mind" — is the piece most relevant to idea generation and is unsolved.

## Key Question 2: Probabilistic language of thought (PLoT), program induction, and library learning as a model of accumulating reusable ideas

### Takeaway
The PLoT program treats concepts as stochastic programs composed from primitives, with learning as Bayesian search over programs favoring simplicity; 2023-2024 work makes this concrete and human-calibrated: Rule et al. (2024) show that search over *metaprograms* (programs that revise programs) fits human rule learning on 100 list functions while using about three orders of magnitude less search than enumeration, within estimated human thinking budgets; Wong et al. (2023) show LLMs can translate language into PLoT code so that inference, physics simulation and planning run over the translated world model; "library learning" (DreamCoder/Stitch/LILO) formalizes how solved problems are compressed into reusable abstractions that bias future search — a direct model of cumulative idea accumulation.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational PLoT (context)**
- Goodman, Tenenbaum, Feldman & Griffiths (2008, Cognitive Science) "A rational analysis of rule-based concept learning" — concepts as rules in a probabilistic grammar, prior favoring short rules; Piantadosi, Tenenbaum & Goodman (2016, Psychological Review) "The logical primitives of thought" fit human Boolean-concept learning curves with LoT grammars of different primitive sets; Rule, Tenenbaum & Piantadosi (2020, TICS) "The child as hacker" frame learning as program synthesis with hacker-like goals (efficiency, modularity, reuse) — [Piantadosi et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0039980); [Rule et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.005) [foundational; DOIs from memory, not re-verified this session]

**Rule et al. 2024 — metaprogram search explains human rule learning**
- Rule, Piantadosi, Cropper, Ellis, Nye & Tenenbaum (2024, Nature Communications 15:6847). Model MPL (MetaProgram Learner) searches with MCMC over metaprograms built from metaprimitives such as MemorizeAll, Recurse and AntiUnify, which restructure candidate programs using the structure of the observed data; prior weighs both metaprogram complexity and resulting program complexity — [PMC11316799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11316799/)
- Benchmark: 250 list functions (lists of numbers 0-99, up to 15 elements); first 100 used for model comparison; 392 Prolific participants (389 analyzed; ~16 per function). Human baseline: 54% of functions learned by at least half of participants within eight trials; only 2% learned by all; mean accuracy 0.521; median response time 14.7 s — [PMC11316799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11316799/)
- Results: with 500K search steps only MPL and Fleet (a prior LoT sampler) matched human performance; Enumerate, Metagol and RobustFill fell at or below the 25th human percentile; Codex (GPT-3-based) fell in between. MPL's posterior at 5K steps exceeded Fleet's at 5M steps; at 5K steps MPL retained at least 84% of its large-budget acquisition rate; after only 50 search steps MPL beat Metagol, Enumerate and RobustFill using far more search. MPL: 97.0% of runs correct on non-recursive problems but only 34.4% on recursive ones; removing either prior substantially hurt performance — [PMC11316799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11316799/)
- Human thinking-time calibration: at 5-10 ms per step people could do ~3,000-6,000 steps in the median 14.7 s; at 500-1,000 ms per step only 30-60 steps; MPL with 500, 50 or even 20 steps still fell in the human range. Claim: human rule learning may rely on metaprogram-like representations that reshape inductive bias; learning is sensitive to multiple kinds of simplicity, not just final program length — [PMC11316799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11316799/)

**Wong et al. 2023 — language to PLoT world models**
- Wong, Grand, Lew, Goodman, Mansinghka, Andreas & Tenenbaum (2023, arXiv 2306.12672, "From Word Models to World Models"): "rational meaning construction" — meaning is a context-sensitive mapping from natural language into a probabilistic language of thought (a probabilistic programming language, Church) which is "a general-purpose symbolic substrate for generative world modeling"; LLMs do the translation, Bayesian inference over the generated programs does the thinking; demonstrated in four domains — probabilistic reasoning, logical/relational reasoning, visual/physical reasoning, social reasoning about agents' plans — with "cognitively-motivated symbolic modules (physics simulators, graphics engines, and planning algorithms)" plugged into the PLoT; final section addresses how language could drive construction of the world models themselves — [arXiv 2306.12672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12672v2)

**Library learning: DreamCoder, Stitch, LILO, concept libraries from language**
- Ellis, Wong, Nye, Sablé-Meyer, Cary, Morales, Hewitt, Solar-Lezama & Tenenbaum (2020 arXiv; PLDI 2021) DreamCoder: wake-sleep algorithm alternating between (wake) searching for programs that solve tasks, (abstraction sleep) refactoring shared sub-programs into a growing domain-specific library, and (dream sleep) training a neural recognition network on imagined and replayed problems; the library makes later search cheaper; it "rediscovers concepts from functional programming, vector algebra, and classical physics, including Newton's and Coulomb's laws", and solves "creative tasks like drawing pictures and building scenes" — [arXiv 2006.08381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381)
- Critique and successors: babble (Cao et al. 2022) notes DreamCoder's abstraction search is "purely syntactic and hence not robust to superficial variation" and candidate generation "imprecise"; uses e-graphs plus anti-unification so that 2+1 and 1+3 can be refactored under λx.x+1 — [arXiv 2212.04596](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04596)
- LILO (Grand, Wong, Bowers, Olausson, Liu, Tenenbaum & Andreas, ICLR 2024) "iteratively synthesizes, compresses, and documents code" — LLM-guided synthesis + Stitch (a symbolic compressor that finds optimal λ-abstractions across large corpora) + an auto-documentation step that gives abstractions natural-language names/docstrings; on string editing, scene reasoning and graphics composition LILO "solves more complex tasks and learns richer libraries grounded in linguistic knowledge" than DreamCoder; naming abstractions measurably improves their later reuse — [arXiv 2310.19791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19791); [ICLR 2024](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18550)
- Human link — Wong, McCarthy, Grand, Friedman, Tenenbaum, Andreas, Hawkins & Fan (CogSci 2022, "Identifying concept libraries from language about object structure"): natural-language descriptions of ~2,000 procedurally generated objects; the part concepts people name reflect "a fundamental information-theoretic tradeoff": a lexicon should let each object be described concisely *and* be small itself; framed as search over program libraries, scored by how well programs in each library align with human language — [arXiv 2205.05666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05666)
- Human link — Zhou, Feinman & Lake (2023 arXiv; published in Cognition 2024 per memory, unverified): people classified and generated "alien figures" with relational structure under few-shot conditions; a Bayesian program induction model over a compositional program space "closely matches" classification data and its fitted parameters reveal which factors people treat as category-invariant (rotation, part attachment); in generation people went beyond the model in structured ways ("completing a set", "reconfiguring existing parts in highly novel ways"), motivating a neuro-symbolic model that composes new concepts from existing parts plus neural modules for residual statistics — [arXiv 2305.19374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19374)
- Human link — Lake, Salakhutdinov & Tenenbaum (2015, Science) Bayesian Program Learning: handwritten characters as motor programs built from strokes/sub-strokes; model generates novel exemplars and novel concepts judged indistinguishable from human productions in visual Turing tests — [DOI 10.1126/science.aab3050](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aab3050) [foundational; from memory, not re-verified this session]

**Meta-learned alternative**
- Binz, Dasgupta, Jagadish, Botvinick, Wang & Schulz (2023 arXiv; BBS target article) "Meta-learned models of cognition": "meta-learning can be used to construct Bayes-optimal learning algorithms" — whatever a Bayesian model explains a meta-learned network can too — and it can be applied where exact Bayesian inference is intractable and can incorporate limited computational resources; proposed as a research program complementary to Bayesian/PLoT models — [arXiv 2304.06729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06729)

### Inferences
- Library learning is the most concrete formal model of "accumulating reusable ideas" found: the loop *solve -> compress shared structure into named abstraction -> bias future search toward it* is fully specified (DreamCoder/Stitch/LILO) and has human behavioral analogues in part-naming (Wong et al. 2022) and in people's structured novel generations (Zhou et al.). The Wong 2022 result also suggests a selection criterion for which abstractions enter the library: minimum description length over corpus + lexicon.
- Rule et al.'s metaprogram result supports a specific human mechanism for hypothesis generation: not blind enumeration but data-driven *revision operators* (memorize, recurse, anti-unify) applied to an existing program, i.e., new hypotheses are edits of old ones guided by the structure of the evidence.
- Implementable procedure (PLoT concept learning): DSL of primitives -> prior ~ exp(-description length) -> MCMC or metaprogram-guided proposals -> posterior over programs -> predict/generate -> compress recurring sub-programs into the DSL. Wong et al. 2023 supply the natural-language front end.

- No full-text access to DreamCoder quantitative results (task-solve rates across its 8 domains) or to LILO numbers; only abstracts/summaries fetched.
- Sablé-Meyer et al. (2022, Cognitive Psychology) "A language of thought for the mental representation of geometric shapes" and Dehaene et al. (2022, TICS) "Symbols and mental programs" are relevant LoT evidence (humans and MEG) that I could not fetch; also Quilty-Dunn, Porot & Mandelbaum (2023, BBS) on the re-emergence of LoT. Not cited for lack of a verified source this session.

## Key Question 3: Cognitive maps and the hippocampal-entorhinal system as a general engine of structured inference

### Takeaway
The dominant formal account (Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine; successor representation; disentanglement theory) is that entorhinal cortex supplies a factorized basis for *structure* (relational/transition rules) and hippocampus binds structure to sensory content, so knowledge of structure can be reused zero-shot in new environments. 2023-2026 evidence: human EC representations generalize across non-spatial hexagonal-graph tasks differing in size and stimuli (Mark et al. 2024/2026 eLife); human hippocampus holds a predictive (SR-like) map whose weighting is re-tuned trial-by-trial by OFC (Garvert et al. 2023); and mouse medial frontal cortex contains goal-progress cells forming "structured memory buffers" that implement an abstract task schema reused across new goal layouts (El-Gaby et al. 2024 Nature).

### Cited Findings

**Foundational (context)**
- Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman (2017, Nature Neuroscience) proposed the hippocampus encodes a *successor representation* (SR) — a predictive map of expected future state occupancy — such that place fields are SR columns and grid cells are SR eigenvectors; predicts backward-skewed place fields (Mehta et al. 2000), fields that respect walls, and grid deformation in polarized rooms (Krupic et al. 2015) — summarized in [Carvalho, Tomov, de Cothi, Barry & Gershman 2024 review](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06590v3); [Stachenfeld 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650) [foundational; DOI from memory]
- Behrens et al. (2018, Neuron, "What is a cognitive map? Organizing knowledge for flexible behavior"); Constantinescu, O'Reilly & Behrens (2016, Science) grid-like fMRI code when humans navigate a 2-D "bird" concept space; Schuck, Cai, Wilson & Niv (2016, Neuron) human OFC represents a cognitive map of hidden task states; Mark et al. (2020, Nature Communications) humans transfer structural knowledge across cognitive maps — [Behrens 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.10.002); [Constantinescu 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0941); [Schuck 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.08.019); [Mark 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18254-6) [foundational; DOIs from memory, not re-verified this session]

**Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine (TEM)**
- Whittington, Muller, Mark, Chen, Barry, Burgess & Behrens (2020, Cell). Architecture: medial entorhinal units g form a recurrent network that path-integrates abstract location (same code regardless of route); hippocampal units p are conjunctions of g and sensory input x (from lateral EC); fast Hebbian weights M link co-active p units and an attractor retrieves memories from partial cues; weights trained by backprop across many environments sharing structure but differing in sensory content; Hebbian memory reset per environment — [PMC7707106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106/)
- Emergent cell types: g units become grid-like, band, border and object-vector cells depending on transition statistics (interpreted as basis functions for predicting transitions); p units become place cells that remap across environments and landmark cells — [PMC7707106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106/)
- Prediction tested on data: remapping is non-random because place cells preserve phase relative to the grid code; in two rodent datasets with simultaneously recorded place and grid cells the correlations were significant, ~0.27-0.32 in data vs ~0.31 in the model — [PMC7707106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106/)
- Non-spatial structural inference: TEM makes first-presentation inferences in transitive inference and social-hierarchy tasks after learning shared relational structure and answers queries about unobserved relations; in a four-lap reward task it reproduces lap-specific hippocampal cells and predicts entorhinal coding of the lap structure — [PMC7707106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106/)
- Whittington, Dorrell, Ganguli & Behrens (ICLR 2023, arXiv 2210.01768, "Disentanglement with biological constraints"): proves that nonnegativity and energy efficiency on activity and weights yield *disentangled* (factorized) representations, "enforcing neurons to become selective for single factors of task variation"; explains why the brain divides cells into types such as grid and object-vector cells, and when it instead entangles factors that are entangled in the task — [arXiv 2210.01768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01768)

**Human EC generalizes abstract structural form (2024-2026)**
- Mark, Schwartenbeck, Hahamy, Samborska, Baram & Behrens (eLife reviewed preprint Sept 2024; version of record 2026, vol. 13, e101134, "Flexible neural representations of abstract structural knowledge in the human Entorhinal Cortex"). 28 participants each learned 4 graphs (two hexagonal/planar, two community/clustered; large and small; different picture sets) over 4 days by viewing only pairwise neighbors; behavioral tests showed above-chance inference of non-neighboring relations and navigation improving with learning (e.g., "can X appear between Y and Z": hex day 1 t(27)=31.2, p<1e-22; navigation at 4-edge distances hex t(27)=3.02, comm t(27)=3.69); 26/28 reported noticing that two sets were grouped — [eLife 101134](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/101134); [UCL PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10216083/1/101134-v1.pdf)
- New fMRI method ("subspace generalization") validated on rodent place vs grid cells (grid cells generalize subspaces across environments far better than place cells, p<0.001 permutation) and simulations; result: right EC representations generalized across hexagonal tasks differing in size and stimuli (peak MNI [28,-10,-40], t(27)=4.2, P_tfce<0.01) — the only shared feature being the rules governing statistical structure; no clear evidence of such generalization for clustered (community) structure; eLife assessment: method "valuable", evidence "solid" — [eLife 101134](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/101134)

**Human hippocampal predictive maps re-weighted by task (2023)**
- Garvert, Saanum, Schulz, Schuck & Doeller (Nature Neuroscience 2023, "Hippocampal spatio-predictive cognitive maps adaptively guide reward generalization"; bioRxiv 2021.10.22.465012). 48 participants learned 12 monsters' locations in a virtual arena over 3 days by free exploration; reward in a choice task depended on spatial location (two contexts), four "inference" monsters never shown in choice. Each participant's SR was estimated from their own visit sequence with TD learning (γ = 0.9), converted to a diffusion-kernel temporal similarity; a Gaussian-process model combining spatial and temporal (SR) similarity explained choices best (model frequency 0.681, exceedance probability >0.999; spatial-only 0.23; temporal-only 0.08) — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.10.22.465012v1.full); journal [DOI 10.1038/s41593-023-01283-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01283-x)
- fMRI adaptation: right hippocampal cross-stimulus enhancement scaled with spatial distance after the choice task (corrected p = 0.045); the neural spatial vs temporal effects tracked each participant's choice weights; stronger hippocampal spatial map predicted smaller inference error (r = -0.44); over trials the hippocampal spatial representation increased while the temporal one decreased (r = -0.62 between changes); a left hippocampal signal tracked trial-wise updates in spatial weighting and medial OFC tracked evidence that outcomes came from the spatial rather than the temporal map — "a role for OFC signal in adjusting the use of an appropriate map to the current task demands" — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.10.22.465012v1.full)

**Cellular schema for abstract task structure (2024)**
- El-Gaby, Harris, Whittington, Dorrell, Bhomick, Walton, Akam & Behrens (Nature 636, 19 Dec 2024, "A cellular basis for mapping behavioural structure"; bioRxiv 2023). 13 mice; 3x3 grid maze; four reward goals A->B->C->D in a repeating loop; goal locations change across tasks but sequence structure is constant; a "rapid-task regime" presented three new tasks per day with limited trials; mice inferred the shared structure and "applied it to new tasks from the first trial" (abstract) — [Nature DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08145-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08145-x); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/neuroscientists-uncover-how-the-brain-maps-behavioral-sequences)
- Most medial frontal cortex neurons tracked *progress to goal* (like place cells over an abstract progress axis), rescaling their tuning to goal distance; progress through the whole ABCD sequence was not coded directly — instead subsets fired at a fixed task-lag from a given behavioural step, and clusters of such cells acted as "task-structured memory buffers"; these dynamics mirrored the abstract structure both during behaviour and during offline sleep; adding a fifth goal (ABCDE) reused the same machinery; computational "structured memory buffer" (SMB) model proposed — [Nature DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08145-x); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/neuroscientists-uncover-how-the-brain-maps-behavioral-sequences)

**Successor representation in humans and animals (review of evidence)**
- Carvalho, Tomov, de Cothi, Barry & Gershman (2024, Neural Computation; arXiv 2402.06590, "Predictive representations: building blocks of intelligence") compile: Momennejad et al. (2017) humans in a two-step task adapt to reward revaluation but not transition or policy revaluation — the SR signature — supporting a hybrid SR/model-based strategy; Tomov et al. (2021) humans reuse previously good policies on new tasks, the behavioral signature of generalized policy improvement over successor features, not explained by pure MB or MF; Garvert et al. (2017) human hippocampal/entorhinal fMRI mirrors predictive structure of a hidden non-spatial graph; Wittkuhn et al. (2022) forward and reverse sequential activity during 10-s pauses in visual/sensorimotor cortex well captured by an SR learned from the graph; Bellmund et al. (2020) human VR distance errors mirror eigenvector-grid distortions; de Cothi & Barry (2020) boundary-vector-cell features push SR eigenvectors toward hexagonal grids; Mattar & Daw (2018) prioritization uses the SR as "need" — [arXiv 2402.06590 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06590v3)

### Inferences
- Across TEM, SR and disentanglement theory, "structural inference" is implemented as *factorization*: a structure code that is invariant across sensory contexts plus a fast binding memory. This gives zero-shot transfer whenever a new problem shares transition statistics with an old one (Mark 2024: EC does this for planar/hex structure; not shown for clustered structure — suggesting humans may hold a small library of "structural forms" with different neural fates, in line with Kemp & Tenenbaum's structural forms cited by Mark et al.).
- Garvert 2023 is the cleanest human evidence that the *choice of which map to use* is itself an online inference (OFC arbitrating between spatial and predictive maps), linking cognitive maps to the control/meta-reasoning literature in Key Question 4.
- Implementable procedure (TEM-style): learn a transition-structure latent (g) by path integration across many tasks; bind to content with a fast associative memory; on a new task, re-instantiate g, fill bindings from a few observations, and read off predictions for unvisited transitions. SR-style alternative: maintain M = Σ γ^t T^t under TD learning and re-weight reward over M for instant revaluation.

## Key Question 4: Resource rationality and meta-reasoning — how people decide how much to think, when to stop, and which strategy/representation to use

### Takeaway
Resource-rational analysis (Lieder & Griffiths 2020) models cognition as the optimal use of limited computation; its most concrete 2022-2024 descendants show that people (i) adapt planning *strategy* to environment structure and computation cost (Callaway et al. 2022), (ii) *simplify the problem representation itself* by weighing an obstacle's relevance against the cost of representing it (Ho et al. 2022, N > 800 across experiments, χ² up to 2297), (iii) decompose tasks into subgoals to reduce planning cost, matching betweenness-centrality heuristics (Correa et al. 2023), (iv) plan deeper with expertise (van Opheusden et al. 2023, Nature), and (v) vary thinking time in proportion to the expected benefit of an imagined rollout (Jensen et al. 2024). Expected value of control (Shenhav 2013) supplies the neural-economic account of how much effort to deploy.

### Cited Findings

**Framework**
- Lieder & Griffiths (2020, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 43:e1, "Resource-rational analysis: Understanding human cognition as the optimal use of limited computational resources"): proposes "the rational use of limited resources" as the unifying principle connecting rational models with cognitive constraints; claims it can "reconcile the mind's most impressive cognitive skills with people's ostensible irrationality" and "revisit classic questions of cognitive psychology within a formal computational framework" — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/resourcerational-analysis-understanding-human-cognition-as-the-optimal-use-of-limited-computational-resources/586866D9AD1D1EA7A1EECE217D392F4A)
- Shenhav, Botvinick & Cohen (2013, Neuron 79:217-240) Expected Value of Control: "EVC represents the net value associated with allocating control to a given task" — expected payoff of a controlled process minus the cost of control; dorsal ACC monitors, estimates EVC of candidate control signals (identity and intensity) and selects the one that maximizes it, implementation left to lateral PFC; cost rises monotonically with intensity, so control is "just strong enough" — [PMC3767969](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3767969/)
- Kool & Botvinick (2018, Nature Human Behaviour, "Mental labour") review evidence that cognitive effort is treated as a cost traded against reward, the behavioral counterpart of EVC — [DOI 10.1038/s41562-018-0401-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0401-9) [foundational; DOI from memory, not re-verified this session]

**Planning strategy adapts to structure and cost**
- Callaway, van Opheusden, Gul, Das, Krueger, Griffiths & Lieder (2022, Nature Human Behaviour 6:1112-1125, "Rational use of cognitive resources in human planning"): planning is modeled as a meta-level MDP in which each information-gathering computation has a cost; predictions tested in a process-tracing paradigm (Mouselab-MDP) where participants click to reveal rewards on a decision tree; the resource-rational model "captures how people's planning strategies adapt to the structure of the environment" (e.g., which nodes to inspect first when variance is concentrated near the leaves vs the root) — abstract-level sources: [RALab listing](https://ralab.psych.ucla.edu/publication/rational-use-of-cognitive-resources-in-human-planning/); [DOI 10.1038/s41562-022-01332-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01332-8); earlier version [Callaway et al. CogSci 2018](https://cocosci.princeton.edu/papers/Callaway_CogSci_2018.pdf)
- Related strategy-discovery work: "Automatic Discovery of Interpretable Planning Strategies" (Skirzyński, Becker & Lieder 2021) and "Improving Human Decision-Making by Discovering Efficient Strategies for Hierarchical Planning" (Consul et al. 2021) derive resource-rational strategies by meta-level RL and teach them to people; the latter states planning strategies "are jointly shaped by the structure of the environment and the cost of planning" — [arXiv 2005.11730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11730); [arXiv 2102.00521](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.00521)

**Problem representation is itself controlled (value-guided construal)**
- Ho, Abel, Correa, Littman, Cohen & Griffiths (2022, Nature 606:129-136; arXiv 2105.06948, "People construct simplified mental representations to plan"): theory of *value-guided construal* — a construal is the subset of obstacles a planner includes; the planner chooses construals by trading the utility of the resulting plan against the cognitive cost of representing more obstacles (softmax over construal value); series of pre-registered maze experiments (Prolific). Initial experiment: n = 161 navigating twelve 2-D mazes each with seven obstacles; awareness probes (0-1 scale) were predicted by value-guided construal probabilities (likelihood-ratio χ²(1) = 2297.21, p < 1e-16), including when obstacles were invisible during movement so all planning was up front (χ²(1) = 726.95, β = 0.115) — [arXiv 2105.06948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06948)
- Obstacle-recall probes (accuracy χ²(1) = 249.34, β = 0.648; confidence χ²(1) = 432.76) and critical-mazes recall (n = 78) replicated the effect; perceptual-control (n = 88) and execution-control "breadcrumb" (n = 80) experiments showed that construal effects are not a simple function of perception or execution (accuracy χ²(1) = 106.36, p = 6.2e-25); a process-tracing version in which participants hover to reveal obstacles showed hovering probability (χ²(1) = 1221.76, β = 0.704) and hover duration tracked construal predictions; the paper concludes people "optimally balance the complexity of a task representation and its utility for planning and acting" — [arXiv 2105.06948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06948)

**Task decomposition / subgoals**
- Correa, Ho, Callaway, Daw & Griffiths (2023, PLOS Computational Biology 19(6):e1011087; arXiv 2211.03890, "Humans decompose tasks by trading off utility and computational cost"): normative framework — "people decompose tasks to reduce the overall cost of planning while maintaining task performance"; analyzed 11,117 graph-structured planning tasks and ran a behavioral study on 30 sampled graphs with N = 806; the framework justifies several existing decomposition heuristics, makes predictions distinct from two alternative normative accounts, and human responses were most consistent with it and with betweenness centrality, a heuristic it justifies — [arXiv 2211.03890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03890)
- Correa, Sanborn, Ho, Callaway, Daw & Griffiths (2023, arXiv 2311.18644) "Exploring the hierarchical structure of human plans via program generation" extends this to plans as programs — listed in search results, not fetched: [arXiv 2311.18644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18644)

**Planning depth and expertise**
- van Opheusden, Kuperwajs, Galbiati, Bnaya, Li & Ma (2023, Nature 618:1000-1005, "Expertise increases planning depth in human gameplay"): four-in-a-row chosen because chess is too complex for quantitative depth estimates and classic lab tasks cap depth; a heuristic-search cognitive model (feature-based board evaluation + best-first tree search with pruning and lapses) predicted choices, response times and eye movements, passed a Turing test, and showed "robust evidence for increased planning depth with expertise in both laboratory and large-scale mobile data"; experts also reconstructed board features more accurately — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v618y2023i7967d10.1038_s41586-023-06124-2.html); [DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06124-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06124-2); related 2021 preprint [NYU Ma lab PDF](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/malab/static/files/publications/2021_van_Opheusden_Galbiati_Kuperwajs_Bnaya_li_Ma.pdf)

**When to think (thinking time as value of computation)**
- Jensen, Hennequin & Mattar (2024, Nature Neuroscience; bioRxiv 2023.01.16.523429): meta-RL agent (100 GRUs) that at each step either acts (400 ms) or performs a "rollout" (an imagined action sequence up to 8 steps sampled from its own policy through a learned world model, costing 120 ms); the agent "learns to plan when planning is beneficial"; 94 Prolific participants navigated 4x4 periodic mazes (20-s episodes, 40 episodes); human thinking time was longer farther from the goal and for the first action of a trial; agent rollout probability correlated with human thinking time (r = 0.186 ± 0.007; r = 0.070 ± 0.006 after controlling for distance to goal; r = 0.098 ± 0.008 during exploration); disabling rollouts cut reward per episode from 7.54 ± 0.03 to 6.54 ± 0.11, shuffling rollout timing to 6.75 ± 0.04; successful rollouts raised the probability of their first action and unsuccessful ones lowered it — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.16.523429v2.full); [Hennequin lab page](https://cbl.eng.cam.ac.uk/hennequin/publications/jensen-natneuro-2024)

**Related: thinking budget in rule learning**
- Rule et al. (2024) explicitly calibrate search steps to human response times (median 14.7 s; 20-6,000 steps depending on per-step cost) — [PMC11316799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11316799/)

### Inferences
- The 2022-2024 work moves resource rationality from "which heuristic" to "which *representation*": Ho et al. show the planner edits the problem it is solving; Correa et al. show it chooses subgoal structure; Garvert et al. (KQ3) show it chooses which map to consult. All three are value-minus-cost selections over representations and give a concrete recipe for "how people represent problems".
- Stopping rules: the only directly tested, mechanistic model of "how long to think" found is Jensen et al.'s learned rollout policy — think while the expected policy improvement from one more rollout exceeds its time cost, with the hippocampus proposed as the rollout engine and PFC as the controller. This dovetails with Rule et al.'s thinking-time calibration and van Opheusden's expertise-depth finding (experts' better evaluation functions make deeper search worth its cost).
- Implementable procedure (meta-level MDP, from Callaway/Ho/Jensen): state = current beliefs about the problem; actions = {compute X (cost c_X), act}; choose the computation with highest VOC = expected improvement in decision value minus cost; terminate when max VOC < 0. Representation construal and subgoal choice can be treated as computations in the same loop.

### Gaps
- Callaway et al. 2022 and van Opheusden et al. 2023 could only be cited at abstract level (Nature and PsyArXiv blocked); quantitative fits and the specific strategy taxonomy are missing.
- No 2023-2026 Lieder-lab paper (e.g., on metacognitive learning or strategy discovery in humans) or Shenhav-lab update to EVC was retrieved before the search budget ran out.
- Russek et al. (chess thinking time reflects value of computation) and Agrawal et al. (2022, Psychological Review, opportunity-cost dynamics of deliberation) are relevant to stopping rules but were not fetched.

## Key Question 5: Compositional generalization and systematicity — humans, meta-learning, and neurocompositional computing

### Takeaway
Human few-shot compositional learning is strong but biased: in Lake & Baroni's instruction-learning task people produced the algebraically correct output 80.7% of the time and their errors followed one-to-one, iconic-concatenation and mutual-exclusivity biases; a transformer *meta-trained on a stream of compositional tasks* (MLC) matched both the accuracy (82.4%) and the error profile (item-level r = 0.788), while a standard seq2seq scored 0%. Smolensky and colleagues argue the deeper requirement is "neurocompositional" computing that honors both Compositionality and Continuity via tensor-product role-filler binding.

### Cited Findings

**Legacy framing**
- Fodor & Pylyshyn (1988, Cognition) argued connectionist networks cannot explain the systematicity and compositionality of thought unless they implement a classical symbol system — the challenge both Lake & Baroni and Smolensky explicitly answer — [DOI 10.1016/0010-0277(88)90031-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(88)90031-5) [foundational; from memory]

**Lake & Baroni 2023**
- Lake & Baroni (2023, Nature 623:115-121, "Human-like systematic generalization through a meta-learning neural network"). Human few-shot task: 25 participants (30 recruited) studied 14 input/output examples of a pseudo-language mapping words ("dax", "zup", function words) to colored-circle sequences and answered 10 queries; 80.7% of responses exactly matched the algebraic answer; 72.5% correctly produced outputs longer than any training output; one-to-one translations were 24.4% of errors, iconic concatenation 23.3% of errors on one function — [PMC10620072](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620072)
- Open-ended task with no study examples: 29 participants; 17/29 (58.6%) produced the modal pattern; 62.1% one-to-one, 79.3% iconic concatenation, 93.1% mutual exclusivity — [PMC10620072](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620072)
- MLC: ~1.4M-parameter transformer meta-trained on 100,000 episodes each defined by a different latent grammar; in the behaviorally informed variant ~20% of query targets were replaced by bias-based heuristic outputs. Few-shot: 100% exact match with greedy decoding; 82.4% systematic outputs with sampling vs humans' 80.7%; errors were 56.3% one-to-one and 13.8% iconic concatenation; item difficulty correlated with humans r = 0.788 (p = 0.031, n = 10). Open-ended: matched the modal human response in 65.0% of samples. Log-likelihood of human data (higher better): MLC-joint -349.2 vs symbolic oracle-with-biases -357.2 vs MLC-algebraic-only -496.9 vs basic seq2seq -1264.7 (few-shot) — [PMC10620072](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620072)
- Benchmarks: SCAN add-jump error 0.22% (MLC) vs 99.27% (basic seq2seq); COGS lexical 0.87%; MLC *fails* SCAN length and COGS structural splits (100% error) — i.e., it does not solve productivity; stated limits: cannot generalize outside the meta-training distribution, no mechanism to emit new symbols; preliminary GPT-4 tests "suggest systematicity remains a challenge, or at least an open question" — [PMC10620072](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620072)

**Neurocompositional computing**
- Smolensky, McCoy, Fernandez, Goldrick & Gao (2022, AI Magazine; arXiv 2205.01128): the "Central Paradox of Cognition" — "the computer in our heads apparently is simultaneously a neural computer and a compositional-structure computer"; resolution requires computation that satisfies both the Continuity Principle (encodings and processing vary continuously, enabling gradient learning and similarity-based generalization) and the Compositionality Principle; a hybrid of separate symbolic and neural modules is rejected because "in central cognitive domains, individual encodings respect both" principles; the proposed substrate is the Tensor Product Representation: a structure is a set of filler:role bindings, each binding is the tensor product of a filler vector and a role vector, bindings are summed — provably supporting symbolic computation in continuous vectors — [arXiv 2205.01128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01128)

**Other human evidence located only indirectly**
- Zhou, Feinman & Lake (see KQ2) — humans compose visual parts into novel wholes and their generalizations are more diverse than a Bayesian program-induction model — [arXiv 2305.19374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19374)
- Lake, Linzen & Baroni (2019, CogSci, "Human few-shot learning of compositional instructions") was the precursor human study — [arXiv 1901.04587](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04587) [from memory; not fetched]

### Inferences
- The human profile is "systematic but biased, with limited productivity": people compose reliably within the length/structure range they have seen and lean on simple heuristics (one-to-one, iconic order, mutual exclusivity) when uncertain. MLC's success and its productivity failures suggest that meta-learning over compositional *experience* can install human-like systematicity without an innate symbolic architecture, but true unbounded productivity may still require program-like (KQ2) or TPR-like (Smolensky) structure.
- For idea generation, the key operation is recombination of known pieces into novel wholes under structural constraints; Lake & Baroni's bias list (one-to-one mapping, iconic concatenation, mutual exclusivity) is a usable set of default priors for "how people compose".

### Gaps
- Dekker, Otto & Summerfield (2022, PNAS, curriculum effects on human compositional generalization) and Russin et al. (2024, human curriculum effects emerging with in-context learning) could not be searched before budget exhaustion.
- Smolensky et al.'s empirical evidence that transformers implicitly use TPR-like structure was not extracted (full-text PDF was extracted locally but the relevant sections were not read in detail).

## Key Question 6: Model-based vs model-free control, successor features, replay and offline planning — how the brain decides what to simulate

### Takeaway
Mattar & Daw's (2018) normative theory prioritizes replay by expected value of a backup (gain x need); 2020-2024 human tests partly support it (replay tracks choice updating) but reveal "pessimized" replay of sub-optimal transitions that Antonov, Gagne, Eldar & Dayan explain by combining optimal gain with forgetting in the agent's own model; Jensen et al. (2024) reframe replay as on-policy rollouts whose statistics in rodent data (wall avoidance, goal over-representation, first replayed action predicting the next move) match a learned planning policy; the SR explains why humans revalue reward but not transitions.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational (context)**
- Daw, Gershman, Seymour, Dayan & Dolan (2011, Neuron) two-step task shows humans mix model-based and model-free control; Momennejad et al. (2017, Nature Human Behaviour) humans show SR-like partial flexibility (reward but not transition revaluation) — [Daw 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.02.027); [Momennejad 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-017-0180-8) [foundational; DOIs from memory]; Momennejad 2017 findings also summarized in [Carvalho et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06590v3)
- Mattar & Daw (2018, Nature Neuroscience 21:1609-1617, "Prioritized memory access explains planning and hippocampal replay"): memories are accessed in order of utility — the extra reward earned from improved choices; utility = *gain* (policy improvement from the backup) x *need* (expected future occupancy, i.e., the SR); balances evaluating imminent choices against propagating new information backwards; reproduces forward replay at trial start, reverse replay after reward, and many replay phenomena in rodent-style simulations — [PMC6203620](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6203620); [bioRxiv v4](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/225664v4.full-text)

**Human tests**
- Eldar, Lièvre, Dayan & Dolan (2020, eLife 9:e56911, "The roles of online and offline replay in planning"): MEG decoding of sequenceness during a planning task on an 8-state torus; subjects with higher model-based flexibility showed more between-trial replay; replayed transitions mostly led to sub-optimal outcomes, and sequenceness correlated with subsequent change in choice probability (Spearman M = 0.04, p = 0.04); more replays of sub-optimal than optimal transitions (W = 2.60, p = 0.009, n = 21) — summarized in [Antonov, Gagne, Eldar & Dayan, bioRxiv 2021.04.27.441454](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.04.27.441454v1.full-text); original [eLife 56911](https://elifesciences.org/articles/56911) (not fetched directly)
- Antonov, Gagne, Eldar & Dayan ("Optimism and pessimism in optimised replay"; bioRxiv 2021; PLOS Computational Biology 2022): keep Mattar & Daw's optimal gain but compute it from the agent's *subjective, forgetful* model: forgetting of MF values pulls sub-optimal values up toward the average and devalues optimal ones, so replaying sub-optimal actions (which yields negative TD errors) is gainful; fitted to individuals, replay raised the probability of optimal choice by 3.36% (t = 3.36, p = 0.003) and objective value by 0.196 reward points/trial (t = 2.79, p = 0.011); model-based forgetting negatively correlated with flexibility (β = -0.65, p << 0.0001); two subjects were predicted to be harmed by replay — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.04.27.441454v1.full-text)
- Liu, Mattar, Behrens, Daw & Dolan (2021, Science, "Experience replay is associated with efficient nonlocal learning") — described by Antonov et al. as "one compelling test" that "well followed Mattar and Daw 2018": MEG replay in humans propagated value to non-local states — [Antonov et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.04.27.441454v1.full-text); [DOI 10.1126/science.abf1357](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abf1357) [DOI from memory]

**Replay as learned on-policy rollouts (2024)**
- Jensen, Hennequin & Mattar (2024, Nature Neuroscience): compared agent rollouts to rodent hippocampal replays from Widloski & Foster (2022): replays avoided walls more than chance (p < 0.001) and over-represented the goal (p < 0.001); the first replayed action matched the next physical action more often for successful replays (p < 0.001) but not on "away" trials where the goal was unknown (p = 0.129); goal over-representation rose across consecutive replays on home trials (second and third vs first: p = 0.068, p = 0.009); interpretation: replays are on-policy rollouts that iteratively refine the policy, with hippocampal replays "triggered by – and in turn adaptively affect – prefrontal dynamics"; authors note the rodent data cannot fully exclude a confound where successful replays reduce further replay — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.16.523429v2.full)

**Predictive representations and dopamine**
- Carvalho et al. (2024) summarize: Russek et al. (2017) SR-TD learning is biologically plausible; Gardner et al. (2018) propose dopamine carries vector-valued TD errors for successor features; Stalnaker et al. (2019) reward identity decodable from dopamine ensembles; Geerts et al. (2020) SR-over-place-cells + MF model reproduces rodents' shift from map-based to response strategies — [arXiv 2402.06590 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06590v3)

### Inferences
- "What to simulate" has two converging normative answers: (a) prioritized backups ordered by gain x need (Mattar & Daw; Antonov et al. add that the agent's *own imperfect beliefs* set the priorities, producing systematic pessimism), and (b) a learned meta-policy that triggers rollouts when their expected policy improvement exceeds their time cost (Jensen et al.). Both predict that imagination concentrates on high-need states (near current position/goal) and on experiences whose re-evaluation would most change behavior — a usable heuristic for modeling spontaneous idea sampling.
- The SR provides the "need" term and simultaneously explains human partial flexibility (Momennejad 2017), making it the connective tissue between cognitive maps (KQ3), replay (KQ6) and resource-rational control (KQ4).

### Gaps
- Eldar et al. 2020 and Liu et al. 2021 are cited through Antonov et al.'s summary, not from the primary texts (eLife and Science blocked).
- Mattar & Lengyel (2022, Neuron, "Planning in the brain") and Momennejad (2020, COBS) reviews were not fetched; no 2025-2026 human replay study was located before the budget ran out.

## Key Question 7: Which models connect directly to creativity or hypothesis generation (creativity as program synthesis, recombination of library primitives, conceptual spaces)?

### Takeaway
The explicit creativity-facing threads in this literature are (i) program induction/library learning, where novelty arises by composing and editing primitives (DreamCoder's "creative tasks like drawing pictures and building scenes"; BPL's novel exemplars; Zhou et al.'s human generations that "reconfigure existing parts in highly novel ways"), (ii) metaprogram revision operators as a model of how new hypotheses are proposed from old ones (Rule et al. 2024), (iii) counterfactual simulation as the engine for "what if" generation (Gerstenberg), and (iv) structural-form reuse in cognitive maps as zero-shot transfer of an old schema to a new domain. No 2023-2026 paper formally connecting Gärdenfors' conceptual spaces to these mechanisms was found.

### Cited Findings
- DreamCoder's authors describe its targets as including "creative tasks like drawing pictures and building scenes" and report rediscovery of concepts from functional programming, vector algebra and classical physics (Newton's and Coulomb's laws) via compositional library growth — [arXiv 2006.08381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381)
- LILO adds naming/documentation of abstractions and shows that linguistically grounded abstractions are reused more effectively by the synthesizer — a formal analogue of "having a word for an idea makes it easier to build on" — [arXiv 2310.19791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19791)
- Wong et al. (CogSci 2022) show that the part concepts people name obey an information-theoretic tradeoff between concise object descriptions and a compact lexicon, i.e., humans' concept libraries look like compression-optimal program libraries — [arXiv 2205.05666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05666)
- Zhou, Feinman & Lake: in few-shot generation, people "went beyond the model in structured ways", making choices that "complete a set" and "reconfigure existing parts in highly novel ways", which required adding a compositional recombination mechanism plus neural residual modules to the Bayesian program model — [arXiv 2305.19374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19374)
- Rule et al. (2024): hypotheses are generated by metaprimitives (MemorizeAll, Recurse, AntiUnify) that revise existing programs using the structure of the data; this both fits humans and cuts search by orders of magnitude — [PMC11316799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11316799/)
- Wong et al. (2023): language can drive the *construction* of new world models by translating into PLoT programs that compose primitives and call simulators/planners — a route from verbal prompts to new structured hypotheses — [arXiv 2306.12672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12672v2)
- Gerstenberg (2024) lists as an outstanding question "What determines what counterfactuals come to mind and how do we choose the appropriate counterfactual intervention" — i.e., the generative step of counterfactual thinking is identified as the open problem — [Gerstenberg 2024 TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.04.012)
- Tsividis et al. (2021): the EMPA agent proposes new game-world theories as programs, and tests them by exploration driven by internal simulation; its exploration trajectories match human patterns — hypothesis generation plus curiosity-driven testing in one loop — [arXiv 2107.12544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12544)
- TEM/El-Gaby: structural knowledge learned in one context is applied "from the first trial" in a new one; this is the neural counterpart of mapping an old schema onto a new domain — [PMC7707106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106/); [El-Gaby 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08145-x)
- Lake, Salakhutdinov & Tenenbaum (2015, Science): BPL generated new exemplars and new concepts that human judges could not distinguish from human-drawn ones in "visual Turing tests" — [DOI 10.1126/science.aab3050](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aab3050) [foundational; from memory]

### Inferences
- A composite "generate ideas" procedure that these models jointly license: (1) hold a library of named abstractions compressed from past solutions (DreamCoder/LILO/Wong 2022); (2) propose candidates by applying revision operators to existing programs rather than sampling from scratch (Rule 2024); (3) score candidates by description length and fit (PLoT prior x likelihood); (4) run partial counterfactual simulations to test consequences (CSM/partial simulation); (5) allocate simulation to high gain x need items and stop when the value of further computation falls below cost (Mattar & Daw; Jensen 2024; Ho 2022); (6) reuse structural forms across domains (TEM/SR). Each step has at least one human-validated model behind it, though no single paper combines them.
- The human excess diversity observed by Zhou et al. (recombining parts beyond the Bayesian model) and the biases catalogued by Lake & Baroni (one-to-one, iconic order, mutual exclusivity) together suggest where human "creative" generations diverge from pure compression-optimal search.
