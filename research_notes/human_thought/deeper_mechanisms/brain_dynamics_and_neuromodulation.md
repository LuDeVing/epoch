## Key Question 1 — Criticality: is the awake brain at / near / slightly below a critical point, does this maximize dynamic range and flexibility, and what moves it (state, sleep loss, anesthesia, psychedelics)? What are the criticisms?

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 consensus is "near, not at": the best-controlled new analyses (a 2026 PRL fMRI study, a 2025 Neuron meta-analysis of 140 datasets, a 2024 Nat Neurosci rat study, a 2024 Sci Adv mouse study) converge on a slightly subcritical awake cortex (effective coupling ≈0.88–0.99 depending on method/scale) whose distance to criticality is a dynamic, homeostatically regulated quantity: it grows with time awake and with vigorous action, is restored by sleep, is pushed off (toward chaos, or toward hyperstable periodicity) by GABAergic anesthesia and seizures, and is nudged toward the critical point by serotonergic psychedelics. The strongest criticisms are that (i) several canonical signatures (PCA eigenspectra, phenomenological RG) are reproducible from non-critical autocorrelated noise plus limited sampling; (ii) non-critical mechanisms (resource/memory coupling, Griffiths phases, hidden low-dimensional modes) can produce the same scale-free statistics; (iii) direct behavioral tests of the "flexibility" benefit remain scarce.

### Cited Findings

**Where the awake brain sits (2024–2026 primary evidence)**
- [Human empirical] Calvo et al. 2026, *Physical Review Letters* ("Robust Scaling in Human Brain Dynamics Despite Correlated Inputs and Limited Sampling Distortions"): resting fMRI from 136 healthy participants (LEMON dataset, 183 regions, ~10 min). Scaling signatures from PCA eigenspectra and phenomenological renormalization group (PRG) were shown to be reproducible in a null model with zero inter-regional coupling (g = 0) purely from temporal autocorrelation + short recordings. Using exponent matching to a linear recurrent model, the pooled effective coupling was ≈0.88 (1.0 = critical). Time-shift randomization destroyed the signatures, indicating they reflect real collective dynamics. Lead author's interpretation: near-critical operation "can retain many of the proposed computational benefits" while avoiding "the drawbacks of sitting exactly at criticality" (instability, reduced robustness). — [Phys.org summary of PRL paper](https://phys.org/news/2026-03-human-brain-critical.html)
- [Review/meta-analysis] Hengen & Shew 2025, *Neuron* 113(16):2582–2598, "Is criticality a unified setpoint of brain function?": meta-analysis of 140 avalanche distributions from 73 papers (2003–2024), 46 from awake animals at single-neuron resolution. Key result: in awake single-neuron data, temporal bin width correlated strongly with the scale-invariant range (Spearman ρ = 0.89; ρ = 0.25 across all 140). Ranges > 2 decades appeared only with bins ≳10 ms; bins < 10 ms rarely exceeded 1.5 decades. The authors attribute early negative results to too-small bins. They argue criticality is a homeostatic set-point because Hebbian plasticity is destabilizing, and that slightly subcritical systems retain a wide scale-invariant range so the benefits are "robust near criticality." Not addressed: power laws from noise, subsampling, quasi-criticality, Griffiths phases, psychedelics. — [PMC12374783](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12374783/)
- [Animal empirical] Fontenele, Sooter, Norman, Gautam & Shew 2024, *Science Advances*, "Low-dimensional criticality embedded in high-dimensional awake brain dynamics": Neuropixels in motor cortex of 4 awake mice (19 recordings; ~104 single units + 44 multi-units each; ~44 min). Avalanche power laws and crackling-noise scaling relations hold only within a low-dimensional subspace spanned by the first few PCs (rarely > 3 dimensions, max 13); the next PCs form a "desynchronized" subspace with weak correlations and near-Gaussian statistics. The critical subspace appears only at coarse bins (> ~10 ms), was stable over ~30 min, and correlated with wheel speed. A Hawkes model reproduced the data only when tuned near criticality (largest eigenvalue Λ = 0.99). — [PMC11051676](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11051676/)
- [Model] Fontenele, Sooter, Ziarati, Barreiro, Ly & Shew 2025 (bioRxiv), "Is critical brain dynamics more prevalent than previously thought?": linear rate model (N = 500) shows that a near-critical eigenmode (λ ≈ 0.999–1) can be invisible to population-average analyses when its eigenvector has near-zero component sum (anticorrelated neurons) or is mixed with oscillatory modes (λ = 0.994 ± 0.08i); scale invariance then appears only in the oscillation's amplitude envelope. They argue the dimension of the critical subspace = number of near-critical modes, and that prior "new kinds" of criticality (Jones 2023, Dahmen 2019) are standard criticality hidden in subspaces. — [bioRxiv 2025.09.02.673722](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.02.673722v1.full)
- [Animal empirical; foundational, labeled] Wilting & Priesemann's subsampling-corrected branching-ratio estimator put spiking activity in macaque PFC, cat V1 and rat hippocampus at σ ≈ 0.97–0.99 (N = 10 experiments): "clearly subcritical…and not critical", i.e., a "reverberating" regime between critical and irregular. — [arXiv 1804.07864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.07864); subsampling method [PMC4697519](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4697519/)
- [Human empirical; foundational, labeled] Toker et al. 2022, *PNAS* 119(7):e2024455119, "Consciousness is supported by near-critical slow cortical electrodynamics": modified 0–1 chaos test on ECoG/MEG from humans and macaques across waking, generalized seizure, anesthesia and psychedelic states; waking low-frequency cortical dynamics sit near the edge-of-chaos critical point; unconscious states drift away; psychedelics "may increase the information richness of cortical activity by moving low-frequency oscillations closer to the critical point"; clinical EEG from disorders-of-consciousness patients used as proof of principle. — [Tübingen repository record](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/135835)

**Distance to criticality as a state variable (wake drift, action, sleep, anesthesia, seizures, psychedelics)**
- [Animal empirical] Xu, Schneider, Wessel & Hengen 2024, *Nature Neuroscience* 27:328–338, "Sleep restores an optimal computational regime in cortical networks": continuous V1 recordings for 10–14 days in 8 freely behaving rats. Extended waking significantly increased distance to criticality (DCC) (P = 2.4e−16 first half of the 24 h; P = 6.7e−4 second half); time asleep correlated negatively with DCC; sleep in the preceding 2 h predicted lower DCC in the next 1 h wake epoch; DCC predicted future sleep/wake-dense epochs better than prior behavior, time of day or slow-wave activity; no light/dark difference (P = 0.165). Abstract claim: waking experience causally perturbs criticality in a context-dependent way and "sleep functions to restore critical dynamics." — [Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01536-9) / [PMC11272063](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272063)
- [Human empirical; foundational, labeled] Meisel et al., "Fading signatures of critical brain dynamics during sustained wakefulness in humans": avalanche power laws become progressively disordered with time awake and are restored by sleep (drift toward excitation dominance). — [record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/fading-signatures-of-critical-brain-dynamics-during-sustained-wak/)
- [Animal empirical] Sooter, Fontenele, Ly, Barreiro & Shew 2024 (bioRxiv), "Cortex deviates from criticality during action and deep sleep: a temporal renormalization group approach": 13 mouse V1 recordings (6.2 ± 1.8 h; 67 ± 18 units; Buzsáki-lab data). A temporal-RG distance d₂ computed in 60-s windows showed the quiet awake state closest to criticality (example d₂ = 0.02 awake vs 0.10 sleep); all 13 mice farther from criticality in NREM; within wake, d₂ rose with EMG and fell with population CV ("elevated body movement causes d₂ to increase"); control with correlations removed gave d₂ = 0.75 ± 0.05. Interpretation: both deep sleep (low arousal) and vigorous action (high arousal) push cortex away, so quiescent wake is the near-critical optimum. — [bioRxiv 2024.05.29.596499](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.29.596499v1.full)
- [Human empirical] Scarpetta et al. 2023, *iScience* 26(10):107840: overnight polysomnography in 10 healthy adults; EEG avalanche exponents τ ≈ 1.44 (size), α ≈ 1.97 (duration), scaling relation (α−1)/(τ−1) ≈ 2.13, branching parameter ≈ 1 — consistent with mean-field directed percolation. Avalanche density was highest in N3 and in cyclic-alternating-pattern phase A1 (42% of CAP-A time contained avalanches vs 19% of total sleep time), which the authors propose (hypothesis) as the stage that "restores brain criticality." — [PMC10520337](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10520337/)
- [Animal + human empirical; Model] Toker, Müller, Miyamoto, Riga, Lladó-Pelfort, Yamakawa, Artigas, Shine, Hudson, Pouratian & Monti 2024, *eLife* 13:e86547, "Criticality supports cross-frequency cortical-thalamic information transfer during conscious states": simultaneous thalamic + cortical field recordings in 10 essential-tremor patients (ViM thalamus), 9 Long-Evans rats, 7 GAERS rats (absence-seizure model) and 5 mice (MD thalamus–mPFC). In waking, 1–13 Hz activity from either region was encoded by the other at high gamma (52–104 Hz) in nearly all subjects. Propofol pushed slow thalamocortical dynamics toward chaos (humans p = 0.065; rats p = 0.002) and reduced low→high transfer in both directions; spike-and-wave seizures made dynamics periodic/hyperstable (p = 0.0078) and also cut transfer; 5-MeO-DMT (5 mg/kg, mice) stabilized dynamics toward criticality from the chaotic side and increased cortex→thalamus transfer (p = 0.0312). Mean-field model: transfer peaked when the largest Lyapunov exponent ≈ 0 (edge of chaos) and decayed with either more chaos (anesthesia) or more periodicity (seizure); the empirical K-statistic correlated with the Lyapunov exponent (ρ = 0.76). — [PMC10805384](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10805384/)
- [Review] Gervais, Boucher, Martinez Villar, Lee & Duclos 2023, *Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience* 17:1085902 (PRISMA scoping review; 427 screened, 49 included to Feb 2022): anesthesia 18 studies (all human studies report a deviation; animal results contradictory, e.g., Ponce-Alvarez 2022 supercritical under anesthesia in monkey fMRI vs Hahn 2017 closer to criticality in cats), sleep 13 (NREM mostly subcritical; Priesemann 2013 the exception), epilepsy 12 (ictal consistently supercritical), DOC 5, psychedelics/trance 4 (toward criticality: Atasoy 2017 LSD harmonics, Varley 2020 LSD/psilocybin fractal dimension, Huels 2021 shamanic trance; away: sub-anesthetic ketamine, Kim 2021), meditation 2, delirium 1. Framework: healthy awake brain slightly subcritical; criticality "confers flexibility" via a wide state repertoire; sedatives for supercritical states and "criticality-enhancing agents (psychedelics)" for subcritical states proposed as therapy logic (hypothesis). — [PMC10248073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10248073/)
- [Human empirical; Model] Ruffini et al. 2023, *PLoS Computational Biology* 19(2):e1010811, "LSD-induced increase of Ising temperature and algorithmic complexity of brain dynamics": pairwise maximum-entropy Ising model fit to resting fMRI of 15 participants (LSD vs placebo, 90 AAL parcels). The placebo brain sat near but above the critical temperature (paramagnetic side); LSD raised individualized temperature by ≈6.7% (±5.1%; paired t p ≈ 6e−5). Block-decomposition complexity correlated with temperature (r(13) = 0.56, p = 0.03); synthetic data showed LZW/BDM track temperature at r = 0.97/0.95. Important nuance: because baseline is modeled as already supercritical, LSD here moves the brain *further from* criticality — the opposite framing from Toker 2022/2024 and the avalanche literature, and the authors flag this model dependence. — [PLOS Comput Biol](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010811)
- [Model] Wu, Chialvo, Zhou & Yu 2025 (arXiv 2512.03409), "Optimal Griffiths Phase in Heterogeneous Human Brain Networks: Brain Criticality Embracing Stability and Flexibility across Individuals": whole-brain modeling on HCP data; modularity + regional excitability heterogeneity produce a Griffiths phase (an extended critical *region* rather than a point) with a "widely extended global excitability range"; each individual's position within it is proposed to set their robustness–flexibility trade-off and cognitive profile. No quantitative values on the abstract page. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03409)

**Criticisms (2025–2026)**
- [Model/critique] Sipling, Zhang & Di Ventra 2026 (arXiv 2604.21071 v3), "A Critical Assessment of the Brain Criticality Hypothesis": argues "the nature and behavior of criticality in cortical systems are still unclear" and that coupling between neurons and slowly varying energetic resources (acting as memory) can alone generate scale-invariant correlations — a "memory-induced long-range order phase" that may explain data "more naturally" than criticality. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21071)
- [Human empirical/critique] Calvo et al. 2026 PRL: PCA-eigenspectrum and PRG signatures are artifacts of autocorrelation + limited sampling in a g = 0 null (see above). — [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-03-human-brain-critical.html)
- [Human empirical/critique] Emergent near-critical oscillations appear in motor-cortex EEG of Parkinson's patients but not healthy controls, so "critical dynamics are not always associated with healthy states." — [PMC12803037](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12803037)
- [Review/critique] Hancock et al. 2025 (Nat Rev Neurosci) Box 3: "Criticality and metastability are different concepts"; multistable switching could arise from critical fluctuations at a weakly stable state, but "metastable dynamics do not require the brain to be operating exclusively 'at criticality', or to refer to criticality at all." — [PDF](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf)
- [Review] Hengen & Shew 2025 concede most functional-benefit evidence (dynamic range in whisker cortex/turtle, information capacity in vitro and in awake mice, in-vitro cultures that learned Pong performing better nearer criticality, fluid intelligence correlating with criticality signatures in humans) is "correlational or indirect," and list as open the direct boundary test, perturbation-plus-behavior experiments, and whether neuromodulators shift proximity (presented as a prediction with "limited direct tests"). — [PMC12374783](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12374783/)

### Inferences
- The field has quietly shifted from "the brain is critical" to "distance-to-criticality is a regulated state variable with a quiescent-wake optimum." Three independent 2024 lines (rat V1 over days; mouse V1 temporal-RG; mouse motor-cortex Neuropixels) agree that distance grows with time awake and with motor/arousal drive and shrinks with restorative sleep — which maps onto the lay observation that novel, exploratory cognition is best after sleep and in relaxed rather than hard-driving states.
- The Toker 2024 model result (information transfer maximal at Lyapunov ≈ 0, dropping on either side) plus Toker 2022's human/macaque data supply the most concrete mechanism by which "near-critical" cashes out as "maximal cross-frequency communication," i.e., maximal capacity to combine slow (contextual) with fast (content) signals — a plausible prerequisite for combinatorial thought.
- The psychedelic direction-of-shift conflict (Toker: toward criticality from the chaotic side; Ruffini: further into the paramagnetic/disordered side) is a model-dependence issue, not a data conflict: both agree psychedelics increase disorder/complexity; they disagree on where baseline sits relative to the critical point. For the report, phrase it as "psychedelics increase distance from the ordered phase."
- The "hidden/low-dimensional criticality" results suggest that flexibility benefits may be concentrated in a few collective modes rather than the whole population, which dovetails with the manifold literature (Q4).

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 human study was found that directly manipulates distance-to-criticality and measures cognitive flexibility or idea generation; Hengen & Shew list this as the field's key missing experiment.
- Priesemann-lab 2023–2026 updates on the branching parameter were not found (search budget exhausted before a targeted retry).
- The 2025 "concrit" consciousness framework (Neurosci Biobehav Rev) and the Tatsukawa & Teramae 2025 Fisher-information-per-metabolic-cost result were only seen in search summaries, not read.

## Key Question 2 — Metastability, attractor itinerancy, winnerless competition and "turbulence": how does the brain move between quasi-stable states, and what controls dwell time and transition probability?

### Takeaway
The 2025 Nature Reviews Neuroscience consensus review (Hancock, Rosas, …, Kelso, Turkheimer) formalizes metastability as transient-state dynamics produced "by design" (saddles/heteroclinic channels, ghost attractors, chimera states, delay-induced itinerancy) rather than noise-kicked multistability, and shows that the controllable drivers are coupling strength, conduction delays, frequency/excitability heterogeneity, network topology (hubs, modularity) and noise; empirically, metastability signatures track cognition in an inverted-U (lower in ageing, AD, anesthesia, TBI; higher in psychosis). Causal/pharmacological work from the Shine lab (2021–2024) shows the attractor landscape is directly reshaped by neuromodulators: noradrenergic bursts flatten it (more improbable transitions), cholinergic drive deepens it (stabilization; muscimol inactivation of nucleus basalis flattens it), which is the clearest answer to "what controls dwell time." Deco & Kringelbach's turbulence program (2020–2026) reframes the same regime as a non-equilibrium, maximally information-cascading state found at the model's maximal-turbulence working point.

### Cited Findings

**Definitions, mechanisms and misconceptions**
- [Review] Hancock, Rosas, Luppi, Zhang, Mediano, Cabral, Deco, Kringelbach, Breakspear, Kelso & Turkheimer 2025, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, "Metastability demystified — the foundational past, the pragmatic present and the promising future" (DOI 10.1038/s41583-024-00883-1). Core claims: (a) "observing switching between distinct [states]" is *not* sufficient to infer metastability; (b) different signatures (std of the Kuramoto order parameter, std of spectral radius, std of intrinsic ignition, mean variance of the leading eigenvector) are "not alternative definitions"; (c) metastability is a property of the attractor landscape, not "in the data"; (d) "noise is necessary for switching within multi-stable systems, [but] metastable cycling can occur in the absence of noise" via time delays, heterogeneous coupling, saddle cycles (winnerless competition), ghost attractors near a saddle-node, and chimera states. Box 1 defines ghost attractors as "memories of fixed points beyond the critical point" where trajectories slow and dwell before escaping. — [PDF](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf)
- [Review] Same source — drivers: "Drivers of metastability from macroscale computational models include influences from structural connectivity, network topology, spectral heterogeneities and intrinsic noise." Metastability (std-KOP) is maximal at a specific global coupling strength; heterogeneous intrinsic frequencies produce clustering/metastable behaviour in connectome-based models even without noise; removing hub nodes with high eigenvector centrality *increases* global metastability in lesion models; higher-order topology (clustering coefficient, small-worldness) correlates positively with metastability. "Symmetry breaking" is identified as the key enabler. — [PDF](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf)
- [Review] Same source — dwell-time statistics as a discriminator: noise-driven multistability yields exponential (or state-dependent stretched-exponential) dwell-time distributions, whereas metastable waves in a delay-coupled network yield gamma-distributed dwell times; the authors propose dwell-time distributions and stringent null models as the way to decide multistability vs metastability in data. — [PDF](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf)
- [Foundational; cited via the review] Winnerless competition / heteroclinic channels: the canonical model is a generalized Lotka–Volterra inhibitory network with asymmetric connectivity producing robust, noise-resistant sequences of saddle states (Rabinovich, Huerta, Varona, Afraimovich; e.g., *PLoS Comput Biol* 4:e1000072, 2008 "Transient cognitive dynamics, metastability, and decision making"). — cited in [Hancock et al. refs](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf)
- [Foundational; from prior knowledge] Tognoli & Kelso 2014 *Neuron* "The Metastable Brain" (coordination dynamics; metastability as the regime past the Hopf/saddle-node where in-phase/antiphase attractors have become ghosts, leaving tendencies rather than states). — summarized in [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/neuroscience/articles/metastable-brain-284885) and [Hancock et al.](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf)
- [Model/theory] "A unified framework of metastability in neuroscience" (arXiv 2305.05328): defines metastable states generically as transient-but-long-lived and lists the dynamical mechanisms. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.05328v2)

**Empirical metastability–cognition links (from the review's Table 1)**
- [Human empirical] std-KOP lower in Alzheimer's disease than controls, correlating with global cognitive performance in AD and MCI; graded decreases from subjective cognitive impairment → MCI → AD; std-IGNITE lower in older vs middle-aged adults; std-SPECT decreases progressively with ageing in rats; std-IGNITE higher in full-term than preterm babies; reductions after anaesthesia and traumatic brain injury; but mean-VAR and std-KOP *higher* in early psychosis/chronic schizophrenia (salience-network increase associated with negative symptoms). Authors' conclusion: "there may be a 'sweet spot' for metastability in healthy brain functioning." — [PDF](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/2024_Hancock_Kelso_NRN.pdf)

**Neuromodulatory control of the attractor landscape (the dwell-time knob)**
- [Human empirical] Munn, Müller, Wainstein & Shine 2021, *Nature Communications* 12:6016: 7T resting fMRI (59 analysed of 65; TR 586 ms) + 14 experienced meditators (3T). Phasic LC bursts (148 LC-leading, 130 BNM-leading, 316 simultaneous across subjects) *lowered* the energy landscape E = ln(1/P(MSD,t)) — flattening it so unlikely transitions become more probable — and were followed by an anterior-to-posterior travelling wave (~0.13 m/s) and increased frontoparietal integration (participation coefficient), peaking 6–9 s after the burst; basal-forebrain (BNM) bursts *raised* the landscape (deepened wells) and produced the inverse sensory-to-frontal wave. Joint bursts were dominated by BNM (LC+BNM ≈ 0.16·LC + 0.84·BNM). In meditators, LC activity peaked ~4 s before the button press signalling awareness of mind-wandering, with MSD and integration rising alongside. Interpretation: LC = multiplicative gain/"network reset" promoting integration; BNM = divisive normalisation stabilising states. — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26268-x)
- [Animal empirical; causal] Taylor, Whyte, Munn, Chang, Lizier, Leopold, Turchi, Zaborszky, Muller & Shine 2024, *Cell Reports* 43(6):114359, "Causal evidence for cholinergic stabilization of attractor landscape dynamics": unilateral muscimol inactivation of nucleus basalis of Meynert subregions (Ch4AM, Ch4AL) in 2 macaques (21 + 33 injected scans, 20 controls; CBV fMRI, 266 ROIs). Inhibition lowered energy barriers for large state transitions at lags of 2–8 s (landscape flattening; permutation p beyond 99.9% null across nearly all bins, both hemispheres, both sites) while zero-lag correlations were unchanged (all p > 0.05); transfer entropy fell among projection targets (p = 0.0216). A bistable Wilson–Cowan pair with adaptation reproduced it (reduced adaptation and increased excitability each deepen wells). Conclusion: acetylcholine "stabilizes macroscale brain states by deepening attractor wells," so removing it makes shifts between states easier. Caveats: n = 2, muscimol is non-selective, hemodynamic proxy. — [PMC11255396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11255396/)
- [Model + animal/human empirical] Munn, Müller, Medel, Naismith, Lizier, Sanders & Shine 2023, *Nature Communications* 14 (PMC10611774), "Neuronal connected burst cascades bridge macroscale adaptive signatures across arousal states": 4,900-neuron layer-5 pyramidal model with two control parameters — apical–basal coupling β (maps to Hobson's "Activation") and spatial correlation of apical input σ (maps to "Modulation"); diffuse projections (LC, raphe) increase σ and promote connected bursting, targeted projections (nucleus basalis, specific thalamus) decrease it. Validated on macaque ECoG (2 animals, sleep→wake) and human EEG (20 participants, dexmedetomidine): awake cascades longer than dreaming (p = 0.025) and unconscious (p = 1e−20); dreaming longer than unconscious (p = 1e−9); active information storage/transfer entropy highest awake; integration (participation coefficient) rises with arousal but dreaming is more segregated than wake. Authors propose noradrenergic input flattens and cholinergic input deepens the energy landscape. — [PMC10611774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10611774/)
- [Human empirical; Model] Singleton, Luppi, Carhart-Harris, Cruzat, Roseman, Nutt, Deco, Kringelbach, Stamatakis & Kuceyeski 2022, *Nature Communications* 13:5812: network control theory on fMRI (LSD 75 µg, n = 15; psilocybin 2 mg IV, n = 9) with 4 k-means states; both drugs lowered the transition energy for *every* pair of states vs placebo; weighting control inputs by a PET 5-HT2A map lowered energy more than uniform inputs and more than 10,000 spin-permuted maps; 5-HT2A gave the largest reduction (5-HT1B second). Under LSD, individuals with larger energy reductions showed larger changes in dwell times/appearance rates and higher entropy rate of state sequences (p < 0.05, uncorrected). Interpretation: "a flatter energy landscape means lower barriers between brain states," linked to REBUS. — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-33578-1)

**Turbulence (Deco & Kringelbach)**
- [Human empirical; Model] Deco & Kringelbach 2020, *Cell Reports*, "Turbulent-like dynamics in the human brain": 1,003 HCP participants; local Kuramoto order parameter shows turbulent-like power-law scaling of local synchronisation; a coupled-oscillator whole-brain model fits best "in a region of maximally developed turbulent-like dynamics." — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211124720314601)
- [Human empirical] Deco, Liebana Garcia, Sanz Perl, Sporns & Kringelbach 2023, *Communications Physics* 6:74: an "edge-centric metastability" measure validated on a ring of non-locally coupled Stuart–Landau oscillators with analytically known turbulence (detects the turbulent regime perfectly even at coarse spatial scale, whereas global metastability and mean Kuramoto order fail), then applied to HCP MEG from 89 participants (DK68 parcellation): turbulence present in all five bands (delta–gamma) vs circular-shift surrogates, converging after ~140 s of data; Edge Spacetime Predictability (information transfer across space-time) significantly above surrogate in all bands, "suggesting the efficiency of the turbulent regime for information transfer." — [PDF](https://www.kringelbach.org/papers/CP_Deco2023.pdf)
- [Human empirical; Model] Deco, Sanz Perl, Feng & Kringelbach 2026, *Network Neuroscience* 10(3):630–654, "The turbulent brain: Modeling vortex interactions for understanding human cognition": brain turbulence is "in the local level of synchronization of brain activity across space and time," measured as the std of the local Kuramoto modulus (4 mm radius); spiral vorticity vs Kuramoto vorticity correlate R = 0.57 (100 HCP participants); a Hopf model in vortex space beat a Kuramoto phase model and matched empirical turbulence; generative effective connectivity in vortex space classified rest vs SOCIAL task at 0.975 ± 0.025 and subtasks at 0.92–0.94 (971 HCP participants), outperformed BOLD-FC models for predicting g-factor and task scores, and a noise-perturbation control moved rest toward task FC (r = 0.67; "preliminary"). The Oxford book chapter describes turbulence as "the non-reversible dynamical regime taking place far from equilibrium, showing strong time asymmetry." — [PMC13418524](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13418524/); [OUP chapter](https://academic.oup.com/book/62453/chapter/556521970)
- [Review; verified citation only] Deco, Sanz Perl, Jerotic, Escrichs & Kringelbach 2025, "Turbulence as a framework for brain dynamics in health and disease," *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 169:105988; and a Nov-2025 bioRxiv "The Turbulent Brain (TTB)" MATLAB toolbox. — [bioRxiv toolbox](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.14.688068v1.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- Put together, the Shine-lab results give a two-knob picture: norepinephrine (phasic LC) lowers barriers → shorter dwell, more improbable transitions, more integration (the "flexibility/exploration" knob); acetylcholine (basal forebrain) raises barriers → longer dwell, stabilized states, more local/segregated processing (the "stability/precision" knob). Serotonergic 5-HT2A agonism (Singleton; Siegel in Q5) acts like a global version of the LC knob, flattening the whole landscape. This is the most direct answer to "what controls dwell time and transition probability" and is consistent across fMRI correlation (humans), causal inactivation (macaque) and biophysical modelling.
- Hancock et al.'s point that hub removal *increases* global metastability, and that metastability is maximal at an intermediate coupling, implies that flexibility of thought is a non-monotonic function of global coupling/arousal: both too-integrated (hub-dominated, high-ACh/high-precision) and too-fragmented regimes reduce the transient-state repertoire — matching the inverted-U clinical pattern (AD/ageing low; psychosis high).
- Turbulence and metastability are the same family of measures at different granularity (turbulence = spatially local metastability); the 2026 vortex work's claim that vortex-space effective connectivity predicts fluid intelligence better than FC is the first explicit link between the turbulent regime and higher cognition, but it is cross-sectional, one dataset, and interpretive.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study directly measured dwell-time distributions during creative/ideational tasks in humans; the dwell-time-distribution test proposed by Hancock et al. remains an unused tool for cognition.
- Rabinovich/Varona 2023–2025 updates on heteroclinic models of creativity were not retrievable this round (search budget exhausted); only the foundational 2008 model is cited.
- The Escrichs 2024 depression-prediction and Deco 2025 NBR review were seen only as citations.

## Key Question 3 — Oscillations and thought: theta–gamma coupling, alpha and internal attention, travelling waves, and oscillatory phase gating of what gets combined

### Takeaway
2024–2026 human intracranial work (93-, 24-, 16- and 5-patient datasets) has upgraded travelling waves from curiosity to candidate mechanism: theta/alpha waves reverse direction between encoding (posterior→anterior, feedforward) and recall (anterior→posterior, feedback), with bidirectional-wave participants encoding 5.8% better; wave *shape* (planar, rotational, concentric source/sink) distinguishes tasks and even individual memory items; and hidden spiral cores shift with behavioural state. Theta–gamma phase–amplitude coupling in hippocampus and auditory cortex decodes correct vs incorrect retention in 12/16 patients with gamma bursts locked to a consistent, high-excitability theta phase across people. Alpha (Jensen/Klimesch) remains the inhibitory gate for internally directed processing. However, the only 2023–2026 claims that phase *gates which information gets combined* are mechanistic interpretations layered on these correlational results, and sceptics (Buzsáki) argue waves just reflect synaptic activity and circuit structure.

### Cited Findings

**Travelling waves**
- [Human empirical] Mohan, Zhang, Ermentrout, … & Jacobs 2024, *Nature Human Behaviour* 8:1124–1135, "The direction of theta and alpha travelling waves modulates human memory processing": 93 patients with grid/strip ECoG, 421 oscillation clusters (2–13 Hz; e.g., 7.8, 8.9, 10.8 Hz), free-recall task. Waves propagated posterior→anterior during successful encoding and anterior→posterior during recall; participants with bidirectional propagation had a 5.8% higher successful-encoding rate than unidirectional/non-directional participants; ~83% of trials survived spatial-aliasing exclusion. Interpretation: waves are "rhythmic pulses of local neuronal spiking that move in different directions for different behaviours," indexing feedforward vs feedback processing and "organizing the timing and direction of network interactions." — [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01838-3)
- [Human empirical] Das, Zabeh, Ermentrout & Jacobs 2024 (bioRxiv v2; reported by Quanta as published in *Nature Communications*, April 2026), "Planar, Spiral, and Concentric Traveling Waves Distinguish Cognitive States in Human Memory": 24 ECoG patients (9 spatial "Treasure Hunt" task, 15 verbal Sternberg), 39 oscillation clusters, ~6 significant complex-ICA modes per cluster. Planar waves = median 59% of wave strength at low and 43% at high frequencies; rotational waves rose to 37% at high frequencies, especially in the spatial task (χ²(3) = 162.3, p < 0.001); in left hemisphere, rotational dominated in the spatial task (χ²(3) = 150.0) while concentric dominated in the verbal task (χ²(3) = 288.1); concentric waves were mostly sources in spatial (78%) and sinks in verbal (68%). Speeds 0.3–3.3 m/s (median 1.1 m/s at 12–26 Hz, 0.5 m/s at 5–12 Hz); stable epochs 80–180 ms; ~86% of electrodes had a narrowband oscillation. Neural networks decoded cognitive state (and in 13/26 verbal clusters, the specific letter) from ICA weights (p < 0.001). — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.01.26.577456v2.full); [Quanta coverage](https://www.quantamagazine.org/surprisingly-complex-waves-reveal-the-brains-inner-workings-20260930/)
- [Human empirical; Model] Das, Zhang, Zabeh, Kolibius, Mohan, Ermentrout & Jacobs 2025 (bioRxiv 2025.11.03.686225), "Hidden Spirals Reveal the Neurocomputational Mechanisms of Traveling Waves in Human Memory": 5 patients with 8×8 ECoG grids, Sternberg task (10 sessions, ~91% accuracy); via frequency-flattening in a Kuramoto model, ~10% of stable epochs resolve as hidden spirals and ~13% as synchrony (spiral share significant vs surrogates); mean propagation 0.7 m/s; planar-wave activation anticorrelated with hidden spirals (e.g., r = −0.40); spiral core locations shifted significantly with behavioural state in all 5 (TFP) and 3/5 (TPF) subjects. Mechanism proposed: spirals arise in uniform oscillator networks; task-driven frequency heterogeneity distorts them into complex patterns and frequency gradients into planar waves. — [PMC13252158](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13252158/)
- [Review; press summary] Muller & Reynolds 2026 *Neuron* review ("Cortical traveling waves in time and space: Physics, physiology, and psychology") argues visual-cortex waves carry recent-experience information alongside current input to support short-term prediction; Ye & Steinmetz (*Science*, June 2026) report mirrored, inter-hemispherically synchronized rotating waves in mouse cortex with a spiral wiring arrangement in somatosensory cortex; Earl Miller: waves are becoming "a major motif of how the cortex processes information"; György Buzsáki's scepticism: waves merely reflect synaptic activity — "You don't need anything happening in the extracellular space"; Jacobs' own caveat that epilepsy-grid sampling means "we might be missing how ubiquitous these patterns really are"; "no consensus on what the waves do." — [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/surprisingly-complex-waves-reveal-the-brains-inner-workings-20260930/); [Neuron abstract](https://www.cell.com/neuron/abstract/S0896-6273(25)00975-4)
- [Human empirical] A 2025 *Journal of Neuroscience* EEG study found a stronger anterior-to-posterior travelling wave for active vs passive removal of items from working memory. — [J Neurosci PDF](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/45/28/e2414242025.full.pdf)
- [Human empirical] Re-analysis of two EEG studies: bidirectional planar waves link visual and frontal sites during working-memory-guided behaviour, appearing only with an overt motor response. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.12.584543v2.full)
- [Human empirical; cross-link to Q2] Munn et al. 2021: LC bursts are followed by an anterior→posterior fMRI travelling wave at ~0.13 m/s; basal-forebrain bursts by the reverse — a neuromodulatory origin for macroscale wave direction. — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26268-x)

**Theta–gamma coupling and phase coding**
- [Human empirical] Borderie, Caclin, Lachaux, Perrone-Bertollotti, Hoyer, Kahane, Catenoix, Tillmann & Albouy 2024, *PLOS Biology* (DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.3002512), "Cross-frequency coupling in corticohippocampal networks supports the maintenance of sequential auditory information in short-term memory": SEEG in 16 drug-resistant epilepsy patients, delayed match-to-sample with tone sequences. Theta power rose during retention across hippocampal/parahippocampal, inferior frontal and temporal regions; theta–gamma PAC (not raw power) distinguished memory from perception; SVM on PAC decoded correct vs incorrect trials above chance in 12/16 participants (all p < .03); PAC in hippocampus and ventral auditory stream correlated with performance; gamma bursts were "consistently restricted to specific phase ranges of the theta oscillations across participants," corresponding to higher excitability phases. — [PDF](https://lead.ube.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Borderie_et_al._2024.pdf)
- [Human empirical; from search summary only] Amour di Chanaz et al. 2023, *Current Biology*: hippocampal LFP in 10 epilepsy patients shows gamma coupled to opposed theta phases during episodic encoding vs retrieval (supporting theta phase-opposition models). — see search summary via [Köster 2018 PMC page context](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6283876/) [verified citation only]
- [Model] Pirazzini & Ursino 2024, *Frontiers in Neural Circuits*: hippocampal model encoding/retrieving event sequences via the theta–gamma code. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2024.1326609/epub)
- [Foundational; from prior knowledge] Lisman & Jensen 2013 *Neuron* "The theta–gamma neural code" (DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2013.03.007); Fries 2015 *Neuron* "Rhythms for cognition: communication through coherence" (DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034) — phase alignment of sender and receiver as the gate on which inputs are effective.

**Alpha and internally directed cognition (brief, as instructed)**
- [Foundational; from prior knowledge] Jensen & Mazaheri 2010 "Shaping functional architecture by oscillatory alpha activity: gating by inhibition" (Front Hum Neurosci, DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2010.00186); Klimesch 2012 *TICS* "Alpha-band oscillations, attention, and controlled access to stored information" (DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2012.10.007); Benedek et al. 2014 *Neuropsychologia* — right-parietal alpha increase during divergent thinking reflects focused internal attention/shielding from sensory input (DOI 10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2014.02.010). Mechanistic reading: alpha is a pulsed-inhibition gate that releases internal (memory/semantic) processing from external drive; the REBUS paper (Q5) treats alpha/beta power as the readout of top-down prior precision, so psychedelic alpha suppression = precision relaxation. — REBUS context: [PMC6588209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6588209/)

### Inferences
- The direction-reversal result (Mohan 2024) is the cleanest human evidence that a global oscillatory parameter (wave direction) indexes the brain's switch between taking in information and generating it from memory; the 5.8% behavioural advantage for bidirectional participants suggests that the *ability to switch wave regime* — not either regime alone — predicts memory success, which is a flexibility claim.
- Rotational waves rising with task complexity (spatial > verbal; distractor periods) plus Das et al.'s decoding of individual letters from wave geometry suggest wave shape is a spatial code for *what* is being held, while theta phase (Borderie) is a temporal code for *when* an item's gamma burst is allowed; together they constitute the current best mechanistic story for "phase gates what gets combined," but no 2023–2026 study has yet shown that manipulating phase changes which items get bound.
- The LC→anterior-to-posterior-wave result (Munn 2021) and the recall→anterior-to-posterior result (Mohan 2024) are suggestively aligned (both are "top-down/feedback" waves), pointing to arousal bursts as a trigger for switching into retrieval/generation mode; this is an inference, not tested.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study found that causally tests oscillatory phase gating of information *combination* (e.g., phase-locked stimulation altering binding) in humans; the search budget expired before targeted queries on communication-through-coherence updates and tACS phase manipulations.
- 2023–2026 alpha–creativity updates (Benedek lab, Lustenberger-type tACS) were not retrieved; only foundational citations are given.
- The published Nature Communications 2026 version of the Das/Jacobs spiral paper was not read (only the bioRxiv v2 and Quanta summary).

## Key Question 4 — Dimensionality and manifolds: does cognitive flexibility correspond to changes in the dimensionality/geometry of neural activity (mixed selectivity, abstract geometry, human iEEG, Shine's neuromodulatory low-dimensional control)?

### Takeaway
The cleanest 2024 human result (Courellis et al., *Nature*; 17 patients, 2,694 neurons) shows that the capacity to generalize by inference is accompanied by the hippocampus reorganizing into an *abstract/disentangled* geometry (higher cross-condition generalization and higher shattering dimensionality, 0.57→0.62) that appears only after learning, only on correct trials, and equally after instruction or experience — i.e., flexibility corresponds to a specific geometry that trades a little dimensionality for generalizability rather than to maximal dimensionality per se. Criticality work in mice (Fontenele 2024) shows the collective, behaviour-coupled part of cortical dynamics lives in a ≤3-D "critical subspace," and Shine-lab fMRI/causal work shows neuromodulators reshape the low-dimensional energy landscape (Q2). A 2025 model argues perceptual manifolds expand (3-D→7-D) to provide a "reservoir" of flexible classifications. No 2023–2026 study was found that directly ties a dimensionality measure to a standard human cognitive-flexibility task.

### Cited Findings
- [Human empirical] Courellis, Minxha, Cardenas, Kimmel, Reed, Valiante, Salzman, Mamelak, Fusi & Rutishauser 2024, *Nature* 632:841–849, "Abstract representations emerge in human hippocampal neurons during inference": 17 epilepsy patients, 42 sessions (36 analysed), 2,694 single units (hippocampus 494, amygdala 889, preSMA 269, dACC 310, vmPFC 463, VTC 269); serial reversal task with two latent contexts enabling one-shot inference. Only hippocampal populations encoded context and stimulus pair simultaneously in an abstract (disentangled) format; shattering dimensionality 0.57 (inference-absent) vs 0.62 (inference-present), P = 2.7e−3; context and stimulus CCGP/parallelism elevated only in inference-present sessions (P = 0.012–0.041); abstract context coding present even pre-stimulus; present on correct but largely absent on error trials; hippocampal firing rates dropped ~60% with inference, with larger centroid distances and lower variance along coding axes. Post-instruction group (5 patients): context became abstract after verbal instruction (PS P = 0.0028; context-tuned neurons 8%→18%); pre-instruction group (3 patients) already abstract; not-exhibited group (4 patients) never abstract. "Trial-and-error learning and verbal instruction can produce hippocampal representations with similar geometry." VTC: stimulus abstract, context not. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07799-x) / [PMC11338822](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338822)
- [Foundational; from prior knowledge] Rigotti et al. 2013 *Nature* 497:585–590 "The importance of mixed selectivity in complex cognitive tasks" (DOI 10.1038/nature12160): high-dimensional mixed-selective PFC representations collapse on error trials; Bernardi et al. 2020 *Cell* 183:954–967 "The geometry of abstraction in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex" (DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.031): CCGP and shattering dimensionality can be simultaneously high in monkey HPC/PFC (abstract yet high-dimensional geometry). — context from [Fusi talk abstract](https://neuroscience.stanford.edu/node/4002)
- [Human empirical; fMRI] Kimmel et al. 2025 (CCN abstract): factorized model of temporal abstraction; OFC, hippocampus and amygdala signals track learning attributed to generalization, with generalization dominance increasing across task instances in entorhinal cortex, OFC and hippocampus. — [CCN 2025 PDF](https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Kimmel_2025_Dynamics_neural_representations_support_generalization_continual.pdf)
- [Human empirical; preprint] "Neural geometry in the human hippocampus enables generalization across spatial position and gaze" (arXiv 2603.04747, 2026) — hippocampal geometry supporting generalization across variables. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04747) [verified citation only]
- [Animal empirical] Fontenele et al. 2024 *Science Advances*: the behaviour-coupled, near-critical component of awake cortical population activity is confined to a low-dimensional subspace (first ≤3 PCs; max 13), while higher PCs are desynchronized; authors hypothesize critical subspace ↔ motor output and desynchronized subspace ↔ sensory encoding. — [PMC11051676](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11051676/)
- [Model] Fontenele et al. 2025 (bioRxiv): "the number of near-critical modes sets the dimension of the critical subspace"; hidden low-dimensional criticality can coexist with high-dimensional desynchronized activity. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.02.673722v1.full)
- [Model] "From sensory to perceptual manifolds: the twist of neural geometry" (2025, PMC12693994): a 3-D sensory manifold expands into a 7-D perceptual manifold whose "high dimensionality…functions as a reservoir of computational solutions, enabling flexible classifications according to downstream task demands" (7-D is task-specific). — [PMC12693994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12693994/)
- [Review] Perich, Narain & Gallego 2025, "A neural manifold view of the brain," *Nature Neuroscience* (DOI 10.1038/s41593-025-02031-z) [verified citation only]; Langdon, Genkin & Engel 2023 *Nat Rev Neurosci* "A unifying perspective on neural manifolds and circuits for cognition" [foundational; from prior knowledge].
- [Review] 2025 *Frontiers in Cognition* review: cortical gradients from dimensionality reduction "provide a low-dimensional manifold framework for understanding flexibility and learning," with motivational signals actively shaping manifold structure during learning; caveats on interpretability of reduction techniques. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cognition/articles/10.3389/fcogn.2025.1690469/full)
- [Model/critique] 2026 arXiv: random neural networks "match observed dimensionality of neural population recordings," so observed dimensionality alone is a weak test of mechanism. — [arXiv 2605.26551](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26551); methods caveat on finite-sample dimensionality estimation — [arXiv 2509.26560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26560)
- [Human empirical; Shine lab] Munn et al. 2021 (Q2): LC bursts flatten the low-dimensional (MSD-based) energy landscape and raise integration; BNM bursts deepen it — the empirical basis of Shine's "neuromodulatory control of low-dimensional dynamics." — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26268-x). [Foundational; from prior knowledge] Shine et al. 2019 *Nature Neuroscience* 22:289–296 "Human cognition involves the dynamic integration of neural activity and neuromodulatory systems" (DOI 10.1038/s41593-018-0312-0): across HCP tasks, a low-dimensional manifold of integration/segregation whose task-related traversal co-varies with pupil-linked arousal and neuromodulatory receptor maps.
- [Human empirical; preprint] 7T fMRI with concurrent pupillometry: arousal organizes functional connectivity into a low-dimensional set of seven connectivity communities (eLife reviewed preprint 110294). — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/110294v1) [verified citation only]; mouse widefield: spatiotemporal dynamics are "largely reducible to a common latent process that is continuously tracked by pupil diameter" ("Arousal as a universal embedding for spatiotemporal brain dynamics"). — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.11.06.565918.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Courellis result reconciles the "high-dimensional = flexible" (Rigotti) and "abstract/low-dimensional = generalizable" (Bernardi) intuitions in humans: flexibility that *transfers* to new situations requires a geometry that is simultaneously abstract (high CCGP) and still fairly high-dimensional (shattering dimensionality rose, not fell). The practical prediction is that new-pattern generation that generalizes depends on first building a disentangled latent-variable code, which can be installed by instruction as well as by experience.
- The arousal/neuromodulation literature treats the *collective* low-dimensional mode as the thing being controlled (LC flattens, ACh deepens; pupil tracks the latent process). Combined with Fontenele's finding that the critical subspace is low-dimensional, this suggests arousal tunes the few collective modes that determine distance-to-criticality, leaving high-dimensional desynchronized coding intact — a possible mechanism for how a brain state can become globally more labile without losing local representational detail.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2025 study found that measures manifold dimensionality against a standard set-shifting/reversal task in humans (the Courellis task is the closest, and it is inference/generalization rather than switching cost).
- Shine-lab 2023–2025 papers combining pupil, gain and manifold dimensionality in one human study were not located; Shine 2019 is cited from prior knowledge.
- The Tye/Miller/Fusi 2024 *Neuron* "Mixed selectivity" review could not be resolved to an open-access copy.

## Key Question 5 — Neuromodulatory control of brain state: adaptive gain (LC-NE), acetylcholine and precision, 5-HT2A/REBUS, and 2023–2026 psychedelic and anesthetic neuroimaging — what is the control knob for flexibility vs stability?

### Takeaway
The best-controlled human psychedelic dataset to date (Siegel et al. 2024 *Nature*; precision functional mapping, n = 6–7, ~18 scans each) shows 25 mg psilocybin produces a >3× larger whole-brain connectivity change than an arousal-matched stimulant (3.52 vs 1.10 units of day-to-day variability), driven by desynchronization measured as increased normalized global spatial complexity (largest in association cortex, scaling with 5-HT2A density r = 0.39), tracking mystical-experience scores at r² = 0.81, and *suppressed by engaging in a perceptual task* — directly demonstrating that external task demand re-stabilizes the brain state. Converging control-theory, Ising and energy-landscape analyses (Singleton 2022; Ruffini 2023; Toker 2024 5-MeO-DMT) agree 5-HT2A agonism flattens barriers/raises temperature. The NE/ACh dissociation (Munn 2021; Taylor 2024) assigns the flexibility knob to phasic LC gain and the stability knob to cholinergic drive. Entropy-as-biomarker, however, has a replication problem: a 28-participant multi-metric study found 7/12 fMRI entropy metrics unchanged by psilocybin.

### Cited Findings

**Psychedelics (5-HT2A)**
- [Human empirical] Siegel, …, Dosenbach et al. 2024, *Nature* 632:131–138, "Psilocybin desynchronizes the human brain": 7 healthy adults (1 excluded; n = 6), 25 mg psilocybin vs 40 mg methylphenidate 1–2 weeks apart, ~18 MRI visits each (129 visits total; two 15-min rest scans per session), replication dose 6–12 months later in n = 4. Normalized whole-brain FC change 3.52 (psilocybin) vs 1.10 (MTP) in units of day-to-day variability; largest in DMN (MTP largest in motor/action networks). Normalized global spatial complexity (NGSC; 0 = identical time courses, 1 = independent) increased (estimate 0.051, 95% CI 0.034–0.068, P = 2e−6), largest in association cortex, minimal in primary cortex, correlated with 5-HT2A density (r = 0.39), replicated in an LSD dataset, back to baseline by the next session. An auditory–visual matching task reduced psilocybin-driven FC change (interaction P = 5.5e−5) and NGSC increase (P = 4.8e−8). **Anesthesia / ketamine / sedation transitions**
- [Human + animal empirical] Toker 2024: propofol pushes slow thalamocortical dynamics toward chaos and cuts cross-frequency transfer (rats p = 0.002); seizures to hyperstable periodicity. — [PMC10805384](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10805384/)
- [Animal empirical] de la Fuente et al. 2023 *Cerebral Cortex* 33(5):1856–1865: macaque ECoG (128 ch; wake 2 animals/8 sessions, deep sleep 21 sessions, ketamine ~5 mg/kg 4 sessions); forward vs time-reversed epochs classified at AUC > 0.75 in wakefulness across all 81 CNN models, degraded in deep sleep (only the most complex model succeeded) and at chance for phase-based models under ketamine; gamma (> 40 Hz) features carried most of the asymmetry. — [Cerebral Cortex](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/33/5/1856/6609497)
- [Human empirical] Munn 2023: dexmedetomidine sedation in 20 humans — unconscious cascades shortest; dreaming intermediate. — [PMC10611774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10611774/)
- [Review] Gervais 2023 scoping review: all human anesthesia studies report a deviation from criticality; sub-anesthetic ketamine reduced pair-correlation function (away from criticality). — [PMC10248073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10248073/)

### Inferences
- Across methods the control knob resolves into a *precision/gain axis*: 5-HT2A agonism and phasic NE lower the effective precision of dominant (top-down, association-cortex) states — flattening barriers, raising spatial complexity, increasing transition rates — whereas ACh (and task engagement, per Siegel's task interaction) raises precision and re-stabilizes. For idea generation, the implication is that novelty-permissive states are those with transiently reduced top-down precision in association cortex, and that externally demanding tasks actively abolish them (Siegel's task effect; Mediano's video effect).
- The persistence finding (hippocampus–DMN decoupling for ~3 weeks after a single dose) is the only human neuroimaging evidence of a *lasting* loosening of a specific coupling, which maps onto REBUS's "afterglow" prediction; but the n is tiny and the effect reverted by 6–12 months.
- The entropy biomarker story is strongest for single-channel temporal complexity (LZ in MEG/EEG) and spatial complexity (NGSC), and weakest for multi-metric fMRI "entropy"; the report should avoid the generic phrase "psychedelics increase brain entropy" and name the measure.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 human study found that pharmacologically manipulates NE or ACh while measuring both brain-state energy landscapes and a creativity/flexibility outcome; the ACh causal evidence is from two macaques.

## Key Question 6 — Thermodynamic / information-theoretic descriptions (entropy of brain states, temporal irreversibility, Lempel–Ziv complexity) and their relationship to conscious flexibility and creativity

### Takeaway
Temporal irreversibility (the "arrow of time" of brain signals) has emerged as the thermodynamic signature of the conscious, cognitively engaged brain: it is high in wakefulness, collapses in deep sleep, ketamine and deep anesthesia (de la Fuente 2023; Camassa 2024), is proposed to be higher in task than rest and in turbulent, far-from-equilibrium regimes (Deco/Kringelbach "Thermodynamics of Mind," 2024), and can be decomposed into higher-order (synergistic) components (Luppi 2023). Entropy/complexity measures (LZ, NGSC, Ising temperature, BDM) consistently rise under serotonergic psychedelics, but they are measure-specific, context-sensitive (external input decouples them from experience), and no 2023–2026 study was found that links any of these thermodynamic measures directly to creative output.

### Cited Findings
- [Animal empirical] de la Fuente, Zamberlan, Bocaccio, Kringelbach, Deco, Sanz Perl, Pallavicini & Tagliazucchi 2023, *Cerebral Cortex* 33(5):1856–1865, "Temporal irreversibility of neural dynamics as a signature of consciousness": 3,759 wake / 4,198 sleep / 2,556 ketamine 4-s ECoG epochs; wake AUC > 0.75 for all 81 models with near-perfect cross-model agreement; sleep and ketamine substantially reduced; largest contribution from > 40 Hz with a secondary ~20 Hz peak; proposed link to the subjective passage of time is "a hypothesis." — [Cerebral Cortex](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/33/5/1856/6609497)
- [Animal empirical; from search summary] Camassa et al. 2024, *Scientific Reports*: rat cortical LFP across slow-wave sleep, awake, deep- and light-anesthesia slow waves and microarousals; lowest irreversibility (closest to equilibrium) in deep anesthesia and SWS, highest in asynchronous wakefulness. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/fa04176120584ac586a58b27ab8413e4) [link returned 404 at fetch; details from search abstract]
- [Human empirical; foundational, from prior knowledge] Lynn et al. 2021 *PNAS* "Broken detailed balance and entropy production in the human brain" (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2109889118): entropy production of fMRI state transitions in HCP data increases from rest to task.

### Inferences
- Irreversibility is the thermodynamic face of the same regime that criticality/turbulence/metastability describe dynamically: wake near-critical turbulence is far from equilibrium and time-asymmetric; sleep/anesthesia are closer to equilibrium. For creativity, the relevant prediction is not "maximize entropy" but "maximize non-equilibrium drive with intact hierarchy": states with high irreversibility (engaged wake) but lowered top-down precision (psychedelic-like, quiet-wake, post-sleep) should be the ones in which new patterns can be generated and still be organized.
- The two strongest *negative* data points — entropy measure heterogeneity (7/12 fMRI metrics null) and the decoupling of LZ from experience under external stimulation — imply that entropy is an index of a state's susceptibility, not a direct correlate of productive thought.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study was found relating temporal irreversibility, entropy production or LZ complexity to measured creative performance; this is an open gap, not a negative result.
- The Kringelbach/Deco TiCS 2024 review and the 2023 "arrow of time in cognition" Network Neuroscience paper were not read in full (paywalled/404); details rest on verified DOIs and summaries.
