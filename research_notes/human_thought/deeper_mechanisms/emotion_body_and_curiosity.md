## Key Question 1: Curiosity and information-seeking — computational accounts and 2023–2026 evidence; effects on memory and idea generation

### Takeaway
The field has converged on a layered account: curiosity is an intrinsic-reward signal computed over expected or realized gains in knowledge, with competing but increasingly unified candidate quantities (novelty, information gap/uncertainty, prediction error, and learning progress). The strongest 2021–2026 human evidence favours learning progress and reinforcement-learning dynamics over simple novelty, shows that curiosity-related memory benefits are real but narrower than first thought (they can even interfere with encoding of unrelated facts), and reveals that "curiosity" is an umbrella term whose trait and task measures only partly overlap. Direct evidence that curiosity causally improves later idea generation remains thin and mostly correlational.

### Cited Findings

Foundational computational accounts (pre-2023, for context):
- Loewenstein's information-gap theory (1994) treats curiosity as a response to a gap between what one knows and wants to know, peaking at intermediate knowledge — described in Dubey & Griffiths' rational analysis — [Dubey & Griffiths, arXiv/Psychological Review](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1705.04351); original: [Loewenstein 1994, Psychological Bulletin (DOI from recall)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75)
- Kidd & Hayden's 2015 Neuron review "The Psychology and Neuroscience of Curiosity" is the standard field review and notes the lack of a unified account — [Kidd & Hayden 2015, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010)
- Gottlieb & Oudeyer (2018) "Towards a neuroscience of active sampling and curiosity" frames curiosity as active sampling driven by intrinsic utilities such as learning progress — [Gottlieb & Oudeyer 2018, Nature Reviews Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-018-0078-0)
- Oudeyer's learning-progress hypothesis: curiosity and learning form a positive feedback loop, and curiosity-driven exploration is most useful when extrinsic rewards are rare or misleading — [Oudeyer, Gottlieb & Lopes; Computational Theories of Curiosity-Driven Learning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.10546); [HAL review](https://hal-ensta-paris.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01404278)
- Dubey & Griffiths (2020) "Reconciling novelty and complexity through a rational analysis of curiosity" (Psychological Review): curiosity is rationally directed at stimuli that maximize future ability to respond appropriately; it operates at the computational level and unifies novelty-based and complexity (information-gap) accounts — [Dubey & Griffiths 2020, Psychological Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000175); summary: [arXiv 1705.04351](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.04351)
- Gruber & Ranganath (2019) PACE framework (Prediction, Appraisal, Curiosity, Exploration) explains how curiosity enhances hippocampus-dependent memory via dopaminergic-hippocampal interactions — [Gruber & Ranganath 2019, Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2019.10.003)
- Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath (2014): fMRI trivia paradigm; memory was better for high-curiosity trivia answers and for incidental faces shown during high-curiosity anticipation, both immediately and after 24 h; curiosity raised activity in the hippocampus and in reward circuitry (nucleus accumbens, midbrain) and strengthened hippocampus-reward interactions — [Gruber et al. 2014, Neuron (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/)
- Kang et al. (2009) "wick in the candle of learning": epistemic curiosity activates reward circuitry and enhances memory — [Kang et al. 2009, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x)
- Marvin & Shohamy (2016): valence of information predicts choice; information prediction errors enhance learning — [Marvin & Shohamy 2016, JEP: General](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000140)
- van Lieshout et al. (2018): induction and relief of curiosity elicit parietal and frontal activity — [van Lieshout et al. 2018, J Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2816-17.2018)
- Murayama (2022) reward-learning framework of knowledge acquisition: knowledge acquisition is an inherent reward that reinforces information seeking; a "self-boosting" loop sustains it over long periods; distinctive features vs. extrinsic reward learning are selectivity, vulnerability and under-appreciation — [Murayama 2022, Psychological Review 129(1):175–198](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000349); summary: [Stirling repository](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/handle/1893/34001)
- Poli et al. (2020): infants tailor attention to maximize learning (learning-progress sensitivity in infancy) — [Poli et al. 2020, Science Advances](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb5053)
- Lydon-Staley et al. (2021): 149 participants browsed Wikipedia >5 h over 21 days; deprivation curiosity was associated with tighter knowledge networks and more returns to previously visited concepts ("hunters" vs "busybodies") — [Lydon-Staley et al. 2021, Nature Human Behaviour (PMC8082236)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-00985-7)

Learning progress in adult humans (key quantitative study, 2021):
- Ten, Kaushik, Oudeyer & Gottlieb (2021): 382 analysed participants (400 recruited) chose freely among four "monster family" learning activities (A1 easiest, A3 hardest learnable, A4 unlearnable) over 250 free-choice trials, after 15 forced trials per activity. An external-goal group (n=196, told to maximize learning) spent most time on the unlearnable A4 (36.9%); the internal-goal group (n=186) favoured the easiest A1 (33.0%). A softmax model over competence (percent correct) plus learning progress (change in percent correct) fit best (mean AIC 492 vs 693 for random), with no interaction with instruction group (p=.716), meaning LP sensitivity appeared without explicit instruction. LP-driven participants mastered at least two activities 90.5% of the time vs 70.6% for competence-driven participants; self-challenge showed an inverted-U relation with final performance — [Ten et al. 2021, Nature Communications (PMC8514490)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8514490/)

2023–2026 theory:
- Modirshanechi, Kondrakiewicz, Gerstner & Haesler (2023) group curiosity studies into three types of information seeking and argue recent machine-learning intrinsic-motivation models can unify them; there is "growing consensus that curious behavior is not goal-directed but related to seeking or reacting to information" — [Modirshanechi et al. 2023, Trends in Neurosciences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2023.10.002)
- Ten et al. (2025) "The Curious U" reviews seven accounts of the inverted-U between knowledge and curiosity and proposes that the U emerges from a drive to maximize learning progress — [Ten et al. 2025, Open Mind](https://doi.org/10.1162/opmi.a.41)
- Erdemli et al. (2025) propose an Integrative Appraisal Model of Epistemic Curiosity combining information-gap, emotion and reward-anticipation accounts; appraisal theory decides which gaps elicit curiosity, and high relevance with low coping potential predicts anxiety and information avoidance rather than curiosity — [Erdemli et al. 2025, Affective Science](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42761-025-00328-7)
- Ivancovsky, Baror & Bar (2023/2024) BBS target article proposes a shared novelty-seeking basis for curiosity and creativity, reviewing default-mode, salience and executive-control network evidence; the model holds that "state of mind" governs how the common process is expressed — [Ivancovsky et al., Behavioral and Brain Sciences](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x23002807). Commentaries dispute this: Holm & Schrater argue novelty is neither necessary nor sufficient and both may reflect an epistemic drive — [Holm & Schrater 2024, BBS](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x23003321); Acar & Fuchs argue novelty-seeking may underlie curiosity and the novelty but not the usefulness dimension of creativity — [Acar & Fuchs 2024, BBS](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x2300331x); Becker et al. argue curiosity, creativity and memory must be separated by the intrinsic motivations involved (surprise vs novelty) to test the model — [Becker et al. 2024, BBS](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x23003424); the authors' reply organizes 29 commentaries into five issues — [Ivancovsky et al. 2024, BBS reply](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x24000293)
- Gruaz et al. (2024/2025) simulation study: the best intrinsic reward (novelty, surprise, information gain, empowerment, etc.) depends on environment and objective; combining novelty with information gain performed close to optimal across conditions — [Gruaz et al., PsyArXiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/evm9n_v3)
- Desvaux et al. (2026) argue curiosity depends on metacognitive monitoring and control; classroom curiosity interventions show mixed results, often working better for struggling learners — [Desvaux et al. 2026, Advances in Child Development and Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.acdb.2026.04.005)

2023–2026 behavioural and neural evidence:
- Jach et al. (2024, incl. Murayama & Gottlieb): 820 participants completed 15 personality measures and 9 cognitive information-demand tasks. Shared variance across tasks formed a single axis contrasting directed (uncertainty-driven) vs random (stochasticity-driven) exploration. The best personality predictors were peripheral traits (need for cognition, thrill seeking, stress tolerance) and extraversion/behavioural inhibition, not the "core" curiosity traits (openness, deprivation sensitivity, joyous exploration); the authors conclude "curiosity" is an umbrella term — [Jach et al. 2024, PNAS 121(45)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2415236121)
- Abir et al. (2026): 5,831 participants decided whether to wait for answers; reading satisfying answers increased subsequent curiosity relative to dissatisfying ones, but only when the new information was semantically related to prior learning, supporting a reinforcement-learning account over an optimal-arousal account — [Abir et al. 2026, PNAS 123(17)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2529176123)
- Recht & Yeung (2026): in two online trivia experiments, the number of candidate answers participants had in mind ("knowability") predicted higher curiosity, moderate confidence, and better memory five days later, replicating while controlling for prior knowledge — [Recht & Yeung 2026, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2026.106626)
- Jiwa et al. (2026): three samples (N=50, 50, 150); a model with non-linear probability and value perception outperformed a linear mixture-of-motives model for instrumental and non-instrumental information seeking; non-linearities correlated with personality and risk attitudes — [Jiwa et al. 2026, Communications Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-026-00398-8)
- Rüterbories et al. (2024): EEG trivia paradigm; high-curiosity answers were recalled more and produced larger reward-sensitive ERPs (reward positivity 250–350 ms, P3, late positive potential 600–1000 ms); RewP and P3 were also larger for later-recalled answers but curiosity did not modulate that encoding effect — [Rüterbories et al. 2024, J Cognitive Neuroscience 36(5):888–900 (PMC7618218)](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_02114)
- Eschmann et al. (2023): pre-pandemic curiosity/anxiety questionnaires plus resting-state fMRI predicted real-life COVID-19 information seeking months later; curiosity (not anxiety) and VTA–nucleus accumbens resting connectivity predicted information-seeking frequency — [Eschmann et al. 2023, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsac050)
- Eschmann et al. (2023 preprint): double dissociation — mesolimbic intrinsic connectivity predicted curiosity-related memory gains but not prediction-error gains; cingulo-hippocampal connectivity predicted prediction-error gains but not curiosity gains — [Eschmann et al. 2023, bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.04.21.537775)
- Keller, Salvi, Leiker, Gruber & Dunsmoor (2024): across three task versions, memory for unrelated scholastic facts presented just before/after a trivia answer was *poorer* after high-curiosity than low-curiosity trivia, contradicting the earlier incidental-face benefit; the authors propose a high-curiosity target interferes with encoding of complex unrelated material close in time — [Keller et al. 2024, npj Science of Learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00234-w)
- Cen, Teichert, Hodgetts & Gruber (2024): VR exploration; pre-exploration curiosity predicted exploration extent; trait Stress Tolerance moderated the link; curiosity and exploration predicted precision of spatial-relational recall on a sketch map — [Cen et al. 2024, Communications Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-024-00174-6)
- Sobczak et al. (2025): three experiments (n=54, 81, 196) in young and older adults; state (not trait) epistemic curiosity predicted long-term memory; surprise amplified the effect more in young adults; trait curiosity influenced state curiosity directly and via formal education — [Sobczak et al. 2025, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-14479-x)
- Ziv et al. (2026): 5–8-year-olds (n=60 behavioural, n=51 fMRI) learned more when curious; high curiosity engaged inferior frontal gyrus, lateral occipital cortex, thalamus and putamen, but did *not* modulate the preregistered hippocampus or nucleus accumbens — [Ziv et al. 2026, Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2026.101687)
- Poh et al. (2026 preprint): after high-confidence errors, information seeking protected updated memories from 24-h decay; when initial curiosity was low, post-error surprise increased seeking and durable updating — [Poh et al. 2026, PsyArXiv](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/yx6ek_v1)
- Alvarez-Martin et al. (2026 preprint): double-blind within-subject (N=26) naltrexone vs placebo in a music exploration task; the opioid antagonist did not change curiosity ratings but reduced exploratory behaviour when curiosity was high — [Alvarez-Martin et al. 2026, bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.05.722646)
- Cardona et al. (2026 preprint): music curiosity increased willingness to pay for information and long-term memory in the lab (N=63) and at a live DJ set (N=150), especially when exploration carried costs — [Cardona et al. 2026, PsyArXiv](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/pbksy_v1)
- Nasiri et al. (2026): 22 adults in VR; intermediate spatial enclosure elicited the most curiosity, exploration and positive affect; positive valence correlated with memory — [Nasiri et al. 2026, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2026.109504)
- Chen et al. (2026 preprint): large (48-pt) fonts raised curiosity and recall of rare-word pairs; the font-size–recall link was mediated by curiosity, not perceived importance — [Chen et al. 2026, PsyArXiv](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/gkm6b_v1)

Curiosity styles at scale and links to creativity:
- Zhou et al. (2024): 482,760 Wikipedia mobile-app readers in 14 languages across 50 countries. Hunter/busybody score = mean of edge count, clustering coefficient and global efficiency minus characteristic path length over the hyperlink network of visited articles; the "dancer" style was operationalized as forward flow (mean semantic dissimilarity between current and all prior articles). Lab-sample network distributions replicated in the app sample (d ≈ 0.18–0.28 for components). Across 74 country-language pairs, loose/broad networks correlated with spatial-navigation ability (ρ=0.54), positive affect (ρ=0.47), negative affect (ρ=−0.28), HDI (ρ=0.58), education (ρ=0.60), gender inequality (ρ=−0.66) and education inequality (ρ=−0.55), Bonferroni-corrected. Forward flow correlated ρ=0.79 with information diversity and only ρ=−0.05 with the hunter/busybody score, interpreted as a distinct creativity-related dimension; readers showed *lower* forward flow than random walks — [Zhou et al. 2024, Science Advances 10(43) (PMC11506172)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11506172/); press: [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/10/241025165758.htm)
- Liu et al. (2026): among five curiosity facets, Stress Tolerance and Joyous Exploration were most strongly associated with creativity; four latent curiosity profiles differed in divergent thinking, creative behaviour and achievement — [Liu et al. 2026, Acta Psychologica](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.106593)
- Bazhydai et al. (2026): 163 British 10–11-year-olds; the 53% who reported a creative activity scored higher on all curiosity and creativity measures; self-reported creativity and curiosity were highly correlated — [Bazhydai et al. 2026, Acta Psychologica](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.107140)
- Chen et al. (2025): 732 fitness coaches; curiosity predicted creativity via knowledge sharing and flow experience (PLS-SEM) — [Chen et al. 2025, PeerJ](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.19684)

### Inferences
- The best-supported steering quantity for *what* humans choose to explore is learning progress (improvement rate), with competence as a separate, instruction-sensitive bias; novelty and uncertainty alone fit less well. This dovetails with reinforcement-learning accounts (Murayama; Abir et al.) in which satisfying answers reinforce curiosity for semantically related material, giving curiosity a path-dependent, topic-clustering character.
- The curiosity-memory benefit is best treated as a focal, reward-tagged encoding boost for the target and simple incidental stimuli, not a general "open encoding window": complex unrelated facts near a high-curiosity target are encoded worse (Keller 2024), and children's curiosity benefits did not pass through hippocampus/accumbens (Ziv 2026).
- The "dancer" forward-flow dimension suggests that creativity-relevant exploration is orthogonal to the hunter/busybody tightness axis; what matters for idea generation may be semantic leap distance rather than network density. Stress tolerance repeatedly moderates curiosity-exploration and curiosity-creativity links, implying affective tolerance for uncertainty gates exploratory breadth.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2023–2026 paper was located for the Gruber-lab 7T fMRI finding (curiosity activates VTA and subiculum; VTA–hippocampus interactions predict memory); it was found only as a talk announcement.
- No causal study was found showing that inducing curiosity improves subsequent divergent-thinking or insight performance; curiosity-creativity links located are correlational or self-report.
- Sample sizes for Keller et al. 2024, Cen et al. 2024, Rüterbories 2024 and Recht & Yeung 2026 were not in the abstracts and full texts could not be fetched (publisher auth redirects).
- No 2023–2026 paper by Celeste Kidd or by Jirout surfaced in database queries.

## Key Question 2: Emotion as information and as a steering signal — affect-as-information, somatic markers, constructed emotion/affective realism, and valence/arousal effects on breadth vs depth of thought

### Takeaway
The modern view has moved from "positive mood broadens, negative narrows" to conditional models: affect acts as feedback that validates or invalidates whatever processing style is currently dominant (Huntsinger, Isbell & Clore), and breadth depends on the interaction of valence with arousal/motivational intensity rather than valence alone. The 2023–2026 evidence is mixed: a naturalistic study finds positive affect broadens conceptual breadth only under low arousal; a 2025/2026 registered replication failed to find the classic motivational-intensity narrowing effect; and experience-sampling work shows creativity peaks when negative mood is *declining* and self-motivation is high. Barrett's constructed-emotion/allostasis program now has 7T anatomical support for a large-scale allostatic-interoceptive system.

### Cited Findings

Foundational (pre-2023, for context):
- Affect-as-information: Schwarz & Clore (1983) showed mood is used as information in judgments unless its source is made salient — [Schwarz & Clore 1983, JPSP (DOI from recall)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.45.3.513); Clore & Huntsinger (2007) "How emotions inform judgment and regulate thought" generalizes this to the regulation of processing style — [Clore & Huntsinger 2007, Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2007.08.005)
- Huntsinger, Isbell & Clore (2014) "The affective control of thought: malleable, not fixed": affect does not fix a processing style; positive affect confers value on whatever cognitive orientation is currently accessible (so it can narrow as well as broaden), negative affect reduces it — [Huntsinger et al. 2014, Psychological Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037669)
- Mood-as-input: Martin et al. (1993) showed mood effects on persistence depend on the stop rule ("have I done enough?" vs "am I enjoying this?") — [Martin et al. 1993, JPSP (DOI from recall)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.64.3.317)
- De Dreu, Baas & Nijstad (2008) dual-pathway model: activating moods raise creativity, positive via flexibility and negative via persistence — [De Dreu et al. 2008, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.5.739)
- Somatic-marker-adjacent evidence: Dunn et al. (2010) found that interoceptive accuracy (heartbeat perception) moderated how bodily signals guided intuitive decisions on a gambling task — [Dunn et al. 2010, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610389191)
- Barrett (2017) theory of constructed emotion as active inference over interoception and categorization — [Barrett 2017, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsw154); Barrett & Simmons (2015) "Interoceptive predictions in the brain" — [Nature Reviews Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3950); Kleckner et al. - Affective realism: Siegel et al. (2018) "Seeing what you feel": unseen affective faces changed perception of structurally neutral faces — [Siegel et al. 2018, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617741718)

Valence x arousal and breadth (2022–2026):
- Naturally occurring affect study (Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, Nov 2022; authors not captured): three conceptual-breadth tasks loaded on one latent factor predicted by the valence x arousal interaction; in the low-arousal group, more positive affect went with greater conceptual breadth, in the high-arousal group the relation reversed; authors attribute broadening to relaxed cognitive control under low arousal — [Cognitive Research 2022](https://cognitiveresearchjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41235-022-00447-w)
- Goodhew et al. (2026; epub Aug 2025) registered conceptual replication of Gable & Harmon-Jones (2008): dessert pictures were rated more positive and higher in motivational intensity than rocks, but attentional breadth did not differ by image type, perceptual focality, or participants' valence/motivational-intensity ratings — [Goodhew et al. 2026, Cognition & Emotion 40(5):1257–1284](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2025.2549318)
- Yue et al. (2026): high approach-motivated positive emotion increased observation-inflation false memories more than low-intensity positive emotion; imitation-inhibition training (reducing motor simulation) reduced the effect — [Yue et al. 2026, Consciousness and Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2026.104076)
- Teunisse et al. (2025): pupil size was larger when attending the global than the local level of Navon stimuli (possible effort confound) — [Teunisse et al. 2025, Perception](https://doi.org/10.1177/03010066251345778)

Mood and idea generation (2023–2026):
- Bledow et al. (2024): 210 employees reported mood three times daily over five workdays; creativity (evening idea generation) was highest when negative mood *declined* over the day and midday self-motivation was high; action-state orientation moderated mood-creativity links — [Bledow et al. 2024, Journal of Personality](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.12913)
- Rosa et al. (2023): 75 students randomly assigned to pleasant/unpleasant/neutral induction; negative affect increased *conventional* thinking on the TCT-DP drawing task; unconventional thinking did not differ — [Rosa et al. 2023, Int J Psychological Research](https://doi.org/10.21500/20112084.6497)
- Deen et al. (2026): 90 participants; positive and negative Indian ragas both raised AUT and TTCT-Figural creativity vs controls, with a larger effect for positive music, and shifted emotional word choices in the primed direction — [Deen et al. 2026, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1723673)
- Avsec & Kocjan (2025 preprint): experience sampling with 143 students (4,183 prompts); momentary self-perceived creativity predicted better affect, buffering negative valence most during the least pleasant activities (lectures, errands) — [Avsec & Kocjan 2025, Research Square](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7713761/v1)
- Bellaiche et al. (2025): N=143 generated emotion-regulation ideas (7,854 techniques coded into 14 strategies); higher divergent-thinking originality predicted more prolific generation across strategies; depressive symptoms predicted narrower deviation from habitual strategies — [Bellaiche et al. 2025, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-32365-4)
- Rini et al. (2026): 344 children aged 8–11; trait-EI Adaptability and Self-Motivation predicted divergent thinking, especially on abstract tasks — [Rini et al. 2026, Behavioral Sciences](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16061016)
- Cheng & Huang (2026): 92 undergraduates using GPT-4 for creative problem solving; social interaction (not perceived cognitive support) predicted positive affect, which linked to convergent thinking — [Cheng & Huang 2026, Journal of Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence14070151)

Constructed emotion and allostasis (2023–2026):
- Zhang et al. (2025): 7T fMRI in 90 participants replicated and extended the allostatic-interoceptive system, refining subgenual/pregenual ACC and brainstem nuclei; functional connectivity corroborated >96% of anatomical connections from non-human tract tracing — [Zhang et al. 2025, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02087-x)
- Theriault, Barrett et al. (2025) "It's not the thought that counts: Allostasis at the core of brain function" argues the brain's core function is predictive regulation of bodily demands, with a distributed allostatic system organizing whole-brain signalling — [Theriault et al. 2025, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.09.028)
- Barrett et al. (2025) reply that constructed-emotion theory and basic-emotion theory are fundamentally incompatible, rejecting a proposed reconciliation — [Barrett et al. 2025, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916251319045)
- Westlin et al. (2026): across three studies (N=453), very few stimulus-evoked experiences met validity benchmarks for a single emotion label; participants often used multiple labels, so group-averaged norms mask individual variation — [Westlin et al. 2026, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001554); a reanalysis failed to reproduce brain-pattern mappings for 34 emotion categories and found large individual variation — [Westlin et al. 2025, PsyArXiv](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/hbg6k)
- Hoemann et al. (2024): Hadza emotion narratives emphasized action, bodily sensations and environment, vs North Carolina narratives emphasizing internal mental states — [Hoemann et al. 2024, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916231178555)
- Vik et al. (2026): preregistered N=992 emBODY mapping; political emotions showed distinct bodily maps from their non-political counterparts; political participation tracked embodiment strength more than felt intensity — [Vik et al. 2026, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2534895123)

### Inferences
- For idea generation, affect appears to act less as a fixed "broadener" and more as a precision/validation signal: positive affect endorses the currently dominant search mode (so it broadens only when a broad mode is already active or when arousal is low enough to relax control), and declining negative affect plus high motivation may be the most productive everyday state (Bledow 2024). This matches Huntsinger's "affect as cognitive feedback" better than Fredrickson-style broaden-and-build.
- The motivational-intensity account of attentional narrowing (approach-motivated positive affect narrows) is now in replication trouble (Goodhew 2026), so claims that high-approach positive moods narrow idea search should be treated as unsettled.
- Barrett's program implies that "emotion as information" is itself an inference over interoceptive predictions, so affective steering of thought and interoceptive steering (Key Question 3) are the same mechanism viewed at different levels.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study on "affective forecasting during idea generation" was found.
- No recent direct test of affect-as-information manipulations (source-attribution discounting) in creativity tasks was found.
- Authors of the 2022 Cognitive Research valence x arousal paper were not captured; sample size unknown.
- No 2023–2026 somatic-marker update paper located; the Dunn 2010 finding has not been checked for replication here.

## Key Question 3: Interoception — how heartbeat, breathing and gut signals influence cognition, decision making and intuition; cardiac-cycle and breathing-phase effects (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Interoceptive rhythms are now treated as gain-control signals that rhythmically re-weight the precision of internal versus external evidence (Engelen/Tallon-Baudry; Allen; Skora). The best 2024–2026 human evidence shows breathing phase shifts speed-accuracy trade-offs and affective decision biases (inspiration lowers decision boundaries and biases faces toward "happy"), raises near-threshold visual sensitivity, and that people entrain breathing to predictable stimuli; cardiac phase modulates ERP amplitudes and emotional face memory but produces small or absent reaction-time effects. No study directly tested cardiac or respiratory phase against creativity or insight.

### Inferences
- The consistent 2024–2026 pattern is that inspiration is a high-gain, action-ready phase (faster responses, lower decision thresholds, higher visual sensitivity, positive affective bias) whereas expiration/late diastole favours accuracy and encoding. For idea generation this predicts phase-dependent alternation between generative/impulsive and evaluative/encoding modes on a 3–5 s timescale, but this has not been tested.
- Cardiac-phase effects on cognition are robust at the neural level (ERP amplitude, beta suppression) but behaviourally small and task-dependent, so claims that heartbeat timing shapes everyday decisions should be modest.
- The right anterior insula result (Mancano & Papagno 2026) is the first causal link between interoceptive processing and the affective/social content of abstract concepts, supporting Barrett/Borghi-style claims that abstract thought is partly grounded in bodily state.

## Key Question 4: Embodied cognition — gesture and thought, motor simulation, body movement and idea generation, replication status

### Takeaway
Gesture is the best-supported embodied route into thinking: meta-analytic comprehension benefits are moderate, producing beats observing, and 2017–2024 experiments show encouraged iconic gestures raise alternate-uses fluency and originality in children and adults, though effects are condition-dependent and spontaneous gestures can even reduce flexibility. By contrast, the classic "motor system is constitutive of language meaning" and "body posture changes the mind" findings have largely failed large pre-registered replications (ACE: 18 labs, effect ~0; M1 rTMS: BF01>30; power-pose hormones: null; elderly priming: experimenter-expectancy). Movement effects on divergent thinking appear to work through release from restriction and widened attention rather than specific metaphoric body states.

### Cited Findings

Gesture theory (foundational):
- Hostetter & Alibali (2008) Gesture-as-Simulated-Action: gestures reflect motor activity that arises automatically when people simulate actions and perceptual states — [Hostetter & Alibali 2008, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review](https://doi.org/10.3758/PBR.15.3.495); their 2019 revisit concludes the available evidence largely supports the framework's six main predictions, while noting challenges — [Hostetter & Alibali 2019, PB&R](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-018-1548-0)
- Goldin-Meadow & Beilock (2010): gesture affects thought by virtue of reflecting real-world actions and carrying perceptual-motor detail absent from speech — [Goldin-Meadow & Beilock 2010, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691610388764)
- Novack & Goldin-Meadow (2017): gestures are abstracted representational actions, not actions tied to specific objects, which supports generalization and transfer — [Novack & Goldin-Meadow 2017, PB&R](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1145-z)
- Dargue, Sweller & Jones (2019) meta-analysis of 83 independent samples: gesture had a moderate beneficial effect on comprehension; producing gestures yielded significantly larger effects than observing — [Dargue et al. 2019, Psychological Bulletin 145(8):765–784](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000202)
- Goldin-Meadow's 2023 book *Thinking with Your Hands* summarizes the program; a review notes GSA theory struggles to explain gestures for shapes, conceptual ideas, or object motion the body does not perform — [Undark review](https://undark.org/2023/06/23/book-review-how-our-hands-reveal-our-thoughts/)

- Kısa, Goldin-Meadow & Casasanto (2024): speakers gestured more during disfluent speech only when listeners could see them, supporting a pragmatic (communicative) rather than lexical-retrieval account of disfluency gestures — [Kısa et al. 2024, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2024.105855)

### Inferences
- The surviving embodiment effects are those in which the body does representational work the thinker can see or feel (gesture as a self-generated external representation; freedom of movement widening attention). Effects that depend on covert metaphoric resonance (posture = power, warmth = social warmth, M1 = meaning) have not survived scrutiny.
- Gesture's contribution to idea generation is plausibly a working-memory/offloading and imagery-packaging effect ("gesture-for-conceptualization") rather than literal motor simulation, consistent with the finding that encouraged (deliberate) gestures help while spontaneous iconic gestures can lock in a single image category.
- For HTSA-type modelling, the robust lesson is "externalize partial representations and keep the search unrestricted" rather than "simulate specific bodily metaphors."

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 direct replications of Slepian & Ambady (2012) or Leung et al. (2012) were found; their replication status is unknown from this search.
- Kirk & Lewis (2017) sample sizes and effect sizes were not available (abstract only).
- No gesture-restriction condition exists in the adult AUT literature located (Hyusein & Göksun note this).
- Körner et al. (2022) Psychological Bulletin DOI could not be verified in Europe PMC.

## Key Question 5: Conceptual metaphor and grounding — Lakoff & Johnson as revised by Barsalou, Pulvermüller, Casasanto; 2023–2026 neuroimaging on grounding of abstract concepts; role of emotion

### Takeaway
The field has moved from "abstract concepts are metaphorically mapped onto sensorimotor domains" to multiple-representation accounts (Kiefer 2026; Borghi; Pulvermüller) in which abstract concepts draw differentially on emotion/interoception, social-interaction and mentalizing systems, language, and amodal hubs depending on content. Emotion as a grounding dimension for abstract words (Kousta/Vigliocco) now has causal support (right anterior insula rTMS affects both heartbeat perception and emotional/social abstract concepts), while hard "simulation" claims for action language are in replication trouble. Casasanto's body-specificity work survives in new large preregistered forms (perceptual asymmetries reverse in left-handers; abstract construal differs across cultures), but masked-priming work suggests metaphor-consistent mappings require conscious processing.

- Diveica et al. (2023): socialness ratings for 8,388 English words, distinct from concreteness and valence, explain unique variance in lexical performance — [Behavior Research Methods](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-022-01810-x)

2024–2026 empirical neuro/behavioural evidence:
- Mancano & Papagno (2026): rTMS over right anterior insula (N=25, 19 female) impaired heartbeat counting and altered similarity judgements on the emotional and social dimensions of abstract concepts; electric-field analyses converged on right anterior insula — [J Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.0238-25.2025)
- Grisoni, Pulvermüller et al. (2024): 128-channel EEG; prediction potentials before predictable animal words arose in posterior visual sources and before tool words in frontocentral sensorimotor sources, in both comprehension and production, scaling with cloze probability — [J Neurosci 44(12)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1723-23.2023)
- Solana et al. (2024): M1 rTMS produced BF01>30 against a role in construing action-sentence meaning (see KQ4) — [Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2024.108832)
- de Zubicaray et al. (2026): event-related fMRI (N=21) auditory lexical decision on form-typical concrete/abstract words: left perisylvian regions including amodal hubs also responded to nonwords carrying concreteness-typical sound features, implying statistical form-meaning knowledge is part of how concreteness is represented — [Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2026.06.013)

- Robinson et al. (2026): four experiments (N=625); thinking about relationship-oriented activities shifted perceived self-location downward toward the heart, and attending to the heart made more people rate activities as relational — [QJEP](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218261468338)
- Nadler et al. (2025): colorblind and colorseeing adults gave highly similar, replicable colour-metaphor associations, GPT diverged and was often incoherent, and painters used embodied reasoning most for novel colour metaphors — [Nadler et al. 2025, Cognitive Science 49(7)](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.70083)
- Morgan & Casasanto (2025): two large preregistered online studies; the right-hander asymmetry in visual spatial-frequency processing was reduced in left-handers and fully reversed for high-frequency stimuli between strong right- and left-handers; dichotic listening suggested language laterality does not explain it ("action asymmetry hypothesis") — [JEP: General 154(9):2482–2502](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001806)
- Singh & Casasanto (2026): three studies (N=1,797; six national samples) found Chinese participants described events more abstractly than U.S. participants; a context-sensitive subculture (n=965) construed more abstractly; Chinese participants solved analogical-reasoning problems correctly more often (N=677) — [Topics in Cognitive Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.70061)
- Yao et al. (2025): socialness x valence effects in lexical decision and emotional Stroop; negative high-socialness words were faster in both tasks — [Behavioral Sciences](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs15020190); Muraki et al. - Dou et al. (2026): corpus analysis of 35,100 images; colour-based semantic spaces re-represented abstract concepts better than emotion-based spaces, with brightness-valence congruency explaining much overlap — [Cognitive Processing](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10339-025-01310-8)

### Inferences
- The contemporary consensus is a hybrid: abstract concepts are grounded, but the grounding is heterogeneous (interoceptive/affective for emotion-laden abstractions, social for socially negotiated ones, sensorimotor for action-laden ones, linguistic/distributional for all), which explains why single-system imaging contrasts and motor-interference paradigms give inconsistent results.
- Emotion/interoception is the best-supported grounding substrate for *abstract* concepts specifically, so affective state is likely to modulate access to abstract concepts during ideation more than it modulates concrete ones; this is a testable link between KQ2/KQ3 and idea generation.
- Metaphor mappings appear to operate at the level of conscious, language-encoded conceptual structure rather than automatic sensorimotor resonance (Saponaro 2026), which favours Lakoff-Johnson-style structural mapping over strong simulation.

- No 2023–2026 fMRI study directly contrasting emotion-region vs sensorimotor-region recruitment for abstract vs concrete words (the Vigliocco 2014 design) was found; Kiefer's group's empirical papers (Harpaintner et al.) were not retrieved.
- Banks & Connell (2023) multidimensional grounding paper and Fernandino et al. experiential-feature decoding were not retrieved.
- No 2023–2026 Barsalou empirical paper surfaced; only an older grounded-cognition citation.
- Sample sizes were missing for Saponaro 2026, Yao 2025, Muraki 2026.

## Key Question 6: Flow and absorption — 2023–2026 neural basis and relation to idea generation

### Takeaway
The 2024 Rosen/Oh/Kounios jazz EEG study supports an "expertise-plus-release" model: in 32 guitarists, high-flow improvisations showed transient hypofrontality (reduced superior frontal gyrus activity) across all players, while only experienced players showed reduced posterior default-mode activity and enhanced left auditory/somatosensory activity, favouring optimized domain-specific processing over DMN-driven ideation. Newer EEG work frames flow as high-dimensional metastable network dynamics with reduced microstate C/E, and a 2024 preprint with >700 sessions found no frontal-midline theta signature. Behaviourally, a 2026 study of 400 participants shows the two components of flow pull in opposite directions for creativity: smooth performance raises idea count but lowers originality, whereas absorption/immersion raises originality and semantic distance.

### Cited Findings

Foundational (pre-2023):
- Dietrich (2004) proposed transient hypofrontality as the neurocognitive mechanism of flow — [Dietrich 2004, Consciousness and Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2004.07.002)
- Ulrich et al. (2014): experimentally induced flow (fMRI) — [Ulrich et al. 2014, NeuroImage](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.08.019)
- van der Linden, Tops & Bakker (2021) argue the locus coeruleus-norepinephrine system (intermediate tonic, high phasic activity) underlies flow — [van der Linden et al. 2021, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.645498)

Rosen, Oh, Chesebrough, Zhang & Kounios (2024):
- Design: 32 jazz guitarists (high vs low experience) improvised to six lead sheets with programmed accompaniment, yielding 192 takes; flow rated after each take with the Core Flow State Scale; four jazz experts rated creativity; high-density EEG with SPM12 source reconstruction. Two hypotheses contrasted: (A) flow = experience-optimized domain-specific processing with minimal DMN interference vs (B) flow = DMN ideation under fronto-parietal supervision — [Rosen et al. 2024, Neuropsychologia 196:108824](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2024.108824); [Drexel release](https://drexel.edu/news/archive/2024/march/new-neuroimaging-study-reveals-how-the-brain-achieves-a-creative-flow-state)
- Results: across all musicians, high-flow takes showed transient hypofrontality (decreased superior frontal gyri) and increased left-hemisphere auditory and somatosensory activity; high-experience musicians showed reduced posterior DMN node activity and greater auditory/visual activity during flow; low-experience musicians showed no flow-related DMN or FPCN modulation and only right-hemisphere deactivations. Conclusion: creative flow reflects optimized domain-specific processing from extensive practice paired with reduced cognitive control ("expertise-plus-release"); Kounios' practical summary: build expertise through practice, then "try letting go" — [Drexel release](https://drexel.edu/news/archive/2024/march/new-neuroimaging-study-reveals-how-the-brain-achieves-a-creative-flow-state); [PsyPost coverage](https://www.psypost.org/scientists-team-up-with-jazz-musicians-to-reveal-the-neuroscience-of-creative-flow/)
- Caveat noted in coverage: single task (jazz) and EEG spatial resolution limit generalization — [Mensa reprint](https://www.mensa.org/?p=7844)

2024–2026 EEG studies of flow:
- Khoshnoud et al. (2026): 43 participants playing Thumper for 25 min; flow (vs boredom, frustration) showed reduced explained variance, duration, coverage and occurrence of microstate E and reduced duration/coverage of microstate C — [Brain Topography](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10548-026-01251-1)
- Eldaly et al. (2026): 64-channel EEG; flow showed the highest global functional connectivity, metastability and dimensionality of dynamic connectivity vs boredom and anxiety; authors propose a "Global Dynamic Flow Model" — [NeuroImage](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.122049)
- Lin et al. (2025): individualized game difficulty; flow showed higher theta, moderate alpha, lower beta; lasso regression predicted flow self-reports from EEG at r=0.571 — [Cognitive Neurodynamics](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11571-024-10205-x)
- Sugino et al. (2024 preprint): 7 participants, >100 trials each over 10 days (>700 sessions) of competitive video games with rank-matched difficulty; frontal-midline theta was not correlated with flow in six of seven participants — [bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.07.11.603158)
- Hang et al. (2024): 28 participants, six tasks, single-channel prefrontal EEG; flow correlated with delta, gamma and theta, peaking ~2 min after onset; max R²=0.163 (0.279 in preprint) — [Sensors](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24061894)
- Beiramvand et al. (2025): wearable-headband flow detection in Tetris, 93% accuracy with random sampling but 82% leave-one-subject-out — [IEEE EMBC](https://doi.org/10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11251885)

Flow and creative thinking (behavioural):
- Koivisto et al. (2026): N=400; the AUT produced only partial "microflow"; the smooth-performance component increased idea count but lowered creativity, especially when conventional associations had to be inhibited; the immersion/absorption component was linked to higher creativity and semantic distance; Forward Flow predicted originality independently of flow — [Koivisto et al. 2026, Consciousness and Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2026.104060)
- Chen et al. (2025): among 732 fitness coaches, flow experience mediated curiosity-to-creativity (survey, PLS-SEM) — [PeerJ](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.19684)
- Daikoku (2026) theoretical: creativity depends on tuning uncertainty, with bodily sensation and interpersonal synchrony as stabilizers — [Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1752280)

### Inferences
- Flow supports idea generation only in its absorption component and only when expertise allows automatic execution; the "smooth" component trades originality for quantity. This parallels the KQ1 finding that creativity-relevant exploration (forward flow) is orthogonal to tight, efficient search.
- The jazz data argue against treating flow as a DMN-driven generative state: for experts, generation during flow is carried by domain-specific sensory/motor cortex with DMN and frontal control dialled down, consistent with the prior report's "control policy over memory search" framing but with the knob turned toward release rather than toward DMN engagement.
- Neural markers of flow remain inconsistent across paradigms (microstates, metastability, theta/alpha/beta, no FM-theta), so no single EEG signature should be assumed.

### Gaps
- Rosen et al. 2024 effect sizes and the experience criterion were not available (abstract plus press release only).
- No 2023–2026 study linked flow physiology (LC-NE, pupil) to creativity outcomes directly.
- No 2023–2026 fMRI study of flow during creative production was found; all new work is EEG in video-game paradigms.
