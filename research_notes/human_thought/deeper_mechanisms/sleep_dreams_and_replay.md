## Key Question 1: What is dreaming for? Competing theories (Hoel's overfitted brain, NEXTUP, protoconsciousness, threat/social simulation, Siclari's local-sleep work) and what 2023–2026 evidence discriminates between them

### Takeaway
No 2023–2026 study directly adjudicates between the major dream-function theories; the field's own 2024 commentary (Dresler target article; Revonsuo/Valli/Tuominen reply) concedes that progress requires risky, mutually exclusive predictions that have mostly not yet been run. The best-supported empirical regularities are narrower: dreams draw on recent memories in a stage- and time-of-night-dependent way, dream experience correlates with posterior cortical activation and local reductions in slow waves, and cueing REM dreams about a problem is possible and can be linked to next-morning solving in responders.

### Cited Findings
- Hoel's overfitted brain hypothesis (OBH, Patterns 2021) holds that daily learning overfits the brain to statistically narrow experience and that dreams supply corrupted, sparse, out-of-distribution top-down input "for increasing generalizability"; dream phenomenology (sparseness, hallucination, narrative) is argued to be functional rather than epiphenomenal — [Hoel 2021, Patterns (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)
- OBH's stated testable predictions: sleep/dream deprivation should impair generalization more than memorization (proposed in mouse context-fear generalization); overtraining on an undersampled task should induce overfitting that dreaming reduces; "dream substitutions" (dream-like VR/video) might offset some sleep-deprivation effects; in silico, cycling between real and top-down-generated corrupted inputs should delay overfitting — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)
- OBH's argument against consolidation-as-dream-function: under 1–2% of dream reports relate to specific episodic memories, dreams rarely repeat events literally, and replay-like neural patterns in sleep are "mostly never-before-seen sequences" — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)
- Hoel's paper explicitly notes that no animal study has separated memorization from generalization under dream deprivation, so the core prediction remained untested at publication — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940); press coverage adds that Hoel said neuroimaging methods are "not nearly as good as they really need to be" to discriminate the hypotheses — [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/ai-research-suggests-weird-dreams-might-help-our-brains-keep-reality-in-check/amp)
- Searches for a 2024–2025 experimental test of OBH returned only the original 2021 proposal and its coverage; no completed deprivation-vs-generalization experiment was found — [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/?p=47065); [Freethink](https://freethink.com/culture/weird-dreams-ai)
- Indirect evidence on sleep and generalization is mixed: a fear-conditioning study found no effect of sleep deprivation on explicit or implicit threat memory but a widened implicit generalization gradient when deprivation fell on the first post-conditioning night; a review of sleep and defensive-response generalization describes results as "mixed and inconsistent"; another line reports that staying awake increased veridical memories but reduced generalization while sleep did the opposite — [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2022 (deprivation/generalization)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.902925/pdf); [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2022 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.1106577/full)
- NEXTUP (Network Exploration To Understand Possibilities; Zadra & Stickgold, When Brains Dream, 2021) frames dreaming as "a form of sleep-dependent memory processing" that discovers connections overlooked in waking life by exploring weak associations and "what-if" scenarios, tied to reduced norepinephrine and increased associative-cortex activity in REM — [New Books Network summary](https://newbooksnetwork.com/when-brains-dream); [IJoDR review](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/download/80335/74446/218941)
- A critique in the International Journal of Dream Research notes NEXTUP is designed to work even for the roughly 95% of dreams never remembered, and calls this feature "not testable" — [IJoDR review](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/download/80335/74446/218941) (the specific IJoDR item was identified from a search summary and not opened)
- The 2024 theoretical exchange: Dresler's target article and the reply by the Turku group argue that dream science must move toward "opposing, competing and mutually exclusive theories" that are "risky, clearly formulated, and empirically testable"; the reply maintains Threat Simulation Theory and Social Simulation Theory as the strongest contenders — [Revonsuo/Valli/Tuominen reply, University of Turku listing](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/3069456?lang=en_US)
- Threat Simulation Theory faces a specificity problem: Zadra's Montreal group found chase/pursuit among the three most prevalent dream themes, but many of the most prevalent themes were positive (sex, flying), which "challenge the specificity of the threat simulation theory" — [Behavioral and Brain Sciences commentary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/prevalence-of-typical-dream-themes-challenges-the-specificity-of-the-threat-simulation-theory/4E21FF951D3E0028B5A218E34CCF8C80); Hoel's OBH likewise argues threat content is rare and dream "plans" rarely transfer to waking behavior — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)
- Hoel contrasts OBH with Hobson & Friston's predictive-processing/protoconsciousness view: that framework predicts dreams should become less surprising across development, whereas dreams become more complex; OBH claims to avoid that prediction — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)
- A 2024 clinical review (Scarpelli, Alfonsi & De Gennaro, Current Opinion in Pulmonary Medicine) concludes dreams "likely serve several functions", from reflecting waking experience to simulating responses to threats, and that COVID-era dream data suggest emotion regulation and social/threat simulation — [Scarpelli et al. 2024](https://unicamillus.iris.cineca.it/bitstream/20.500.14245/5777/1/exploring_the_role_of_dreams__insights_from_recent.190.pdf)
- Blagrove (The Psychologist, 2024) summarizes the main hypotheses and adds a group-bonding/empathy function of sharing dreams — [BPS Psychologist 2024](https://bps.org.uk/psychologist/does-dreaming-have-function)
- Siclari lab neural correlates (foundational, 2018): in 14 adults with high-density EEG and serial awakenings, dreaming in both REM and NREM was associated with local decreases in slow-wave activity in posterior regions; NREM dreaming tracked sparse, small, shallow slow waves centrally/posteriorly, while large steep frontal slow waves linked to micro-arousals preceded successful recall of content — [Siclari et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6199409)
- Siclari lab 2024 (Cataldi, Stephan, Haba-Rubio & Siclari, Nature Communications): in parasomnia episodes recorded with hd-EEG, reports of conscious experience (56% of episodes) were preceded by high-amplitude anterior slow waves plus posterior activation, mirroring dream correlates; recall of content (56%) vs no recall (25%) was associated with higher right medial temporal activation before movement onset — [Cataldi et al. 2024, Nat Commun](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v15y2024i1d10.1038_s41467-024-48337-7.html)
- A 2020 methodological critique argues serial-awakening designs can only establish correlates of dream recall, not of dreaming, because "no recall" mixes dreamful and dreamless sleep — [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2020 commentary](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2020.585470/pdf)
- Dream recall determinants (Elce, Bergamo, Bontempi, Pedreschi, Bellesi, Handjaras & Bernardi, Communications Psychology 2025; N = 217 adults aged 18–70, 116 female, data 2020–2024, >2,900 recordings over 15 days per person): probability of reporting a dream on waking was predicted by positive attitude toward dreams, proneness to mind wandering, and sleep patterns; likelihood of recalling content was predicted by age and vulnerability to interference; recall fluctuated with night-by-night sleep changes and season — [AlphaGalileo press release](https://www.alphagalileo.org/en-gb/Item-Display/ItemId/257739); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/what-makes-us-more-likely-remember-dreams) (the digest gives N = 204; the abstract gives 217)
- Memory sources of dreams (Picard-Deland, Konkoly, Raider, Paller, Nielsen, Pigeon & Carr, Sleep 2023; 20 participants, ~12 awakenings each, 164 reports): memory sources identified in 87.2% of dreams, ~1.8 sources per dream; recent past 55.5%, day residues alone 39.6%, distant past 29.9%, semantic 31.1%, future 6.7%; ~49% of dreams combined multiple sources; lab incorporation 34.2%, more in REM (46.9%) than NREM (28.7%); N1 and REM dreams contained more sources and more recent episodic memories than N2/N3; proportion of recent sources fell across the night independent of stage — [Picard-Deland et al. 2023, Sleep (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091095)
- The same authors propose a sequential model: salient experiences are tagged and protected from downscaling in NREM, integrated through "hyperassociative" processing in REM dreams, then remote memories surfaced in REM get re-tagged and reprocessed in later NREM; they state their interpretation "rest[s] on the assumption that memory incorporation in dreaming is directly related to underlying memory consolidation" — [Picard-Deland et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091095)
- A 2023 review (Picard-Deland, Bernardi, Genzel, Dresler & Schoch) on dreams and replay reports that NREM dream incorporations have been associated with declarative memory performance and REM incorporations with procedural performance, but cautions that incorporation rates are often low and not all studies find an effect — [Dream-replay review preprint (IMT Lucca)](https://iris.imtlucca.it/bitstream/20.500.11771/22918/3/ms_DreamReplayReview_06_03_23_vpreprint.pdf)
- Schoch et al. 2018 (22 participants, word-picture task): task incorporation into NREM but not REM dreams positively predicted next-morning memory; incorporations were only detected with night-time awakenings, not morning reports; repeated awakenings impaired sleep quality but did not impair ongoing consolidation — [Schoch et al. bioRxiv/JSR](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/323667.full.pdf)
- Direct manipulation of REM dream content is now possible (Konkoly et al., Neuroscience of Consciousness 2026): cueing unsolved puzzles' soundtracks during REM "reliably increased dreaming about the associated puzzles" and, post hoc, improved next-morning solving in participants whose cue-related dreaming increased (details in Key Question 6) — [Konkoly et al. 2026](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The theories differ most sharply on whether dream content is functional. OBH and NEXTUP both predict that bizarre or weakly associated content is the point (noise for generalization; exploration of weak links), whereas consolidation-based views treat content as a byproduct. Konkoly 2026 is the first design that can in principle separate dreaming from mere reactivation (by comparing responders vs non-responders to the same cues), and its post-hoc responder effect is weak evidence for content-is-functional accounts.
- The Picard-Deland 2023 memory-source data (half of dreams combine multiple sources; REM and N1 carry the most sources) are compatible with both NEXTUP's "network exploration" and OBH's "corrupted input" framing, so they do not discriminate.
- The social/threat simulation theories remain difficult to falsify with content analysis alone; the 2024 Dresler exchange signals that the field itself views the current evidence as non-discriminating.

### Gaps
- No completed empirical test of Hoel's OBH (dream deprivation vs generalization; dream-substitution) was found for 2023–2026.
- No experimental test of NEXTUP's distinctive predictions was found; the evidence base is the book's synthesis.
- Could not retrieve the full text of the 2024 Dresler target article/replies (University of Turku page blocked), the Scarpelli 2024 review, or a 2025 Siclari paper on dream-recall predictors (none surfaced; the Bernardi 2025 paper is the relevant 2025 recall study).
- Hobson's protoconsciousness theory: only Hoel's critique was retrieved; no 2023–2026 primary tests were found.

## Key Question 2: Human replay content (MEG/fMRI/intracranial): does the brain replay never-experienced sequences, recombine fragments, compress/abstract? What is "generative replay"?

### Takeaway
Human MEG work from the Behrens/Dolan/Liu line shows replay that follows abstract learned structure rather than experienced order (2019), prioritizes non-local, never-directly-traversed paths in proportion to their utility (2021), and, in the 2023 "generative replay" study, assembles elements into compound hypotheses whose content converges from uncertain to correct configurations within a trial. Replay therefore recombines and abstracts rather than literally recapitulating, though the 2023 study found no replay-performance correlation and a 2025 eLife paper warns that TDLM replay detection in resting state is methodologically fragile.

### Cited Findings
- Foundational (2019, Cell): Liu, Dolan, Kurth-Nelson & Behrens trained participants on an ordering rule, then showed novel objects in scrambled order; during rest, MEG replay "followed instead a sequence implied by learned abstract knowledge" rather than visual experience; replay was time-compressed and reversed direction after reward; a factorized code (sequence position x identity) preceded the sensory code by ~50 ms, argued to support generalization of structure to new objects — [Liu et al. 2019, Cell (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6657653); [Cosyne 2019 abstract](https://users.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/~behrens/Cosyne2019/Liu_Cosyne19.pdf)
- Liu, Mattar, Behrens, Daw & Dolan (Science 2021; 29 adults analysed, 17 female): in a model-based RL task where end states were shared across three starting arms (so reward could propagate to untraversed paths), MEG at reward receipt showed two replay regimes: forward at 20–30 ms lag (local path, ripple-band power) and backward at 130–170 ms lag (peak ~160 ms). The 160 ms backward replay represented non-local paths more than local ones (p = 0.03); non-local learning rates were higher on trials with significant 160 ms replay (0.70 vs 0.61, p = 0.023); backward replay was stronger for high-utility (need x gain) non-local paths (p = 0.003); across participants, stronger 160 ms replay correlated with reward per trial (r = 0.41, p = 0.03); participants chose paths to previously rewarded end states even from a different starting arm (p = 9.5e-23) — [Liu et al. 2021, Science (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7610948)
- "Generative replay" (Schwartenbeck, Baram, Liu, Mark, Muller, Dolan, Botvinick, Kurth-Nelson & Behrens, Cell 2023; fMRI N = 30, MEG N = 20 with 19 analysed): participants trained 2 days to construct silhouettes from building blocks (9 blocks in fMRI; 4 textured blocks in MEG, one "stable" block present in every silhouette, two "present" blocks to be inferred, one "absent"). fMRI: representations of novel constructed objects in frontal cortex and hippocampus were relational and compositional. MEG with TDLM (classifiers peak decoding at 200 ms): little sequenceness from the stable block but strong sequenceness from present blocks at lags 30–200 ms (peaks ~60 and ~170 ms); present-vs-stable difference cluster 260–1,660 ms (cluster-corrected p < 0.05); length-3 sequences ending at the stable block significant ~100–1,650 ms — [Schwartenbeck et al. 2023, Cell (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914680)
- Within-trial dynamics in the same study: early (~180–1,620 ms) unspecific sequences toward the stable block from present, distant-present and absent blocks; middle (~260–1,650 ms) sequences involving only present blocks; late (~1,590–3,500 ms) sequences toward the stable block involving only the correct present blocks, i.e., content "progressing from predictable to uncertain elements and gradually converging on the correct configuration"; each sequence is interpreted as "a hypothesis about a possible configuration of elements" — [Schwartenbeck et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914680); [Oxford WIN listing](https://www.win.ox.ac.uk/publications/1543970/modal)
- Caveats stated in the 2023 paper: no significant replay during the probe phase and no relationship between replay strength and performance; the authors describe generative replay for hypothesis testing as a candidate mechanism rather than a proven one; the silhouettes were novel, but the analysis measures sequences between building blocks rather than replay of whole novel configurations as units — [Schwartenbeck et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914680)
- fMRI replay of non-spatial structure (foundational): Schuck & Niv (Science 2019) adapted rodent replay to humans by detecting ordered activation of sequential fMRI patterns during rest in an abstract, non-spatial task — [Schuck & Niv 2019, Science (PDF)](https://nivlab.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/938/2026/01/science.aaw5181.pdf)
- Prioritization of weak memories (foundational): Schapiro et al. (Nature Communications 2018) found with fMRI pattern analysis that objects remembered less well were replayed more during awake rest, and that rest replay predicted later improvement only in participants who slept during the interval — [Schapiro et al. 2018, Nat Commun](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v9y2018i1d10.1038_s41467-018-06213-1.html)
- Methodological warning (eLife 2025 reviewed preprint, "Challenges in replay detection by TDLM in post-encoding resting state"): a MEG study of consolidation of graph-based task structures examines the pitfalls of temporally delayed linear modelling for detecting replay in rest; the authors note that most recent human replay research relies on MEG/EEG and that TDLM-based rest detection faces specific challenges — [eLife 108023](https://elifesciences.org/articles/108023) (full text could not be loaded; description from search snippet)
- Compositional generalization of structure (eLife reviewed preprint 107162, 2025): behavior plus MEG tested whether people generalize learned graph structures to new experiences sharing the same structure but different stimuli — [eLife 107162 reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/107162/reviews) (authors and results not retrievable; listed for follow-up)
- Intracranial awake reactivation (Duan, Lu, Xu, Wang, Lu, Wang, Paller, Axmacher & Wang, Progress in Neurobiology 2025; 11 drug-resistant epilepsy patients, object-location learning, ~30-min post-learning rest): among multiple cortical memory traces for the same object generated across learning rounds, the trace with stronger reactivation during wakeful rest was the one later retrieved; hippocampal ripple rate was significantly elevated from -240 to +80 ms around cortical reactivation peaks (p < 0.001); theta-band (4–8 Hz) hippocampal-cortical coupling increased; hippocampal interactions with default-mode cortical regions preceded cortical reactivation (t(10) = -2.89, p = 0.016, FDR p = 0.048) — [Duan et al. 2025, Progress in Neurobiology (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/Progress%20in%20Neurobiology%202025.pdf)
- Hoel's reading of the rodent literature: replay-like neural sequences during sleep are "mostly never-before-seen sequences", used as an argument that sleep sequences serve generalization rather than verbatim memory transfer — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)

### Inferences
- Across 2019, 2021 and 2023, the human MEG evidence converges on replay as inference over a learned structure: it reorders experience to match a rule, propagates value to untraversed paths according to expected utility, and composes element-level hypotheses that narrow toward a correct configuration. This is the operational meaning of "generative replay" in humans: sampling candidate configurations from a structured model rather than retrieving episodes.
- The absence of a replay-performance correlation in Schwartenbeck 2023 and the TDLM caveats mean that the causal contribution of generative replay to successful inference in humans is not yet demonstrated; the strongest causal-ish link remains the 2021 learning-rate difference (0.70 vs 0.61).
- The 2025 iEEG finding that hippocampal-DMN interactions precede cortical reactivation provides a mechanistic bridge between the "default mode consolidation" idea (Key Question 7) and ripple-locked reactivation in awake humans.

- Norman-lab human replay work beyond the 2018 Schapiro/Norman collaboration and the 2022 model was not retrieved.

## Key Question 3: Evidence for sleep-dependent abstraction, gist extraction, schema formation and rule discovery (iOtA; Wagner number-reduction task and replications; TMR 2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Sleep-dependent rule discovery remains supported but contested: the 2004 number-reduction result (more than twice as many insights after sleep) has been followed by several null or no-difference studies, and a preregistered 2024 nap study found the insight benefit in N2 rather than N1 and in aperiodic 1/f slope rather than oscillations. The most direct 2023 evidence that sleep processes can be targeted at abstraction is Pereira et al.'s REM-TMR study (N = 27), where cueing abstraction problems in REM but not SWS improved later performance, significant only at the one-week retest.

### Cited Findings
- Wagner et al. 2004 (Nature; foundational): in the number-reduction task with a hidden shortcut, participants used the shortcut more often at delayed test when the interval was spent asleep; "more than twice as many subjects gaining insight into a hidden task rule after sleep" — [Löwe, Petzka, Tzegka & Schuck, CCN 2024 summary](https://2024.ccneuro.org/pdf/29_Paper_authored_CCN_2024.pdf); [Stickgold & Walker 2004, TICS](https://walkerlab.berkeley.edu/reprints/Stickgold%20&%20Walker%20TICS_2004.pdf)
- Follow-ups with the same task: Darsaud et al. 2011 found sleep-related neural changes only in participants who gained awareness of the hidden rule — [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2018 review context](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00072/full)
- The 2024 CCN paper states that "the evidence for sleep supporting insight, however, is inconclusive", citing studies that "did not find any benefits of sleep on insight, or no difference between sleep and awake rest (Cordi & Rasch, 2021; Schönauer et al., 2018; Brodt, Pöhlchen, Täumer, Gais, & Schönauer, 2018)" — [Löwe et al. CCN 2024](https://2024.ccneuro.org/pdf/29_Paper_authored_CCN_2024.pdf)
- Preregistered nap study (Löwe, Petzka, Tzegka & Schuck, CCN 2024; N = 90; perceptual insight task with a hidden colour strategy; 20-min nap): 70.6% (48/68) of participants without pre-nap insight gained insight after the nap vs a 48.5% (48/99) no-nap baseline (Fisher p = .007). By stage: 85.7% (24/28) of the N2 group, 63.6% (14/22) of the N1 group and 55.5% (10/18) of the Wake group gained insight; N1 vs Wake p = 0.75; N1 vs N2 p = 0.1; N2 vs Wake p = 0.038; in a GLM the N2 coefficient was significant (p = 0.03) while N1 (p = 0.6) and Wake (p = 0.64) were not; 1/f aperiodic slope predicted insight above and beyond sleep stage — [Löwe et al. CCN 2024](https://2024.ccneuro.org/pdf/29_Paper_authored_CCN_2024.pdf)
- iOtA (information Overlap to Abstract; Lewis & Durrant 2011, foundational): hippocampal input biases neocortex to replay thematically linked memories together; overlapping content is strengthened by Hebbian plasticity while episodic details are forgotten, leaving shared gist; this is the NREM "rule abstraction" component of the 2018 Lewis, Knoblich & Poe model — [Lewis, Knoblich & Poe 2018, TICS (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7543772/)
- REM-targeted abstraction (Pereira et al., Journal of Neuroscience 2023, 43(21):3838–3848; N = 27): abstraction problems were paired with sounds and the sounds replayed during SWS or REM; performance improved on problems cued in REM but not SWS; "the cue-related improvement was not significant until a follow up retest one week after the manipulation" — [Pereira et al. 2023, Nottingham repository](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/19010035/rule-abstraction-is-facilitated-by-auditory-cuing-in-rem-sleep); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/153975/)
- Schema and spindles (foundational, 2016): Hennies et al. (Journal of Neuroscience) found that sleep spindle density predicted the increase in the schema benefit to memory across the retention interval and predicted increased hippocampal disengagement over 24 h for schema-related memories only — [Hennies et al. 2016 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4812136)
- TMR and the specificity/generalization trade-off: Witkowski, Schechtman & Paller frame TMR research around the tension between remembering specific experiences and generalizing across them, drawing on complementary learning systems; a related study comparing TMR in SWS vs REM on mnemonic discrimination and generalization found no significant difference between the two TMR groups, with only an exploratory hint that TMR enhanced discrimination for highly similar items — [Witkowski, Schechtman & Paller (NSF PAR)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10148570); [ESRS TMR overview](https://esrs.eu/news/sleep-science-friday/targeted-memory-reactivation-during-sleep/) (the SWS-vs-REM null finding is from a search summary; the specific paper was not opened)
- 2025 conference work on sleep-dependent category abstraction: Solomon, Krishnamurthy & Schapiro (CNS 2025 poster, "Memory reactivation during sleep facilitates abstraction of category structure") trained participants on insect categories with different feature co-occurrence structures to test whether sleep converts details to an abstracted format — [CNS 2025 poster listing](https://cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6285) (results not visible; the poster page could not be read)
- Emotional generalization after a nap (Reichardt et al. 2024; N = 113, nap vs wake, emotional Mnemonic Separation Task): discrimination of similar lures was enhanced for negative relative to neutral and positive stimuli; the authors frame REM as a likely driver of emotional generalization — [Reichardt et al. 2024 (PDF)](https://real.mtak.hu/223331/1/2024_Reichardt_sleepMST.pdf)
- Picard-Deland and colleagues' night-course finding that recent memory sources decline across the night while remote and semantic sources persist is used to argue for progressive integration of new into old knowledge across sleep cycles — [Picard-Deland et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091095)
- Hoel's summary of older evidence: sleep improved cognitive more than perceptual tasks, helped resist interfering associations more than direct memorization, rescued an overtrained texture-discrimination task, and "improves abstraction and reasoning" — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)

### Inferences
- The number-reduction effect is best treated as real-but-fragile: a large original effect, mixed replications, and a 2024 preregistered nap study whose stage-specific result (N2, not N1) contradicts Lacaux 2021. The shared signal across Wagner 2004 and Löwe 2024 is that some sleep beyond N1 helps rule discovery; the disagreement is about which stage and whether oscillatory or aperiodic activity is the mechanism.
- Pereira 2023 is the strongest 2023–2026 causal evidence for stage-specific abstraction, and its delayed (one-week) emergence fits iOtA-style gradual gist extraction better than immediate insight.
- TMR evidence for generalization specifically (as opposed to strengthening) is thin; the SWS-vs-REM null and small samples mean no robust TMR-induced abstraction effect in 2023–2026 beyond Pereira.

### Gaps
- No formal direct replication of Wagner 2004 (same task, same design) was found; the "null" studies used different insight tasks.
- The Pereira 2023 full statistics (effect sizes, exact test values) were not retrievable (repository returned 403).
- No 2024–2025 meta-analysis of sleep and generalization/gist/abstraction was found; the closest is a 2023 spindle-consolidation meta-analysis (Kumral et al., Neuropsychologia 189:108661) whose generalization-specific content was not accessible — [Freiburg publication list](https://uni-freiburg.de/neuropsychologie/publikationen/)
- Schapiro CNS 2025 abstraction results could not be read.

## Key Question 4: REM vs NREM vs their alternation (Lewis, Knoblich & Poe 2018; "REM as creativity stage"; spindles and integration) and what 2023–2026 adds

### Takeaway
The 2018 Lewis-Knoblich-Poe model (NREM abstracts gist, REM links distant schemas, their ~90-min alternation builds and restructures knowledge frameworks) remains the dominant framework, but its explicit computational and behavioral tests were not found in the 2023–2026 literature; the main 2023–2026 additions are Pereira's REM-TMR abstraction result, the Singh/Norman/Schapiro 2022 model showing cycling is needed to avoid overwriting, and the Deperrois model assigning REM to semantic concept extraction and NREM to robustness.

### Cited Findings
- Lewis, Knoblich & Poe (TICS 2018, 22(6):491–503): NREM replay abstracts general rules (iOtA), REM replay forms novel associations between distant knowledge; alternation across ~90-min cycles builds restructurable knowledge; in REM, hippocampal-neocortical synchrony is weak so neocortex mainly replays what it already codes; PGO waves may trigger replay of randomly chosen cortical schemas; high cortical acetylcholine in REM is linked to plasticity (the text is described as not fully consistent on ACh); semantic priming spreads further in REM; "only connections with some structural overlap are presumably retained, so REM acts as an unconscious search for shared structure" — [Lewis, Knoblich & Poe 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7543772/)
- The 2018 model's "BiOtA" proposal: a parallel distributed network with a hippocampal layer feeding a first cortical layer trained in NREM; in REM that layer trains a further, more compressed layer and is compared with older knowledge, with an autoencoder-like check deciding which memories need no further NREM replay; proposed tests were behavioral (manipulate replay in REM vs NREM; measure gist abstraction and distant links) and computational (implement the network) — [Lewis, Knoblich & Poe 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7543772/)
- Press coverage at the time quotes Lewis: "The idea is to lay out this model in a formal way so that it's there and people can explicitly test it", with a five-year grant for testing — [ScienceDaily 2018](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/05/180515113629.htm); no published outcome of those tests was found in 2023–2026 searches
- The first behavioral test consistent with the REM prediction is Pereira et al. 2023 (REM cueing, not SWS cueing, improved abstraction; N = 27; one-week delayed effect) — [Pereira et al. 2023](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/19010035/rule-abstraction-is-facilitated-by-auditory-cuing-in-rem-sleep)
- "REM as a creativity stage" (foundational): Cai et al. 2009 nap study found REM, compared with quiet rest and NREM, enhanced integration of unassociated information on the Remote Associates Test, attributed to cholinergic/noradrenergic neuromodulation; the same paper notes that before it "no study has demonstrated that REM enhances creativity more than wake, NREM, or simply the passage of time" — [Cai et al. 2009 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2700890)
- Stage-dependent dream content supports a division of labor: REM dreams incorporate lab material more (46.9% vs 28.7% in NREM), N1 and REM dreams carry more memory sources, and themes "often repeated across the night, frequently switching between NREM and REM" — [Picard-Deland et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091095)
- Modeling of NREM/REM alternation (Singh, Norman & Schapiro, PNAS 2022): the hippocampus "teaches" neocortex during NREM; during REM the neocortex reactivates and replays its own knowledge, stabilizing it; "when the neocortex doesn't have a chance to replay its own information, we see that the information there gets overwritten"; Schapiro: "We still need to test this" — [Neuroscience News on Singh et al. 2022](https://neurosciencenews.com/memory-sleep-hippocampus-neocortex-21719); [Penn announcement](https://www.sas.upenn.edu/graduate-division/announcements/during-sleep-one-brain-region-teaches-another-converting-novel-data)
- Deperrois et al. (eLife 2022) assign complementary functions: REM "adversarial dreaming" is "essential for extracting semantic concepts", NREM "perturbed dreaming" (replay of episodic memories with perturbation) "improves the robustness of latent representations" (details in Key Question 5) — [Deperrois et al. 2022, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/76384)
- Spindles and integration: Antony, Schönauer, Staresina & Cairney (Trends in Neurosciences 2019) propose a framework in which spindles gate memory reprocessing — [Antony et al. 2019, TINS](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/55718300/Antony_etal_Sleep_Spindles_and_Memory_Reprocessing_Trends_in_Neuroscience.pdf); Cairney et al. 2018 (Current Biology) showed that memory cues during naps evoked spindle increases during which cued content could be decoded, and cueing improved next-day retrieval — [Sleep Review summary of Cairney 2018](https://sleepreviewmag.com/sleep-health/sleep-whole-body/brain/memories-can-decoded-brain-waves-sleep-finds-study-using-word-picture-pairs/); spindle density predicted schema benefit (Hennies 2016) — [Hennies et al. 2016 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4812136)
- Lewis-Knoblich-Poe also propose that spindles help integrate new information with existing schemas and that REM restructuring may help overcome functional fixedness ("seeing a hat as a kitten bed") — [Lewis, Knoblich & Poe 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7543772/)
- Konkoly et al. 2026 note that TMR in REM has been shown to improve emotional memory and rule abstraction (citing Pereira 2023) and that REM is thought to promote "abstraction and generalization", motivating their REM-cueing design — [Konkoly et al. 2026](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- Cross-reference: the 2024 Löwe/Schuck nap study locating insight in N2 and aperiodic activity (Key Question 3) is the main new NREM-side datum; the round-1 report covers N1/hypnagogia.

### Inferences
- Three independent lines (Lewis 2018 theory; Singh/Norman/Schapiro 2022 model; Deperrois 2022 model) converge on a division of labor in which NREM does hippocampus-driven, veridical-plus-gist replay and REM does neocortex-internal, recombinative or generative processing, and all three claim the alternation itself is functional (restructuring; preventing overwriting; complementary objectives). Human empirical confirmation of the alternation claim is still missing.
- The only 2023–2026 human causal datum for REM-specific abstraction (Pereira 2023) is small (N = 27) and delayed, so the "REM as creativity stage" claim rests on one modern TMR study plus the 2009 nap study.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 publication implementing or testing the BiOtA network was found.
- No 2023–2026 study of REM/NREM alternation per se (e.g., manipulating cycle structure) in humans was found.
- Searches for a 2024 Cairney Nature Communications spindle-integration paper returned nothing matching; it may not exist as described.
- Walker-lab 2023–2026 primary papers on REM and creativity were not retrieved (only podcast commentary surfaced, which was excluded).

## Key Question 5: Computational models (CLS updates; wake-sleep; "dreaming" networks; Deperrois et al. 2022; replay in RL agents) and the function of offline recombination

### Takeaway
Models agree that offline generation of inputs that were never experienced (perturbed or adversarial dreams, generative replay of category instances, prioritized or exploratory replay of untraversed paths) improves generalization, robustness and planning, and that NREM-like veridical replay and REM-like generative replay optimize different objectives; the strongest quantitative claims come from Deperrois 2022 (REM adversarial dreaming is required for linearly separable semantic representations, ~59% CIFAR-10 / 79% SVHN linear-readout accuracy with the full model) and from 2024–2025 RL models in which replay emerges or is directed by uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- Deperrois, Petrovici, Senn & Jordan (eLife 2022, "Learning cortical representations through perturbed and adversarial dreaming"): a GAN-inspired cortical architecture in which learning is split across three brain states (wake, NREM, REM) with complementary objectives; in the PAD model, NREM replays single episodic memories with perturbation (occlusion) and REM generates new virtual inputs by linearly combining multiple random hippocampal episodic memories with spontaneous cortical activity, with feedforward and feedback pathways in an adversarial game — [Deperrois et al. 2022, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/76384)
- Quantitative results: on CIFAR-10 and SVHN the latent representation reached ~59% (CIFAR-10) and 79% (SVHN) linear-readout test accuracy, "comparable to less biologically plausible machine learning models"; removing REM (adversarial dreaming) made linear separability increase "much slower" and remain "significantly below" PAD even after many epochs; replacing REM's mixed-memory-plus-noise input with single episodic memories (as in NREM) likewise slowed learning and on SVHN never reached PAD performance; removing NREM reduced linear separability under occlusion (robustness) while leaving unoccluded performance equal or better — [Deperrois et al. 2022, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/76384)
- The authors' conclusion: "combining different, possibly nonrelated episodic memories, together with spontaneous cortical activity, as reported during REM dreaming ... leads to significantly faster representation learning", and "the characteristic creative nature of dreams suggests that learning semantic representations may go beyond merely replaying previous experiences" — [Deperrois et al. 2022, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/76384)
- Deperrois et al. 2023 review (Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, "Learning beyond sensations: how dreams organize neuronal representations"): proposes two principles, adversarial dreaming (feedback and feedforward pathways "trying to fool each other") and contrastive dreaming (invariance acquired by mapping similar virtual experiences together), argued to be compatible with cortical structure, dynamics and sleep phenomenology — [Deperrois et al. 2023, arXiv 2308.01830](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.01830v2); [Bern repository](https://boris.unibe.ch/190384/)
- Complementary learning systems in modeling (Singh, Norman & Schapiro, PNAS 2022): a neural-network model of autonomous hippocampal-neocortical interactions in which NREM replay is hippocampus-guided and REM replay is neocortex-internal; cycling prevents overwriting of neocortical knowledge — [Neuroscience News on Singh et al. 2022](https://neurosciencenews.com/memory-sleep-hippocampus-neocortex-21719)
- Schapiro's 2025 CCN keynote extends CLS with a model in which the hippocampus itself contains two pathways, one rapidly learning regularities and one for episodes, tested across statistical learning, inference and category learning, plus modeling of how hippocampal replay shapes neocortical representations during sleep — [CCN 2025 keynote](https://2025.ccneuro.org/keynote-anna-schapiro/)
- Generative replay for category learning (Barry & Love, Cerebral Cortex 2022): in a network approximating ventral visual stream and hippocampus, generative replay "akin to imagining new category instances" facilitated generalization to new experiences, with benefits confined to early learning when novel category knowledge is being acquired — [Barry & Love 2022 (UCL Discovery PDF)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10145147/1/bhac054.pdf)
- Replay emerging in RL agents (Wang, Tang, Chen, Mattar & Song, arXiv 2024, "Brain-Like Replay Naturally Emerges in Reinforcement Learning Agents"): in a modular RL network trained end-to-end on a task, replay arises from structural biases of the modular architecture "without complex added assumptions", helps complete the task, and reproduces key phenomena observed in biological agents (the abstract gives no numbers; not peer reviewed at retrieval) — [Wang et al. 2024, arXiv 2402.01467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01467)
- Exploratory replay (Antonov & Dayan, Nature Communications Feb 2025): extends the Mattar-Daw prioritized-replay theory to exploration, noting that existing replay models "only apply to known environments" and cannot predict replay during learning under uncertainty; the model offers a normative account of experimental data suggestive of exploratory replay, i.e., planning exploratory choices offline "during the equivalent of quiet wakefulness or sleep" — [Antonov & Dayan 2025, Nat Commun (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/06e92193edb74484b770405f03c68d52)
- Roscow, Howe, Lepora & Jones (Nature Communications 2025) provide a behavioral and theoretical toolkit for measuring which features of experience drive replay prioritisation to optimise adaptive behaviour, linking replay and reinforcement learning — [Roscow et al. 2025, Nat Commun](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-025-65354-2.html)
- A 2025 Frontiers research-topic editorial (Daou & Lee) highlights Dyna-style models: Lee & Jung have CA3 simulate multiple scenarios while CA1 evaluates each for potential reward, strengthening high-value links; Kim & Lee's "Meta-Dyna" combines hippocampal replay with prefrontal meta-control — [Daou & Lee 2025, Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2025.1595369/full)
- Hoel's in-silico predictions: dream-like sparse input distributions should best reduce overfitting in deep nets; cycling training between real and corrupted top-down inputs should delay overfitting; lowering learning rate during dream-like input may help — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)
- Human empirical anchor for the RL models: Liu et al. 2021 found backward replay prioritized by need x gain utility (p = 0.003), the signature predicted by prioritized-replay theory — [Liu et al. 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7610948)

### Inferences
- The models cluster into two functional claims about offline recombination: (a) representation learning (Deperrois; Barry & Love; Hoel): generating off-distribution or recombined samples regularizes the cortex and yields more abstract, linearly readable, robust features; (b) planning/credit assignment (Mattar-Daw lineage; Antonov & Dayan; Dyna models): replaying untraversed or uncertain transitions propagates value and plans exploration. Human replay data fit (b) directly (Liu 2021) and (a) only indirectly (Schwartenbeck 2023 compositional hypotheses).
- The distinctive mechanistic bet of the Deperrois model, that REM input is a linear mixture of multiple unrelated episodes plus noise, maps onto the dream-content finding that ~49% of dreams combine multiple memory sources (Picard-Deland 2023), but no study has tested whether the degree of mixing in dreams predicts next-day abstraction.

### Gaps
- Hinton's wake-sleep algorithm (1995) and formal CLS updates (Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016) were not retrieved within budget; cite from primary sources before use.
- No 2024–2026 "sleep-inspired continual learning" or catastrophic-forgetting paper was retrievable (search budget exhausted before this query).
- Quantitative results of the Wang et al. 2024 RL-replay paper and of Antonov & Dayan 2025 beyond the abstract were not read.

## Key Question 6: Lucid dreaming for problem solving; dream incubation and dream content related to creative work (Barrett; 2023–2026 experiments)

### Takeaway
The first controlled REM-dream-engineering study (Konkoly et al. 2026, N = 20 frequent lucid dreamers, 39 lab nights) shows that cues can reliably insert unsolved puzzles into dreams (cued vs uncued incorporation beta = 1.84, p < .001), that conceptual incorporation predicts next-morning solving (beta = 1.24, p = .037), and, post hoc, that cueing raised solving only in the 12 "targeted dreamers" (interaction p = .04); lucid dreams incorporated cues more often than non-lucid ones but had the lowest solving rate. Independent lucid-dreaming creativity work (haiku, 2025) is small and exploratory.

### Cited Findings
- Konkoly, Morris, Hurka, Martinez, Sanders & Paller (Neuroscience of Consciousness 2026, "Creative problem-solving after experimentally provoking dreams of unsolved puzzles during REM sleep"): 20 frequent lucid dreamers (14 female, 5 male, 1 non-binary) selected for lucid dreams at least monthly; data over 39 nights (two nights each); participants attempted brainteaser puzzles each with a unique soundtrack until ~4 remained unsolved per night; two of the four unsolved puzzles were randomly selected for cueing in REM; lucid dreams were induced with targeted lucidity reactivation (TLR); participants were told to work on a puzzle if they heard its soundtrack in a dream and to signal with eye movements or sniffs — [Konkoly et al. 2026 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- Results: 2.02 dreams per night on average, 71 reports in lab; 15 of 20 participants had at least one puzzle-related dream; participants dreamed of 26% of their unsolved puzzles (SD 25%, range 0–75%); cued puzzles were incorporated more than uncued (beta = 1.84, 95% CI 0.76–2.93, p < .001); 7.8% of puzzles cued in non-lucid dreams (16 puzzles) and 24.6% cued in lucid dreams (15 puzzles) were incorporated; 13 verified lucid dreams from 6 participants — [Konkoly et al. 2026 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- Conceptual incorporation predicted solving next morning (beta = 1.241, 95% CI 0.07–2.40, p = .037); sensory incorporation of the sound itself did not (beta = -0.86, p = .31); overall 30% of cued vs 22% of uncued puzzles were solved (no main effect of cueing, ps > .6; incorporation-by-cueing model trend chi-square(1) = 3.61, p = .057), so the planned mediation analysis could not be run — [Konkoly et al. 2026 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- Post-hoc responder analysis: 12 "targeted dreamers" dreamed on average 43% more of cued than uncued puzzles; 8 non-responders dreamed 5% less; responder status x cueing interaction chi-square(1) = 4.18, p = .04; in targeted dreamers cued puzzles were solved more often than uncued (reported odds ratio 0.32, SE 0.18, z = -2.0, p = .046 in the paper's coding), with no cueing effect in non-responders (OR = 2.66, p = .25) — [Konkoly et al. 2026 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- Lucidity and solving: solving rates were lower after incorporation in lucid dreams (1 of 9, 11%) than non-lucid dreams (6 of 13, 46%), and highest for puzzles with real-time cue responses but no remembered incorporation (4 of 6, 67%) — [Konkoly et al. 2026 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- At home, participants incorporated 14.3% of puzzles left unsolved (SD 12%) across 134 dreams — [Konkoly et al. 2026 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- The authors' motivation: earlier work "could not separate dreaming from waking cognition or from memory reactivation in general", and the REM-dream/problem-solving link had been "supported mostly by anecdotal evidence" — [Konkoly et al. 2026 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf)
- Štuikytė & Stumbrys (IJoDR 2025): lucid and non-lucid dreamers wrote haiku; non-lucid dreamers wrote awake, lucid dreamers wrote both awake and while lucid dreaming; only the haiku written during lucid dreams ranked higher in rated creativity; awake haiku by lucid dreamers did not differ from those of non-lucid dreamers — [Štuikytė & Stumbrys 2025, IJoDR](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/48667) (page blocked to fetch; details from search summary)
- Foundational pilot: Stumbrys & Daniels (2010): nine lucid and nine non-lucid dreamers over ten nights attempted a logic puzzle or a metaphor task; lucid dreams "can contribute to problem solving when dealing with more creative rather than logical tasks" — [Stumbrys & Daniels 2010, IJoDR](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/6167)
- Bourke & Shaw (2014) reported frequent lucid dreamers solved more insight (remote associates) problems than non-lucid dreamers — [Psychology Today 2026 summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/the-secret-mind/202604/how-lucid-dreaming-can-make-us-more-creative)
- An 8-week lucid dreaming and fiction-writing study (Roklicer, accepted Dec 2025) exists but its findings were not retrievable — [Gigazine coverage of 2026 dream-engineering work](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260212-manipulate-dream-ability-solve-puzzles)
- Dream-content cross-check: Hoel notes that overtraining on novel tasks (Tetris, ski simulators, mirror tracing, inverted-goggle reading, driving) reliably produces dreams involving those tasks, "though not literal replays" — [Hoel 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8134940)
- Cross-reference: sleep-onset dream incubation with Dormio (Horowitz et al. 2023) is covered in round 1 and is cited by Konkoly 2026 as the N1 precedent for incubation effects on creativity.

### Inferences
- Konkoly 2026 moves dream incubation from N1 (Dormio) to REM and adds a within-subject control (cued vs uncued puzzles); the pattern "incorporation predicts solving, cueing alone does not" supports the view that dream content, not cue exposure, carries the benefit, but the responder split is post hoc and the sample small.
- The inverse relation between lucidity and solving (11% vs 46%) suggests that deliberate, controlled dream cognition may re-impose the constraints that offline recombination is supposed to relax, consistent with round-1's "loosen but do not remove control" theme.

- No 2023–2026 Barrett dream-incubation experiment was found (search budget exhausted before a dedicated query); Barrett's older incubation work was not retrieved.
- Štuikytė & Stumbrys 2025 sample sizes, rating method and statistics could not be read (site blocked).
- Konkoly 2026's stated limitations section was not read in full.

## Key Question 7: Does awake rest / quiet wakefulness perform similar functions (awake replay, default-mode consolidation), and how quickly?

### Takeaway
Yes, on the scale of seconds to minutes: human iEEG in 2025 shows awake cortical reactivation locked to hippocampal ripples and theta coupling within a ~30-minute rest that predicts which of several competing memory traces is later retrieved, with default-mode-network interactions preceding reactivation; behavioral work shows a 15-minute eyes-closed rest benefits memory and that even seconds-long rest breaks produce memory-predictive activity, while awake replay prioritizes weak memories but converts into durable gains mainly when followed by sleep.

### Cited Findings
- Duan, Lu, Xu, Wang, Lu, Wang, Paller, Axmacher & Wang (Progress in Neurobiology 2025; 11 patients with drug-resistant epilepsy; object-location associations; rest ~30–40 min after learning): among competing cortical traces for the same object, the one with stronger reactivation during wakeful rest was more likely to be retrieved later; reactivation peaks coincided with elevated hippocampal ripple rate (cluster -240 to +80 ms, p < 0.001) and increased theta-band (4–8 Hz) hippocampal-cortical phase coupling; hippocampal interactions with default-mode cortical regions preceded cortical reactivation (t(10) = -2.89, p = 0.016, FDR p = 0.048); the paper also reports a strong negative correlation (r = -0.66, p < 0.001) between rest-period reactivation strength and a learning-stage measure in its Fig. 1 — [Duan et al. 2025 (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/~paller/Progress%20in%20Neurobiology%202025.pdf)
- Wamsley (Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2019, review): a 15-minute eyes-closed rest after encoding improved declarative and procedural memory relative to a distractor task; benefits persisted a week or more in some studies; rest helped hard-to-rehearse material; low-frequency EEG oscillations linked to sleep consolidation predicted retention across quiet rest; hippocampal encoding patterns persisted into rest and predicted memory; "even seconds-long rest breaks during learning produced memory-related activity that predicted later test performance"; internally focused autobiographical thinking blocked the benefit — [Wamsley 2019, TICS (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7024394)
- Wamsley & Summer (Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience 2020; 46 participants; hd-EEG plus pupillometry over a 30-min retention interval): proposes that humans may spend up to half of waking hours "offline" and that rapid switching between online and offline states lets the brain alternate between encoding and consolidating — [Wamsley & Summer 2020 (NSF PAR)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10462348) (page refused connection; details from search summary)
- Sleep vs rest comparison (Tucker, Humiston, Summer & Wamsley, Nature and Science of Sleep 2020) — [Tucker et al. 2020](https://www.dovepress.com/comparing-the-effects-of-sleep-and-rest-on-memory-consolidation-peer-reviewed-article-NSS) (findings not accessible)
- Awake replay prioritization and the sleep dependency (foundational): Schapiro et al. 2018 found less-well-remembered objects were replayed more during awake rest, and rest replay predicted across-session improvement only for participants who slept in between — [Schapiro et al. 2018, Nat Commun](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v9y2018i1d10.1038_s41467-018-06213-1.html)
- Timescale of human replay during task: in Liu et al. 2021, replay occurred at reward receipt within trials, with forward sequences at ~30 ms/state and backward at ~160 ms/state — [Liu et al. 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7610948); in Schwartenbeck 2023, replay-sequence content evolved within ~3.5 s of a single inference trial — [Schwartenbeck et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914680)
- A 2025 Chinese-language review (Lei, Weng & Yu, Advances in Psychological Science 33(5):729–743) synthesizes EEG and fMRI evidence on consolidation during wakeful rest — [Lei, Weng & Yu 2025](https://journal.psych.ac.cn/xlkxjz/EN/Y2025/V33/I5/729) (contents not accessed)
- Offline planning during quiet wakefulness is explicitly modeled: Antonov & Dayan 2025 treat exploratory replay as planning "during the equivalent of quiet wakefulness or sleep" — [Antonov & Dayan 2025 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/06e92193edb74484b770405f03c68d52)
- Cross-reference: round 1 covers Iwata et al. 2024 (ripple rate rises during vivid, task-unrelated spontaneous thought) and He et al. 2025 (ripples predict idea originality during alternate-uses), which are the awake-ideation counterparts of the consolidation findings here.

### Inferences
- The Duan 2025 iEEG data close a loop between "default-mode consolidation" and ripple-locked reactivation: DMN-hippocampal coupling precedes, rather than follows, cortical reactivation, which suggests the DMN sets the context in which specific traces are reactivated during rest.
- Awake rest appears to do selection and stabilization (which trace wins; weak traces prioritized) within minutes, while the abstraction/restructuring functions attributed to REM-NREM cycling (Key Question 4) have, so far, only been demonstrated after sleep (Pereira 2023 one-week effect; Schapiro 2018 sleep-gated conversion). This is a difference of function, not just speed.

### Gaps
- No dedicated 2024–2025 human fMRI/MEG awake-replay study was retrieved (the 2025 items found are iEEG, EEG or methodological).
- Wamsley lab's 2023–2026 NSF-funded studies on offline wakefulness (grant $562,000, three years) have no retrievable published results in this round — [Furman University news](https://www.furman.edu/news/wamsley-awarded-nsf-grant-to-study-the-impact-of-wakeful-rest-on-memory/)
- The exact quantitative conclusions of Wamsley & Summer 2020 and Tucker et al. 2020 were not accessible.
