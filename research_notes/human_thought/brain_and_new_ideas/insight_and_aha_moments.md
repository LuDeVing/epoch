# Insight and "Aha!" Moments: Cognitive Science and Neuroscience (emphasis 2023–2026)

Scope note: These notes cover the phases leading to insight (impasse, incubation, restructuring), neural/physiological markers, insight vs. analysis, the insight–memory link, accuracy of insights and the "eureka heuristic", predictive-processing accounts, and neuromodulatory/state influences (mood, sleep, psychedelics, dopamine, noradrenaline). Older foundational work is included only for context and is labeled "[foundational, pre-2023]". Where I could only read an abstract or secondary coverage rather than the full paper, that is flagged.

Research date: 2026-10-08.

---

## Key Question 1: Newest findings on the neural correlates of insight (gamma burst, alpha, hippocampus, dopaminergic reward, pupil), and what Kounios, Beeman, Salvi, Danek and others published 2023–2026

### Takeaway
The 2023–2026 literature has shifted the neural story of insight from a single right-temporal gamma burst toward a distributed, integrated event: (1) representational change in solution-relevant cortex, (2) a simultaneous amygdala / anterior-hippocampus / nucleus-accumbens response scaled to the strength of the Aha!, (3) phasic pupil dilation consistent with locus-coeruleus noradrenaline, and (4) transient network integration among these regions. Kounios and Beeman's labs published little new primary EEG work in this window (their output was mainly reviews, a Scientific American piece, a 2026 DTI paper, and conference posters); the most important new primary data came from Maxi Becker, Roberto Cabeza and Tobias Sommer (Humboldt/Duke/Hamburg) and from Carola Salvi.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational context [pre-2023]**
- Jung-Beeman, Bowden, Kounios et al. (2004, PLoS Biology): fMRI showed greater right anterior superior temporal gyrus (aSTG) activity for insight vs. non-insight solutions to compound remote associates; EEG showed a burst of gamma-band activity over the same region beginning ~0.3 s before insight solutions, and an increase in alpha power over right posterior cortex ~1.5 s before insight solutions (interpreted as sensory gating / "brain blink") — [Jung-Beeman et al. 2004, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC387276)
- Oh, Chesebrough, Erickson, Zhang & Kounios (2020, NeuroImage): high-density EEG during anagram solving; insight solutions were followed ~0.1 s later by an additional gamma burst over orbitofrontal cortex (reward system), present mainly in participants high in trait reward sensitivity; low scorers had as many insights but no significant neural reward response — [Drexel News 2020](https://drexel.edu/news/archive/2020/april/creative-insight-triggers-neural-reward); [Drexel repository record](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/An-insight-related-neural-reward-signal/991019167321004721)
- Sandkühler & Bhattacharya (2008, PLoS ONE): increased upper-alpha in right temporal regions for initially failed trials later solved after a hint; decreased alpha in right prefrontal cortex for high-restructuring trials — [Goldsmiths repository](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/4211)
- Salvi et al. (2020, NeuroImage): in 38 participants, pupil diameter increased ~500 ms before solution only on trials reported as insight; microsaccade rate increased only before non-insight (analytic) solutions; authors interpret the pupil signal as a marker of the switch of unconscious processing into awareness and propose locus-coeruleus noradrenaline involvement — [Salvi et al. 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7440842)

**2023–2026 primary findings**
- Becker, Sommer & Cabeza (2025, Nature Communications 16:4341, published 9 May 2025), "Insight predicts subsequent memory via cortical representational change and hippocampal activity": fMRI in N=31 (38 scanned, 7 excluded) solving 120 Mooney images; insight measured as summed 4-point ratings of positive emotion (M=2.8), suddenness (M=3.0), certainty (M=3.16), which loaded on one latent factor (λ=0.53–0.65). Single-trial insight strength predicted amygdala activity (z=7.55, p<0.001, β=0.18) and anterior hippocampus activity (z=5.57, p<0.001, β=0.13) but not posterior hippocampus (z=1.11, p=0.27). Of the three insight dimensions, certainty was the strongest predictor of anterior hippocampus (t=6.77, β=0.17) and amygdala (t=5.39, β=0.13); emotion weakly predicted amygdala (t=2.02, p=0.044) — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Same study: representational change in ventral occipito-temporal cortex (VOTC). Pre- vs. post-solution multivoxel pattern similarity decreased with insight in bilateral inferior lateral occipital cortex (iLOC; t=−8.08, p-Bonf<0.001) and bilateral posterior fusiform gyrus (pFusG; t=−6.09, p-Bonf<0.001); AlexNet-model representational strength increased more with insight (insight×time χ²(1)=15.19, p<0.001), as did Word2Vec (semantic) representational strength (χ²(1)=16.12, p<0.001) — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Same study: on high-insight trials an 18-node "solution network" (VOTC + amygdala + hippocampus) became more integrated: functional connectivity high > low insight (mass=364.03, p-FWE=0.009), higher degree (t(30)=3.17, p=0.003), higher clustering (t=3.08, p=0.003), shorter path length (t=−2.73, p=0.005) — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Same study, behavioral: correct solutions were 65.3% high-insight vs 34.7% low-insight; incorrect solutions 40.5% vs 59.5%; high-insight solutions were faster (median 2.7 s vs 5.1 s; χ²(1)=423.8, p<0.001). Note: the accuracy odds ratio is printed as OR=1.31 with 95% CI [1.63, 2.09]; the point estimate lies outside its own CI, which looks like a typographical error in the paper — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Becker, Yu & Cabeza (2023, Scientific Reports 13:17159), "The influence of insight on risky decision making and nucleus accumbens activation": fMRI (N=32 analysed of 38) with 120 Mooney images; high-insight solutions produced greater nucleus accumbens (NAcc) BOLD than low-insight (χ²(1)=16.78, p<0.0001; continuous insight χ²(1)=97.21, p<0.0001). Positive emotion uniquely predicted NAcc activity beyond suddenness and certainty (χ²(1)=6.22, p=0.013). Exploratory whole-brain: strongest cluster in ventral striatum (mostly NAcc) plus amygdala and caudate, with additional clusters in anterior/posterior cingulate and bilateral angular gyrus (DMN-like). Preregistered online Study 1 (N=103 analysed) found risky choices after high-insight solutions 38% vs 33% after low-insight (χ²(1)=9.41, p<0.005, OR=1.18; marginal R² ≈0.3–0.5%). Authors frame insight as an internal reward signal, "possibly via dopaminergic signaling", but note dopamine was not measured — [Scientific Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44293-2)
- Bieth et al. (Human Brain Mapping 45(1), online 7 Dec 2023; Volle lab, Paris), "Time course of EEG power during creative problem-solving with insight or remote thinking": modified Remote Associates Test with varying semantic remoteness; insight (Eureka-reported) solutions were preceded by increased alpha and gamma (31–60 Hz) power in a left temporal cluster and theta (3–7 Hz) in a frontal cluster; semantic remoteness was associated with left parieto-temporal alpha, right fronto-temporal beta early, and bilateral frontal theta just before response — [NeuInfo record, PMID 38060194](https://neuinfo.org/literature/pmid:38060194?rpKey=on); [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.26.470102v1)
- Salvi, Luchini, Pestilli, Hanekamp, Parrish, Beeman & Grafman (BMC Psychology, dated Feb 2026; DOI prefix 2025), "The white matter of Aha! moments": DTI in 38 participants; higher propensity to solve CRA problems by self-reported insight was associated with lower fractional anisotropy in the left dorsal language network (arcuate fasciculus / superior longitudinal fasciculus); no structural correlate for analytic solving; interpretation: less constrained left-hemisphere semantic pathways permit remote associations. No effect sizes reported in coverage; correlational, small N — [PsyPost coverage](https://www.psypost.org/neuroscientists-identify-a-unique-feature-in-the-brains-wiring-that-predicts-sudden-epiphanies/)
- Chesebrough et al. (Creativity Research Journal, 13 Mar 2023; Kounios lab lineage), "Conceptual Change Induced by Analogical Reasoning Sparks Aha Moments": verbal analogies adapted as an insight task; more semantically distant analogies produced stronger Aha and more representational change; Aha strength rose linearly with changes in participants' verbal descriptions across a trial; subjective difficulty and confidence each had inverted-U relations with Aha strength; positive mood increased Aha strength — [Drexel repository](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Conceptual-Change-Induced-by-Analogical-Reasoning/991020228261604721)
- Cognitive Neuroscience Society 2026 abstract (March 2026, authors not retrieved; design matches the Becker/Cabeza Mooney paradigm): a steeper, non-linear image-reveal schedule reliably increased self-reported Aha! intensity (preregistered online); in an fMRI + pupillometry study with delayed memory test, a sustained signal rise began ~2 s before response in object-selective cortex and prefrontal control regions; pupil dilation showed a phasic non-linear increase in the same window ("consistent with noradrenergic arousal"); Aha!-scaled univariate responses in hippocampus, SN/VTA, nucleus accumbens and locus coeruleus; SEMs linked cortical updating, hippocampus and "catecholaminergic proxies" to Aha intensity; both Aha intensity and hippocampus predicted better delayed memory. Conference abstract — preliminary, no N or statistics available — [CNS 2026 abstract](https://www.cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6811)
- Supporting methodological point for pupil-as-brainstem-proxy: eLife 2023 (N=74) found resting pupil size optimally correlated with activity in all six ascending-arousal-system nuclei (including LC) at 0–2 s lags — [eLife 2023](https://elifesciences.org/articles/84822)
- Kounios and Beeman output 2023–2026 that I could verify: Kounios & Kounios, "The Brain Science of Elusive 'Aha! Moments'", Scientific American 332(3):20–27, Feb 2025 (popular synthesis, not new data) — [Drexel News](https://drexel.edu/news/in-the-news/2025/February/Feb-18-Kounios-Scientific-American); Beeman lab CNS 2025 poster on N1-sleep incubation (McClendon, Gomez, Beeman; n=60 design, no results in abstract) — [CNS 2025 poster](https://cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6502); Beeman is co-author on the 2026 DTI paper above.
- Ultra-high-field (7T) fMRI of Aha (Tik et al., 2018, Human Brain Mapping) reported insight-related activation in dopaminergic midbrain and NAcc [foundational, pre-2023] — [HKBU record](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/ultra-high-field-fmri-insights-on-insight-neural-correlates-of-th/)

### Inferences
- The 2004 right-aSTG gamma burst has not been overturned, but the 2023–2026 data reframe it as one component of a multi-system event; new work emphasises hippocampus + amygdala + ventral striatum scaled to Aha strength, with "certainty" (not pleasure) as the strongest single predictor of hippocampal engagement in Becker et al. 2025.
- Convergent but still indirect evidence (NAcc BOLD, SN/VTA, LC, pupil) points to a joint dopaminergic + noradrenergic signature at the moment of insight; no 2023–2026 study measured dopamine or noradrenaline directly in humans during insight.
- Across labs, the physiological signal begins 0.3–2 s before the button press (gamma at −0.3 s, pupil at −0.5 s, sustained cortical rise from −2 s), supporting the view that the solution is computed pre-consciously and that the "Aha" is a read-out of a rapid, non-linear update.

### Gaps
- I found no new primary EEG paper from Kounios's lab (2023–2026) on insight oscillations; searches returned only the 2020 NeuroImage reward paper and media coverage.
- Author list for the CNS 2026 fMRI/pupillometry abstract was not retrievable; treat as preliminary.
- Full text of the 2026 Salvi et al. DTI paper was not accessed; effect sizes unknown.
- No 2023–2026 replication of the right-aSTG gamma burst specifically was found.

## Key Question 2: Are insight solutions more accurate than analytic ones? What explains the Aha feeling (metacognitive/fluency/reward accounts)? The "eureka heuristic" and "false insight" literature

### Takeaway
Insight-labelled solutions are reliably more accurate than analytic ones across verbal, visual and spatial tasks (foundational 2016 result; replicated in 2021 and again in Becker et al. 2025 and a 2023 preregistered control), but the feeling is a fallible heuristic: it can be experimentally induced for wrong answers, inflates belief in unrelated false statements, resists warnings, and is not confined to any cognitive-style subgroup. Current explanatory accounts converge on the Aha as a precision/confidence signal generated by a (metacognitive) prediction error, with the surprise component most tightly tied to prediction error and the pleasure component to striatal reward.

### Cited Findings

**Accuracy of insight vs. analysis**
- [foundational, pre-2023] Salvi, Bricolo, Kounios, Bowden & Beeman (2016, Thinking & Reasoning): four experiments; self-reported insight solutions were more often correct than analytic solutions; on the visual (fragmented picture) task 78% of insight solutions vs 42% of analytic solutions were correct; insight solvers were more likely to time out than to give a wrong in-time answer; explanation: insight solutions emerge into consciousness in an all-or-nothing fashion once unconscious processing completes, whereas analytic answers can be premature guesses — [Salvi et al. 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5035115); [Drexel News](https://drexel.edu/news/archive/2016/March/Insight_Correctness/)
- A separate dataset (15 s limit per problem, no feedback; n=40): 91% of insight-labelled answers correct vs 81% of analytic-labelled — [University repository entry](https://boa.unimib.it/handle/10281/49774)
- Laukkonen, Ingledew, Grimmer, Schooler & Tangen (2021), "Getting a grip on insight: real-time and embodied Aha experiences predict correct solutions" (participants squeezed a dynamometer to report insight in real time): real-time feelings of insight predicted correct solutions, especially for problems requiring implicit processing; embodied intensity predicted accuracy over and above mere presence of Aha [pre-2023 but central to the 2023 theory] — [Schooler lab publication page](https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/schooler/jonathan/publications/2086)
- Becker, Sommer & Cabeza (2025): correct Mooney solutions were 65.3% high-insight vs 34.7% low-insight; incorrect solutions were 40.5% high- vs 59.5% low-insight (χ²(1)=94.98, p<0.001); the insight–accuracy link was replicated in a preregistered control experiment (28 Nov 2023) — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Chao, Hsieh & Wu (2025, Communications Psychology): misleading "fixation" cues lowered accuracy on a Japanese kanji RAT (39.5% vs 53.8%; t(696)=−10.61, d=−0.80) but did not reduce Aha rate (59.8% vs 61.8%, p=0.57), i.e., the Aha is not simply a by-product of overcoming fixation and occurs at the same rate even when accuracy drops — [Chao et al. 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11947100)

**Eureka heuristic and false insights (Laukkonen, Tangen, Grimmer)**
- Laukkonen, Webb, Salvi, Tangen, Slagter & Schooler (2023, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews 153:105363), "Insight and the selection of ideas": proposes the "Eureka heuristic" — feelings of insight select ideas from the stream of consciousness by capturing attention and conferring intuitive confidence, enabling fast action under uncertainty. Mechanism in active-inference terms: (1) implicit restructuring via Bayesian model reduction produces a higher-order prediction error (the content of the insight); (2) dopaminergic precision-weighting of that prediction error produces the feeling (confidence, pleasure, attentional capture). The account is argued to fit the phenomenology, accuracy, neural unfolding, and belief/decision effects of insight; the same heuristic can entrench false beliefs and is vulnerable under psychoactive substances and misinformation — [VU Amsterdam record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/insight-and-the-selection-of-ideas/); [SCU record](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Insight-and-the-Selection-of-Ideas/991013136978402368)
- Laukkonen (2023 book chapter), "The Adaptive Function of Insight": reviews accuracy of insights, where and why insights go wrong, and implications for delusions, false beliefs and misinformation — [SCU record](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/bookChapter/The-Adaptive-Function-of-Insight/991013372745202368)
- [foundational, 2020] Laukkonen, Kaveladze, Tangen & Schooler (Cognition 196), "The dark side of Eureka": preregistered N=300; statements paired with solved anagrams received higher truth ratings even when false, and the effect was strongest when participants reported an Aha (d=0.63) — [SCU record](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-dark-side-of-Eureka-Artificially/991013062313802368)
- Grimmer, Laukkonen, Tangen & von Hippel (2022, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review), "Eliciting false insights with semantic priming": two experiments, N=300; semantic priming plus visual similarity (e.g., MONUMENT for the anagram of MOMENTUM) produced feelings of insight for wrong anagram solutions; false insights were common but felt weaker than correct insights — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/eliciting-false-insights-with-semantic-priming/)
- Grimmer, Laukkonen, Freydenzon, von Hippel & Tangen (2022, Consciousness & Cognition), N=200: false-insight susceptibility was unrelated to schizotypy, need for cognition, jumping-to-conclusions, aberrant salience, faith in intuition, or cognitive reflection — "false insights may affect anyone under the right conditions" — [PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/illusion-of-depth/2022-grimmer.pdf)
- Grimmer, Tangen, Freydenzon & Laukkonen (2023, Cognition and Emotion 37(2)), "The illusion of insight: detailed warnings reduce but do not prevent false Aha! moments": simple warnings had no effect on false-insight rate; a detailed explanation of the manipulation produced only a small reduction — [SCU record](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-illusion-of-insight-detailed-warnings/991013093873602368)
- Grimmer PhD thesis (University of Queensland, 2023), "When 'Aha!' moments are wrong: a new paradigm for experimentally induced false insights" — [UQ record](https://business.uq.edu.au/node/13396)

**What explains the feeling: metacognitive prediction error, reward, surprise**
- Dubey, Ho, Mehta & Griffiths, "Aha! moments correspond to meta-cognitive prediction errors" (preprint 2021; listed as Cognition vol. 274, 2026): people maintain a metacognitive model of their ability and a prediction of time-to-solve; an Aha arises when the problem is solved well ahead of schedule, analogous to a positive reward prediction error ("one is more capable than previously believed"); supported by two large preregistered experiments — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/aha-moments-correspond-to-metacognitive-prediction-errors/)
- Becker, Wang & Cabeza (2024, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 31(6):2714–2723), "Surprise!—Clarifying the link between insight and prediction error": preregistered online study with verbal insight problems (CRA); Aha was positively associated with metacognitive prediction error specifically for predicted solvability; surprise was the Aha dimension that tracked the prediction error; suddenness was negatively correlated with it; pleasure and certainty showed no such relationship; authors conclude the Aha is multidimensional and that surprise is the metacognitive-PE component — [HU Berlin edoc](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/32738); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-024-02517-0)
- Becker, Yu & Cabeza (2023): positive emotion component uniquely predicts NAcc activity, consistent with a reward account of the pleasure dimension — [Scientific Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44293-2)
- Su & Ye (2025, Journal of Intelligence 13(7):83), "Temporal Synchrony in Bodily Interaction Enhances the Aha! Experience": three groups (immediate-mirror, delayed-mirror, no-interaction) with a prediction–execution–feedback riddle design; synchronous bodily interaction significantly increased Aha intensity, interpreted via implicit metacognitive predictive processing; fNIRS linked low-frequency amplitude during time-prediction to the somato-cognitive action network and a reward-network channel to Aha — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/13/7/83)
- Danek lab (TeaP 2025 conference abstract, "Metacognitive aspects of insight problem solving") exists but the page returned HTTP 403; content not verified — [TeaP 2025 abstract](https://coms.events/TeaP2025/data/abstracts/en/abstract_0078_0265_0067.html)

### Inferences
- The accuracy advantage of insight is robust in the lab, but every demonstration relies on self-labelled solutions and typically on timed, closed-answer puzzles; the false-insight paradigm shows the label can be dissociated from correctness, so "aha → correct" is a probabilistic cue (Laukkonen's "heuristic"), not a guarantee.
- The dimension structure matters: surprise maps to metacognitive prediction error (Becker 2024), pleasure to NAcc/reward (Becker 2023), certainty to hippocampus (Becker 2025). "Fluency" per se was not a primary explanatory construct in the 2023–2026 papers I found; prediction-error and precision accounts have largely replaced fluency accounts.
- Because detailed warnings barely reduce false insights, the feeling appears to be generated at a level below deliberate control, consistent with its proposed role as a fast precision signal.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis of insight-vs-analysis accuracy was found.
- Full text of Laukkonen et al. 2023 (ScienceDirect) returned 403; specific accuracy percentages cited within the review were not extracted.
- No 2024–2026 empirical papers specifically titled "false insight" from the Grimmer/Laukkonen/Tangen group were found beyond the 2023 Cognition & Emotion paper and thesis.
- Sample sizes and statistics for Becker, Wang & Cabeza 2024 were not retrievable (PDF was binary; abstract pages lack N).

## Key Question 3: How does insight restructure memory? (2025 Nature Communications hippocampal / representational-change work and the insight memory advantage)

### Takeaway
Becker, Sommer & Cabeza (2025) provide the first integrated neural account of the "insight memory advantage": solutions reached with stronger Aha were roughly twice as likely to be remembered five days later as low-insight solutions (OR ≈ 2.1) and ~7.8× more likely than unsolved items, and this advantage was statistically carried by (a) the magnitude of representational change in visual cortex (pFusG, iLOC) and (b) anterior-hippocampal activity at the moment of solution — not by amygdala activity or by network connectivity. Salvi's 2024 work shows the advantage spills over to unrelated information encoded in the seconds around an Aha.

### Cited Findings
- Becker, Sommer & Cabeza (2025, Nature Communications): memory tested 5 days later (180 images: 120 old, 60 new). Recognition 67.0% of old images (false alarms 19.3%); "correct solution + recognition" 36.0% (chance-adjusted); plus recall of having solved 32.7%; plus correct name 29.2%. Versus unsolved items, high-insight items OR=7.78 (95% CI 6.42–9.53), low-insight OR=3.72 (3.12–4.44); high vs low insight OR=2.10 (1.80–2.45); continuous insight controlling for solution time: OR=1.47 (1.33–1.61), χ²(1)=76.34, p<0.001. Recognition RTs: high-insight 1.93 s, low-insight 2.39 s, new 3.73 s — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Same study — brain predictors of memory (permutation tests, n=999): representational change (MVPS decrease) interacted with insight to predict memory (insight×memory χ²(1)=9.27, p=0.003, β=−0.07), in pFusG (χ²=5.84, p=0.014) and iLOC (χ²=4.08, p=0.038); Word2Vec semantic representational strength showed an insight×memory×time interaction (χ²(2)=10.46, p=0.004, β=0.09); anterior hippocampus activity × insight predicted memory (χ²(1)=5.68, p=0.015, β=0.09), posterior hippocampus marginal (p=0.087), amygdala n.s. (p=0.334); exploratory: suddenness×memory in anterior hippocampus (t=2.69, p-Bonf=0.028). Functional connectivity and graph metrics did not consistently predict memory — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Proposed mechanism (same paper): cognitive component = representational change converting a meaningless image into a meaningful conceptual representation (strengthening visual and semantic codes); evaluative component = sudden awareness/certainty/positive emotion engaging amygdala and anterior hippocampus; the hippocampus is cast as a mismatch / prediction-error detector and encoder; memory strengthening proportional to how unexpected the solution was. Limitations: ~18.7% of in-scanner "correct" responses may be misclassified (estimated by control experiment); few forgotten trials limited power; solution time correlated with insight and was covaried — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4); author quotes ("insight doesn't just feel good—it also helps us remember what we've learned"; Mooney images are a limitation) — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/neuroscientists-discover-how-aha-moments-rewire-the-brain-to-enhance-memory)
- Duke record of the paper (title, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-59355-4, vol. 16 art. 4341, May 2025) — [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1675032)
- Salvi, Keller, Cooper, Leiker & Dunsmoor (2024, Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience), "Aha! and D'oh! experiences enhance learning for incidental information": N=291; incidental scholastic facts were shown immediately after participants indicated they had solved a rebus puzzle but before typing the answer; memory for those facts was better when the solution was accompanied by an Aha than when it was not, extending the insight memory advantage to unrelated material encoded in the temporal window of the insight (details from indexed excerpts; full text not retrieved) — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10557988)
- Salvi (2024) chapter "The insight memory advantage" in The Emergence of Insight (Cambridge University Press; eds. Salvi, Wiley & Smith) reviews the evidence and candidate explanations — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/emergence-of-insight/emergence-of-insight-research/63E712F6E7EC1E554CEF94E9F38D85AF)
- [foundational, 2020] Danek & Wiley (Cognition), "What causes the insight memory advantage?": magic-trick solutions tested after a one-week delay; separates cognitive (generation/restructuring) and affective (Aha) contributions — [PsychArchives](https://www.psycharchives.org/handle/20.500.12034/2732)
- Older Kizilirmak work (2016, Psychological Research) found that generation and the subjective Aha feeling are independently related to learning from insight; a 2019 Consciousness & Cognition paper from the same group reported sudden-comprehension learning associated with a hippocampus-independent network — partially conflicting with the 2025 hippocampal result [pre-2023; from search summaries, URLs not individually verified]
- CNS 2026 abstract: both Aha intensity and hippocampal response predicted insight-related better memory in a delayed test (conference-level, preliminary) — [CNS 2026 abstract](https://www.cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6811)

### Inferences
- The 2025 data support a two-route model: representational change in content-specific cortex (what is remembered) plus hippocampal/amygdalar tagging at the moment of solution (how strongly). Because amygdala predicted insight but not memory while anterior hippocampus predicted both, the "emotional" component may be less central to the memory advantage than the "certainty/mismatch" component.
- Salvi 2024 implies the memory boost is a temporally extended state (arousal/dopaminergic window) rather than solely a property of the solved item — consistent with reward/novelty-driven hippocampal encoding windows.
- The earlier hippocampus-independent finding (Kizilirmak 2019) vs. the 2025 hippocampal result suggests task dependence: Mooney object recognition may recruit hippocampal mismatch detection more than verbal comprehension tasks.

### Gaps
- Exact Salvi et al. 2024 statistics and the "D'oh!" (realising a wrong answer) comparison could not be retrieved (NSF PAR fetch failed).
- No 2023–2026 study tested whether insight-induced memory changes persist beyond ~1 week or alter semantic (not episodic) memory structure.
- The 2025 paper's Methods section beyond Results (e.g., exact rating instructions) was not read in full (page truncated at 100k characters).

## Key Question 4: Impasse, fixation, forgetting of fixated ideas, and the status of Representational Change Theory (RCT)

### Takeaway
RCT remains the dominant cognitive framework, but 2023–2025 work has complicated each of its stages: restructuring is often, but not always, sudden (detectable only with non-linear statistics); impasse is multidimensional and not a necessary precondition for insight (Ross & Arfini 2024, N=856); Aha experiences and restructuring frequently dissociate (Wiley & Danek 2024 review); and new behavioral/computational work shows that overcoming fixation and experiencing insight are separable — insight tracks the breadth of search rather than de-fixation per se (Chao et al. 2025). Forgetting-fixation remains the best-supported account of incubation effects, but evidence for it is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- Wiley & Danek (2024, Nature Reviews Psychology 3:42–55, DOI 10.1038/s44159-023-00257-x), "Restructuring processes and Aha! experiences in insight problem solving": contrasts the restructuring tradition (RCT: insight defined by representational change) with the phenomenological tradition (Aha experience); reviews how often the two coincide and "the critical observation that sometimes they do not"; recommends combining methods (abstract only accessed) — [Nature Reviews Psychology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-023-00257-x); [Heidelberg record](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1910628654)
- Graf, Danek, Vaci & Bilalić (2023, Journal of Intelligence), "Tracing cognitive processes in insight problem solving: using GAMs and change point analysis to uncover restructuring": matchstick arithmetic with eye tracking; classical ANOVA cannot capture sudden representational change; only non-linear models (GAMs) and change-point analysis correctly identified the abrupt restructuring; explicit hints reorient attention in a qualitatively different manner and change restructuring dynamics — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/e0ff8278bee8442a8b63ef0abd2338eb); [Northumbria record](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/tracing-cognitive-processes-in-insight-problem-solving-using-gams/)
- Bilalić, Graf, Vaci & Danek (2021, Thinking & Reasoning), "The temporal dynamics of insight problem solving – restructuring might not always be sudden" [pre-2023 context, cited in the above]
- Ross & Arfini (2024, Cognition 246:105746), "Impasse-driven problem solving: the multidimensional nature of feeling stuck": four preregistered experiments, total N=856; new impasse measure derived from qualitative reports; impasse is dynamic (resolved vs unresolved) and multidimensional (cognitive speediness, motivation, affect); resolved and unresolved impasse differ in speediness and positive affect, not motivation; insight was reliably elicited by resolving an impasse AND occurred without any impasse — "more than one path to insight"; robust across problem types. Press coverage additionally reports that trials with fast impasse had higher solution and insight rates, and conflicts with the abstract on whether motivation differed — [London Met repository](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/9186/); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/new-psychology-research-reveals-the-hidden-benefits-of-hitting-a-mental-wall)
- Chao, Hsieh & Wu (2025, Communications Psychology), "Long-distance exploration in insightful problem-solving": Exp 1 N=349 (kanji RAT with fixation vs neutral cues), Exp 2 N=105 thought-tracing version (every candidate typed; semantic distance computed from a 1.1-billion-word corpus); simulation model with fixation strength, de-fixation and exploration-capacity parameters (1,975,000 runs). Aha trials had greater mean exploration distance than no-Aha trials (5.7 vs 5.4; t(868)=3.53, p=0.0004, d=0.24); number of thoughts was unrelated to Aha (r=−0.002); fixation cues reduced accuracy (d=−0.80) and slowed RT (18.5 vs 14.7 s) but left Aha rate unchanged; simulation: greater exploration capacity reproduced Aha-like patterns. Conclusion: de-fixation matters for solving but does not determine whether an insight occurs; insight is associated with broader exploration, not more ideas — [Chao et al. 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11947100)
- Savinova & Korovkin (2023, Experimental Psychology (Russia)): insight problems requiring representational change were more demanding on working memory than hypothesis-production problems, and WM load increased closer to the final answer — [PsyJournals](https://psyjournals.ru/en/journals/exppsy/archive/2023_n4/Savinova_Korovkin)
- Salvi, Wiley & Smith (2024, chapter in The Emergence of Insight), "Past and future of research on so-called incubation effects": the term "incubation" wrongly implies unconscious work; forgetting of fixating responses can occur via temporal delay, retrieval inhibition, or context shifts, each producing incubation effects; recommends studying the moments just before insight when fixation has weakened — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/emergence-of-insight/past-and-future-of-research-on-socalled-incubation-effects/4ADD1527830639E05DFB4D4E4A5766E2)
- Counter-evidence to forgetting-fixation [2020, context]: Valueva & Lapteva found incubation did not change the number of fixations, concluding the theory is an inaccurate account of mechanism even though incubation helped solvers escape inappropriate sets — [HSE Psychology Journal abstracts 2020](https://psy-journal.hse.ru/en/2020-17-4-abstracts.html)
- Incubation and the Aha [context]: in a CRA study, initial fixation did not predict post-incubation improvement, and participants were more likely to report insight on problems solved after incubation, suggesting a shift to a more implicit, semantic-integration strategy — [MetaTOC record](https://metatoc.com/papers/53597-the-experience-of-insight-follows-incubation-in-the-compound-remote-associates-task)
- [foundational] Danek et al. (2014, Frontiers in Psychology): magic-trick paradigm; 41% of suggested solutions were accompanied by an Aha; Danek's 2017 work established that Aha can accompany incorrect solutions ("false insights") in 70 participants solving 37 tricks — [Frontiers 2014](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01408/text)
- Danek's 2024 BIAL symposium slides ("Breaking Barriers: Emerging Topics in Creativity and Insight Research") summarise the Wiley & Danek 2024 position — [BIAL PDF](https://www.fundacaobial.com/media/4809/breaking-barriers_amory-danek.pdf)

### Inferences
- RCT's core claim — that insight involves a change of problem representation — is still supported (eye-tracking change points; Mooney representational change in VOTC; verbal-description change in Chesebrough 2023), but its corollaries (necessary impasse; sudden restructuring; restructuring ⇔ Aha) are each now qualified by 2023–2025 data.
- Chao et al. 2025 and Ross & Arfini 2024 jointly suggest two dissociable processes: escaping fixation (which raises accuracy) and wide/long-distance search (which produces the Aha). This dovetails with Salvi's 2026 DTI finding that insight propensity relates to less-constrained left-hemisphere semantic pathways.
- The field is moving from "was there an impasse?" to continuous, trial-level dynamics measured with eye tracking, thought tracing, and non-linear statistics.

### Gaps
- Full text of Wiley & Danek 2024 was paywalled; the specific percentages of Aha–restructuring co-occurrence they report were not retrieved.
- No 2024–2026 empirical paper directly testing forgetting-fixation with modern methods was found; the strongest recent treatment is a 2024 review chapter.
- No 2024–2025 Danek empirical paper on representational change was found by search (the TeaP 2025 abstract returned 403).

## Key Question 5: Predictive processing / Bayesian-brain accounts of insight (insight as sudden precision-weighted model update)

### Takeaway
Between 2023 and 2026, predictive processing became the leading theoretical frame for insight. Two complementary versions exist: (a) Laukkonen et al. (2023) — insight = Bayesian model reduction producing a higher-order prediction error, with dopaminergic precision-weighting generating the feeling; (b) Dubey/Griffiths and Becker/Cabeza — the Aha is a metacognitive prediction error (solved sooner/when not expected). Empirical tests so far support the metacognitive-PE version for the surprise dimension (Becker et al. 2024), link the hippocampus (a mismatch detector) and striatum to Aha strength (Becker 2023, 2025), and show that increasing the non-linearity of the solution update increases Aha intensity (CNS 2026 abstract). Direct neural tests of precision-weighting remain lacking.

### Cited Findings
- Laukkonen et al. (2023, Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev.): two-step active-inference mechanism — implicit restructuring via Bayesian reduction → higher-order prediction error (content); dopaminergic precision-weighting → confidence, pleasure, attentional capture (feeling); described as "insight as precision"; predicts vulnerability to false beliefs, psychoactive substances and misinformation — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/insight-and-the-selection-of-ideas/)
- Earlier predictive-processing work by Laukkonen & Slagter proposed that meditation-related insight experiences arise through Bayesian model reduction [pre-2023 context, cited in search results] — [SCU profile](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/profile/ruben_laukkonen)
- Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria (2025, Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev.), "A Beautiful Loop": active-inference theory of consciousness (predictions turning back on themselves); not an insight paper, but the same group's current theoretical substrate; the profile notes a programme on "insight and belief updating — how restructuring changes what a system models" — [theconsciousness.ai profile](https://theconsciousness.ai/researchers/ruben-laukkonen/)
- Dubey, Ho, Mehta & Griffiths (Cognition 274, 2026; preprint 2021): metacognitive model of ability + predicted time-to-solve; Aha = problem cracked well ahead of schedule, compared to a positive reward prediction error; two large preregistered experiments — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/aha-moments-correspond-to-metacognitive-prediction-errors/)
- Becker, Wang & Cabeza (2024, PB&R): preregistered test — Aha positively associated with metacognitive PE for predicted solvability; surprise tracks PE; suddenness negatively correlated with PE; pleasure and certainty unrelated; conclusion links insight to learning theory — [edoc HU Berlin](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/32738)
- Becker, Sommer & Cabeza (2025): hippocampus proposed as mismatch / prediction-error detector and encoder; memory strengthening proportional to how unexpected the solution was; anterior hippocampus tracked certainty (t=6.77, β=0.17) and suddenness×memory (p-Bonf=0.028) — [Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)
- Becker's Nov 2024 FU Berlin talk framed Aha as "cognitive and metacognitive prediction errors generating surprise, reward, and confidence" — [FU Berlin seminar page](https://www.ewi-psy.fu-berlin.de/en/psychologie/einrichtungen/ccnb/seminar/2024_11_11_Maxi-Becker.html)
- CNS 2026 abstract: manipulating the reveal schedule to make the solution update more non-linear (late rapid reveal vs gradual) reliably increased Aha intensity; neural signal rose from ~2 s pre-response in object-selective and prefrontal regions with phasic pupil dilation; interpreted as a "catecholamine-synchronized solution update" — [CNS 2026 abstract](https://www.cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6811)
- Su & Ye (2025, J. Intelligence): "implicit metacognitive predictive processing" — synchronous bodily interaction during the prediction stage altered subsequent Aha intensity; framed as first empirical test of this mechanism for embodied effects — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/13/7/83)
- Chesebrough et al. (2023): Aha strength followed an inverted-U with subjective difficulty and with confidence — consistent with a prediction-error account in which Aha is maximal when a solution arrives against moderate (not extreme) expectations of failure — [Drexel repository](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Conceptual-Change-Induced-by-Analogical-Reasoning/991020228261604721)

### Inferences
- The two PP accounts are compatible: Laukkonen's "content" PE (restructured model) and Dubey/Becker's metacognitive PE (about one's own solving) can be two levels of the same hierarchical update; the surprise dimension indexes the latter, while certainty/pleasure may index precision and reward respectively.
- The negative correlation between suddenness and metacognitive PE (Becker 2024) is a notable wrinkle: very sudden solutions may be ones the solver did not have time to form a pessimistic prediction about, so PE-based theories must specify when the prediction is formed.
- Precision-weighting is still inferred from indirect proxies (NAcc BOLD, pupil, SN/VTA, LC); no study has pharmacologically manipulated dopamine or noradrenaline during insight.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 study directly measured "precision" (e.g., via computational model fitting of confidence to neural gain) during insight.
- Full text of Dubey et al. (Cognition 2026) and its two experiments' statistics were not retrieved.
- I found no formal computational implementation (e.g., active-inference simulation) of insight restructuring published 2023–2026, other than Chao et al.'s search-model simulation.

## Key Question 6: How mood, sleep, psychedelics, dopamine and noradrenaline modulate insight and idea generation

### Takeaway
Positive mood facilitates insight solving (foundational 2009 finding; replicated in Chesebrough 2023 as stronger Aha). Sleep onset (N1) roughly triples insight on a hidden-rule task (83% vs 30%; 2021), but 2023–2026 N1 work is still at the tool/poster stage and full-night sleep effects remain mixed. Psilocybin acutely increases spontaneous "creative insights" while impairing deliberate creativity; pooled microdosing trials (N≈171) improve originality-to-fluency ratio but not fluency or convergent thinking; a 2026 Nature Communications study (N=28) links acute brain entropy under 25 mg psilocybin to next-day psychological insight and one-month cognitive flexibility. Dopamine (NAcc/SN-VTA) and noradrenaline (pupil/LC) signatures accompany Aha, but only correlationally.

### Cited Findings

**Mood**
- [foundational] Subramaniam, Kounios, Parrish & Jung-Beeman (2009, J. Cognitive Neuroscience): among 79 participants, higher positive mood → more problems solved and more solved by insight; mediated by anterior cingulate activity during the preparatory interval; anxiety showed a weaker opposite effect — [Drexel repository](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-Brain-Mechanism-for-Facilitation-of/991014878237204721)
- Shen et al. (2019, Thinking Skills and Creativity): induced negative mood increased probability of analytic solutions; spontaneous positive mood facilitated performance regardless of strategy [pre-2023 context] — [Leiden repository PDF](https://openaccess.leidenuniv.nl/bitstream/handle/1887/77956/Shen_et_al_2019_T.pdf?sequence=1)
- Chesebrough et al. (2023): participants in a more positive mood rated Aha experiences as stronger — [Drexel repository](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Conceptual-Change-Induced-by-Analogical-Reasoning/991020228261604721)
- Valueva & Lapteva (2023, Psychology, HSE): mood-inducing videos before anagram tasks improved mood and reduced anxiety/fatigue; authors argue emotion–insight links depend on the type of insight and attentional focus — [HSE Psychology Journal](https://psy-journal.hse.ru/en/2023-20-3/859948525.html)

**Sleep and incubation**
- [foundational, 2021] Lacaux et al. (Science Advances, Oudiette lab), "Sleep onset is a creative sweet spot": N=103; number-reduction task with hidden rule; ≥15 s of N1 during a 20-min "Edison nap" raised the probability of discovering the rule to 83% vs 30% for those who stayed awake; benefit vanished if participants reached N2 — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8654287)
- Horowitz, Maes, Stickgold et al. (Scientific Reports, 2023): targeted dream incubation during N1 using the Dormio device extended N1 benefits to creative storytelling (details from MIT summary) — [MIT Media Lab](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/posts/dreams-and-creativity/)
- Frontiers in Sleep (28 May 2024, Duke-led): a remote, sensor-free tool for incubating hypnagogic dreams/mind-wandering (methods paper) — [Frontiers in Sleep 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2024.1258345/pdf)
- McClendon, Gomez & Beeman (CNS 2025 poster): n=60; matchstick puzzles, AUT, tower task before/after N1-sleep incubation (headband with serial awakenings) vs control; no results in abstract — [CNS 2025 poster](https://cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6502)
- Full-night sleep (mixed, pre-2023 context): Sanders, Osburn, Paller & Beeman (2019, Psychological Science): targeted memory reactivation with sound cues during sleep → 31.7% of cued puzzles solved next morning vs 20.5% uncued — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797619873344); 2021 Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience: 62% of sleep group vs 24% of wake group solved; TMR added nothing — [Frontiers 2021](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2021.645110/text); 2018 Frontiers in Human Neuroscience (N=77, insight problems + magic tricks): sleep affected neither solution rates nor number of Aha solutions — [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00072/full)

**Psychedelics**
- [foundational, 2021] Mason et al. (Translational Psychiatry; Maastricht): placebo-controlled; psilocybin 0.17 mg/kg acutely increased ratings of spontaneous creative insights while decreasing deliberate task-based creativity; more novel ideas one week later; effects predicted by DMN within/between-network connectivity — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8032715/)
- Prochazkova, Marschall, van Elk, Rifkin, Schon, Fiacchino, Fejer, Kuchar & Hommel (Neuropharmacology; DOI 2025, coverage Jan 2026), "Microdosing psilocybin and its effect on creativity: lessons learned from three double-blind placebo-controlled longitudinal trials": pooled mega-analysis N≈171 (trial Ns 59, 61, 27; truffle doses ~0.65 g, ~1 g, ~1.5 g; ≈0.74–1.71 mg psilocybin); no improvement in convergent thinking or fluency; higher originality-to-fluency ratio under psilocybin, surviving control for guessed condition; dose/kg predicted originality; Exp 2 alone did not replicate Exp 1; blinding broke in Exp 3 — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/major-new-study-finds-psilocybin-microdoses-improve-the-quality-of-creative-ideas-but-not-the-quantity/)
- Lyons, Spriggs, ... Nutt & Carhart-Harris (Nature Communications, May 2026), "Human brain changes after first psilocybin use": 28 psychedelic-naive adults (mean age 41); fixed order 1 mg (placebo) then 25 mg one month later; 25 mg robustly increased EEG signal entropy within 60 min; acute entropy predicted next-day psychological insight, which predicted well-being at 2 and 4 weeks; one month post-dose: improved cognitive flexibility (computer tasks), decreased axial diffusivity in prefrontal–subcortical tracts correlating with reduced network modularity; none of these with 1 mg; 27/28 rated it the most unusual conscious state of their lives. Limitations: fixed order (practice effects), N=28, ambiguous DTI meaning; critics question entropy as a reliable marker — [PsyPost](https://psypost.org/psychedelic-science-breakthrough-increased-brain-entropy-from-psilocybin-predicts-lasting-psychological-insight-and-well-being); [Science Media Centre Spain](https://sciencemediacentre.es/en/single-dose-psilocybin-produces-brain-changes-lasting-month-after-experience)
- Preclinical: acute psilocybin improved set-shifting (cognitive flexibility) in rats without affecting appetitive/aversive learning (2023) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10209151/)
- Laukkonen et al. (2023) explicitly flag psychoactive substances as a condition under which the Eureka heuristic may misfire (insights feel compelling without being accurate) — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/insight-and-the-selection-of-ideas/)

**Dopamine and noradrenaline**
- Dopamine (indirect): NAcc BOLD scales with insight strength and specifically with its pleasure component (Becker, Yu & Cabeza 2023); SN/VTA and NAcc Aha-scaled responses (CNS 2026 abstract); orbitofrontal gamma reward burst ~100 ms after insight in reward-sensitive individuals (Oh et al. 2020) — [Scientific Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44293-2); [CNS 2026](https://www.cogneurosociety.org/poster/?id=6811); [Drexel 2020](https://drexel.edu/news/archive/2020/april/creative-insight-triggers-neural-reward)
- Noradrenaline (indirect): pupil dilation ~500 ms before insight but not analytic solutions (Salvi 2020); phasic non-linear pupil increase plus Aha-scaled locus coeruleus response (CNS 2026); Suzuki et al. (2018, Scientific Reports) pupil dilation tracks prior awareness of object recognition in ambiguous images, attributed to LC-NA — [Salvi 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7440842); [Suzuki 2018, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5931995/)
- Risk-taking downstream of insight (behavioral marker of a reward state): 38% vs 33% risky choices after high vs low insight — [Scientific Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44293-2)

### Inferences
- The state-manipulation literature (mood, N1 sleep, psilocybin) consistently shifts the balance from constrained/deliberate to broad/spontaneous processing — the same axis implicated by Chao et al. 2025 (exploration distance) and Salvi 2026 (less-constrained semantic white matter). This offers a unifying "breadth of search" interpretation of why these states raise Aha frequency without necessarily raising accuracy (psilocybin and microdosing improve originality, not convergent accuracy).
- Dopaminergic and noradrenergic involvement is plausible and multiply convergent but remains correlational; the field needs pharmacological or pupil-linked causal designs.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 peer-reviewed study found on mood induction and insight; the newest is 2023 (Russian-language journal) and Chesebrough 2023 (mood as covariate).
- No peer-reviewed 2023–2026 results paper found testing N1 sleep on classic insight problems (only tools and a poster).
- Exact journal (Neuropharmacology vs Neuropsychopharmacology) and publication date of the Prochazkova pooled paper are inconsistent across sources; verify at the DOI.
- No human study 2023–2026 pharmacologically manipulated dopamine or noradrenaline during insight problem solving.
- The 2026 psilocybin paper's "psychological insight" is a self-report construct (PIQ-type), not problem-solving insight; the two should not be conflated.
