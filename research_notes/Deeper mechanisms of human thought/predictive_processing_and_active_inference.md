# Predictive Processing, the Bayesian Brain and Active Inference as Accounts of Thought, Imagination and New Ideas (emphasis 2023–2026)

Scope note: an earlier round covered DMN/ECN creativity networks, mind-wandering dynamics, insight/aha (incl. Laukkonen's Eureka heuristic and Bayesian-model-reduction account of insight), semantic networks and practical modulators. Those are only cross-referenced here. Throughout, items are tagged **[theory]**, **[simulation]**, **[human empirical]**, **[animal]**, or **[AI/engineering]** so the writer can separate claims from evidence. Items before 2023 are labelled "(foundational, pre-window)".

## 1. Current formulations of predictive processing / active inference applied to cognition and thought; active inference as a theory of planning, imagination and offline thought

### Takeaway
The 2023–2026 formulations treat thinking as *planning as inference over counterfactual futures*: a hierarchical, temporally deep generative model rolls out imagined policies, scores them by expected free energy (EFE = expected information gain + expected value), and selects among them; offline thought (rest, dreaming, replay) is cast as the same machinery run without sensory input to optimise the model itself (reduce complexity, build compositional hypotheses). This is a mature theoretical programme with strong simulation support, but the specifically "thought"-level claims (imagination, counterfactual reasoning, offline model improvement) rest mainly on theory, simulations and indirect neural evidence (replay/MEG), not on direct human tests of active-inference thinking.

### Cited Findings
- **Canonical statement (2024) [theory].** Pezzulo, Parr & Friston's review "Active inference as a theory of sentient behavior" (Biological Psychology, 2024) defines the framework as the claim that "sentient behavior depends upon our brains' implicit use of internal models to predict, infer, and direct action," and names the key developments as predictive-coding message passing, "the use of sequential models for planning and policy optimization, and the importance of hierarchical (temporally) deep internal (i.e., generative or world) models"; psychopathology is cast as "aberrant precision control" — [Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2024, Biol Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2023.108741)
- **Thinking = counterfactual planning scored by EFE (Dec 2025) [theory + simulation].** Friston, Da Costa, Tschantz, Heins, Buckley, Verbelen & Parr, "Active inference and artificial reasoning" (arXiv 2512.21129, 24 Dec 2025): actions are chosen to "sample outcomes that reveal the most about the structure of the underlying world model", policies are selected by EFE combining expected information gain and value, information gain is defined as "the KL divergence between predictive posteriors with, and without, the consequences of action", posteriors over candidate models are computed quickly via Bayesian model reduction, and the scheme is demonstrated on a partially observed discrete "three-ball" task previously used to describe "artificial insight and 'aha moments' through synthetic introspection or sleep". No quantitative results are given in the abstract — [Friston et al. 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21129)
- **Sophisticated inference = beliefs about beliefs, deep tree search over belief states (foundational, 2021) [theory + simulation].** Friston, Da Costa, Hafner, Hesp & Parr show that a recursive EFE "effectively implements a deep tree search over actions and outcomes in the future", moving from "what would happen if I did that" to "what I would believe about what would happen if I did that"; the search is "over sequences of belief states as opposed to states per se" — [Friston et al. 2021, Neural Computation](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01351)
- **Offline thought as model optimisation (foundational, 2021) [theory].** Pezzulo, Zorzi & Corbetta propose that spontaneous activity in rest and dreaming "underlies top-down dynamics of generative models"; when stimuli are weak or absent, "top-down dynamics optimize the generative models for future interactions by maximizing the entropy of explanations and minimizing model complexity", and spontaneous fluctuations reflect transitions between "generic priors" (low-dimensional latent variables) — [Pezzulo, Zorzi & Corbetta 2021, TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2021.05.007)
- **Mental action and cognitive effort formalised (2023) [theory + simulation].** Parr, Holmes, Friston & Pezzulo model Stroop performance as *covert* (mental) action: "Mental actions change our beliefs but have no (direct) effect on the outside world—much like deploying covert attention"; the model generates choice, RT and electrophysiological data and the effort parameters are recoverable from performance data "provided they are within a certain range" — [Parr et al. 2023, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2023.108562)
- **Generative replay as imagination/future thinking (2024) [simulation, biologically constrained].** Spens & Burgess (Nature Human Behaviour, 2024): hippocampal replay from an autoassociative network trains neocortical generative models (VAEs) whose latent variables sit in entorhinal, medial prefrontal and anterolateral temporal cortex; simulations "provide mechanisms for semantic memory, imagination, episodic future thinking, relational inference and schema-based distortions including boundary extension" — [Spens & Burgess 2024, Nat Hum Behav](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01799-z)
- **Replay as compositional hypothesis generation (2023) [human empirical, fMRI + MEG, plus theory].** Schwartenbeck, Baram, Liu, Mark, Muller, Dolan, Botvinick, Kurth-Nelson & Behrens show with fMRI that scene representations in the frontal-hippocampal circuit are "relational and compositional", and with MEG that rapid replay-like sequences "form constructive hypotheses about possible scene configurations", playing out "in an optimal order for relational inference, progressing from predictable to uncertain scene elements" — a role for "generative replay in constructive inference and hypothesis testing" (bioRxiv 2021; published in Cell 2023) — [Schwartenbeck et al., bioRxiv/Cell](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.06.06.447249)
- **Replay as computation producing "qualitatively new knowledge" (2023) [theory].** Kurth-Nelson, Behrens, Wayne, Miller, Luettgau, Dolan, Liu & Schwartenbeck (Neuron, 2023) hypothesise that replay "is able to implement a form of compositional computation where entities are assembled into relationally bound structures to derive qualitatively new knowledge", with the hippocampus binding objects to generalisable roles and replay stringing "role-bound objects into compound statements" — [Kurth-Nelson et al. 2023, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.12.028)
- **Imagination and perception share one signal axis (2023) [human empirical].** Dijkstra & Fleming (Nature Communications, 2023), using one-trial-per-participant psychophysics, modelling and neuroimaging, find "imagined and perceived signals are in fact intermixed, with judgments of reality being determined by whether this intermixed signal is strong enough to cross a reality threshold"; sufficiently strong imagined signals "become subjectively indistinguishable from reality" — [Dijkstra & Fleming 2023, Nat Commun](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37322-1)
- **Hierarchical, long-range prediction in human cortex during language (2023) [human empirical, fMRI, N = 304].** Caucheteux, Gramfort & King show that enhancing language models with predictions spanning multiple timescales improves brain mapping, and that "frontoparietal cortices predict higher-level, longer-range and more contextual representations than temporal cortices" — [Caucheteux et al. 2023, Nat Hum Behav](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01516-2)
- **Clark (2023) [theory/popular].** *The Experience Machine* (Pantheon, 2023) presents experience as "controlled hallucination", treats perception and imagery as drawing on the same predictive machinery, and extends PP to depression, chronic pain and "aberrant predictions" — [Penguin Random House listing](https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/isbn/9781524748456); [Marginalian review](https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/06/20/the-experience-machine-andy-clark/). Clark (2025) also published "Extending Minds with Generative AI" (Nature Communications) — [Clark 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59906-9)
- **Hohwy (2023–2026) [theory].** Hohwy co-edited *Expected Experiences: The Predictive Mind in an Uncertain World* (Routledge, 2024) — [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Expected-Experiences-The-Predictive-Mind-in-an-Uncertain-World/Cheng-Sato-Hohwy/p/book/9780367540197); co-authored "Accelerating scientific progress through Bayesian adversarial collaboration" (Neuron, 2023) — [Corcoran, Hohwy & Friston 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.08.027); and with Whyte, Parr, Friston, Seth and others "On the minimal theory of consciousness implicit in active inference" (Physics of Life Reviews, 2026), which extracts "a small set of theoretical commitments that point to a minimal (and testable) theory of consciousness" — [Whyte et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2025.11.002)
- **Seth group (2023) [theory + simulation].** Tschantz, Millidge, Seth & Buckley's "Hybrid predictive coding: Inferring, fast and slow" (PLOS Comput Biol, 2023) proposes that the feedforward sweep performs *amortized* inference and recurrent processing *iterative* inference, unified as "a dual optimization of a single objective function" — [Tschantz et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011280). Seth's 2025 "biological naturalism" (BBS target) is critiqued by Baltieri & Kanai's commentary "Steam-engine naturalism" (BBS 2026) — [Baltieri & Kanai 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x25103531)
- **Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria (2025) "A beautiful loop" [theory].** Proposes three conditions for consciousness under active inference — a simulated world model ("epistemic field"), "inferential competition to enter the world model" where "only the inferences that coherently reduce long-term uncertainty win" ("Bayesian binding"), and "epistemic depth" — and formally "a hyper-model for precision-control" whose latent states encode "the overall structure and weighting rules for all layers of inference", described as enacting "the epistemic agency and flexibility reminiscent of general intelligence" — [Laukkonen et al. 2025, PsyArXiv](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/daf5n_v1)
- **Narrative as active inference (2024) [theory].** Bouizegarene, Ramstead, Constant, Friston & Kirmayer argue narratives function "to generate more useful (i.e., accurate, parsimonious) predictions" and are "for the future—even when they are focused on recollecting or recounting the past" — [Bouizegarene et al. 2024, Front Psychol](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1345480)
- **Older critique relevant to imagination claims (pre-window).** Jones & Wilkinson (2020) argue PP proponents (Hohwy 2013, Clark 2016) "conflate imagery and imagination": PP may explain imagery (offline generation of the predictive hypothesis by down-weighting prediction error) but "imagery is not sufficient for imagination, nor, arguably, is it even necessary" — [Cambridge Handbook of the Imagination chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-the-imagination/from-prediction-to-imagination/ADDB8048880BB1BDEF441EBFBCA97D28); a 2023 ENS seminar set out what a PP account of imagination would still need — [ENS seminar page](https://cognition.ens.fr/en/agenda/predictive-processing-imagery-and-imagination-17434)

### Inferences
- Across these sources, "thinking" in the active-inference sense decomposes into three operations that recur in the formal papers: (i) rollout of counterfactual policies through a temporally deep generative model; (ii) scoring each rollout by EFE (information gain + preference satisfaction); (iii) offline refinement of the model itself (complexity reduction / BMR, compositional recombination via replay). This is a usable procedural skeleton.
- The best human evidence for "offline thought as hypothesis generation" is the MEG replay work (Schwartenbeck 2023; Kurth-Nelson 2023), which shows ordered hypothesis sequences (predictable to uncertain elements). That ordering principle (resolve what is most constrained first, then the uncertain remainder) is a specific, testable heuristic.
- The imagery/imagination distinction remains unresolved: PP convincingly explains sensory imagery (Dijkstra & Fleming), but propositional/counterfactual imagination is handled only by the sophisticated-inference formalism, which has simulation support but no direct human test.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 human experiment was found that directly tests sophisticated (recursive) inference or counterfactual tree search as a model of human reasoning; the recursive-EFE account is simulation-only.
- Pezzulo's 2024 publication list includes "Neural representation in active inference" and other titles whose abstracts I could not retrieve (CNR repository returned titles only) — [CNR IRIS listing](https://iris.cnr.it/cris/rp/rp13232?type=all).
- Hohwy's and Seth's 2023–2026 output located here concerns consciousness and adversarial collaboration, not thought/idea generation specifically; I found no Hohwy or Seth paper in-window on imagination or hypothesis generation.

## 2. Epistemic foraging, expected free energy and curiosity; 2023–2026 human experiments on information- and novelty-seeking

### Takeaway
Under active inference, curiosity is the epistemic (information-gain) term of expected free energy, and simulations show it produces systematic exploration and "insight". Human studies from 2022–2026 give partial support (active-inference models beat RL in some bandit/two-step datasets; EEG correlates of EFE in frontal pole; pupil tracks information gain), but the largest, most careful 2025 behavioural test found human exploration best explained by *novelty*, not information gain or surprise, and other work shows information demand is multidimensional — so the EFE information-gain term is at best one component of human curiosity.

### Cited Findings
- **Formal curiosity and insight (foundational, 2017) [simulation].** Friston, Lin, Frith, Pezzulo, Hobson & Ondobaka: minimising expected free energy "leads to active sampling of novel contingencies", which "closes explanatory gaps in generative models"; moving to structure learning, "abductive processes emerge when agents test plausible hypotheses about symmetries (i.e., invariances or rules)", and "the ensuing Bayesian model reduction evinces mechanisms associated with sleep and has all the hallmarks of 'aha' moments"; focus is on insight "using just a handful of observations" — [Friston et al. 2017, Neural Computation](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00999)
- **Is exploration really implied by free-energy minimisation? (foundational, 2021) [theory].** Millidge, Tschantz & Buckley show EFE "is not simply 'the free energy in the future'"; the natural extension of variational free energy into the future "actively discourages exploratory behavior, thus demonstrating that exploration does not directly follow from free energy minimization into the future"; they propose an alternative objective (FEEF) that retains the epistemic term — [Millidge et al. 2021, Neural Computation](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01354)
- **Novelty, not information gain, drives human exploration (2025) [human empirical].** Modirshanechi, Lin, Xu, Herzog & Gerstner (PNAS, 25 Sept 2025): in a multistep task with "a highly stochastic but reward-free subregion", participants "persistently explore the stochastic subregion, and their decisions are best explained by a novelty-driven exploration strategy, compared to alternatives driven by information gain or surprise"; conclusion: "novelty and extrinsic rewards jointly control human exploration" — [Modirshanechi et al. 2025, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2502193122). The 2023 CCN poster version reported N = 21 humans and 500 simulated agents per reward type — [CCN 2023 poster](https://ic-services.epfl.ch/posters/appdemosposters_get/%7Bdcc47b20-f02f-47d6-90e3-63947a6183b3%7D/CCN2023_Modirshanechi.pdf/)
- **Review of curiosity mechanisms (2023) [review].** Modirshanechi, Kondrakiewicz, Gerstner & Haesler (Trends in Neurosciences, 2023) note "no single, widely accepted definition or framework for curiosity has emerged", group studies into three types of information seeking, and argue ML models of curiosity can integrate them — [Modirshanechi et al. 2023, TINS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2023.10.002)
- **Active inference beats RL under novelty/variability; EFE localised (2025) [human empirical, EEG, N = 25].** Zhang, Tian, Liu & Wu (eLife, 2025), contextual two-armed bandit: "active inference better explains human decision-making under novelty and variability"; source-level EEG indicates "the expected free energy is encoded in the frontal pole and middle frontal gyrus", with novelty linked to frontal/central/parietal activity and variability to frontal/central activity — [Zhang et al. 2025, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.92892)
- **Mixed support in two-step tasks (2022, pre-window) [human empirical, re-analysis of four datasets].** Gijsen, Grundei & Blankenburg: datasets showing "more model-based inference and behaviour indicative of directed exploration" were better fit by active inference than RL, others tied; "approximately half of all participants showed sensitivity to information gain as formulated under active inference" — [Gijsen et al. 2022, Sci Rep](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-21766-4)
- **Empowerment as intrinsic motive in open-ended creation (2023) [human empirical, N = 29,493 players].** Brändle, Stocks, Tenenbaum, Gershman & Schulz (Nature Human Behaviour): in "Little Alchemy 2" (4 starting elements, up to 720 objects), players show "an intrinsic motivation to create objects that empower them to create even more objects"; the drive "is eliminated when playing a game variant that lacks recognizable semantics" — [Brändle et al. 2023, Nat Hum Behav](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01661-2)
- **Information demand is low-dimensional and only loosely tied to "curiosity" traits (2024) [human empirical, N = 820].** Jach, Cools et al. (PNAS, 2024), 15 trait measures and 9 cognitive tasks: shared task variance was "a dimension reflecting directed (uncertainty-driven) versus random (stochasticity-driven) exploration"; the best trait predictors were "need for cognition, thrill seeking, and stress tolerance" and "extraversion and behavioral inhibition", not openness or joyous exploration — [Jach et al. 2024, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2415236121)
- **Curiosity as "common currency" with learning-progress dynamics (2024) [review/theory].** Poli, O'Reilly, Mars & Hunnius (TICS, 2024) map "the temporal dynamics of various factors underlying curiosity-based exploration, such as uncertainty, information gain, and learning progress" and propose curiosity as "a 'common currency' for exploration" balanced against safety and hunger — [Poli et al. 2024, TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.02.001)
- **Learning progress (foundational, 2021) [human empirical].** Ten, Kaushik, Oudeyer & Gottlieb: in free-choice task selection "humans rely on competence information to avoid easy tasks", but "models that include a learning-progress component provide the best fit" — [Ten et al. 2021, Nat Commun](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26196-w)
- **Rational curiosity reconciles novelty vs. intermediate complexity (foundational, 2020) [theory + experiments].** Dubey & Griffiths: "the causal structure of the environment can determine whether curiosity is driven by either highly uncertain or moderately uncertain stimuli" — [Dubey & Griffiths 2020, Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000175)
- **Shared novelty-seeking basis for creativity and curiosity (BBS 2024) [theory].** Ivancovsky, Baror & Bar propose both "emanate from the same mechanism of novelty seeking", governed by "state of mind", via DMN/salience/ECN interplay; 29 commentaries; cites a meta-analysis of 10 studies with a positive curiosity–creativity correlation — [Ivancovsky et al. 2024, BBS 47:e89](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/shared-noveltyseeking-basis-for-creativity-and-curiosity/F812089A4E78C25A4A01C86EB2C873A1); authors' response e119 — [response](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/a-shared-novelty-seeking-basis-for-creativity-and-curiosity-respo-2/)
- **"Epistemic arc" (2024) [theory].** Van de Cruys, Frascaroli & Friston (Phil Trans B, 2024) cast aesthetic/sense-making episodes as an "epistemic arc" of "curiosity, epistemic action and aha experiences", and defend the account against the objection that it over-emphasises closure over "openness and lingering uncertainty" — [Van de Cruys et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2022.0411)
- **Pupil tracks information gain (2026) [human empirical].** Colizoli, van Leeuwen, Rutar & Bekkering (eLife, 2026), two perceptual-decision datasets: "a significant proportion of variability in the post-feedback pupil response was explained by information gain" (KL divergence posterior vs prior of an ideal observer), and "whether the pupil dilates or constricts along with information gain was context dependent" — [Colizoli et al. 2026, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.105287)
- **Visual foraging (foundational, 2018) [human empirical].** Mirza et al. found "substantial evidence for epistemic foraging in the visual exploration of even simple scenes" but had to add heuristic (left-to-right) policies; better categorisation went with less heuristic reliance — [Mirza et al. 2018, PLOS ONE](https://iris.sissa.it/retrieve/dd8a4bf7-e5b0-20a0-e053-d805fe0a8cb0/journal.pone.0190429.pdf)
- **Deep active-inference agent with EFE fails to explore (2024) [AI simulation].** Champion, Grześ, Bonheme & Bowman: in dSprites, "the CHMM minimizing expected free energy almost always picks the same action, which makes it unable to solve the dSprites environment", while the reward-maximising variant succeeds; "the only difference ... is the epistemic value" — [Champion et al. 2024, Neural Computation](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01697)

### Inferences
- The human literature converges on *multiple* intrinsic drives (novelty, information gain, learning progress, empowerment) with task-dependent dominance; a faithful "human-like" idea-seeking procedure should therefore combine a novelty bonus (count-based, Modirshanechi), a learning-progress signal (Ten), and an empowerment/affordance-expansion heuristic (Brändle), rather than information gain alone.
- The Brändle result (empowerment disappears without recognisable semantics) suggests that generative exploration in humans is guided by world knowledge about what combinations *could* enable; this is a direct design hint for idea-generation procedures: prefer moves that increase the number of future possible moves.
- Pupil/LC evidence (Colizoli 2026) supports information gain as a physiologically signalled quantity even if it is not the dominant *choice* driver.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 human study that directly pits EFE-based policy selection against novelty- or learning-progress models in an open-ended idea-generation (rather than bandit/navigation) task.
- Zhang et al. 2025 has N = 25 and sensor/source EEG; no replication was located.
- The Seth et al. "Curious inferences" reply (TICS 2020) argues long-term prediction-error minimisation "naturally prescribes ... epistemic, curious actions", but Millidge et al. 2021 and Champion et al. 2024 show the derivation and the practical behaviour are contested; I found no 2023–2026 paper that resolves this formally (an arXiv 2606.04935 theory paper reportedly reframes EFE as VFE on an augmented model, but I could only see a snippet — [papers.cool listing](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2606.04935)).

## 3. How these accounts explain genuinely new hypotheses: structure learning, Bayesian model reduction/expansion, Bayesian surprise; computational demonstrations

### Takeaway
The framework explains new ideas through two complementary moves: *model expansion* (adding latent states/components when existing ones cannot explain data; "spare slots") and *model reduction* (post hoc pruning/merging of parameters or components via Bayesian model reduction, often cast as "sleep"/offline consolidation and linked to aha moments), with EFE used to choose which observations best disambiguate candidate structures. Computational demonstrations have scaled from toy rule-learning (2017–2022) to MNIST/Tower of Hanoi (2024), renormalising generative models (2025) and AXIOM's game-playing agent (2025), where BMR ablation produced 3–4-fold score drops; human evidence for these specific mechanisms is indirect (sampling accounts of hypothesis generation; replay).

### Cited Findings
- **Structure learning with spare capacity + BMR (foundational, 2020) [simulation].** Smith, Schwartenbeck, Parr & Friston: a generative model with spare "slots" for hidden states switched on by novel inputs, and BMR that resets slot learning in favour of simpler models; agents add new concepts "with relatively few observations", increase granularity, and show "one-shot" generalisation — [Smith et al. 2020, Front Comput Neurosci](https://frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2020.00041/full)
- **BMR as learning without new evidence (2022) [simulation].** Neacsu, Mirza, Adams & Friston: concept formation in a spatial foraging task is "enhanced and nuanced by Bayesian model reduction, a special case of structure learning that typifies learning in the absence of new evidence"; three processes — inference, parametric learning, structure learning — "produce a diversity of beliefs and belief structures" — [Neacsu et al. 2022, PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0277199)
- **Supervised structure learning (2024) [simulation/AI].** Friston, Da Costa, Tschantz, Kiefer, Salvatori, Neacsu, Koudahl, Heins, Sajid et al. (Biological Psychology, 2024): priors on model selection based on EFE, which "reduces to a constrained mutual information"; emphasis on "the order in which data are ingested"; demonstrated on MNIST, a sprite-based disentanglement task and Tower of Hanoi, where models are "constructed autodidactically to recover (i.e., disentangle) the factorial structure of latent states" — [Friston et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2024.108891)
- **Renormalising generative models (2025) [simulation/AI].** Friston, Heins, Verbelen, Da Costa, Salvatori, Markovic, Tschantz, Koudahl, Buckley et al.: discrete hierarchical models built with the renormalisation group that "learn compositionality over space and time, furnishing models of paths or orbits", with "automatic discovery, learning, and deployment" across image classification, movie compression/generation and more — [Friston et al. 2025, Front Netw Physiol](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnetp.2025.1521963)
- **Model disambiguation by optimal experimental design (Dec 2025) [theory + simulation].** The "artificial reasoning" paper (above) frames structure learning as choosing actions that "distinguish between alternative models, in the spirit of optimal experimental design", with BMR evaluating "posteriors over models ... quickly from accumulated posterior beliefs about model parameters" — [Friston et al. 2025, arXiv 2512.21129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21129)
- **AXIOM: expansion + BMR at game scale (2025) [AI].** Heins, Van de Maele, Tschantz, Linander, Salvatori, Pezzato, Catal, Wei, Koudahl, Perin, Verbelen, Buckley, Markovic & Friston (VERSES / Sussex / TU Dresden / UCL). Four mixture models (slot sMM, identity iMM, transition tMM, recurrent rMM); components are added online "when no existing component explains new data well enough"; every 500 frames BMR "samples up to 2,000 rMM components and greedily tests merges", keeping a merge "only if it lowers the expected free energy". Gameworld 10k (10k interaction steps, 10 seeds) cumulative reward: Explode 180 ± 30 vs 101 ± 13 (BBF) and 35 ± 59 (DreamerV3); Gold 190 ± 18 vs −26 ± 12 and −21 ± 10; Hunt 206 ± 20 vs 4 ± 12 and 6 ± 9; but Cross (−68 ± 36) and Drive (−49 ± 4) fall below BBF (−48 ± 7, −37 ± 6). Parameters 0.3–1.6 M vs 6.47 M (BBF) and 420 M (DreamerV3); model update 18 ± 3 ms vs 135 ± 36 and 221 ± 37 ms. **BMR ablation:** Gold drops 190 → 45 ± 15, Hunt 206 → 48 ± 13; BMR hurts Cross "because early merging reduces the information-gain term and discourages exploration". Limitations: core priors hand-engineered; simplified single-colour sprites; not tested on Atari/Minecraft — [Heins et al. 2025, arXiv 2505.24784](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24784v1). Company coverage claims AXIOM beat DreamerV3 on Gameworld 10K — [VERSES press release](https://www.verses.ai/news/verses-digital-brain-beats-googles-top-ai-at-gameworld-10k-atari-challenge) (company source, treat with caution)
- **Hypotheses as samples from an approximate posterior (foundational, 2017) [human empirical + model].** Dasgupta, Schulz & Gershman: "hypotheses are generated stochastically from a sampling process, such that the sampled hypotheses form a Monte Carlo approximation of the posterior" with few samples; this reproduces anchoring, subadditivity/superadditivity, "the crowd within", the self-generation effect, weak-evidence and dud-alternative effects, and the predicted switch between sub- and superadditivity by manipulating unpacking/typicality was confirmed — [Dasgupta et al. 2017, Cognitive Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2017.05.001)
- **Replay as ordered hypothesis testing / compositional novelty (2023) [human MEG + theory].** See Schwartenbeck et al. 2023 and Kurth-Nelson et al. 2023 in Section 1 — [Schwartenbeck](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.06.06.447249); [Kurth-Nelson](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.12.028)
- **False insights as the dark side of the same mechanism (2024) [theory].** McGovern, Grimmer, Doss, Hutchinson, Timmermann, Lyon, Corlett & Laukkonen (Communications Psychology, 2024) propose that psychedelics increase "the quantity and subjective intensity of insights and thence beliefs, including false ones", framed in active inference — [McGovern et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-024-00120-6) (cross-ref: Laukkonen et al. 2023 BMR/insight account covered in the earlier round)
- **Spontaneous activity as complexity minimisation (foundational, 2021).** Offline "maximizing the entropy of explanations and minimizing model complexity" — [Pezzulo, Zorzi & Corbetta 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2021.05.007)

### Inferences
- The field's operational definition of a "new idea" is a change in model *structure* (new latent factor, merged/pruned parameters, new symmetry/rule) rather than a new belief within a fixed model. The two-phase loop — expand greedily online when data are unexplained, then reduce/merge offline when the reduced model has higher evidence — is explicitly implemented in AXIOM and is the most procedure-ready mechanism found.
- AXIOM's ablation is the first quantitative demonstration that BMR materially improves generalisation (3–4× score gains on Gold/Hunt), and also that reduction can suppress exploration (Cross), implying a schedule trade-off between consolidation and curiosity.
- The Dasgupta sampling account supplies a human-validated algorithmic layer: generating a few posterior samples (not exhaustive enumeration) reproduces human hypothesis-generation biases; combined with the AXIOM expansion rule, this suggests "sample a handful of structural hypotheses, expand only if none explains the data, then prune".

### Gaps
- No human neuroimaging or behavioural study was found that directly measures Bayesian model reduction (as opposed to parameter updating) during problem solving; the link between BMR and human insight remains theoretical (see earlier round for Laukkonen et al. 2023).
- "Bayesian surprise" as a specific driver of idea generation (vs. of attention) was not located in 2023–2026 human work; the only in-window physiological link is pupil ↔ information gain (Colizoli 2026).
- Full text of Friston et al. 2024 and 2025 (RGM) was not retrieved; quantitative results for Tower of Hanoi/MNIST are not reported here.

## 4. Evidence for prediction-error and precision signalling (dopamine, noradrenaline, acetylcholine) during thinking and problem solving, especially 2023–2026 human studies

### Takeaway
2023–2026 human evidence for neuromodulatory precision control comes mostly from learning/belief-updating tasks rather than problem solving: a D2/D3 antagonist increased belief volatility and precision weights on prediction errors in a dose- and genotype-dependent way (N = 76); trait anxiety was linked to over-estimated volatility with gamma/alpha-beta signatures of precision-weighted PEs in ACC/dmPFC/OFC (MEG); pupil responses track information gain; a muscarinic antagonist raised learning rates (preprint, N = 43). Circuit-level mechanisms for prediction error (thalamocortical disinhibition; cholinergic sharpening) are established only in rodents. A 2026 preregistered study failed to replicate a prior volatility-overweighting finding in autism, and a 2026 Annual Review states the neural PE evidence is "inconsistent".

### Cited Findings
- **Dopamine D2/D3 and precision of beliefs (2023) [human empirical, pharmacology, N = 76 men].** Mikus, Eisenegger, Mathys, Clark, Müller, Robbins, Lamm & Naef (Nature Communications): high-dose sulpiride "increases the volatility of beliefs, which leads to higher precision weights on prediction errors", "driven by participants with genetically conferred higher dopamine availability (Taq1a polymorphism)", robust to working-memory control; higher precision weights produced more reciprocity in repeated but not single-round Trust games — [Mikus et al. 2023, Nat Commun](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39823-5)
- **Precision-weighted PEs in anxiety, MEG (2023) [human empirical, N = 39 per group].** Hein, Gong, Ivanova, Fedele, Nikulin & Herrojo Ruiz (Communications Biology): high trait anxiety "undermined learning through an overestimation of volatility, leading to faster belief updating, more stochastic decisions and pronounced lose-shift tendencies", with "increased gamma activity in the ACC, dmPFC, and OFC during encoding of precision-weighted prediction errors" and suppressed ACC alpha/beta — [Hein et al. 2023, Commun Biol](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-023-04628-1)
- **Pupil as read-out of belief updating (2026) [human empirical].** Colizoli et al.: post-feedback pupil response explained by information gain (KL posterior‖prior), reasoned to be "proportional to the precision-weighted prediction error signals potentially arising from neuromodulatory arousal networks"; sign of the pupil effect was context dependent — [Colizoli et al. 2026, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.105287)
- **Muscarinic acetylcholine and learning rate (2024 preprint) [human empirical, N = 43, within-subject].** Biperiden 4 mg "reduced the sensitivity to the learnt reward probabilities", attributed to "maladaptively increased learning rates under biperiden", with no effect on a no-learning gambling task — [bioRxiv 2024.09.20.614105](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.20.614105.full.pdf) (preprint)
- **Acetylcholine sharpens prediction-error precision (2024) [animal].** Pérez-González, Lao-Rodríguez, Aedo-Sánchez & Malmierca (eLife): microiontophoretic ACh in rat auditory cortex "modulates the nMM, affecting prediction error responses but not repetition suppression", predominantly in infragranular layers, "consistent with the representational sharpening that accompanies an increase in the precision of prediction errors" — [Pérez-González et al. 2024, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.91475)
- **Circuit mechanism of sensory prediction errors (2024) [animal].** Furutachi, Franklin, Aldea, Mrsic-Flogel & Hofer (Nature): in mouse V1 unexpected stimuli "preferentially boost responses of the layer 2/3 V1 neurons that are most selective for that stimulus"; errors "specifically amplify the unexpected visual input, rather than representing non-specific surprise or difference signals", via pulvinar input gated by VIP→SST disinhibition — [Furutachi et al. 2024, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07851-w)
- **Noradrenaline, rodent OFC (2026) [animal].** OFC noradrenaline release "closely tracked trial-by-trial, model-derived volatility estimates", and disrupting LC→OFC input "reproduced the model-predicted impairment in adaptive learning-rate adjustment" (PNAS, rats) — [Bordeaux Neurocampus summary](https://www.bordeaux-neurocampus.fr/?p=204757)
- **Preregistered non-replication (2026) [human empirical, 22 autistic + 22 non-autistic].** Plank, Pior, Yurova, Nowak, Shi & Falter-Wagner (Molecular Autism) extended Lawson et al.'s HGF paradigm to emotional faces and "did not find robust differences between autistic and non-autistic adults", with "largely comparable probabilistic associative learning ... reflected in response times and pupil sizes" — [Plank et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13229-026-00730-3)
- **Psilocybin and belief updating under active inference (2024) [animal].** Fisher, Smith, Conn, Corcoran, Milton, Hohwy & Foldi fit active inference and RL models to rat bandit-reversal behaviour: psilocybin increased reward through engagement "mediated by modification of forgetting rates and reduced loss aversion" — [Fisher et al. 2024, Transl Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-024-03103-7)
- **EFE neural correlate during exploration (2025) [human EEG].** Zhang et al.: EFE encoded in frontal pole / middle frontal gyrus (see Section 2) — [Zhang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.92892)
- **Foundational human pharmacology (pre-window, for context).** Haarsma et al. (Mol Psychiatry 2021; bromocriptine/sulpiride/placebo, N = 59): precision weighting of unsigned PEs benefits learning, is coded in superior frontal cortex, perturbed by dopaminergic drugs and impaired in psychosis — [Haarsma et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8589669/). Iglesias et al. (NeuroImage 2021): low-level precision-weighted PEs → dopaminergic midbrain; volatility-updating PEs → cholinergic basal forebrain — [Iglesias et al. 2021](https://doaj.org/article/df2429dc46ee44349e143dd7ffd86586). Lawson et al. (Current Biology 2021; propranolol 40 mg): reduced learning rates, more reliance on priors under high uncertainty, and abolished phasic pupil effects of PE and volatility — [Lawson et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7808754/)
- **Theory of precision as the lever of psychopathology and planning (2023) [theory].** Friston's "Computational psychiatry: from synapses to sentience" frames synaptic gain control as mediating "attentional set, active inference, learning and planning" — [Friston 2023, Mol Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-022-01743-z)

### Inferences
- The in-window human data support a consistent qualitative picture — dopamine (D2) and noradrenaline modulate how strongly prediction errors update beliefs (precision/learning rate), acetylcholine modulates sensory PE precision — but all human studies are learning tasks; none measures these signals during creative problem solving or idea generation.
- The anxiety result (volatility over-estimation → faster, noisier updating) and the sulpiride result (D2 blockade → higher precision on PEs) together suggest that *state* manipulations of perceived volatility are a lever on how readily beliefs are revised, which maps onto "openness to restructuring" in idea generation; this is an inference, not a tested link.
- The 2026 non-replication and the Annual Review's "inconsistent evidence" verdict counsel caution about strong precision-pathology claims.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 human study was found measuring dopaminergic, noradrenergic or cholinergic signals (PET, pharmacology, pupil) during insight, divergent thinking or hypothesis generation.
- No 2023–2026 human atomoxetine or levodopa belief-updating study was located (searches returned only pre-2023 work).
- The biperiden study is a preprint; the OFC-noradrenaline study is rodent.

## 5. Strongest criticisms (unfalsifiability, dark room, Litwin & Miłkowski, Bowers & Davis, Sun & Firestone) and recent responses

### Takeaway
The main critiques are: PP is too flexible to be falsified and unifies "by fiat" (Litwin & Miłkowski); the dark-room problem (Sun & Firestone 2020) shows prediction-error minimisation can be "hacked"; Markov-blanket metaphysics conflates epistemic and physical boundaries (Bruineberg et al., BBS 2022); and neural evidence for prediction errors is definitionally loose and inconsistent (Walsh et al. 2020; Furutachi & Hofer 2026). Responses have been (a) EFE/epistemic value and evolved priors dissolve the dark room (Seth et al.; Van de Cruys, Friston & Clark 2020), (b) reframing adversarial testing as Bayesian model comparison rather than falsification (Corcoran, Hohwy & Friston 2023), and (c) formal work on Bayesian mechanics; but internal results (Millidge 2021; Champion 2024; Modirshanechi 2025) show the exploration derivation and its human fit are themselves contestable.

### Cited Findings
- **Dark room (2020) [critique].** Sun & Firestone: prediction-error minimisation "can be 'hacked', by placing oneself in highly predictable environments where nothing happens"; they call this "a surprisingly serious challenge, highlighting the obstacles facing 'theories-of-everything' in psychology" — [Sun & Firestone 2020, TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.02.006)
- **Replies (2020).** Seth, Millidge, Buckley & Tschantz: "minimisation of prediction error in the long term (expected free energy) naturally prescribes the sort of epistemic, curious actions that lead to escaping from dark rooms" — [Seth et al. 2020, TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.05.011) (preprint abstract: [PsyArXiv](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/w8y9p)). Van de Cruys, Friston & Clark, "Controlled optimism" (TICS 24(9):680–681) — [Van de Cruys et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.05.012). A philosophical analysis treats the dark room as "a cluster of related concerns" including whether PP "has substantive empirical content at very general levels" — [Sims, "The Problems with Prediction"](https://predictive-mind.net/epubs/the-problems-with-prediction/OEBPS/23_Sims.xhtml)
- **Unification by fiat (2020) [critique].** Litwin & Miłkowski (Cognitive Science): PP "fails to deliver general, simple, homogeneous, and systematic explanations", shows "systematic equivocations in PP-based models, or outright contradictions with its avowed principles", and "PP-based models are seldom empirically validated, and they are frequently offered as mere just-so stories" — [Litwin & Miłkowski 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12867). Their follow-up "Testable or bust" argues even hierarchical PP "is insufficiently restrictive to disallow incorrect models and may be adjusted to explain any neurocognitive phenomenon, including non-existent or impossible ones", so PP should be seen as "a computational framework" rather than a unifying theory — [IFiS PAN summary](https://ifispan.pl/en/testable-or-bust-theoretical-lessons-for-predictive-processing/)
- **Markov blankets (BBS 2022) [critique].** Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst & Baltieri identify "a persistent confusion ... between the formal use of Markov blankets as an epistemic tool for Bayesian inference, and their novel metaphysical use in the free energy framework", proposing "Pearl blankets" vs "Friston blankets" — [Bruineberg et al. 2022, BBS](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x21002351)
- **Neural evidence (2020; 2026) [critique/review].** Walsh, McGovern, Clark & O'Connell ask whether PP "lacks the empirical foundation to support its broad claims" — [Walsh et al. 2020, Ann NY Acad Sci](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7187369/). Furutachi & Hofer, "Rethinking Predictive Processing" (Annual Review of Neuroscience, 2026): "varied definitions and inconsistent empirical evidence have raised questions about its validity and explanatory scope"; PE signals "may appear similar in their responses yet reflect fundamentally different underlying computations"; they "revisit predictive coding, highlighting alternative accounts" — [Furutachi & Hofer 2026](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-102124-031410)
- **Internal critiques of the exploration derivation [theory/AI].** Millidge et al. 2021: "exploration does not directly follow from free energy minimization into the future" — [Millidge et al.](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01354). Champion et al. 2024: EFE-minimising deep agent "almost always picks the same action" and fails dSprites — [Champion et al.](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01697). Mirza et al. needed non-epistemic heuristic policies to fit human scan paths — [Mirza et al. 2018](https://iris.sissa.it/retrieve/dd8a4bf7-e5b0-20a0-e053-d805fe0a8cb0/journal.pone.0190429.pdf)
- **Empirical challenge to information-gain curiosity [human].** Modirshanechi et al. 2025: novelty beats information gain and surprise — [PNAS 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2502193122); Gijsen et al. 2022: only ~half of participants show information-gain sensitivity — [Sci Rep 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-21766-4)
- **Imagery ≠ imagination [philosophical critique].** Jones & Wilkinson 2020 (Section 1) — [Cambridge Handbook chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-the-imagination/from-prediction-to-imagination/ADDB8048880BB1BDEF441EBFBCA97D28)
- **Responses: Bayesian adversarial collaboration (2023) [theory/methodology].** Corcoran, Hohwy & Friston argue adversarial collaboration "has been stymied by an overly restrictive concern with the falsification of scientific theories" and should instead be framed "in terms of Bayesian belief updating, model comparison, and evidence accumulation", scoring theories "in terms of a common metric of evidence" — [Corcoran et al. 2023, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.08.027)
- **Responses: Bayesian mechanics (2023) [theory].** Ramstead, Sakthivadivel, Heins, Koudahl, Millidge, Da Costa, Klein & Friston formalise FEP as "a physics of and by beliefs", distinguishing path-tracking, mode-tracking and mode-matching applications and a duality with constrained maximum entropy — [Ramstead et al. 2023, Interface Focus](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2022.0029)
- **Responses: minimal testable commitments (2026).** Whyte et al. derive "a minimal (and testable) theory of consciousness" from shared commitments of active-inference models — [Whyte et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2025.11.002)
- **Recent critique of scope (2026) [critique].** Baltieri & Kanai, "Steam-engine naturalism", find Seth's PP/FEP-based argument against artificial consciousness "problematic for a few different reasons" — [BBS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x25103531)
- **Non-peer-reviewed 2026 commentary** summarising falsifiability worries ("capable of accommodating any outcome") and recommending PP as "a heuristic framework for specific domains than as a universal theory" — [againstprofphil.org series](https://againstprofphil.org/2026/04/05/the-bayesian-brain-and-predictive-processing-a-critique-2/) (blog; low weight)

### Inferences
- The most consequential criticism for an idea-generation application is not the dark room (largely answered by EFE/epistemic value in principle) but the *instability of the epistemic term*: it is not uniquely derivable (Millidge), can collapse in practice (Champion), and does not best fit human exploration (Modirshanechi). A procedure built on PP should therefore treat "information gain" as one heuristic among several rather than as a normative guarantee.
- The 2026 Annual Review's definitional critique (PE signals that look alike but compute different things) implies that any "prediction error" step in a procedure must specify *what* is being predicted (sensory feature vs. hypothesis-level model evidence), echoing the earlier round's distinction between surprise and metacognitive prediction error in aha experiences.

### Gaps
- Bowers & Davis (2012) was not re-retrieved in this round and no in-window reply was found; I cannot report its current status.
- I found no 2023–2026 direct reply from Litwin & Miłkowski's targets to "Testable or bust", nor an in-window rejoinder from Sun & Firestone.
- The "Controlled optimism" reply's full text was inaccessible (403); its specific arguments (beyond the title's appeal to evolved optimistic priors) are not summarised here.

## 6. 2023–2026 attempts to build AI / LLM agents on active-inference principles and what they showed

### Takeaway
Three strands exist: (1) conceptual arguments that LLMs are "atypical" active-inference agents lacking an action–perception loop (Kulveit et al. 2023) and that passive generative AI lacks the embodied model-testing needed for "genuine understanding" (Pezzulo, Parr, Cisek, Clark & Friston, TICS 2024); (2) active-inference layers wrapped around LLMs (prompt/search selection, multi-agent orchestration, question-asking by information gain, a clinical actor–critic protocol) — mostly preprints with limited or no benchmark numbers; (3) non-LLM active-inference agents (AXIOM, RGMs) that do report quantitative results, showing strong sample efficiency and interpretable structure learning on simplified games but with hand-engineered priors.

### Cited Findings
- **LLMs as atypical active-inference agents (2023) [theory].** Kulveit et al., NeurIPS workshop: LLMs fit the paradigm "but currently lack a tight feedback loop between acting in the world and perceiving the results of those actions", a loop that "may soon be closed" — [Kulveit et al. 2023, arXiv 2311.10215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.10215)
- **Passive vs. active generative models (TICS 2024) [theory].** Pezzulo, Parr, Cisek, Clark & Friston: biological generative models "must capture and control the sensory consequences of action", allowing agents to "intervene upon their worlds in ways that constantly put their best models to the test", which the authors argue is "essential to the development of genuine understanding", unlike "the passive models learned by generative AI systems" — [Pezzulo et al. 2024, TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2023.10.002)
- **Active inference layer over LLM agents (2024–25) [AI preprint].** Prakki, "Active Inference for Self-Organizing Multi-LLM Systems": a layer "above an LLM-based agent, dynamically adjusting prompts and search strategies through principled information-seeking behavior", with three state factors (prompt, search, information) and seven observation modalities; reported qualitatively that the agent "builds accurate models of its environment and shifts from information gathering to targeted prompt testing" — [arXiv 2412.10425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10425v1)
- **Multi-agent orchestration (Sept 2025) [AI preprint].** "Orchestrator: Active Inference for Multi-Agent Systems in Long-Horizon Tasks" — only the reference list was retrievable; method/results unverified — [arXiv 2509.05651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05651)
- **Language-mediated active inference for safer AGI (Aug 2025) [AI proposal].** Proposes beliefs and preferences kept separate in natural language and "resource-aware free energy minimization", with an ARC-benchmark research agenda; "experiments are proposed rather than reported" — [arXiv 2508.05766](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05766v1)
- **Optimal question asking via EFE (2026) [AI preprint, snippet only].** Applies active inference to choosing what context to acquire before answering: epistemic term "reduces to expected information gain, optionally normalized by token cost"; benchmarks frontier LLMs on categorical tasks with 25–300 candidates, with exact posteriors and a dynamic-programming oracle; no numbers visible in the snippet — reported as arXiv 2608.19202 (seen only via search summary; verify)
- **Clinical actor–critic prompting "grounded in active inference" (2025) [AI + human raters].** Shusterman, Waters, O'Neill, Bangs, Luu & Tucker (npj Digital Medicine): Therapist and Supervisor agents; in a blind study of 100 CBT-I patient queries, "the LLM often received higher ratings than the appropriate [therapist-crafted] responses" — [Shusterman et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-01516-2) (the active-inference content is a prompting protocol, not an EFE implementation)
- **AXIOM (2025) [AI].** Quantitative results and BMR ablation in Section 3 — [Heins et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24784v1)
- **Renormalising generative models (2025) [AI].** Automatic discovery and deployment across image classification, movie compression/generation and planning — [Friston et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnetp.2025.1521963)
- **Scalability obstacle [AI theory].** "The computational burden of EFE minimization has remained a major obstacle to its scalability" — [arXiv 2504.14898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14898v1)
- **Negative result for deep EFE agents (2024).** Champion et al. (Section 2) — [Neural Computation 2024](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01697)
- **Adjacent: LLMs as predictive models of science (2025) [human vs LLM].** Luo et al. (Nature Human Behaviour): on BrainBench, "LLMs surpass experts in predicting experimental outcomes", and a neuroscience-tuned BrainGPT does better still; confidence calibrated with accuracy — [Luo et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02046-9)
- **Clark on LLMs as cognitive extensions (2025).** — [Clark 2025, Nat Commun](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59906-9)

### Inferences
- The only active-inference AI results with hard numbers in-window are non-LLM (AXIOM, RGM). Their lesson for idea generation is specific: online component expansion + periodic evidence-based merging yields fast, interpretable structure learning, and merging schedule interacts with exploration.
- LLM-wrapped active inference so far uses the framework as a *control policy over prompting/search* (what to ask, which agent to call, when to stop) rather than changing the LLM's internal inference; this is exactly the layer where a "procedure for generating ideas" would live (select the next epistemic action by expected information gain per token).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2023–2026 benchmark shows an LLM agent governed by EFE outperforming standard agentic baselines; the OQA preprint may contain such numbers but I could not read it.
- Orchestrator (2509.05651) content is unverified beyond title.

## 7. Which specific mechanisms could be turned into procedures (synthesis across sections)

### Takeaway
Six mechanisms have enough formal specification (and some empirical support) to be written as steps: EFE-scored counterfactual rollout; expand-then-reduce structure learning; ordered hypothesis replay; few-sample hypothesis generation; multi-signal curiosity (novelty + learning progress + empowerment); and precision/volatility manipulation as a state lever. Their evidential status differs sharply and is noted for each.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) Counterfactual rollout scored by information gain + value; recurse over beliefs-about-beliefs; stop when expected information gain per cost falls.** Formal basis: [Friston et al. 2021 Sophisticated inference](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01351); [Friston et al. 2025 artificial reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21129); token-cost-normalised variant in the OQA preprint (snippet only). Status: theory/simulation; human EEG correlate of EFE in frontal pole [Zhang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.92892).
- **(b) Expand-then-reduce.** Add a new latent component only when no existing one explains the datum; periodically propose merges and keep only those that raise model evidence / lower EFE; expect reduction to improve generalisation (Gold 45→190, Hunt 48→206) but to suppress exploration if done too early (Cross) — [Heins et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24784v1); conceptual roots [Smith et al. 2020](https://frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2020.00041/full), [Neacsu et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0277199); data-ordering matters [Friston et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2024.108891). Status: AI demonstrations; human link theoretical.
- **(c) Ordered constructive replay.** Assemble a candidate configuration by fixing the most predictable elements first and the most uncertain last, iterating until one configuration remains — [Schwartenbeck et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.06.06.447249); compositional recombination of role-bound elements to "derive qualitatively new knowledge" — [Kurth-Nelson et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.12.028). Status: human MEG/fMRI evidence for the ordering; novelty claim theoretical.
- **(d) Few-sample hypothesis generation.** Draw a small number of hypotheses from an approximate posterior rather than enumerating; expect anchoring and subadditivity, and counteract by "unpacking" alternatives — [Dasgupta et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2017.05.001). Status: human behavioural evidence (pre-window).
- **(e) Multi-signal curiosity.** Weight next-step choice by novelty (count-based), learning progress and empowerment (number of future options opened), with information gain as a secondary term — [Modirshanechi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2502193122); [Ten et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26196-w); [Brändle et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01661-2); synthesis [Poli et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.02.001). Status: human behavioural evidence.
- **(f) Precision/volatility as a state lever.** Raising estimated volatility increases learning rate and belief revision (anxiety: [Hein et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-023-04628-1); D2 blockade: [Mikus et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39823-5)); offline "mental action" alters beliefs without acting ([Parr et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2023.108562)). Status: human evidence in learning tasks only; transfer to idea generation is an inference.

### Inferences
- A faithful PP-style idea-generation procedure would alternate an online phase (sample a few hypotheses; expand model only when data are unexplained; choose next probe by multi-signal curiosity) with an offline phase (ordered constructive replay; evidence-based merging/pruning), with an explicit schedule that delays pruning until exploration has plateaued.

### Gaps
- No study was found that implements and tests such a combined procedure with human participants or against human idea-generation benchmarks.
