## control

| ID | Mechanism | Claim | Evidence | Maps to | Agent translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CTL-01 | Separate generation and selection into distinct control regimes | Strengthening top-down control favours convergent over divergent thinking. For mindfulness, convergent d=0.80 vs divergent d=0.52. Anodal tDCS (32 studies, N=1,538) improved convergent but not divergent thinking. In two resting-state samples (N=236 and N=234), higher lifetime creative achievement went with more functionally distinct default and frontoparietal control regions. Switching between control modes costs something because people undershoot the target control level, and the cost grows with switch frequency and target distance (four experiments). MemiC theory treats novelty evaluation and effectiveness evaluation as separate stages. | meta-analysis | think | Each subagent stays in one mode for its whole context. Generator SKILL.md files (forage, wander, analogize) are loose: no checklists, no self-critique, no evaluative language. Evaluator SKILL.md files (evaluate, insight-check) are tight: structured rubrics, explicit verification steps, and a ban on proposing new ideas. Modes change only at orchestrator boundaries, by spawning a fresh agent. /evaluate scores novelty and effectiveness in two separate passes, each run by its own fresh subagent. Intake is also its own phase: a web or file-search subagent writes runs/<id>/sources.md, and /forage reads only problem.md and memory. A harness script flags evaluative hedges in ideas.jsonl and new proposals in evaluations.jsonl and reports a role-leakage rate. Ablations at equal budget: a merged generate-and-critique agent vs separated agents; swapped profiles (tight generation, loose evaluation), where novelty and verified-solve rate should drop; merged vs separate novelty/effectiveness passes; and no intake vs intake at the start vs alternating intake and generation. |
| CTL-02 | Incubation after real preparation releases fixation | Incubation improves creativity, d=0.39 [0.25, 0.53] across 52 studies, most when the initial cues were misleading. Sio & Ormerod 2009 found larger effects after longer preparation and with an undemanding filler than with a demanding filler or none. In over 300 students, mind wandering aided incubation during a moderately engaging clip but not during a boring one. | meta-analysis | incubate | /incubate fires only after a real preparation phase (at least one full forage+evaluate pass), preferably on an impasse verdict (see CTL-15). The filler is light but real: a constraint-0 wander, a summary of an unrelated article fetched with web tools, or tidying the memory index. It is never a hard reasoning task and never idle make-work. The returning generator is a fresh subagent that gets the restructured problem.md and not the failed ideas, which drops the misleading cues. Harness: a three-way ablation of no filler (fresh context only), undemanding filler and demanding filler. |
| CTL-03 | Constraints help on average; time budget is inverted-U | A meta-analysis of over 100 studies finds a significant positive overall link between constraints and creativity, moderated by constraint type and timing. Time-pressure evidence is mixed: design ideation suffers from both too little and too much time. | meta-analysis | forage | /forage seeds include explicit constraint injections drawn from a typed list (e.g. 'no electricity', 'use only the listed resources', 'must cost zero'), tagged by type in ideas.jsonl so the harness can find which types help. The token or step budget per subagent is a harness sweep variable (0.5x, 1x, 2x, 4x), and the expected result is an inverted U on novelty and solve rate. |
| CTL-04 | Bounded switch budget with an inverted-U optimum | Across 10 resting-state samples (N=2,433, five countries), the number of DMN-ECN switches predicted divergent thinking but not general intelligence, with an inverted U: too little and too much switching both go with lower creativity. Task fMRI (N=31) showed more switching during ideation. Metastability signatures are lower in ageing, Alzheimer's, anaesthesia and brain injury, and higher in psychosis with worse function, so more state variability is not monotonically better. | replicated | think | /think keeps a switch counter in state.json. It counts each generate->evaluate pass, each cluster switch in /forage and each jump in /wander's trajectory.md. The harness sweeps the switch budget (1, 2, 4, 6, 8) and the wander jump interval (every 2, 3, 5 or 8 thoughts) over the benchmark, fits an inverted U on solve rate and rubric score, and the orchestrator uses the fitted peak, never the maximum. Each run logs its switch count to memory/episodic/ so later runs can relate budget to outcome by problem type. |
| CTL-05 | Explicit stopping rule: declining relative threshold, opt-out and abstention | People deliberate until confidence meets a goal that drops over time, with a per-item time limit and an opt-out criterion whose confidence level stayed stable across incentives, tasks and wording (N=596). In sequential search they use a position-dependent acceptance threshold scaled to outcome variance, but lower it too little for longer horizons (N=199). Insight solvers are more likely to time out than to answer wrongly, while analytic answers can be premature guesses. | replicated | think | /think writes an explicit stopping rule to state.json. /evaluate converts each score to a percentile within the run and within memory/episodic/ distributions for similar problems. The acceptance threshold starts high and drops by a fixed step after each switch. There is a hard token and time cap, and below an opt-out threshold the run ends with verdict 'impasse-report' (what was tried, best partial idea). /forage and /evaluate may output 'no answer yet'. When the budget runs out, the orchestrator reports 'unsolved' instead of forcing a guess. Stopping is tied only to verified results: an insight flag never skips remaining passes or verification. The harness scores opt-outs and abstentions separately from wrong answers and tunes thresholds and the decline rate for cost. |
| CTL-06 | Metacognitive and prediction-error signals trigger deliberation and probing | A low feeling of rightness from competing intuitions, driven by answer fluency, triggers deliberation. In 289 participants it predicted whether and in which direction answers changed. An Aha arises when a problem is solved well ahead of one's own predicted time-to-solve, a metacognitive prediction error (two large preregistered experiments), and surprise is the Aha dimension that tracks it. Eleven-month-olds (N=110) learned more about expectancy-violating objects, tested hypotheses matched to the violation and stopped once it was explained (replicated). RL agents given a model of their own attention controlled attention better. | replicated | think | Four signals go in state.json. (a) Confidence from sample agreement, not self-report. After a first solve, 3-5 fresh subagents answer quickly from problem.md alone and python embeds the answers to measure agreement. High agreement plus a passing cheap check skips further switches. Low agreement spends switch budget on evaluate or restructure. (b) Solvability prediction error. Before each pass, write predicted solvability (0-1) and expected passes. After /evaluate, compute the error. Large positive errors go to /insight-check first and get priority in /consolidate, and repeated negative errors trigger /restructure. (c) Verification surprise. When a test, simulation or counterexample search contradicts its stated prediction, log it to surprises.jsonl with a violation type and set an evidence-weight flag to 'external', so claims must be grounded in tool output. Then spawn one probing subagent that writes 2-3 discriminating tests. Close the surprise and reset the flag once one explanation passes. (d) attention.md records the current framing, cluster and seed domain, cycles spent there and what is neglected. /think reads it before each cycle to persist, switch or restructure. The harness ablates each signal and calibrates self-predictions. |
| CTL-07 | Allocate computation where expected value exceeds cost | People invest more control when it is both more rewarding and more efficacious, and they update efficacy beliefs from prediction errors (N=91, replicated). Human planning fits a meta-level MDP with costed computations, and people inspect options where reward variance lies. A meta-RL agent that ran imagined rollouts only when they paid off predicted human thinking time (r=0.186). Removing rollouts cut reward from 7.54 to 6.54, and shuffling only their timing cut it to 6.75. Related resource-rational results: people place subgoals at bottleneck states to cut planning cost (N=806), partial simulation of only the relevant parts explains the intuitive-physics conjunction fallacy, and planning depth rises with evaluation expertise. | replicated | think | After each round, /think runs a short control step. For each available action (forage, analogize, wander, restructure, run a test), it writes the predicted outcome, which open question the action resolves, and the expected gain in best evaluator score minus token cost. History comes from state.json and memory/episodic/efficacy.json. That file holds running per-skill, per-problem-class efficacy, which /consolidate updates by prediction error whenever a call moves an idea into the final shortlist. The highest positive action runs, a small exploration floor stays, and the run stops when all values are negative. Extra rounds fire on state (the first decision, or a best idea still far from solved), not on a fixed clock. Search goes deep (more ideas per cluster, revisions of revisions) only on problem types where /evaluate agrees with verification; otherwise it stays shallow and broad, and the budget goes to verification. When a problem breaks into states, /encode builds a JSON state graph and networkx betweenness picks 1-3 subgoals. Simulations in /evaluate cover only the elements on the idea's causal path, followed by a wake-up pass for flagged interactions. Ablate against the fixed switch budget and against fixed or random round timing. |
| CTL-08 | Learning progress, not raw novelty, steers where effort goes | In free task choice (N=382), a softmax over competence plus learning progress fit best (AIC 492 vs 693 for random), and LP-driven learners mastered two or more activities more often (90.5% vs 70.6%). Four-year-olds (N=102) preferred options with high learning progress, and that sensitivity predicted learning. Infants look away from both too-predictable and too-surprising events. Curiosity peaks at intermediate prior knowledge, and more candidate answers in mind predicted higher curiosity, moderate confidence and better memory (two experiments). | replicated | think | /think logs the best evaluator score per strategy (skill, seed, cluster, framing) per round in state.json. A python helper computes a smoothed improvement slope from evaluations.jsonl. The next round's subagents are allocated by softmax over recent improvement plus current level. A strategy is dropped for the run after two calls with no gain, and both flat-and-low ('unlearnable') and saturated strategies get fewer agents. In /encode, a cheap subagent splits the problem into sub-questions with candidate answers and confidence. Deep foraging goes to sub-questions with several candidates and middling confidence; those with no candidates or one confident answer are skipped. Ablate against uniform allocation and against chasing the most novel cluster. |
| CTL-09 | Global workspace: binary admission into a small serial workspace | Conscious access is a sudden, all-or-none, self-sustaining frontoparietal ignition about 200-300 ms after the stimulus. In monkeys the report threshold matched the PFC ignition threshold, and unreported stimuli did not propagate. Perceptual stages run in parallel while a central decision stage runs serially, a frontoparietal bottleneck shared by the attentional blink and the PRP. Chaining two novel operations required conscious access, whereas single operations partly ran without it (Sackur & Dehaene 2009). In a shared-workspace network, performance peaked at 5 slots (ARI 0.915, vs 0.154 at 2 and 0.351 at 10), and a workspace-routing controller generalized multi-step addition better than LSTMs and Transformers (preprint). | replicated | think | After each generation round, a python script combines evaluator scores with distance values and admits only candidates above a threshold, binary and not weighted, into runs/<id>/workspace.md. Admission is capped at N slots, set in state.json (default 5, swept over 2/5/10). Next-cycle subagents see only workspace.md. Generation and retrieval run on many parallel subagents, but the orchestrator makes one decision per cycle (admit, route to which skill with what content, restructure, incubate or stop) and logs it with a reason in state.json. Multi-step transformations become explicit operations, each writing a file that is the next step's only input (steps.jsonl: operation, input_ref, output_ref). A routing check runs at the start of /think: on a close verified match in memory/semantic, try a direct call plus verification and enter the full loop only if that fails. Harness: tune the threshold, sweep slots, and compare explicit chaining vs one-shot, and the router vs the fixed schedule vs a monolithic prompt. |
| CTL-10 | Cluster persistence: leave when returns drop, but originality arrives late | Semantic search left a cluster once inter-response time reached the long-run average (the Marginal Value Theorem rule), and closer conformity produced more responses, but this fluency-maximising policy differs from the one that favours creativity. Original ideas tend to emerge later in divergent thinking, and DLPFC tDCS accelerated their emergence. In N=300, inhibition of earlier ideas predicted originality, more strongly in later generation phases. Productivity theory says good ideas come from generating many and discarding most. | replicated | forage | Each /forage subagent generates in rounds up to a minimum count (e.g. 12) and records each idea's position. From round two on, the prompt carries a compressed off-limits list of the run's earlier ideas plus the default schema, and python rejects near-duplicates and asks for replacements. A python helper computes marginal novelty (minimum distance to earlier ideas in the cluster) and signals a cluster exit when it falls below the run's running mean. Run this as an option: an MVT-switch agent vs a deliberately over-persisting agent, keeping whichever scores better on originality. Fan-out is 5-10x the shortlist size, and the evaluator sees ideas shuffled with position hidden. Harness: plot distance and rating against position, and vary fan-out (8/24/48) at a fixed evaluation budget, counting only after dedup. |
| CTL-11 | Stagnation detection triggers a reset into a clean context | Several single studies link getting stuck to narrowing and recovery to a reset. Reduced breadth between successive thoughts and longer negative associate chains mark rumination. Phasic locus coeruleus bursts flatten the attractor landscape so improbable transitions become likelier, and frontoparietal integration rises 6-9 s later (7T, N=59). LC arousal at context shifts resets hippocampal event representations, and self-generated topic boundaries evoke event-boundary responses. Insight can occur after an impasse or with none (four preregistered experiments, N=856). Satisfying answers raise curiosity only for related topics (N=5,831), a route to lock-in. Restructuring problems load working memory more as the answer nears. | single-study | think | After each generation pass, python computes over ideas.jsonl: novelty gain (mean distance of new ideas from all earlier ones), near-duplicate rate (similarity above about 0.9), mean consecutive distance, and the distance between consecutive round centroids. /think declares impasse immediately, logging the time in state.json, when gain falls below a threshold, duplicates spike, two consecutive centroids stay within a threshold distance, or two consecutive /evaluate passes add no new cluster and no score gain. The reset never asks the same context to try harder. It spawns a fresh subagent that does not see the repeated ideas and gets condensed input only: problem.md, the fixated cues and an impasse summary, at most about 3 framings. Alternatively the fresh agent gets the shortlist and must link two distant clusters, or a forced far-seed forager runs. /restructure is also called proactively once per run. In /wander, each jump writes a boundary line with a one-line summary of the clump, and long walks use a fresh subagent per segment that receives only summaries. Harness: stagnation-triggered vs fixed-budget switching, the reset ablated, and /restructure on condensed vs full input. |
| CTL-12 | Separable constraint knobs on a small control surface, cooled over rounds | In the dynamic framework of thought, thought moves freely to the degree it escapes deliberate (control) and automatic (salience, habit) constraints. Default/control coupling predicted stable, clustered thought, and hippocampal coupling predicted variable thought (r=-.42). Nucleus basalis inactivation in 2 macaques lowered energy barriers to large transitions, causal evidence that acetylcholine stabilizes states separately from noradrenergic flattening. Salient current concerns peaked before topic jumps, acting as attractors. Behaviour-coupled dynamics in mice rarely exceed 3 principal components. Across ages 5-55 (N=281), random and directed exploration cooled on a fast schedule that matched stochastic hill-climbing, and in AXIOM early merging suppressed exploration. | single-study | think | state.json holds a few named parameters that the orchestrator adjusts between rounds. The deliberate constraint is goal-return frequency (the one-line goal from problem.md as attractor, returned to every 2nd, 3rd or 5th jump) plus on-task instructions. The automatic constraint is how much the walk may reuse recent ideas, top memory hits or fixated cues. Anchor strength is how strongly persistent /forage agents restate their cluster's principle and build on their previous idea. The other parameters are the flexible-agent share and coupling between subagents. Subagent content stays rich and is never compressed to fit the controls. The schedule is tied to the round index, not to evaluator mood. Round 1 uses a constraint-0 wander and flexible foragers, sends some agents to the least-covered clusters, and /evaluate only scores, with no pruning, merging or dedup. Later rounds raise the constraint and anchor, shrink the flexible share and seed from the shortlist. Pruning starts only when new distinct clusters per pass fall below a threshold, and memory merges wait for the first K runs on a new problem family. Harness: ablate each knob separately, compare fixed-high, fixed-low and cooled schedules, and regress run-to-run score variance on the parameters, dropping any that explain nothing. |
| CTL-13 | Exploration bonuses: count-based novelty plus uncertainty, aimed by value | Humans kept exploring a stochastic, reward-free region, and their choices fit count-based novelty better than information gain or surprise; novelty and reward jointly control exploration. Directed (uncertainty-driven) versus random exploration formed one dimension across 9 tasks (N=820). In simulation, novelty combined with information gain was close to optimal across environments (preprint). At reward, backward replay favoured never-traversed paths with high need times gain (N=29). Humans arbitrated online between spatial and predictive similarity maps to generalize value (N=48), with medial OFC tracking which map to trust. | single-study | forage | memory/seed_counts.json keeps use counts for every seed domain, cluster label and analogy source across runs. A python sampler draws seeds with weight about 1/sqrt(1+count), multiplied by goal relevance (embedding similarity to problem.md). /forage has two tagged knobs. The directed knob targets clusters with few ideas or high score variance across judges, and the random knob draws uniformly. Between rounds, a planning step gives seeds and fan-out to clusters that are both uncertain and valuable. After /evaluate, a propagate step finds unevaluated ideas that share mechanism or components with high scorers and ranks them by need (unmet constraint coverage) times gain. Promise is predicted from two kernels, embedding similarity and lineage (shared parent, operator or seed), whose weights a regression updates each round. The top-k get expanded instead of resampling around the winner. Ablate novelty-only, planned vs uniform seeding, and each knob's contribution to solve rate and diversity. |
| CTL-14 | ANTI: Thinking longer corrects strongly biased or fixated answers | A meta-analysis (N=2,428) found that thinking longer did not substantially improve error correction among highly biased participants. Choices, confidence and response times reflected one shared evidence signal that overweighted stereotypes. | preprint | restructure | Do not answer an obvious or wrong shortlist by extending the same agent's reasoning or giving it more tokens. /think changes what enters the candidate set instead: /restructure drops fixated cues, and a fresh agent without the earlier transcript takes over (CTL-11). Harness ablation at equal budget: more tokens in the same context vs a fresh agent on the restructured problem. |
| CTL-15 | ANTI: Incubation as a default booster, empty filler, or deliberate offline solving | Mind wandering during an incubation break had no significant effect on divergent thinking (N=85). It helped only low-difficulty repeated convergent items when people were aware of it and hurt novel items without awareness. A scoping review of 27 studies found the claim that boredom boosts creativity 'not sufficiently supported' (5 positive, 4 negative, 3 null), and induced boredom predicted poorer divergent thinking. Puzzles that appeared in lucid, controlled dreams were solved less often (1 of 9) than those in non-lucid dreams (6 of 13). | contested | incubate | Do not run /incubate after every forage pass to raise idea count; trigger it only on an impasse verdict for convergent problems. Never use monotone make-work filler (copying, repetitive formatting). Do not tell the incubation /wander agent to solve the problem, because that brings back the goal-directed constraints incubation is meant to relax. The problem stays only as a weak attractor, and solving is left to the fresh return agent. The returning agent sees serendipity candidates explicitly flagged as coming from wandering. The harness reports the incubate ablation separately for convergent and divergent problems, and compares monotone vs light-engaging filler vs plain fresh context, and a solve instruction vs attractor only. |
| CTL-16 | ANTI: Information gain or a formal EFE solver as the control signal | The epistemic term does not follow uniquely from free-energy minimisation (Millidge 2021). A deep EFE agent 'almost always picks the same action' and fails dSprites (Champion 2024), and novelty beats information gain in explaining human exploration (Modirshanechi 2025). No peer-reviewed 2023-2026 benchmark shows an EFE-governed LLM agent beating standard agentic baselines, and EFE's computational cost is a recognised obstacle to scaling. | contested | think | Do not build an explicit probabilistic state-space model with an EFE solver over the skills, and do not let the orchestrator's next-action choice depend only on a self-estimated 'what would I learn' score. Use the coarse heuristic of CTL-07 and CTL-13: self-rated information value per token, combined with a novelty count and a learning-progress signal. Log in state.json which term drove each choice so the harness can ablate each term separately. Add formal machinery only if the harness first shows the heuristic beats a fixed schedule. |
| CTL-17 | Prefrontal self-monitoring suppresses novelty | In two coordinate sets (857 and 691 participants), 86% of creativity studies fell in a circuit negatively connected to the right frontal pole. Lateral frontal lesions impaired creativity while frontopolar lesions spared or raised it, and FTD patients with new-onset creativity showed atrophy negatively connected to creativity foci (d=-0.36). Lesion network mapping as a method is disputed. | contested | forage | /forage generators may not self-monitor: no feasibility comments, no hedging, and no use of the default schema in the first half of the output. All feasibility filtering is deferred to /evaluate. Ablation: generators told to check feasibility as they go vs generators with self-monitoring banned, measuring distance-window hits and integration. |
| CTL-18 | Incubation's active ingredient is unresolved | Incubation may work by forgetting fixating responses through delay, retrieval inhibition or a context shift. But incubation did not change fixation counts (Valueva & Lapteva 2020), and initial fixation did not predict post-incubation gains in a CRA study. In a preregistered writing study, more mind wandering during a break predicted within-person semantic-distance gains only when the same prompt was given again, while another study found no effect of mind wandering itself. Cueing unsolved puzzles during REM increased dreaming about them, and conceptual incorporation predicted solving (beta=1.24), but cueing had no main effect (30% vs 22%). | contested | incubate | Ablate the components of /incubate instead of assuming one. Arms: a fresh agent without the failed ideas, a fresh agent given them, and the same agent after unrelated work. On return, give the original prompt verbatim vs a restructured prompt, and compare a wander-filled break vs an empty one. Within each run, plant the core relation (not the wording) of a random half of open subproblems as a cue in the /wander seed and leave the other half uncued. A classifier subagent labels trajectory steps as conceptual engagement or mere mention, and only engaged steps count as serendipity candidates. Log distance gain and cued vs uncued solve rates in state.json. If removing the failed ideas alone does the work, keep the context wipe and skip costly filler. |

### Sources and caveats

- **CTL-01**
  - Key sources: Hughes et al. 2023, https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-023-02327-w; Chen et al. 2024 tDCS meta-analysis, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383874996_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Effects_of_Transcranial_Direct_Current_Stimulation_on_Creative_Thinking; Sassenberg, Jung & DeYoung 2025, https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhaf046; Grahek et al. 2026, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13023295/
  - Caveats: The mindfulness divergent-convergent difference was not significant, and tDCS effects depend on the stimulation site. The asymmetry is inferred across meta-analyses rather than tested directly. Sassenberg's outcome is self-reported achievement, the analysis is correlational, and both samples are in one paper. The inertia result comes from speed-accuracy tasks, and a fresh LLM context may have no inertia, so the expected gain is cleaner separation. MemiC stages are theory. The intake/generation split rests on a correlational ECoG memory study (Mohan 2024) and is a loose mapping. The stronger claim that self-monitoring actively suppresses novelty is disputed and kept separately as CTL-17.

  - Key sources: Fromer et al. 2021, https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v12y2021i1d10.1038_s41467-021-21315-z.html; Callaway et al. 2022, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01332-8; Jensen, Hennequin & Mattar 2024, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.16.523429v2.full; Correa et al. 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03890
