# Critique: neuroscience of creativity and insight

## What the set supports
- Creative work pairs a loose, memory-driven generator with a control process (neuro-a-01, a-10, a-12). This fits our own result that forced divergence helps open tasks.
- Two meta-analyses say divergent thinking and insight use different regions (a-23, a-24). An agent should not treat them as one skill.
- Unconscious thought ("sleep on it" for decisions) fails replication (c-01 to c-03). Do not build a step on it.
- Intrinsic self-correction can hurt (c-22, b-19). This matches our always-doubt loss on single-answer tasks.

## What could be wrong
- Independence is thin. Most lines come from one lab family (Beaty, Silvia, Benedek; Kounios, Beeman). Counts of "established" overstate this.
- Sio and Ormerod is cited three times. The effect size conflicts: 0.41 (a-16, fetched) vs 0.29 (b-10 snippet, c-04 via Gilhooly). One must be checked against the paper.
- Incubation is contested on three points: break demand (a-18 vs c-05), replication of the mind-wandering gain (b-11, a snippet at credibility 50), and mechanism (a-22 vs c-06).
- Neuroimaging claims are correlational and small-sample (c-12, c-15, c-18). The generate/evaluate split blurs in time (c-13). Control is several networks (c-16).
- c-09 says most "insight" problems are solved analytically. If true, much of the insight brain literature describes a rare event.
- The aha feeling misleads (b-12). Yet b-13 finds a link to accuracy on some problems. Mixed.

## Memory or snippet only
Weak grounding: a-14, a-15 (abstract or snippet), b-03, b-05 to b-08, b-10 to b-14, b-21, b-22, c-19, c-20, c-21. Lines with credibility under 60 should not drive design. b-21 and b-22 are unopened.

## What is missing
1. Any direct test of these ideas in an LLM agent loop. b-22 found none. Brain findings are analogies, not evidence for agents.
2. Task-type gating. Our data show forced divergence hurts single-answer tasks. The neuroscience here says control depends on how goal-directed the task is (a-06) but gives no rule for choosing a mode.
3. Evaluation quality. We have almost nothing on how good thinkers judge ideas (c-21 is a snippet).
4. Expert versus novice differences, and incubation in knowledge-rich versus knowledge-lean tasks (c-07 is one review sentence).
5. Whether serial-order gains (later ideas more original) transfer to LLM sampling, where samples are correlated.

## Candidate steps that survive
- Generate loosely first, evaluate later, with different prompts. Support: moderate.
- Longer divergent runs, since later ideas are more original. Support: moderate for humans, unverified for LLMs.
- Only open-ended tasks get divergence. Support: our own data, not this field.
- Do not trust a felt "aha" as evidence of correctness. Support: one study.
- No incubation-style delay step until tested. Support: effect small and contested.
