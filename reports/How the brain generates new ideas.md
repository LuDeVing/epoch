# Tuning Memory Search Sparks New Ideas

The newest research (2023–2026) converges on a single picture: the brain generates new ideas not from a dedicated "creativity center" but by running a controlled, foraging-style search over memory, with the default mode network (DMN) supplying candidate associations, executive control networks steering and filtering them, and the key individual-difference variable being how often and how cleanly the brain *switches* between those modes rather than how strongly they couple. The largest study to date (10 samples, N = 2,433) found that the rate of DMN–executive switching, with an inverted-U, predicts divergent thinking but not intelligence, and 2024–2025 work delivered the first causal evidence that this coupling matters: covert neurofeedback that trained DMN–executive coupling raised idea originality, while direct electrical stimulation of DMN nodes in epilepsy patients lowered it. Thought itself moves in clumps of roughly three related thoughts per topic punctuated by discrete jumps, and those internally generated jumps light up the same posterior-medial cortex that marks event boundaries in a film, while hippocampal sharp-wave ripples rise during vivid self-generated thought and, in a 2025 intracranial preprint, predicted the originality of ideas by transiently loosening medial prefrontal "schema" constraints. Insight has been re-cast as a precision-weighted prediction error: an Aha scales activity in the amygdala, anterior hippocampus and nucleus accumbens, roughly doubles the odds of remembering the solution five days later, and is reliably more accurate than analytic solving, yet it can be faked by semantic priming and barely yields to warnings. The semantic distance of an idea predicts rated creativity only up to a cosine-distance "sweet spot" of about 0.9–1.0, after which remoteness stops counting, which is why both human "far leaps" and LLM temperature hit a coherence ceiling. On the practical side, the best-supported levers are those that loosen top-down control without removing it: walking (d ≈ 0.9 for divergent thinking), incubation breaks (d ≈ 0.39), sleep-onset hypnagogia, and positive activating mood, whereas creativity training shrinks to about 0.3 SD after publication-bias correction, tDCS is null for divergent thinking on average, and placebo-controlled psychedelic microdosing is essentially null on objective measures. The field's biggest caveats are that nearly all causal evidence rests on small, single-session alternate-uses tasks whose link to real-world creative achievement explains only about 3% of variance, and that individual-level neural prediction has plateaued at r ≈ 0.3–0.4.

## Creativity recruits general-purpose networks that switch, not a dedicated circuit

The dual-network model of creativity, in which DMN regions (medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate, lateral temporal cortex) generate spontaneous associations and executive control network (ECN) regions evaluate and direct them, was built on correlational fMRI from 2015 onward ([Beaty et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep10964); [Beaty et al. 2018](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1713532115)). What changed between 2023 and 2026 is both the explanatory variable and the inferential standard. Chen and colleagues pooled **10 independent resting-state and divergent-thinking samples from Austria, Canada, China, Japan and the United States (N = 2,433)**, the largest and most ethnically diverse creativity-neuroscience dataset to date, and found that the **number of DMN–ECN switches predicted creative ability but not general intelligence, with an inverted-U relationship**; a task-fMRI validation in 31 people confirmed more switching during idea generation than in a control condition ([Chen et al. 2025, Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07470-9)). The authors interpret high creativity as a balance between segregated and integrated network states, maintained by frequent transitions. This reframing is reinforced from two directions. Rex Jung's lab, using macroscale cortical gradients in two community samples (N = 236 and 234), found that lifetime creative *achievement* tracked **greater functional differentiation between default and frontoparietal control networks**, consistent with the idea that well-separated networks can switch more cleanly ([Sassenberg, Jung & DeYoung 2025, Cerebral Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhaf046)). And Kenett's group showed, across two scanning sessions a week apart in 42 people, that reconfiguration within default-mode and salience subsystems characterizes idea *generation* but not *evaluation*, whereas whole-brain integration is higher during evaluation (p < 0.001), the first direct neural dissociation of the two stages that nearly every cognitive model assumes ([Kenett et al. 2025, Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09018-3)).

The causal turn is the other headline. Luchini, Beaty and colleagues randomized 30 students to covert fNIRS connectivity neurofeedback (participants were told only to "reveal" a hidden picture) that trained either mPFC–DLPFC coupling (a DMN hub plus an ECN hub) or a control mPFC–SMA coupling. Only the default–executive group improved alternate-uses originality (session × condition interaction F = 4.15, P = 0.025), while only the control group sped up on a go/no-go task, a behavioral double dissociation, and mPFC–DLPFC coupling during the task rose only in the trained group (|d| = 1.03) ([Luchini et al. 2025, Cerebral Cortex](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/35/4/bhaf065/8108110)). The authors state that before this work "all evidence so far has been correlational" ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40197641/)). Complementing this, Bartoli and colleagues recorded from 333 DMN and 107 frontoparietal electrodes in 13 epilepsy patients and found that the DMN showed higher gamma and lower theta than the frontoparietal network during the response phase of the alternate-uses task; in 9 patients, **direct stimulation of DMN nodes (anterior cingulate, superior frontal gyrus, middle temporal gyrus) selectively reduced the originality of ideas (P < 0.001) without changing fluency, flexibility or mind-wandering** ([Bartoli et al. 2024, Brain](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/10/3409/7695856)). A follow-up preprint with single-unit recordings from 10 patients found that about 41% of neurons were selective for creative versus arithmetic trials and that originality could be decoded from spiking (p = 0.007) ([Rabinovich et al. 2025, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.03.657648v1.full)).

A second causal theme is "release from control." Kutsche and colleagues mapped 857 participants' creativity-task coordinates onto a normative connectome and found that **86% of studies fell in a circuit defined by negative connectivity to the right frontal pole**; in 56 lesion patients, lateral frontal lesions (associated with impaired creativity) fell in regions negatively connected to this pole, while frontopolar lesions (associated with preserved or increased creativity) fell in positively connected regions, and frontotemporal-dementia patients with new-onset creativity showed atrophy negatively connected to creativity foci (d = −0.36, P = .04) ([Kutsche et al. 2025, JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2830230)). Their interpretation is that the right frontal pole actively suppresses novelty and that damaging or inhibiting it releases creative output, "paradoxical functional facilitation." Consistent with this, three within-subject tDCS experiments (N = 114) found that cathodal (inhibitory) stimulation of left inferior frontal gyrus combined with anodal right temporoparietal junction improved fluency (d = 0.42), flexibility (d = 0.59) and originality (d = 0.39) without reducing the rationality of ideas ([Huang et al. 2024, Cerebral Cortex](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/34/2/bhad531/7513583)). The combined picture from independent methods is that the DMN is needed to produce original associations at all, while prefrontal control gates how freely they surface.

### The "no creativity faculty" critique is partly right, and the field's answer is dynamics

The largest meta-analysis of creativity neuroimaging, an ALE analysis of **787 experiments and 17,228 participants**, found that creativity tasks activate a left-lateralized fronto-temporo-parietal set (inferior frontal gyrus, anterior insula, anterior middle temporal gyrus, inferior parietal lobule) that overlaps almost entirely with semantic cognition, controlled episodic retrieval and executive tasks; only small left inferior parietal and amygdala clusters were creativity-preferential, leading the authors to conclude that creativity "does not uniquely engage additional brain regions" (their "Cognitive Cornerstones Hypothesis"; about 76% of included tasks were verbal) ([Chan, Lambon Ralph & Robinson 2025, bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.19.639155v1.full)). Dietrich argues more pointedly that searching for a creativity substrate is a "creativity faculty fallacy," that shortened in-scanner alternate-uses tasks lack ecological validity, and that creative-versus-noncreative subtraction designs control away the very processes of interest ([Dietrich 2024, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1373299/full)). The strongest rebuttal is not localization but discriminant validity: switching predicted creativity but not intelligence ([Chen et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07470-9)), and a multivariate divergent-thinking pattern (positive weights in default and frontoparietal networks, negative in visual cortex) aligned with the principal sensory-to-abstract connectivity gradient and with dopamine-related receptor maps and genes ([Liu et al. 2024, Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07007-6)). Even so, predictive accuracy has plateaued: a 256-channel resting-EEG model predicted creative achievement at **r = 0.36 (R² = 0.12)** in 90 people and r = 0.35 in 41 external participants, but only in the gamma band and only under leave-one-out validation, which 5- and 10-fold schemes did not reproduce ([Chhade et al. 2024, Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06461-6)). Every causal study is small (9 to 40 patients, 15 per neurofeedback arm), single-session and alternate-uses-based, and the lesion-network-mapping method itself has been challenged as repeated sampling of one normative connectivity matrix ([Nature Neuroscience 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-02196-7)).

## Thoughts move in clumps of three and jump at default-network event boundaries

The dominant framework for spontaneous thought remains the Dynamic Framework of Thought, which holds that thought "moves freely" to the extent it escapes both deliberate constraints (cognitive control) and automatic constraints (salience, habit), placing mind-wandering, creative thought and dreaming on one family ([Christoff et al. 2016](https://scottbarrykaufman.com///wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Christoff-et-al.-2016.pdf)). Since 2023 this framework has acquired an operational proxy. Mallett, Christoff and colleagues embedded consecutive phrases from experience-sampling reports (N = 46, up to six probes a day for a week) and 1,199 dream-journal authors, and found that **semantic incoherence between consecutive thoughts correlated with self-rated freedom of movement (r = 0.13, p = 0.002), was higher off-task than on-task (medians 0.730 vs 0.696, CLES = 0.644), and was higher in non-lucid than lucid dreams**, though the dream effect was tiny (CLES = 0.510) ([Mallett et al. 2025, Philosophy and the Mind Sciences](https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/download/10307/11817?inline=1)). The small effect sizes mean self-reported "freedom" and text-derived variability capture overlapping but distinct constructs.

Three independent labs now describe the same structural fact with different pipelines: spontaneous thought is clumpy. Mildner and Tamir collected 2-minute think-aloud reports from 1,524 participants, transcribed them with Whisper, split them into thought units and clustered topics; the average report contained **10.8 thoughts and 5.6 topics, about three thoughts per topic**, with episodic detail peaking in the first thought after a topic jump and declining within a topic, and COVID-related "current concern" content peaking just before jumps, which the authors read as anxiety-laden content acting as an attractor; human agreement on where a thought ends was only fair to moderate (Fleiss' κ = 0.41) ([Mildner & Tamir 2024, PNAS Nexus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11210302)). Su, Honey, Chen and Lee then put the think-aloud paradigm in the scanner: 75 people verbalized unconstrained thought for 10 minutes during fMRI. Internally oriented thoughts (memory and future) made up 86.8% of all thoughts; consecutive thoughts were far more semantically similar than distant ones; and **strong topic boundaries activated medial frontal and parietal default and control regions, with posterior medial cortex peaking 4.5–10.5 s after a boundary and scaling with boundary strength, in a pattern resembling event boundaries during movie watching** and not explained by speech pauses. Crucially for the question of what steers thought, stronger default-A/control-B connectivity predicted more stable, clustered trajectories, while stronger parahippocampal–hippocampal connectivity predicted more variable ones (r = −.42) ([Su et al. 2025, Nature Communications](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.02.616300v1.full)). This dissociation is exactly the control-versus-medial-temporal division the Dynamic Framework posits, and it suggests a common segmentation mechanism for experience whether the input is perceptual or self-generated.

### What a transition looks like in the brain

Hippocampal replay supplies the raw content. Iwata and colleagues recorded hippocampal local field potentials for up to 15 days in 10 epilepsy patients with experience sampling and found that **sharp-wave ripple rates were higher during periods when thoughts were more vivid, more imaginable, less desirable and less tied to the external task** ([Iwata et al. 2024, Nature Communications](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11111804/)). Noradrenaline marks the breaks: Clewett, Huang and Davachi showed that context shifts engage pupil-linked locus coeruleus (LC) activity, that LC activity at boundaries selectively impaired memory for order across (not within) events, and that boundaries promoted temporal pattern separation in the dentate gyrus tracking LC responses; elevated background LC activity predicted weaker boundary responses, so "hyperarousal disrupts event segmentation" ([Clewett, Huang & Davachi 2025, Neuron](https://clewettlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2026/01/2025_Clewett_et_al_NEURON.pdf)). Together with the boundary signature in posterior medial cortex, a composite model emerges: medial-temporal replay generates candidate content and drives variability, phasic LC bursts reset and separate successive mental events, and posterior-medial default cortex registers the switch. This composite is a synthesis across studies, not a single finding, and no study has yet time-locked an LC burst to a self-reported spontaneous thought transition in humans.

### The thought trajectory is a temperature-controlled walk over a semantic map

Computational work has reframed the old debate between strategic two-stage foraging and a simple random walk. A 2025 preprint showed that a plain softmax random walk over modern embeddings (OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, enriched with descriptive sentences) reproduces the Marginal Value Theorem signature of human verbal fluency (slope −18.0, p ≈ 7e-6), whereas Metropolis–Hastings "decide-to-leave" rules did not ([Moore 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12759v1)). Because a random walk on a good map already looks like optimal foraging, conformity to foraging theory is weak evidence of strategy; the creativity signal lies in the *deviations*. Ovando-Tellez, Volle and colleagues tested 86 people on a polysemous-word fluency task with resting-state fMRI and replicated in 92 more: most retrieval followed the Marginal Value Theorem, but two deviant patterns carried the creativity signal. **Fast switching between clusters predicted remote-associate combination and was mediated by default-plus-control network connectivity, whereas slow, persistent clustering predicted divergent-thinking fluency and originality and relied on control, salience and attention networks** ([Ovando-Tellez et al. 2025, Imaging Neuroscience](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12628016/)). Nath, Dayan and Stevenson found the same two pathways, persistent (deep within few semantic spaces) and flexible (broad across many), with similar creativity scores in humans, while LLMs were biased toward one pathway ([Nath et al. 2024, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00899)). Hills and Kenett's entropy-modulation theory offers the control parameter: executive processes regulate the entropy of competitive retrieval, and higher activation strength and variance raise both entropy and speed, reproducing the "faster-and-further" phenomenon in which creative people produce ideas that are both quicker and more novel; strikingly, they argue that apparent differences in semantic network structure may themselves arise from this entropy-tuning process ([Hills & Kenett 2025, Psychological Review](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/187197)). Softmax temperature, retrieval entropy and Dynamic-Framework "constraint" are three vocabularies for the same knob: cognitive control sets it, salience biases the landscape with attractors.

## Ideas are built by controlled search over flexible memory, gated by hippocampal–prefrontal dynamics

The 2023 MemiC framework from Benedek, Beaty, Schacter and Kenett sets the agenda: creative ideas "usually come from purposeful memory retrieval rather than appearing unprompted," through four stages of memory search, candidate construction, novelty evaluation and effectiveness evaluation, drawing on both semantic and episodic memory ([Benedek et al. 2023, Nature Reviews Psychology](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-memory-in-creative-ideation/)). Beaty and Kenett's companion review argues that associative thinking, quantifiable as movement through semantic space, sits at the core of creativity ([Beaty & Kenett 2023, Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364661323000943)). The structure of that space matters. More creative people have semantic networks that are "less structured, more clustered and more interconnected"; this "flexible network" signature was replicated in high-vocabulary adults across two studies (N = 506 and 161), suggesting knowledge breadth and flexible access go together ([Li et al. 2024, Learning and Individual Differences](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-semantic-memory-networks-in-crystallized-intelligence/)), and in 77 older adults (mean age ~78) versus 81 younger adults, where typical aging brought more rigid networks and lower creativity, but **high-creativity older adults kept the flexibility of lower-creativity young adults** ([Campidelli et al. 2025, Cognition](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/creativity-the-fountain-of-youth-association-between-creativity-a/)). An fMRI study of 93 people judging semantic relatedness found that more creative participants rated remote concepts as more related, had more flexible networks, and showed *lower* activity in semantic-control regions when judging remote pairs, a neural-efficiency signature ([Herault et al. 2024, Communications Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06493-y)). Two caveats: global network metrics such as clustering coefficient and path length are less reliable as individual-difference measures than local ones ([Behavior Research Methods 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-023-02271-6)), and no 2023–2026 study has experimentally changed network structure and measured downstream creativity.

The hippocampus's role is being clarified as abstraction and constraint-loosening rather than literal episodic recall. In 40 epilepsy patients with 247 hippocampal contacts, **hippocampal ripple rate was higher during alternate-uses generation than during feature listing (P < 1e-41), predicted the originality of individual ideas (β = 0.162, P = 0.009), and correlated with everyday creativity (r = 0.419)**; ripples were accompanied by reduced medial prefrontal high-frequency activity and divergence of mPFC patterns from the task's schema-like pattern, leading the authors to propose that ripples transiently "loosen" prefrontal schema constraints to admit unconventional ideas ([He et al. 2025, bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.22.666046v1)). In a learning paradigm, Zhang and colleagues found that studying creative exemplars improved later creation, and the exemplar trials that predicted better creation showed *reduced* hippocampal representational dimensionality and increased hippocampal–ventrolateral prefrontal connectivity, independent of memory for the exemplars themselves, a mechanism they call "distinct from memory acquisition" ([Zhang et al. 2025, Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67157-x)). Meanwhile the constructive-episodic-simulation account, which holds that creative ideas recombine episodic details, looks weaker on the behavioral side: a 2026 preprint found semantic memory details alone explained **36% of divergent-thinking variance (β = 0.51)** while episodic memory was not a significant predictor (BF10 = 0.31), supporting a "semantic scaffold" hypothesis ([Thakral et al. 2026, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.08.704672v1.full)); three experiments found "no clear evidence" that episodic or semantic specificity inductions facilitate creative thinking ([Ahmed, Pauly-Takacs & Abraham 2023, PLOS ONE](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10237455/)); and a meta-analysis of 79 studies (N = 12,846) put the overall memory–creativity correlation at r = .19, driven mainly by semantic memory and verbal fluency ([Gerver et al. 2023, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review](https://link.springer.com/10.3758/s13423-023-02303-4)). The most defensible synthesis is that the hippocampus contributes generative recombination and abstraction over semantic-episodic content, gated by hippocampal–prefrontal dynamics.

### Remote is necessary but not sufficient: the 0.9–1.0 sweet spot

Mednick's remote-association idea is now testable at scale with embeddings, and the result is a refinement rather than a confirmation. Orwig, Beaty and Schacter fit generalized additive models to 3,448 alternate-uses responses, 2,430 question-asking responses and 4,376 metaphors and found that **human creativity ratings rose with semantic distance up to a cosine-distance threshold of 0.9–1.0, after which more distance bought nothing**; "extreme distance may hinder creativity by producing ideas that lack coherence or relevance" ([Orwig et al. 2025, CCN abstract](https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Orwig_2025_A_sweet_spot_creative_ideation_Non-linear.pdf)). Fine-tuned language-model raters predicted human creativity ratings of 4,589 metaphors at r = .72 versus r = .42 for raw semantic distance, implying that judges reward integration, not just remoteness ([DiStefano, Patterson & Beaty 2024, Creativity Research Journal](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10623591)). Forward flow, a chained-association measure of how far thought travels, correlated with alternate-uses creativity at r = .26 and r = .37 in two 2024 experiments, a modest predictor ([Baror, Aminoff & Kenett 2024, Memory & Cognition](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-024-01672-y)). Analogy is the mechanism that makes far combinations coherent. Holyoak, Ichien and Lu argue that creativity includes "the capacity to build new mental representations of unfamiliar situations by completing a partly specified relation or analogy" ([Holyoak et al. 2023, Creativity Research Journal](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10435749)); inducing a relational mindset with cross-domain analogies increased metaphor production ([George et al. 2025, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review](https://experts.boisestate.edu/en/publications/generating-distant-analogies-increases-metaphor-production/)); and in a 2026 human–LLM comparison, humans reliably benefited from cross-domain inspiration at nearly any source distance, tending to transfer surface features, whereas LLMs benefited only from very remote sources and transferred structural properties ([Liu et al. 2026, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19087)). Human analogy is also more robust: on counterfactual letter-string analogies human performance "remains high" while GPT models "decline sharply" ([Lewis & Mitchell 2025, TMLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14215)), though GPT-4 reached roughly human level when allowed to run code ([Webb, Holyoak & Lu 2025, PNAS Nexus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13070)). The LLM comparisons sharpen what is distinctive about human ideation: in a comparison of about 9,000–10,000 humans against more than 200,000 LLM observations on the Divergent Association Task, humans scored slightly higher on average with much greater variance and a higher ceiling, raising LLM temperature helped only until outputs "became nonsensical," and creative-genius persona prompts made models worse ([Huang et al. 2025, Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.shanghaitech.edu.cn/2025/0606/c14749a1111628/page.htm)). The temperature ceiling and the human distance plateau are the same constraint seen from two sides: beyond a coherence boundary, remoteness stops counting as creativity.

## Aha moments are prediction errors that double later recall

The neural story of insight has moved from a single right-temporal gamma burst 0.3 s before solution ([Jung-Beeman et al. 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC387276)) to a distributed, multi-system event. Becker, Sommer and Cabeza scanned 31 people solving 120 Mooney images and measured insight as the sum of suddenness, certainty and positive emotion ratings, which loaded on one factor. **Trial-level insight strength predicted amygdala (β = 0.18) and anterior-hippocampal (β = 0.13) activity but not posterior hippocampus; certainty, not pleasure, was the strongest predictor of both.** Insight also produced representational change in ventral occipito-temporal cortex (pattern similarity fell in lateral occipital cortex, t = −8.08, and fusiform gyrus, t = −6.09) and transient integration of an 18-node "solution network" (higher degree, clustering and shorter path length on high-insight trials). Five days later, **high-insight solutions were about twice as likely to be remembered as low-insight ones (OR = 2.10, 95% CI 1.80–2.45)** and 7.8 times more likely than unsolved items, and this memory advantage was statistically carried by representational change in visual cortex and anterior-hippocampal activity, not by amygdala activity or connectivity ([Becker, Sommer & Cabeza 2025, Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59355-4)). The same group showed that high-insight solutions drive nucleus accumbens activity, with the positive-emotion component uniquely predicting striatal response, and that insights nudge risk-taking (38% vs 33% risky choices afterward), framing the Aha as an internal reward signal ([Becker, Yu & Cabeza 2023, Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44293-2)). Salvi's lab adds physiology and structure: pupils dilate about 500 ms before insight but not analytic solutions, consistent with locus-coeruleus noradrenaline ([Salvi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7440842)), and in 38 people a higher propensity for insight solving was associated with *lower* fractional anisotropy in the left dorsal language pathway, interpreted as less-constrained semantic routes that permit remote associations ([Salvi et al. 2026, BMC Psychology, via PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/neuroscientists-identify-a-unique-feature-in-the-brains-wiring-that-predicts-sudden-epiphanies/)). The insight memory boost spills over to unrelated facts shown in the seconds around an Aha (N = 291) ([Salvi et al. 2024, CABN](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10557988)), implying a temporally extended encoding window rather than a property of the solved item. Across labs the signal begins 0.3–2 s before the button press, supporting the view that the solution is computed pre-consciously and the Aha is a read-out of a rapid, non-linear update. No 2023–2026 study measured dopamine or noradrenaline directly during insight; the catecholamine story is convergent but correlational.

Theory has crystallized around predictive processing. Laukkonen and colleagues propose the "Eureka heuristic": implicit restructuring via Bayesian model reduction yields a higher-order prediction error (the content), and dopaminergic precision-weighting of that error yields the feeling of confidence, pleasure and attentional capture, which lets the mind select ideas from the stream of consciousness and act fast under uncertainty ([Laukkonen et al. 2023, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/insight-and-the-selection-of-ideas/)). Dubey and Griffiths offer the metacognitive version, in which an Aha arises when a problem is solved well ahead of one's own predicted schedule, like a positive reward prediction error about one's ability ([Dubey et al. 2026, Cognition](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/aha-moments-correspond-to-metacognitive-prediction-errors/)). A preregistered test found that only the *surprise* dimension of the Aha tracked metacognitive prediction error, suddenness correlated negatively with it, and pleasure and certainty were unrelated, so the Aha is multidimensional: surprise indexes prediction error, pleasure maps to striatal reward, certainty to the hippocampus ([Becker, Wang & Cabeza 2024, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/32738)).

### Insight is usually right, but the feeling is a fallible heuristic

Insight-labelled solutions are reliably more accurate than analytic ones: in the 2025 Mooney data, correct solutions were 65% high-insight while incorrect ones were 60% low-insight, and high-insight solutions were faster (median 2.7 vs 5.1 s), replicating the foundational finding that 78% of insight versus 42% of analytic solutions were correct on a visual task ([Salvi et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5035115)). Yet the feeling can be manufactured. Semantic priming plus visual similarity produced feelings of insight for wrong anagram solutions in two experiments (N = 300) ([Grimmer et al. 2022](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/eliciting-false-insights-with-semantic-priming/)), susceptibility was unrelated to schizotypy, need for cognition or cognitive reflection, so "false insights may affect anyone" ([Grimmer et al. 2022, Consciousness & Cognition](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/illusion-of-depth/2022-grimmer.pdf)), and **simple warnings had no effect while detailed explanations only slightly reduced false Ahas** ([Grimmer et al. 2023, Cognition and Emotion](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-illusion-of-insight-detailed-warnings/991013093873602368)). Because the feeling resists deliberate control, it behaves like a fast precision signal generated below the level of reasoning, which is also why it can entrench false beliefs.

The classic stages of insight, impasse, incubation and restructuring, have each been qualified. Four preregistered experiments (N = 856) showed that impasse is multidimensional and dynamic, and that **insight was reliably elicited by resolving an impasse and also occurred without any impasse**, so there is "more than one path to insight" ([Ross & Arfini 2024, Cognition](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/9186/)). Chao, Hsieh and Wu separated de-fixation from insight in 349 and 105 participants: misleading cues cut accuracy sharply (d = −0.80) but left the Aha rate unchanged (59.8% vs 61.8%), and **Aha trials had greater mean semantic exploration distance than no-Aha trials while the number of thoughts was unrelated to Aha (r = −0.002)**; a simulation with nearly 2 million runs reproduced the pattern when exploration capacity was increased ([Chao et al. 2025, Communications Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11947100)). Escaping fixation raises accuracy; wide search produces the Aha. These are separable processes. Wiley and Danek's review makes the parallel point that restructuring and the Aha experience frequently dissociate ([Wiley & Danek 2024, Nature Reviews Psychology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-023-00257-x)), and eye-tracking work shows restructuring is often sudden but detectable only with non-linear statistics ([Graf et al. 2023, Journal of Intelligence](https://doaj.org/article/e0ff8278bee8442a8b63ef0abd2338eb)). The forgetting-fixation account of incubation remains the best-supported mechanism, with the caveat that "incubation" wrongly implies unconscious work when temporal delay, retrieval inhibition and context shifts each suffice ([Salvi, Wiley & Smith 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/emergence-of-insight/past-and-future-of-research-on-socalled-incubation-effects/4ADD1527830639E05DFB4D4E4A5766E2)).

## Walking, incubation and loosened control outperform drugs and training

The intervention literature sorts cleanly along the control axis established above: states and practices that loosen top-down constraint while preserving enough control to recognize a good idea help generation; those that increase control help selection and convergent solving. The table summarizes the best-supported effect sizes.

Walking is now the best-supported acute lever, and the fact that it helps divergent but not convergent performance fits the control-loosening account ([PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0347878)). Sleep onset is the most dramatic but least replicated. In the foundational study, **83% of people who spent at least 15 s in N1 sleep during a 20-minute "Edison nap" discovered a hidden mathematical rule, versus 30% who stayed awake and 14% who reached N2** ([Lacaux et al. 2021, Science Advances](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8654287)), and targeted dream incubation at sleep onset with the Dormio device (N = 49) increased both rated creativity and the semantic distance of outputs on tree-themed tasks, with sleep-plus-incubation beating sleep alone ([Horowitz et al. 2023, Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-31361-w)). But a preregistered 90-person nap study with a perceptual insight task found the benefit in **N2, not N1**, and in the aperiodic 1/f EEG slope rather than alpha or delta power ([bioRxiv 2024](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.24.600359.full.pdf)); no independent replication of either the Lacaux or Horowitz effect has appeared in five years. The two results are reconcilable if the relevant variable is a neural regime of reduced constraint reachable in N1 or N2 depending on task, rather than N1 itself. Mind-wandering during a break helps only under specific conditions: it predicted gains in creative writing after a 10-minute break ([McDaniel et al. 2025, Scientific Reports](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12241419/)) but had no effect on divergent fluency in an 85-person study, where mind-wandering *with* awareness helped easy repeated items and wandering *without* awareness hurt novel ones ([Du, Gordon & Tolmie 2025, Brain Sciences](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12190454/)). Boredom's reputation is not supported: a scoping review of 27 studies found the claim that boredom increases creativity "not sufficiently supported" ([Zeißig et al. 2024](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rev3.3470)), and induced boredom produced *poorer* divergent thinking in two experiments ([Nettinga et al. 2023](https://www.boredomsociety.com/jbs/index.php/journal/article/view/18)). The better mechanism is undemanding activity, not boredom.

Pharmacology is weak. Beversdorf's 2025 systematic review found that of 29 dopamine studies of divergent tasks, 6 showed better performance with higher dopamine, 1 worse, 3 null, with genetic findings "interaction-dependent" and motivation a likely confound; the one replicable pattern is noradrenergic, where 17 of 21 studies showed better *convergent* performance with beta-blockade, mostly under stress ([Beversdorf 2025, Behavioral Sciences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12466433/)). The psychedelic microdosing trials are the clearest demonstration of expectancy in this literature: participants felt more creative on LSD dose days with no objective change ([Murphy et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-024-06680-z)), and the one positive psilocybin signal failed within-program replication ([Prochazkova et al. 2025, via PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/major-new-study-finds-psilocybin-microdoses-improve-the-quality-of-creative-ideas-but-not-the-quantity/)). Full-dose psilocybin acutely increases spontaneous "creative insights" while impairing deliberate task creativity ([Mason et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8032715/)), and in 28 psychedelic-naive adults 25 mg raised EEG entropy, which predicted next-day psychological insight and one-month cognitive flexibility, though "psychological insight" there is a self-report construct, not problem-solving ([Lyons et al. 2026, Nature Communications, via PsyPost](https://psypost.org/psychedelic-science-breakthrough-increased-brain-entropy-from-psilocybin-predicts-lasting-psychological-insight-and-well-being)).

### Traits matter less than states, and the AI partner homogenizes

Trait predictors are modest. Intelligence correlates with divergent thinking at **r = .25 across 112 studies (N = 34,610)**, rising to .31–.37 with "be creative" instructions and originality scoring ([Gerwig et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8167550/)); broad retrieval ability is the strongest cognitive correlate at r = .47 ([Miroshnik et al. 2023, Intelligence](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016028962300020X)); openness r = .20 ([Grajzel et al. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886923002611)); working-memory capacity only r = .08 ([WMC meta-analysis 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36662145/)). That retrieval ability outranks working memory is consistent with the controlled-memory-search account. Subclinical ADHD traits link to higher creative achievement, with deliberate mind-wandering mediating the link in two samples totaling 750 people, though these conference data are not yet peer reviewed ([Fang et al. 2025, ECNP](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/10/251012054608.htm)). The sobering context is that divergent-thinking scores share only about 3% of variance with real-world creative achievement ([DT–creative achievement meta-analysis, PACA 2024](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361681918_Divergent_thinking_and_creative_achievement-How_strong_is_the_link_An_updated_meta-analysis)), so most of the effects above concern the laboratory construct.

Generative AI reveals a classic property of human ideation at population scale: anchoring. In a randomized study of ~300 writers, access to five GPT-4 story ideas made stories about 8% more novel and 9% more useful, with less creative writers gaining most, but **embedding similarity among stories rose by 10.7%**, an individual gain and a collective loss ([Doshi & Hauser 2024, Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290)). A reanalysis of ChatGPT-assisted ideation found that 94% of ChatGPT-group toy ideas shared overlapping concepts and nine participants independently named their toy "Build-a-Breeze Castle" ([Meincke, Nave & Terwiesch 2025, Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02173-x)); a 19-experiment meta-analysis confirmed a significant homogenization effect that persists into subsequent tasks ([de Rooij & Biskjaer 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2026.2726451)); and 22 LLMs produced responses far more similar to one another than more than 100 humans were to each other ([Wenger & Kenett 2026, PNAS Nexus](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1123936)). Group work shows the same generation-versus-selection split: videoconferencing inhibited the production of creative ideas in a lab study and a five-country field experiment but not the selection of good ones ([Brucks & Levav 2022, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04643-y)), and across 20 million papers and 4 million patents remote teams were consistently less likely to make breakthroughs and less likely to join idea-conceiving tasks ([Lin, Frey & Wu 2023, Nature](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v623y2023i7989d10.1038_s41586-023-06767-1.html)). The actionable design, unchanged since the brainstorming meta-analyses, is to generate individually or in person before converging, and to bring the LLM in after independent generation.

## Conclusion

The deepest shift of 2023–2026 is that "creativity" has stopped being a place in the brain and become a control policy over memory search. Three previously separate literatures now describe the same knob: cognitive control sets the entropy or temperature of retrieval, hippocampal replay and ripples supply candidate content while transiently loosening prefrontal schemas, and noradrenergic bursts punctuate the stream into discrete events. What distinguishes creative people is not a fixed tendency to leap far but the ability to alternate between persistent local search and fast switching, to reach the semantic sweet spot without crossing into incoherence, and, in the case of insight, to let a pre-conscious update complete before acting on a precision signal that is usually, but not always, trustworthy. The same policy explains why the effective practical levers (walking, incubation, hypnagogia, activating mood) all reduce constraint without abolishing it, why interventions that add control (mindfulness, DLPFC stimulation) favor convergent selection over generation, and why every LLM comparison finds a coherence ceiling on the machine side and a variance advantage on the human side.

The weak points are equally clear and should temper enthusiasm. The causal evidence is new, consistent in direction, and thin: single-session neurofeedback in 30 students, stimulation in 9 patients, retrospective lesion mapping with a contested method, all read out on a 12-second alternate-uses task that shares about 3% of variance with real creative achievement. Individual-level neural prediction has stalled at r ≈ 0.3–0.4 regardless of modality or sample size, the N1 "creative sweet spot" has no independent replication after five years, and no human study has yet pharmacologically manipulated dopamine or noradrenaline during insight or time-locked a locus coeruleus burst to a spontaneous thought transition. The next phase of the field will be decided less by bigger scanners than by pre-registered replications, naturalistic think-aloud trajectories as outcome measures, and tests of whether network switching predicts ideas that matter outside the laboratory.
